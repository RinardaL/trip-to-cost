4 Days in Berlin: Wall History, Museum Island & Potsdam

The Brandenburg Gate, Wall remnants, Museum Island and Potsdam's Sanssouci Palace: four days in one of Europe's most open, creative capitals.

Ideal SeasonMay – Sep
Est. Budget$750-1,000
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at BER & the Airport Express into Mitte

Berlin Brandenburg Airport (BER) sits about 18 km south-east of the center, and the Airport Express (FEX) and regional RE7 and RB14 trains run from the station beneath the terminals to Berlin Hauptbahnhof in roughly 30 minutes, with the slower S-Bahn as a backup.

🚆 ~30 min to Hauptbahnhof 💵 ~€4-5 for an ABC single ticket 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time
Afternoon

The Brandenburg Gate & the Reichstag dome

Finished in 1791, the Brandenburg Gate was left stranded in the border zone during the Cold War and is now the symbol of reunification. A five-minute walk north, Norman Foster's glass dome on the Reichstag gives a free panoramic view over the government quarter, but you need to register online with the Bundestag in advance and bring photo ID.

🚶 ~5 min walk between the two 💵 Free (Reichstag dome needs advance registration) 🕐 Mid-afternoon, check current slot times
Evening

Unter den Linden to the Spree & Museum Island at dusk

Walk east along the linden-lined Unter den Linden boulevard to the Spree, where the Museum Island colonnades and the green dome of Berlin Cathedral light up against the water, then pick a dinner spot in the side streets of Mitte.

🚶 ~25 min walk from the Reichstag 💵 Free to wander 🕐 Around sunset, when the lights come on
Morning

Berlin Wall Memorial at Bernauer Straße

This is the one place where a section of the border strip survives with its Wall and death-zone layout, and the open-air exhibition stretches about 1.4 km along Bernauer Straße, with a free viewing platform at the Documentation Center looking out over the strip.

🚇 ~15 min by S-Bahn or U-Bahn from Mitte 💵 Free 🕐 Morning, allow about 2 hours (indoor centers are closed Mondays, check current hours)
Afternoon

East Side Gallery murals

The longest surviving stretch of the Wall, about 1.3 km along Mühlenstraße, was turned into an open-air gallery by artists in 1990 and now carries over 100 murals, from Thierry Noir's bright cartoon-like heads to Dmitri Vrubel's famous "Fraternal Kiss". The murals are protected, so admire them without adding your own marks.

🚇 ~25 min by S-Bahn or U-Bahn to Ostbahnhof 💵 Free, open around the clock 🕐 Afternoon, allow about 90 minutes to walk it all
Evening

Oberbaumbrücke at sunset & dinner in Kreuzberg

At the far end of the gallery, the double-deck red-brick Oberbaumbrücke, finished in 1896, carries the U1 line over the Spree into Kreuzberg. Cross it for dinner at Markthalle Neun, a restored 1891 market hall, where the Thursday evening Street Food Thursday is a Berlin institution (check current hours).

🚶 ~15 min walk from the East Side Gallery across the bridge 💵 ~€12-20 per person 🕐 Early evening, around sunset
Morning

Museum Island: the Neues Museum & Altes Museum

The Neues Museum holds the 3,300-year-old bust of Nefertiti, and the Altes Museum next door covers Greek and Roman antiquities. The Pergamon Museum's main building is closed for renovation, so these two are the best antiquities stops right now (check current status).

🚶 ~10 min walk from Friedrichstraße station 💵 ~€14 per museum (check combined passes) 🕐 Right at opening, book a timed ticket ahead
Afternoon

Berlin Cathedral & the Hackesche Höfe

Climb the cathedral's roughly 270 steps for a dome-level view over Mitte, then walk north to Hackescher Markt to wander the Art Nouveau tilework of the Hackesche Höfe, a chain of eight connected courtyards full of cafes, galleries, and small shops.

🚶 ~5 min walk from Museum Island, ~12 min on to Hackescher Markt 💵 ~€10 cathedral entry, courtyards free 🕐 Afternoon, check current cathedral hours
Evening

Fernsehturm (TV Tower) at Alexanderplatz

Built by East Germany in the 1960s, the Fernsehturm is 368 meters tall and Berlin's highest structure, with an observation deck at about 203 meters and a revolving restaurant just above. Go up around sunset and book a timed slot online to avoid the queue.

🚆 1 stop on the S-Bahn from Hackescher Markt (or a ~15 min walk) 💵 ~€25-30 for the observation deck 🕐 Around sunset, with a pre-booked slot
Morning

Train to Potsdam & Sanssouci Palace and Park

The RE1 regional train reaches Potsdam from Berlin Hauptbahnhof in about 25 minutes. Frederick the Great's yellow rococo summer palace, finished in 1747, sits above vineyard terraces in a UNESCO-listed park. The paths are free to walk, while the palace interior is by timed ticket and closed on Mondays (check current hours).

🚆 ~25 min on the RE1, ABC ticket 💵 ~€14-20 for the palace interior 🕐 Catch a train by about 9:00, book the timed entry ahead
Afternoon

Potsdam's Dutch Quarter

These 134 red-brick gabled houses were built in the 1730s for Dutch craftsmen and form the largest ensemble of Dutch-style buildings outside the Netherlands, now lined with cafes, galleries, and small shops. If time allows, the New Palace at the far end of the park and Cecilienhof, where the 1945 Potsdam Conference took place, make good add-ons.

🚶 ~20 min walk or a short bus from Sanssouci 💵 Free to wander, ~€10-15 for lunch 🕐 Early afternoon, allow 1-2 hours
Evening

Return to Berlin & transfer to BER

Take the RE1 back to Berlin Hauptbahnhof, then the Airport Express, RE7, or RB14 on to BER in roughly 30 minutes. Both Potsdam and the airport sit in fare zone C, so one ABC ticket covers the whole trip with connections.

🚆 ~25 min to Hauptbahnhof, then ~30 min to BER 💵 ~€4-5 on an ABC single ticket 🕐 Leave Potsdam at least 3.5 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is included in German menu prices, but rounding up or adding 5 to 10% for good service is standard in restaurants.
  • Tell the server the total you want to pay when they bring the bill ("make it 25"), rather than leaving coins on the table.
  • For coffee, a bakery, or a döner stand, a euro or simply rounding up is plenty, and tipping taxi drivers 5 to 10% is appreciated but optional.

👗 Dress Code & Layers

  • Berlin is casual, and jeans and sneakers work almost everywhere, including most dinner spots and museums.
  • Wear shoes with real support, since the cobblestones of Mitte, the long walk along the East Side Gallery, and the Sanssouci park paths add up to a lot of steps.
  • Bring layers, as a sunny afternoon can turn cool by the Spree or on the TV Tower deck, and a rain layer is wise even in summer.

🚇 Getting Around Berlin

  • The BVG network of U-Bahn, S-Bahn, trams, and buses covers the whole city, and the AB ticket zone includes every central sight in this itinerary.
  • BER airport and Potsdam both sit in zone C, so buy an ABC ticket for Days 1 and 4. The Deutschlandticket (a monthly subscription of roughly €60) covers all of it, but only pays off if you cancel it before it renews.
  • Paper tickets must be validated at the stamping machines on the platform or in the vehicle before you ride, and digital tickets in the BVG app are valid straight away. Fare inspectors are common, and the fine is €60.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • The Memorial to the Murdered Jews of Europe lies just south of the Brandenburg Gate. Treat it, and the Berlin Wall Memorial, as places of remembrance by not climbing on the stelae, posing for jokey photos, or being loud.
  • Nazi symbols and gestures are illegal in Germany, so never mimic them, even as a joke.
  • Pickpockets work crowded spots like Alexanderplatz, Hauptbahnhof, the Brandenburg Gate, and busy U-Bahn lines, so keep bags zipped in front of you. Pedestrians also wait for the green walking figure even on empty streets, and red-paved sidewalk strips are bike lanes.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in German

  • Hallo: hello
  • Danke schön: thank you very much
  • Die Rechnung, bitte: the bill, please
  • Sprechen Sie Englisch?: do you speak English?

💳 Money & Payments

  • Germany uses the euro (EUR). Cards and phone payments work in most hotels, museums, and larger restaurants, but plenty of smaller places, including some cafes, bakeries, market stalls, and bars, are cash-only or only take local Girocard debit cards.
  • Carry €50 to €100 in cash, ask "Kann ich mit Karte zahlen?" before you order, and use ATMs attached to banks rather than standalone machines in tourist areas.
  • Most supermarkets and shops close on Sundays, but train stations like Hauptbahnhof and late-night Spätis stay open. Bottles carry a small deposit (Pfand) that you get back at any supermarket return machine.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Use free landmarks and a museum pass

The Brandenburg Gate, Reichstag dome, Wall Memorial, East Side Gallery, and Sanssouci park are free, so Days 1 and 2 cost almost nothing. For Day 3, a Museum Pass Berlin (roughly €30 for three days) covers Museum Island and dozens more, so check whether it beats paying per museum.

🥐

Eat your big meal at lunch

Many Berlin restaurants run weekday Mittagstisch lunch deals that cost noticeably less than dinner, and a döner kebab in Kreuzberg is a filling meal for around €7 to €9.

🚶

Join a tip-based walking tour

Guided walks through Mitte and the Wall sites leave daily from the Brandenburg Gate area, and you pay what the tour was worth at the end.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🛩️

Tempelhofer Feld

The former Tempelhof airport is now a huge public park where you can walk, skate, or cycle straight down the old runways, with wide open sky in the middle of the city.

🎤

Mauerpark on a Sunday

This park follows the former border strip in Prenzlauer Berg, with a Sunday flea market and an open-air karaoke session in the amphitheater in summer, and it is a short walk from the Wall Memorial.

📡

Teufelsberg

This hill in the Grunewald forest is topped by an abandoned Cold War listening station covered in street art, with a small entry fee and sweeping views over the city (check current opening days).

✈️ Flights

  • Berlin Brandenburg Airport (BER) is the city's only commercial airport, about 18 km south-east of the center.
  • The Airport Express (FEX) and regional RE7 and RB14 trains reach Hauptbahnhof in roughly 30 minutes from the station under the terminals.
  • The S-Bahn (S9 and S45) is slower, but it runs to stations in the east and south and is covered by the same ABC ticket.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Metered taxis can be hailed at ranks or booked through apps like FREENOW, and Uber and Bolt also operate in Berlin.
  • A taxi between BER and central Berlin is often roughly €55 to €75, so the train is much cheaper and about as fast.
  • Taxis are best kept as a late-night backup, since Berlin's night buses and many U-Bahn lines run through the night on weekends.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Not needed for this itinerary, since every stop from the Brandenburg Gate to Potsdam is covered by U-Bahn, S-Bahn, tram, or regional train.
  • The inner city is a low-emission zone, and parking is scarce and often costly in Mitte and Kreuzberg.
  • Skip the rental and put the budget toward museum tickets and a good dinner instead.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Mitte: the most convenient base, with the Brandenburg Gate, Museum Island, and the TV Tower all within walking distance.
  • Kreuzberg: a lively food and nightlife base near the East Side Gallery and Oberbaumbrücke, often a little cheaper than Mitte.
  • Prenzlauer Berg: leafy and cafe-filled, close to the Wall Memorial and Mauerpark, with easy tram and U-Bahn links.
  • Charlottenburg: calmer and more traditional in the west, with shopping on Kurfürstendamm and good rail links via Zoologischer Garten.

🎫 Transit Passes

  • An AB single ticket covers the central city, and day and 7-day AB tickets (the 7-day pass is roughly €40 to €45) work out cheaper than singles on a busy trip.
  • Add the C zone (ABC) for BER and Potsdam on Days 1 and 4, or buy an ABC day ticket for Potsdam day.
  • The Berlin WelcomeCard bundles transit with discounts on attractions, and the Deutschlandticket works across Germany but is a monthly subscription.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Germany is in the Schengen Area, so standard Schengen entry rules apply, and visa-exempt visitors should check current requirements such as ETIAS before traveling.
  • The currency is the euro, and shops are mostly closed on Sundays, so buy groceries and essentials on Saturday.
  • Travel insurance is worth it given the pre-booked timed tickets for the Neues Museum, Fernsehturm, and Sanssouci, which are often non-refundable.
