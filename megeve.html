4 Days in Megève: Gentle Slopes, Gourmet Dining & Sleigh Rides

Four days in the French Alps' most low-key luxury ski village are enough to glide down beginner-friendly slopes, ride a horse-drawn sleigh through snow-dusted lanes, and close out each day with a spa hour or a celebrated table, without a single technical climb along the way.

Ideal SeasonDec – Mar
Est. Budget$1,900-2,600
Duration4 Days
PaceRelaxed to Moderate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Private or shared transfer from Geneva

Megève has no commercial airport of its own, so the trip starts with a scenic 1.5-hour drive from Geneva through the Arve valley, climbing steadily until the first chalet roofs and the Mont Blanc massif come into view.

🚐 ~1.5 hr transfer from Geneva Airport 💵 ~€50 shared shuttle, or ~€140 private transfer for up to 4 🕐 Book a midday transfer to arrive with daylight for a first walk
Afternoon

Check in and a first walk through Place de l'Église

Megève's historic core has been closed to through traffic for decades, so the square by the church and the streets radiating from it make for an easy, unhurried first stroll past chalet facades and boutique windows.

🚶 Flat walk through the pedestrian center 💵 Free to explore 🕐 Mid-afternoon, once you've checked in and dropped bags
Evening

Fondue or raclette at a chalet restaurant

A first dinner of melted Beaufort cheese or raclette scraped tableside is as much a local tradition as the mountains themselves, best paired with a bottle of Savoie wine after a long travel day.

🚶 Most restaurants are a short walk from the center 💵 ~€40-50 for a fondue or raclette dinner 🕐 Early evening, before the well-known addresses fill up
Morning

Cable car up to Rochebrune and a first run

The Téléphérique de Rochebrune lifts you directly from the village center to wide, gently pitched slopes built for learners and families, none of the exposure or technical terrain that defines resorts further up the valley.

🚡 ~8 min cable car from the village center 💵 ~€35 half-day lift pass, ~€65 group ski lesson 🕐 Morning, while the beginner runs are freshly groomed
Midday

A long lunch on a sun terrace

Megève's mountain restaurants are built for lingering, deck chairs and blankets on a snow-covered terrace, a plate of tartiflette, and a view back over the valley instead of a rushed cafeteria stop.

🚶 On the mountain, a short ski or walk from the lift 💵 ~€40-55 per person 🕐 Midday, while the terrace is still in the sun
Afternoon

Gondola to Mont d'Arbois for the Mont Blanc panorama

The Télécabine du Mont d'Arbois climbs to a wide viewing terrace facing the entire Mont Blanc massif, a panorama earned without a single technical step, then an easy blue run or the same gondola carries you back down.

🚡 ~15 min gondola from the village 💵 Included on the Évasion Mont-Blanc lift pass 🕐 Mid-afternoon, for the clearest light on the massif
Morning

A traditional sleigh ride through the snow

Horse-drawn carriages and sleighs have worked as village transport in Megève since the 1920s, and a booked ride out along the snow-covered lanes and forest edges is the unhurried, low-key alternative to anything requiring skis.

🛷 ~45 min sleigh ride, booked through the village stables 💵 ~€90-120 per sleigh, shared among riders 🕐 Late morning, when the light is best for photos
Midday

Browsing Rue Charles Feige and Rue Ambroise Martin

Megève has drawn a well-heeled, low-key crowd since it was developed as a ski resort in the 1920s, and the boutiques lining its two main shopping streets, from ski-wear ateliers to jewelers, still reflect that quietly upscale identity.

🚶 Flat walk through the two main shopping streets 💵 Free to browse 🕐 Early afternoon, once the slopes close for the day
Evening

A reserved table at one of Megève's celebrated restaurants

Megève punches well above its size for fine dining, anchored by Emmanuel Renaut's three-Michelin-star Flocons de Sel, and a proper tasting menu here is worth building the whole evening, and the reservation, around.

🚕 Short taxi or walk depending on the address 💵 ~€120-320 per person depending on the restaurant 🕐 Reserve weeks ahead for peak winter season
Morning

A slow morning at the hotel spa

With the skiing and sleigh ride behind you, a spa morning, sauna, steam room, or a wood-fired hot tub with the peaks still visible through the steam, is the fitting way to ease into the last day.

🚶 Most hotel spas are on-site 💵 ~€70-100 spa day pass where not already included 🕐 Morning, before the spa fills up with other departing guests
Midday

A few laps on the Place de la Résistance rink

The open-air rink right in the village center is an easy, low-key way to spend a last hour outdoors, skate rental included, with the same chalet rooftops and mountain backdrop as everywhere else on this trip.

🚶 In the village center, no transport needed 💵 ~€10-15 including skate rental 🕐 Midday, leaving the afternoon free to pack
Afternoon

Last coffee and the transfer back to Geneva

One more espresso on a village terrace, then the same 1.5-hour drive back down through the Arve valley to Geneva, with the Mont Blanc massif receding in the rearview mirror.

🚐 ~1.5 hr transfer to Geneva Airport 💵 ~€50 shared shuttle, or ~€140 private transfer 🕐 Leave at least 4 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is included by law in French restaurant and hotel bills (service compris), so tipping isn't obligatory the way it is in the US.
  • Rounding up or leaving a few euros for excellent service is appreciated, especially at Michelin-tier tables, but never demanded.
  • Ski instructors and mountain-restaurant staff don't expect tips beyond the usual French rounding-up gesture.

👗 Dress Code

  • Megève's identity is chic mountain, not backcountry: technical layers are fine on the slopes, but village dinners and bars lean toward polished apres-ski style, think good boots and a nice coat over a battered puffer.
  • Layer for fast-changing alpine weather regardless: a moisture-wicking base, an insulating mid-layer, and a windproof or waterproof shell.
  • Even a casual lunch on a mountain-restaurant terrace expects a slightly tidier look than typical ski-resort cafeteria fare elsewhere.

🛷 Getting Around the Village

  • The historic center around Place de l'Église is largely pedestrian, easy to cross end-to-end on foot in about 15 minutes.
  • Horse-drawn carriages and sleighs have operated as village taxis since the 1920s and still do, alongside a free local shuttle bus connecting the center to the ski lifts.
  • Both Rochebrune and Mont d'Arbois, Megève's two main ski areas, have lift stations within a short walk of the village center.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Restaurant tables, especially at well-known addresses, fill up weeks ahead in ski season, book before you land, not after you arrive.
  • Sun exposure on the slopes is stronger than it looks even on a gentle beginner run, sunscreen and eye protection matter regardless of skill level.
  • Lifts occasionally pause for high wind, build slack into any plans that hinge on the Mont d'Arbois cable car specifically.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in French

  • Bonjour: hello
  • Merci beaucoup: thank you very much
  • L'addition, s'il vous plaît: the bill, please
  • Une table réservée au nom de...: a table reserved under the name of..., worth knowing given how far ahead the best tables book

💳 Money & Payments

  • France uses the euro (€); cards and contactless payments are accepted almost everywhere, including lift-ticket counters and mountain restaurants.
  • Megève sits at the upscale end of French ski resorts, on par with or above Switzerland's grand-hotel towns, budget accordingly for dining and lodging.
  • Many top restaurants require a credit card to hold a reservation and may charge a fee for late cancellations.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Ski a half-day pass instead of a full day

Megève's easygoing pace suits a half-day lift pass on Rochebrune or Mont d'Arbois just fine, meaningfully cheaper than a full-day pass built for resorts with far more terrain to cover.

🍽️

Take the fixed-price weekday lunch menu

Many mountain-restaurant terraces run a set-price weekday lunch menu that comes in well under ordering à la carte, without sacrificing the view.

🏨

Book a half-board hotel rate

Given how much a Michelin-caliber dinner runs here, a half-board hotel package takes the pressure off booking every single meal out.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🚡

Take the Jaillet gondola for a quieter panorama

The smaller Télécabine du Jaillet gets a fraction of the Mont d'Arbois crowds for a similarly sweeping view over the village and the Mont Blanc massif.

⛷️

Try the valley's cross-country trails

Groomed Nordic ski loops thread the valley floor below the main slopes, a quiet, lift-line-free alternative for an easy morning on skis.

⛪

Walk up to the Calvaire chapels

A short path above the village climbs past a row of 18th-century chapels to a rooftop view over Megève, often nearly empty even in high season.

✈️ Flights

  • Geneva Airport (GVA) is the closest major gateway, about 70 km and roughly 1.5 hours by road from Megève.
  • Chambéry Airport is closer in a straight line for some winter charters, but Geneva has far more scheduled flights and remains the practical choice for most visitors.
  • Megève has its own small altiport used for private aviation, but it isn't a commercial airport.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Taxis and pre-booked private transfers run the Geneva-to-Megève route directly; shared shuttles cost less but add stops along the way.
  • Within the village, horse-drawn carriages and sleighs plus a free local shuttle bus cover most short trips.
  • Ride-hailing apps have limited coverage in this part of the Alps; hotels and the taxi rank near the center are the more reliable option.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • A car isn't necessary for this itinerary, the village center, both ski areas, and most restaurants sit within walking distance or a short shuttle ride.
  • Renting does help for day trips beyond Megève, toward Annecy or Chamonix, if extending the stay.
  • Winter tires or snow chains are legally required on alpine roads in season, factor that into any rental during the Dec-Mar window.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Village center, around Place de l'Église: closest to restaurants, boutiques, and the pedestrian streets on this itinerary.
  • Near the Rochebrune or Mont d'Arbois lift stations: shortest walk to the slopes each morning.
  • Just outside the center: often the address for Megève's grand luxury chalets and five-star hotels, a short taxi or shuttle from everything else.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • Megève is a landlocked mountain village at over 1,100 meters elevation, so boat trips simply aren't part of this itinerary.
  • The nearest lake excursions are around Annecy or Lake Geneva, both over an hour away, better suited to a separate add-on trip.
  • Lac de Javen, the small alpine lake above the village, is a summer photo stop, not a boating destination, and is typically snowed over in ski season.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Reserve restaurant tables, especially any Michelin-starred address, well before arrival, Megève's dining reputation means the best tables go early.
  • France is part of the Schengen area, so some non-EU travelers should still double-check passport and visa rules before booking.
  • Travel insurance covering ski activities and lift disruptions is worth adding given the Day 2 mountain excursions.
