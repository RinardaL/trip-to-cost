4 Days in San Francisco: An Itinerary and Budget for First-Timers

The Golden Gate Bridge and the Presidio, Alcatraz and the cable cars, Chinatown, North Beach and the Mission: four days in San Francisco with BART from the airport and a Muni pass.

Ideal SeasonSep – Nov (warmest and clearest; summer brings fog)
Est. Budget$800-1050
Duration4 Days

4-day San Francisco itinerary

How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at SFO & BART into the city

SFO is about 21 km south of downtown. BART runs from the airport station to Powell Street and Montgomery in around 30 minutes for about $11 (check current). Weekday trains start near 5 AM and run until midnight. Tap a contactless card or Clipper at the gates. A taxi or rideshare costs roughly $50 to $70 (check current).

🚆 ~30 min by BART to Powell or Montgomery 💵 ~$11 BART, ~$50-70 taxi or rideshare (check current) 🕐 Plan around your landing time, then drop your bags
Afternoon

Chinatown: Dragon Gate & Stockton Street

Enter through the Dragon Gate at Grant Avenue and Bush Street, about 10 minutes on foot from Powell Street station. Grant Avenue is mostly souvenirs, so also walk Stockton Street, where locals buy produce and bakery snacks. Dim sum or egg tarts cost about $10 to $15 (check current). Many small places take cash only.

🚶 ~10 min on foot from Powell Street BART 💵 Free to walk, ~$10-15 for dim sum (check current) 🕐 Early afternoon, when the bakeries and markets are busy
Evening

North Beach, City Lights & a first dinner

North Beach is the old Italian quarter, about 10 minutes on foot from Chinatown. City Lights bookshop on Columbus Avenue is free to browse, and Washington Square is a pleasant place to rest. A pasta or pizza dinner costs about $20 to $35 (check current). Coit Tower is a steep climb, so check its hours before you go.

🚶 ~10 min on foot from Chinatown 💵 Free sights, ~$20-35 for dinner (check current) 🕐 Dinner from about 6 PM, book ahead at weekends
Morning

Walking the Golden Gate Bridge

Take a bus such as Muni 28 or a rideshare to the bridge's south end (check the route). Pedestrians use the east sidewalk, open 5 AM to 6:30 PM in winter and later in summer. Walking to the first tower and back takes about an hour. Crossing is free. Fog and wind are common, so wear layers.

🚌 Muni bus or rideshare to the south end of the bridge 💵 Free to walk, ~$2.85 per Muni ride on Clipper (check current) 🕐 Mornings, before the fog and the crowds build up
Afternoon

Crissy Field & the Palace of Fine Arts

Walk east along the flat Crissy Field waterfront path, with the bridge behind you, then continue to the Palace of Fine Arts, a rotunda and lagoon left from the 1915 exposition. The whole walk is about 4 km, and buses are an option. Entry to the grounds is free. Pack lunch or buy it in the Presidio.

🚶 ~4 km on foot, or take a bus part of the way 💵 Free 🕐 Afternoon light on the Palace is soft, around 4 PM
Evening

Alamo Square, the Painted Ladies & Hayes Valley

The hill in Alamo Square Park gives the classic view of the Painted Ladies, the row of Victorian houses on Steiner Street, with downtown behind. Muni 21 passes nearby (check the route), or take a rideshare. Then walk about 10 minutes to Hayes Valley for dinner, usually $25 to $40 (check current).

🚌 Muni 21 or a rideshare, then ~10 min on foot to Hayes Valley 💵 Free view, ~$25-40 for dinner (check current) 🕐 Late afternoon light, dinner from about 6:30 PM
Morning

Alcatraz Island

Book ahead, only through Alcatraz City Cruises, the official seller. The adult day tour is $47.95 per the National Park Service (check current), sold up to 90 days ahead and often sold out. Ferries leave from Pier 33 on the Embarcadero. Choose the earliest slot, allow 2.5 to 3 hours with the audio tour, and wear layers.

⛴️ Ferry from Pier 33, a short ride across the bay 💵 ~$47.95 adult, ferry and audio tour included (check current) 🕐 Earliest slot you can get, booked weeks ahead
Afternoon

Pier 39 & Fisherman's Wharf

From Pier 33 it is about 10 minutes on foot along the Embarcadero to Pier 39, where sea lions rest on floating docks, free to watch (numbers vary by season). Fisherman's Wharf is touristy, but a sourdough bread bowl of clam chowder is a fair lunch at about $15 to $20 (check current).

🚶 ~10 min on foot along the Embarcadero 💵 Sea lions free, ~$15-20 for lunch (check current) 🕐 Lunchtime, when the docks are busiest with sea lions
Evening

Powell-Hyde cable car & Lombard Street

Walk to Hyde and Beach, the Powell-Hyde cable car terminus, and ride up Hyde Street to Lombard Street. A single ride is $9 (check current), and a Muni Visitor Passport includes cable cars. Get off at Lombard for the crooked block. Queues at the Powell Street end are long, so board here or mid-route.

🚋 Cable car up Hyde Street, ~15 min to Lombard Street 💵 ~$9 per ride, or included in a Visitor Passport (check current) 🕐 Late afternoon, before the evening rush
Morning

Mission murals: Balmy Alley

Ride BART to 24th Street Mission, then walk about 10 minutes to Balmy Alley, a lane of murals painted since the 1970s. It is free and takes under an hour. Mission Dolores Park is about 25 minutes' walk north. Pick up a burrito for lunch, about $13 to $16 (check current).

🚇 BART to 24th Street Mission, then ~10 min on foot 💵 Free, ~$13-16 for a burrito (check current) 🕐 Morning light, when the lane is quiet
Afternoon

Golden Gate Park: Conservatory of Flowers

Golden Gate Park is free to enter, and a Muni bus or light rail from the Mission reaches it in about 30 minutes (check the route). The Conservatory of Flowers, a Victorian glasshouse from 1879, charges for entry (check current). Stow Lake has a waterfall and rowboats. Allow 2 to 3 hours and bring a layer.

🚌 ~30 min by Muni from the Mission (check route) 💵 Park free, Conservatory entry extra (check current) 🕐 Afternoon, when the glasshouse is open (check current)
Evening

Ferry Building & BART to SFO

The Ferry Building Marketplace is a food hall on the Embarcadero, and the Saturday farmers market runs 8 AM to 2 PM (other days vary, check current). Then ride BART from Embarcadero to SFO in about 30 minutes for around $11 (check current). Aim to reach the airport 3 hours before an international flight.

🚆 ~30 min by BART from Embarcadero to SFO 💵 ~$11 BART, ~$12-20 for a food hall meal (check current) 🕐 Leave 3 hours before an international flight

San Francisco: what to know before you go

💵 Tipping & Sales Tax

  • Tipping is expected in the US. In restaurants leave 18 to 20 per cent of the bill, or a bit more for very good service. Tip bartenders about $1 to $2 per drink and taxi or rideshare drivers about 10 to 15 per cent.
  • Menu prices exclude sales tax, which is roughly 8.6 per cent in San Francisco (check current), so the bill is higher than the menu suggests.
  • Some San Francisco restaurants add a surcharge for local employee health costs, often shown as a percentage on the bill. That is separate from the tip, so check the receipt before adding one.

🥖 Eating & Drinking

  • Sourdough, clam chowder in a bread bowl, Mission-style burritos and dim sum are the classics. Dungeness crab is in season from about November to June, and most places sell it at market price.
  • You must be 21 to buy alcohol and many bars check ID, so carry your passport or a photo copy. Drinking alcohol in the street or in most parks is not allowed.
  • Many restaurants are busy on Friday and Saturday evenings, so book a table or arrive early. Water is served free, and tap water is safe to drink.

🌫️ Weather, Layers & Transport Manners

  • San Francisco is cool and foggy in summer, often 15 to 20 °C, and warmest from September to October. Always carry a jacket, even on a sunny morning, since the temperature can change within a few blocks.
  • On Muni and cable cars, hold on tight, let people off before boarding and give up priority seats. Do not stand on the cable car running boards near the grips.
  • Clipper cards or contactless payment work on Muni and BART. Keep your ticket until you leave the system, since inspectors check.

⚠️ Safety, Scams & Etiquette Warnings

  • Car break-ins are common in tourist areas such as Fisherman's Wharf and the Painted Ladies. Never leave bags or valuables visible in a parked car, and if you hire a car, take everything with you.
  • Some areas, including parts of the Tenderloin, Civic Center and Sixth Street, can feel unsafe, especially after dark. Walk with purpose, avoid empty streets at night and check recent reviews before booking a hotel.
  • Keep phones and wallets out of sight on Muni, BART and busy streets, and be wary of unlicensed drivers offering rides at the airport. Use official taxi ranks or a rideshare app.
  • Check your government's travel advice and the US State Department (travel.state.gov) before you travel. In an emergency, dial 911.

🗣️ Useful Local Words & Phrases

  • "Muni" (the city bus, tram and cable car network), "BART" (the regional train), "Clipper" (the transit card)
  • "The City" (San Francisco, as locals call it), "the Mission" (a neighbourhood), "the Wharf" (Fisherman's Wharf)
  • "Could I have the check, please?" (the bill), "To go, please" (takeaway), "Is tip included?" (service)
  • "Do you take cards?", "Where is the nearest BART station?", "How much is it?"

💳 Money & Payments

  • The US uses the dollar. Cards and contactless work nearly everywhere, and some small places in Chinatown or the Mission are cash only, so carry some small notes.
  • Use bank ATMs rather than standalone machines, and if a terminal offers to charge you in your home currency, choose dollars.
  • Hotel bills often add a city occupancy tax of about 14 per cent plus possible resort or destination fees, so check the total before you book (check current).

Insider tips for San Francisco

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Buy a Visitor Passport, not single rides

A single Muni ride is $2.85 with Clipper and lasts 120 minutes. A 3-Day Visitor Passport is $35 and includes cable cars, which cost $9 per ride (check current). Fares are due to rise on 4 January 2027.

🍞

Eat the local value meals

A burrito in the Mission, dim sum in Chinatown and a food hall lunch at the Ferry Building cost far less than a sit-down dinner. Aim for about $15 for lunch and spend more on one good dinner.

🏨

Compare hotel total cost

San Francisco hotels add about 14 per cent tax and sometimes daily fees. Compare the full price, consider a hostel or a hotel away from Union Square, and stay near a BART or Muni line.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌉

View the bridge from Battery Spencer

Battery Spencer, on the Marin side, gives a wide view of the bridge and the city. Allow a half day by car or bus, and check the weather first, since fog often hides the view.

🌲

Add Muir Woods if you have a spare day

Muir Woods needs a reservation for a parking space or a shuttle, booked at gomuirwoods.com, plus a $15 entrance fee for ages 16 and up (check current). Book ahead, since slots sell out.

🌇

Watch sunset from Twin Peaks

Twin Peaks gives a free view over the whole city, best at sunset on a clear evening. It is windy and cold at the top, so bring a jacket, and take a rideshare back after dark.

Travel services for San Francisco

✈️ Flights

  • San Francisco International (SFO) is the main airport, about 21 km south of downtown, with a BART station inside the airport complex. Oakland (OAK) and San Jose (SJC) are alternatives, sometimes cheaper, with their own transport links.
  • BART takes about 30 minutes to downtown and costs about $11 (check current). Weekday trains from SFO run from about 5 AM to midnight, so plan a different ride for an early flight.
  • Compare fares into all three airports, but add the cost of getting to the city before choosing the cheapest.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Taxis, Uber and Lyft all serve SFO, and a ride into town costs roughly $50 to $70 (check current). Pick-ups use designated areas, so follow the airport signs.
  • Rideshare fares rise at busy times. For a group of three or four, it can cost about the same as BART.
  • Waymo driverless cars also operate in parts of the city (check current). Use official taxis or rideshare apps rather than unlicensed offers.

🚇 Public Transport & Tickets

  • Muni runs buses, light rail and cable cars. A single ride is $2.85 with Clipper or a card (120 minutes), and the cable car is $9. The 1, 3 and 7-day Visitor Passports are $15, $35 and $47 (check current).
  • BART runs to the airport and the Mission. Fares depend on distance, and you can tap a contactless card or Clipper at the gates.
  • SFMTA has announced Muni fare changes from 4 January 2027, so check sfmta.com/fares before you travel.
  • Keep your bag zipped and in front of you on crowded buses and trains.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Union Square: central, near Powell Street BART and cable cars, but pricey and busy at night.
  • Fisherman's Wharf: handy for Alcatraz and Pier 39, but touristy and further from BART.
  • Financial District and Embarcadero: good for BART to the airport and the Ferry Building, quiet at weekends.
  • Marina and Cow Hollow: calmer and near the Presidio, with fewer budget options.
  • Hostels in Union Square and North Beach cost less (check current prices).

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Visitors can use an eSIM or a local prepaid SIM, and many cafés, hotels and libraries offer Wi-Fi.
  • Cards work almost everywhere, but carry small notes for small shops and some Chinatown stalls.
  • Use bank ATMs and avoid standalone exchange booths.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Check entry rules for your passport before booking. Many visitors need an ESTA or a visa, and the fee is set by US authorities (check the official CBP and State Department sites).
  • Book Alcatraz as soon as you know your dates, through Alcatraz City Cruises only. Tickets can be bought up to 90 days ahead.
  • You do not need a car. Parking is costly and car break-ins are common, and BART, Muni and walking cover this itinerary.
  • US sockets are type A and B at 120 V, so most visitors from Europe need an adapter.

How much does a 4-day trip to San Francisco cost?

About $800-1,050 per person for 4 days, roughly $200-265 a day, excluding international flights. The estimate covers lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and real costs vary with the season and your spending habits.
