💴 Tipping Etiquette

  • Tipping is not expected anywhere in Japan: restaurants, taxis, or hotels.
  • Leaving cash on a table may cause staff to chase you down to return it.
  • Exceptional service is better thanked with words than money.

👘 Dress Code

  • Remove shoes before entering temples, ryokans, and many homes/restaurants.
  • Cover shoulders and knees when visiting temples and shrines.
  • Avoid overly revealing clothing at religious sites, even in summer.

🚇 Transit Nuances

  • Stand on the left on Tokyo escalators, right in Osaka.
  • Phone calls on trains are considered rude, so keep devices on silent.
  • Get a Suica or Pasmo IC card immediately for seamless transit and konbini payments.

⚠️ Safety & Scam Warnings

  • Japan is very low-crime, but Kabukicho touts still run overpriced bar scams, so just walk past.
  • Earthquakes are common; note the nearest evacuation signage in your hotel.
  • Carry your passport or residence card: random ID checks, while rare, do happen.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases

  • Sumimasen: excuse me / sorry (use constantly)
  • Arigatou gozaimasu: thank you
  • Kore o kudasai: "this one, please" (great for pointing at menus)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Japan is still cash-heavy outside major chains, so carry yen for small shops and shrines.
  • 7-Eleven ATMs reliably accept foreign cards, 24/7.
  • IC cards (Suica/Pasmo) work for nearly all transit and convenience stores.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Get the JR Pass before you land

Only sold to foreign visitors before arrival (or at a markup after), and it covers most Shinkansen rides.

🍱

Eat at depachika food halls

Department store basements sell discounted bento in the evening: restaurant quality, convenience store price.

🏨

Business hotel chains beat Airbnb

Toyoko Inn and APA Hotels are cheaper and more central than most short-term rentals.

✈️ Flights

  • Narita (NRT) and Haneda (HND) are Tokyo's two airports; Haneda is closer to the city and often cheaper to reach.
  • The Narita Express or Keisei Skyliner run directly into central Tokyo in about an hour from Narita.
  • Book your JR Pass exchange voucher before you fly: it's the same train system you'll use to get in from the airport.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Uber barely operates in Japan outside a few Tokyo zones; regular metered taxis are the norm and easy to hail.
  • Doors open and close automatically, so don't touch them yourself.
  • No tipping, and fares are steep, so trains and subways remain the better choice for most of this itinerary.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Not needed for this itinerary: the Shinkansen and subway networks cover Tokyo and Kyoto completely.
  • Parking is scarce and expensive in both cities, and city driving involves narrow streets and unfamiliar signage.
  • Skip it entirely unless extending into rural areas like the Japanese Alps that trains don't reach.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Shinjuku or Shibuya: central for the Tokyo days, well-connected by train, close to Day 1's evening izakaya scene.
  • Asakusa: a quieter, more traditional base near Senso-ji for the old Tokyo day.
  • Near Kyoto Station or Gion: puts you close to Nishiki Market, Pontocho, and the Fushimi Inari/Higashiyama days.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • Not part of this itinerary: the Tokyo-to-Kyoto route runs entirely by Shinkansen and local trains.
  • There's no ferry leg to plan for or budget around on this trip.
  • If you have extra days, a Lake Ashi cruise near Hakone is a common add-on, but it's outside this 7-day plan.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Many nationalities get visa-free entry for short tourist stays in Japan, but confirm your country's requirement before booking.
  • The JR Pass must be purchased before you land (or at a markup after) and covers most of the Shinkansen rides in this plan.
  • Get a portable Wi-Fi router or eSIM on arrival, and travel insurance is worth it given the flights, trains, and timed-entry tickets involved.
