4 Days in Copenhagen: Tivoli Christmas, Nyhavn Lights & Danish Design

Tivoli's Christmas lights, Nyhavn and Kongens Nytorv after dark, glögg and æbleskiver, Danish design and a winter day at Louisiana: four cosy days in Copenhagen.

Ideal SeasonNov – Dec (Christmas Season), May – Sep
Est. Budget$1,350-1,850
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival & transfer into central Copenhagen

Copenhagen Airport (CPH) sits about 8 km south of downtown, and the driverless Metro M2 reaches Kongens Nytorv, right by Nyhavn, in around 13 minutes. The metro runs 24 hours a day, with trains every 15 to 20 minutes at night. Snow and de-icing can delay flights, so check your flight status before you leave.

🚇 ~13 min on the M2 metro 💵 ~40 DKK for the 4-zone airport fare (check current) 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time
Midday

Amalienborg Palace & the changing of the Guard

The Danish royal family's winter residence is four identical rococo palaces around an octagonal courtyard. The changing of the guard takes place in the palace square at noon, so wrap up warm for the wait. The Amalienborg Museum is open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 16:00 until 23 December.

🚶 ~10 min walk from Nyhavn 💵 Free to watch, museum ~125 DKK online or 135 DKK at the door (free with the Copenhagen Card) 🕐 Guard change at 12:00 noon, museum closed Mondays and 24-25 December
Evening

Nyhavn, Kongens Nytorv lights & a heated canal boat

Dusk falls around 3:30 to 4 PM in midwinter, so Nyhavn's 17th-century townhouses are lit by late afternoon. Its Christmas market has no announced 2026 dates, so check first. Then board a Stromma Classic Canal Tour from Nyhavn, about an hour on covered, heated boats in winter.

⛴️ Canal boats depart from Nyhavn, covered and heated in winter 💵 Canal tour from ~149 DKK (the Copenhagen Card covers the Ved Stranden departure, other piers may add a surcharge) 🕐 Late afternoon departure, check the last sailing for your date
Morning

Design Museum Denmark or the National Gallery (SMK)

Design Museum Denmark traces a century of Danish furniture and industrial design inside a former royal hospital on Bredgade. It is open Tuesday to Sunday 10:00 to 18:00 (Thursday until 20:00) and closed on Mondays and on 24 and 25 December. For paintings, the National Gallery (SMK) holds Denmark's largest art collection.

🚶 Bredgade, ~10 min walk from Nyhavn and Amalienborg 💵 140 DKK entry (free with the Copenhagen Card) 🕐 Opens 10:00, allow about two hours
Afternoon

Strøget, the Nytorv market & Danish design shopping

Europe's longest pedestrian shopping street runs from City Hall Square to Kongens Nytorv, lined with Danish names like Georg Jensen, Royal Copenhagen and Hay. Julemarked Nytorv & Gammeltorv, beside Strøget, is listed for 6 November to 21 December 2026, with free entry and a free ice rink. Try gløgg, the Danish mulled wine.

🚶 Gammel Strand metro is ~5 min from the market, Strøget runs to Kongens Nytorv 💵 Free entry, gløgg and food priced per stall (check the boards) 🕐 Afternoon, since market hours differ by weekday
Evening

Tivoli Gardens at Christmas

Opened in 1843, Tivoli is one of the world's oldest amusement parks, and its 2026 Christmas season is listed as 13 November to 3 January. After dark a free Christmas light show runs in front of the Nimb building from 4:30 PM. Tivoli has been closed on 24 December in recent seasons, so check the calendar.

🚶 Directly across from Copenhagen Central Station 💵 Entry from ~200 DKK (priced by date), rides extra, entry included with the Copenhagen Card 🕐 After dark for the lights, hours vary by day (check tivoli.dk)
Morning

Freetown Christiania & its Christmas market

This self-governing commune, founded in 1971 in a former military barracks, has car-free lanes and murals. Its Christmas market in the Grey Hall (Den Grå Hal) has around a hundred stalls and free entry. Past dates fell in mid-December, so check Christiania's channels. Carry some cash, and do not photograph Pusher Street.

🚶 ~5 min walk from Christianshavn metro 💵 Free to enter, bring cash for stalls 🕐 Late morning, market hours vary (check current)
Afternoon

A winter walk through Christianshavn's canals

Christianshavn's canals are best done on foot in winter, past gabled warehouses and houseboats, with the spire of the Church of Our Saviour as the landmark. The spire climb closes in snow, ice and high wind, so check first. Cross Knippelsbro for harbour views, then warm up in a cafe with a cinnamon snail.

🚶 Christianshavn metro (M1/M2), then on foot 💵 Free to walk, spire ticket extra when it is open 🕐 Early afternoon, while there is daylight
Evening

The Opera House, Playhouse waterfront & a julefrokost

The Opera House on Holmen and the Royal Danish Playhouse beside Nyhavn both sit on the harbourfront. Harbour buses dock at Nyhavn, and line 993 shuttles to Holmen around Opera performances, with fewer winter departures (check Movia). It is dark by about 4 PM, so walk in the evening, and book a julefrokost, the traditional Danish Christmas lunch, well ahead.

⛴️ Harbour bus 991/992 to Nyhavn, line 993 shuttle to Holmen 💵 Harbour bus fares as a normal bus ticket; julefrokost menus vary (check current) 🕐 After dark, and book the julefrokost weeks ahead
Morning

Bakery breakfast & Danish Christmas sweets

A cinnamon snail or a spandauer at a neighbourhood bakery is a good start to the last day. In December look for æbleskiver, round pancake puffs served with jam and powdered sugar; klejner, twisted dough pastries; and brunkager and pebernødder, spiced Christmas cookies. Have them with gløgg or coffee.

🚶 Neighborhood bakeries around Indre By 💵 ~40 DKK per pastry and coffee (check current) 🕐 Mid-morning, before the train to Louisiana
Midday

Louisiana Museum of Modern Art

Louisiana is about 40 minutes north of the city by train, set in a clifftop garden overlooking the Øresund strait. In winter the galleries are the draw. It is open Tuesday to Friday 11:00 to 22:00 and weekends 11:00 to 18:00, closed Mondays, and closed on 24, 25 and 31 December and 1 January.

🚆 ~40 min direct train to Humlebæk, then a 10-15 min walk 💵 145 DKK adult entry (free with the Copenhagen Card) 🕐 Opens 11:00, allow at least two hours
Afternoon

Transfer to the airport

The Metro M2 runs back to Copenhagen Airport from central stations such as Kongens Nytorv and Nørreport in about 13 to 15 minutes, and it runs 24 hours a day, so even a late return from Louisiana works. Allow extra time if snow is forecast, and check your flight status before you leave.

🚇 ~13-15 min on the M2 metro 💵 ~40 DKK for the 4-zone airport fare (check current) 🕐 Leave at least 3 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is already built into prices at Danish restaurants and bars, so tipping is not expected the way it is in North America.
  • Rounding up the bill or leaving loose change is plenty for casual meals and coffee.
  • At a nicer sit-down dinner or a julefrokost, an extra 5 to 10% for excellent service is a kind gesture, never an obligation.

🧥 Winter Dress & Hygge

  • December days sit around 0 to 5°C, damp and windy off the harbour, and the sun sets around 3:30 to 4 PM. Wear a windproof, insulated coat, hat, gloves and a scarf.
  • Wear waterproof boots with grip, since wet cobbles and icy pavements are slippery. Copenhagen is casual and understated, and smart-casual is enough for a julefrokost or an evening on the Opera House waterfront.
  • Hygge, the Danish habit of candles, warm drinks and lingering over a table, is what December is for, so a cafe will not rush you out.

🚇 Getting Around Copenhagen

  • The driverless Metro (M1 to M4), S-trains and buses share one zone-based ticket system. Buy tickets in the Rejsekort or DSB apps, at platform machines or at 7-Eleven kiosks, and a metro ticket also works on buses and harbour buses.
  • Copenhagen Airport is in zone 4. The metro runs 24 hours a day, with trains every 15 to 20 minutes at night, which helps on the late-closing days around Christmas.
  • Buses, S-trains and regional trains run holiday timetables around 24 to 26 December, so check DSB and Movia, and expect fewer harbour bus departures in the winter timetable.

⚠️ Safety, Ice & Etiquette Warnings

  • Never walk or stand in a bike lane, the red-painted lanes are for cyclists moving at real speed, and locals keep cycling all winter, even in the dark.
  • Pavements and cobbles ice over, so walk slowly near the harbour and canals. Photography is banned on Christiania's Pusher Street, put the camera away as soon as you see the signs.
  • Copenhagen is genuinely low-crime, but keep an eye on bags at crowded spots like Nyhavn, Strøget and the Christmas markets.
  • Most shops must close on public holidays and on 24 December, apart from small grocery shops and those at the airport and stations, and many sights close too, so plan 24 to 26 December ahead.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Danish

  • Hej: hi
  • Mange tak: thank you very much
  • Regningen, tak: the bill, please
  • Glædelig jul: Merry Christmas
  • En gløgg, tak: a mulled wine, please

💳 Money & Payments

  • Denmark uses the Danish krone (DKK), not the euro, despite being an EU member.
  • Contactless card and phone payments are accepted almost everywhere, including bakeries, transit machines and most market stalls.
  • Cash is rarely needed, but carry some for Christiania's Christmas market, where not every stall accepts cards or MobilePay.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Do the sums on a Copenhagen Card

The card covers Tivoli entry (rides not included), the Amalienborg Museum, Design Museum Denmark, Louisiana, a canal tour and, on the Discover version, public transport. Prices start around 589 DKK for 24 hours (check current), so add up your own sights before buying.

⛸️

Skate for free at the Nytorv market

Julemarked Nytorv & Gammeltorv advertises a free ice rink with free skate hire and free entry, so you can skate without paying for rental or tickets. Check the rink hours before you go.

🥐

Ask for the late-day bakery discount

Many neighborhood bakeries mark down unsold pastries and bread late in the afternoon, so ask what is on offer before they close. Check at each shop, since policies differ.

🚶

Join a tip-based walking tour

Several companies run free, guide-led walks through Indre By, and you pay what the tour was worth to you at the end. Winter schedules can be shorter, so check departure times first.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌳

Assistens Cemetery

A park-like cemetery in Nørrebro with the graves of Hans Christian Andersen and Søren Kierkegaard, and a quiet winter walk away from the markets.

🎄

Højbro Plads market

A smaller market is listed for Højbro Plads from 14 November to 20 December 2026, with stalls from Denmark, Greenland and the Faroe Islands. It is new this year, so check the dates before you go.

🪟

Magasin du Nord's Christmas windows

The department store on Kongens Nytorv is known for its long-running Christmas window displays, which are best seen after dark, a few minutes from Nyhavn.

✈️ Flights

  • Copenhagen Airport (CPH) is Scandinavia's busiest hub, about 8 km south of downtown.
  • The M2 metro reaches Kongens Nytorv and central stations in around 13 to 15 minutes for a fraction of a taxi fare, and it runs 24 hours a day.
  • Winter snow and de-icing can delay flights, so check your flight status the night before and leave a buffer on the last day.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Metered taxis can be flagged on the street or booked through apps like Taxi 4x35 or Drivr.
  • Taxis are relatively expensive in Copenhagen, best kept as a backup rather than a default option.
  • Ride-hailing apps operate in a more limited way here than in many cities, so a licensed taxi app is usually the more reliable choice.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Not needed for this itinerary, every stop from Nyhavn to the Louisiana Museum sits on the metro, train, bike lane, or walking network.
  • Parking in Indre By is scarce, expensive, and largely unnecessary given how compact the center is, and winter roads add to the hassle.
  • Skip the rental and put the budget toward museum tickets and a julefrokost instead.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Indre By (city center): puts Nyhavn, Amalienborg, Strøget and the Nytorv market within easy walking distance.
  • Vesterbro: a trendy base near Tivoli and Central Station, with the city's best food scene right outside.
  • Christianshavn: canal-side and quieter at night, closest to Day 3's freetown. Book early for the Christmas market weeks.

🚲 Bikes in Winter

  • Locals keep cycling all winter, so the bike lanes stay busy in the dark and cold.
  • App-based rentals from operators like Donkey Republic exist, but snow, ice and a 4 PM sunset make walking and the metro easier for this itinerary.
  • If you do ride, use lights, wear gloves, and keep to the red lanes.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Denmark is in the Schengen Area, so standard Schengen entry rules apply for non-EU visitors.
  • Remember the currency is the Danish krone (DKK), not the euro, even though Denmark is an EU member.
  • Check 24 to 26 December closures: Tivoli has been closed on 24 December, and the Amalienborg Museum, Design Museum Denmark and Louisiana are closed on 24 and 25 December. Travel insurance is worth it given the pre-booked tickets, which are usually non-refundable.
