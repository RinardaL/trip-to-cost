Germany Itineraries and Trip Costs

5 complete day-by-day trips in Germany, with the price per person up front.

Last updated: November 2026

TripToCost has 5 Germany itineraries: Bavaria, Berlin, Nuremberg, Dresden, Munich. Each is a complete short trip planned day by day from your airport arrival, with the price shown up front.

Budgets run from $460 to $1,400 per person for the whole trip, excluding international flights. The cheapest per day is Dresden at about $150 a day, and the most expensive is Bavaria at about $200 a day.

Trips run from 4 to 6 days. Prices cover lodging, meals, local transport and activities for two people sharing a room, and every page has a day picker with a price for each trip length.

Four market cities and one road trip

Four of these five trips are city breaks built around Christmas markets, and they are the cheapest per day. Dresden is the lowest at $150, Nuremberg and Munich come in at $155, and Berlin at $165. Dresden's Striezelmarkt is traced back to 1434, and the Nuremberg plan adds a day trip to Bamberg.

Berlin is the broadest city trip, adding Wall history, Museum Island and a day in Potsdam. It is also the only one of the four with a spring and early autumn season. Munich adds the Harry Potter market on the Praterinsel, but that free pop-up runs for only nine days, from 28 November to 6 December 2026.

Bavaria is the odd one out: six days, a rental car for the castles, the Zugspitze cable car and the Königssee, at $200 a day. It is the only trip here where driving matters.

Winter markets or a spring road trip?

The market trips run in November and December, with Munich listed from late November to 24 December. Daylight is short at that time: the Dresden page notes about eight hours in December, so the plans put sights in the daytime and markets after dark.

Berlin is the only city that also lists April to June and September, which makes it the sensible choice for a visitor who cannot travel in winter. Bavaria is listed for May, June and September, which suits a drive between castles and mountain cable cars.

If your dates fall in late November or December, any of the four cities works. In May or September, the choice narrows to Berlin for a city or Bavaria for a longer trip with a car.

Linking the trips and trimming the cost

Bavaria begins in Munich, so it overlaps with the Munich trip. If you want castles and markets together, start in Munich and collect the car only when the castle days begin, as the Bavaria page suggests. Nuremberg lies in Franconia, in the same state. Berlin and Dresden are both in the east, and direct trains run between them in roughly two hours.

Entry to the main Munich market is free, though mulled wine usually comes in a deposit mug that is refunded when you return it. Pick one sit-down meal per trip, such as the Franconian dinner in Nuremberg or the Hofbräuhaus in Munich, and use market stalls and bakeries for the rest.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a trip to Germany cost?

Our 5 Germany itineraries cost from $460 to $1,400 per person for the whole trip, which is about $150 to $200 a day. Prices cover lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and exclude international flights.

Which Germany itinerary is the cheapest?

Dresden ($460-720), Nuremberg ($480-740), Munich ($500-720) are the three lowest, per person and excluding flights.

How many days do I need in Germany?

Our Germany itineraries run from 4 to 6 days, and the most common length is 4 days. Each page has a day picker with a price for every length from one day up to the full plan.

When is the best time to visit Germany?

It depends on the trip, and each itinerary lists its ideal season. For example: Bavaria, May – Jun, Sep; Berlin, Nov – Dec (Christmas Markets), Apr – Jun, Sep; Nuremberg, Nov – Dec (Christmas Markets). The table above shows the ideal season for every Germany trip.

Do I need a rental car in Bavaria?

Not at the start. The Bavaria itinerary rides the airport train into Munich for two days in the city, then picks up a car for the castle day at Neuschwanstein, the Zugspitze and the lakes.

Can I reach Bamberg from Nuremberg without a car?

Yes. The Nuremberg itinerary has a Bamberg day trip by train, about 40 minutes each way, with its UNESCO-listed old town and the local smoked beer at lunch.
