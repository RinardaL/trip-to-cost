3 Days in Brussels: Grand Place, Comics & the Atomium

Belgium's capital packs a UNESCO-listed square, the world's densest concentration of comic-strip art, a giant illuminated molecule from 1958, and a chocolate-and-beer culture that takes itself seriously, into three genuinely varied days.

Ideal SeasonApr – Sep
Est. Budget$650-900/person
Duration3 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival & transfer into central Brussels

Brussels Airport (BRU) sits about 12 km northeast of downtown, and a direct train reaches Brussels-Central, right by the Grand Place, in around 20 minutes, making it one of the easiest capital-city arrivals in Europe.

🚆 ~20 min direct train to Brussels-Central 💵 ~€11.60 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time
Afternoon

The Grand Place, Town Hall & Manneken Pis

Victor Hugo called this gilded, guild-lined square "the most beautiful in Europe," and its 96-meter Gothic Town Hall tower still anchors what UNESCO recognizes as a near-perfectly preserved 17th-century marketplace, a five-minute walk from the small bronze Manneken Pis fountain.

🚶 Central to almost everything else in Brussels 💵 Free to visit the square 🕐 Early afternoon, before evening crowds build
Evening

Galeries Royales Saint-Hubert & a cone of frites

Europe's oldest covered shopping arcade, opened in 1847, is a glass-vaulted stroll past chocolate shops and cafes, and the natural detour afterward is a paper cone of frites from a proper friterie, twice-fried in the style Belgians insist the French never quite mastered, then back to see the Grand Place lit up for the night.

🚶 ~3 min walk from the Grand Place 💵 ~€4-5 for a cone of frites 🕐 After sunset, once the guildhall lights come on
Morning

The Comic Strip Route & the Belgian Comic Strip Center

Belgium is genuinely the heartland of European comic art, home to Tintin, the Smurfs, and Gaston Lagaffe, and Brussels has painted more than 60 building-sized murals across the city center as a self-guided Comic Strip Route, anchored by the Belgian Comic Strip Center inside a former Victor Horta department store.

🚶 Murals scattered through the city center on foot 💵 Free for the murals, ~€12 for the Center 🕐 Late morning, allow at least two hours
Afternoon

A Belgian chocolate-making workshop

Belgium has protected its "Belgian chocolate" label by law since 1894, and a hands-on praline-making workshop with a small chocolatier goes well beyond the tasting-shop crawl, tempering couverture and molding your own box to take home.

🚶 Workshops run throughout the historic center 💵 ~€45-65 per person for a workshop 🕐 Mid-afternoon, book a slot ahead
Evening

The Atomium at sunset

Built for the 1958 World's Fair as a 165-billion-times-magnified iron crystal, this 102-meter cluster of steel spheres remains Brussels' most photographed structure, and its top-sphere panorama is at its best as the sky turns pink over the surrounding Heysel park.

🚇 ~20 min metro (Line 6) from the center 💵 ~€22 entry 🕐 Late afternoon into sunset
Morning

Mont des Arts & the Royal District

The terraced gardens of Mont des Arts frame one of the city's best rooftop views, stepping up toward the Royal Palace, the Magritte Museum, and the Coudenberg's excavated medieval palace ruins in the compact museum quarter above.

🚶 ~10 min walk from the Grand Place 💵 Free gardens, ~€12 for the Magritte Museum 🕐 Morning, before the afternoon EU-quarter crowds
Afternoon

The European Quarter & Cinquantenaire Park

For those curious about the EU institutions angle, the Parliamentarium offers a free, well-produced look at how the European Parliament actually works, a short walk from the triumphal arch and green expanse of Cinquantenaire Park.

🚇 ~10 min metro to Schuman/Maalbeek 💵 Free entry to the Parliamentarium 🕐 Early afternoon, closed Mondays
Evening

A Belgian beer tasting & farewell waffle

Belgian beer culture made UNESCO's intangible heritage list in 2016, and a tasting flight at a working lambic brewery like Cantillon or a classic beer cafe stacked with hundreds of styles is the way to understand why, capped off with a warm, sugar-crusted Brussels waffle before the flight home.

🚋 ~15 min tram/walk from the center to Cantillon 💵 ~€15-20 for a tasting flight 🕐 Early evening, before a last night in the Grand Place area

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is included by law in Belgian restaurants and cafes, so tipping is a small gesture rather than an expectation.
  • Rounding up the bill or leaving a euro or two for good service is common practice, including at friteries and beer cafes.
  • At a nicer sit-down dinner, an extra 5 to 10% for excellent service is appreciated but never obligatory.

👗 Dress Code

  • Brussels is a working capital city, smart-casual covers restaurants, museums, and even the Parliamentarium without issue.
  • Comfortable, sturdy shoes matter most, the Grand Place and surrounding lanes are cobblestone throughout.
  • Bring a light rain layer regardless of season, Belgium's weather changes quickly and showers can arrive with little warning.

🌍 Belgium's Linguistic Duality

  • Belgium is genuinely split between Dutch-speaking Flanders and French-speaking Wallonia, a divide that runs through national politics as much as language.
  • Brussels itself is officially bilingual, French and Dutch, so street signs and menus typically appear in both.
  • English is widely understood in the city center and tourist areas, but a French or Dutch greeting is always appreciated.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Brussels is generally safe, but pickpocketing around the Grand Place, Central Station, and crowded trams is worth a moment's caution.
  • Jaywalking is technically illegal and trams move faster and quieter than expected, look both ways at tram crossings even on quiet-looking streets.
  • Some neighborhoods around the North Station are best avoided late at night; the historic center and EU Quarter are well-lit and busy after dark.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases (French & Dutch)

  • Bonjour / Goedendag: hello
  • Merci beaucoup / Dank u wel: thank you very much
  • L'addition, s'il vous plaît / De rekening, alstublieft: the bill, please

💳 Money & Payments

  • The euro is the currency, and contactless card and phone payments are accepted almost everywhere, including friteries and chocolate shops.
  • Some small friteries and comic shops still prefer cash for orders under €10, so carry a little.
  • Bank-branded ATMs around the Grand Place and Central Station avoid the higher fees some standalone kiosks charge.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Get a Brussels Card

A flat daily rate covers free entry to dozens of museums, including the Comic Strip Center or Magritte Museum, plus unlimited transit, and pays for itself after two or three stops.

🍟

Skip the Grand Place friteries

The frites stands directly on the square carry a tourist markup; walk two streets back for the same quality at local prices.

🏛️

The Parliamentarium is free

One of the city's best-produced museum experiences costs nothing and rarely gets crowded outside of school-group hours.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🎨

Hunt murals off the main route

Beyond the official Comic Strip Route map, side streets in the Marolles and Sablon neighborhoods hide lesser-known murals with far fewer visitors.

🛍️

The Marolles flea market

The Vieux Marché on Place du Jeu de Balle runs every morning, a genuinely local antiques-and-bric-a-brac market a world away from the Grand Place crowds.

🍺

Visit Cantillon on a brewing day

This working lambic brewery still ferments the traditional way in Anderlecht, and timing a visit for an active brewing session makes the self-guided tour far more interesting.

✈️ Flights

  • Brussels Airport (BRU) in Zaventem is Belgium's main hub, about 12 km northeast of downtown with a direct 20-minute train to Brussels-Central.
  • Brussels South Charleroi (CRL) is a common budget-carrier alternative, about an hour south by shuttle bus.
  • The Eurostar and Thalys/Eurostar high-speed trains connect Brussels-Midi directly to London, Paris, and Amsterdam in under two hours, a genuinely practical way to arrive.

🚇 Public Transit

  • STIB/MIVB runs an efficient network of metro, tram, and bus lines covering the entire historic center and the EU Quarter.
  • A MOBIB card or the STIB app covers single rides or day passes, and the compact, walkable center means transit is mostly needed for the Atomium and European Quarter.
  • Trams are the fastest way out to Cantillon brewery in Anderlecht for Day 3's evening tasting.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Skip a rental car for this itinerary, the historic center is largely pedestrianized and the metro reaches the Atomium and EU Quarter easily.
  • Parking is scarce and expensive downtown, and Brussels traffic can be genuinely congested during weekday rush hours.
  • A rental only makes sense if extending the trip into the Ardennes or wider Belgium afterward.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Grand Place / Îlot Sacré: puts Day 1 and the Galeries Royales within a two-minute walk, the liveliest and most convenient base.
  • Sablon: quieter, upscale, and close to Mont des Arts and the Royal District for Day 3.
  • Saint-Gilles or Ixelles: a more local, Art Nouveau residential base with easy tram access into the center.

🚄 Bruges as a Day Trip

  • Bruges sits under an hour away by direct train, an easy add-on for travelers with extra time who want a contrast with Brussels' capital-city pace.
  • Trains run frequently from Brussels-Central and Brussels-Midi throughout the day, no advance booking required outside of peak summer weekends.
  • See our separate 3-day Bruges itinerary for the medieval canal-town side of Belgium this trip doesn't cover.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Belgium is in the Schengen Area, so standard Schengen entry rules apply for non-EU visitors.
  • Travel insurance is worth it given the pre-booked Atomium and chocolate workshop slots, which are usually non-refundable.
  • As the de facto capital of the EU, Brussels sees occasional protests and roadblocks near the European Quarter; check local news if visiting during a summit.
