4 Days in Istanbul: Byzantine Domes, Grand Bazaars & the Bosphorus

A cathedral that became a mosque, became a museum, became a mosque again; spice-scented bazaars that still run on centuries-old bargaining; and a short ferry ride that carries you from Europe to Asia and back before dinner.

Ideal SeasonApr – Jun, Sep – Oct
Est. Budget$650-900
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Istanbul Airport transfer & check-in

The HAVAIST shuttle bus runs directly from the airport into Sultanahmet, simpler with luggage than the metro-plus-tram transfer.

🚌 ~60-75 min by HAVAIST bus 💵 ~€10 🕐 Confirm check-in time
Afternoon

The Hagia Sophia, four lives in one dome

Built as a Byzantine cathedral in 537 AD, converted to a mosque after 1453, turned into a museum in 1935, and reconverted to a working mosque in 2020: few buildings anywhere carry this much layered history under one roof.

🚶 ~5 min from Sultanahmet hotels 💵 Free entry 🕐 Outside the five daily prayer windows
Evening

First look at the Blue Mosque across the square

Sultanahmet Square sits between two of the world's great domes, facing each other across the old Byzantine hippodrome. Save the interior for tomorrow and just take in the skyline tonight.

🚶 ~3 min across the square 💵 Free 🕐 Sunset, as the square's lights come on
Morning

Sultan Ahmed (Blue) Mosque interior

Over 20,000 handmade İznik tiles give the mosque its name and its glow. Six minarets caused a stir in 1616, since only Mecca's mosque had that many at the time.

🚶 ~5 min from the Hagia Sophia 💵 Free, modest dress required 🕐 Before 10 AM, closed during prayer
Afternoon

Topkapi Palace & the Imperial Treasury

Home to Ottoman sultans for almost 400 years. Four courtyards lead back to the Treasury, with the Topkapi Dagger and the Spoonmaker's Diamond, and terraces looking out over the Bosphorus.

🚶 ~5 min from the Blue Mosque 💵 ~€25 (~€15 more for the Harem) 🕐 Allow 2-3 hours, arrive early afternoon
Evening

The Basilica Cistern

A 6th-century underground reservoir with 336 marble columns, two upside-down Medusa heads at its base, and moody lighting reflected across the water below street level.

🚶 ~5 min from Sultanahmet Square 💵 ~€25, timed ticket 🕐 Later entry slots are quieter
Morning

The Grand Bazaar

One of the world's oldest and largest covered markets, open since 1461, with around 4,000 shops along 61 streets. Bargaining is expected and part of the experience, not an inconvenience.

🚶 ~10 min from Sultanahmet 💵 Free to browse 🕐 Weekday morning, before tour groups
Afternoon

Spice Bazaar, then a ferry to the Asian side

Mountains of spices, Turkish delight, and dried fruit fill the Egyptian Bazaar. Walk down to the Eminönü docks after and cross the Bosphorus to Üsküdar: a coffee break spent switching continents.

⛴️ ~15-20 min ferry crossing 💵 ~€1 with an Istanbulkart 🕐 Mid-afternoon, ferries run every 15-20 min
Evening

Bosphorus sunset cruise back to Europe

A boat back up the strait passes Ottoman waterfront mansions, the fortress of Rumeli Hisarı, and the suspension bridges linking the two continents, all lit up as the sun goes down.

⛴️ ~1.5-2 hr cruise 💵 ~€15-25 tour, or ~€1 public ferry 🕐 Sunset departure
Morning

A traditional hammam

Centuries-old ritual: a heated marble platform, a firm scrub with a coarse mitt, and a soap-foam massage. Several of the city's historic bathhouses still run the same routine they always have.

🚶 Walkable from Sultanahmet 💵 ~€40-60 🕐 Morning, before it gets busy
Afternoon

Galata Tower & Karaköy

A Genoese-built 14th-century watchtower with a 360-degree view over the Golden Horn and both continents at once. Karaköy below is filled with galleries and independent coffee roasters.

🚋 ~15 min tram, then walk across Galata Bridge 💵 ~€25 tower entry 🕐 Book a timed slot ahead
Evening

İstiklal Avenue, tea & the airport transfer

Beyoğlu's grand pedestrian avenue runs past a nostalgic red tram, rooftop bars, and çay houses. Sit for one last glass of Turkish tea and a plate of baklava before heading back to the airport.

🚌 ~60-75 min HAVAIST to the airport 💵 ~€8 tea & dessert, ~€10 bus 🕐 3 hrs before your flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Not obligatory: round up or leave 5-10% at sit-down restaurants.
  • No tipping expected at tea houses, street food stalls, or bakeries.
  • A small tip for hammam attendants and tour guides is appreciated but not required.

👗 Dress Code

  • Modest dress is required inside the Blue Mosque, the Hagia Sophia, and any working mosque: covered shoulders and knees for everyone, a headscarf for women.
  • Headscarves and wraps are provided free at the entrance if you arrive without one, and simple robes are sometimes offered for bare arms or legs.
  • Comfortable, sturdy shoes matter more than usual here: Sultanahmet's stone streets and the hills toward Beyoğlu add up fast on foot.

🚊 Getting Around

  • An Istanbulkart covers the tram, metro, bus, and ferries city-wide, and is cheaper per ride than single tickets.
  • Buy or top up the card at machines in any metro or tram station, and tap it again for a second passenger if traveling together.
  • The tram (T1 line) is the easiest way between Sultanahmet, the Grand Bazaar, and Galata Bridge.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Mosques close to visitors during the five daily prayer times; check the schedule and plan visits around it.
  • Overly friendly strangers steering you toward a specific rug shop, bar, or restaurant near Sultanahmet or Taksim are a known scam approach: politely decline and walk on.
  • Keep bags zipped and in front on crowded trams and inside the Grand Bazaar, where pickpocketing does occur.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Turkish

  • Merhaba: hello
  • Teşekkür ederim: thank you
  • Hesap, lütfen: the bill, please

💳 Money & Bargaining

  • Turkish Lira (TRY) is the currency; cards are widely accepted, but carry cash for bazaar bargaining and small tea houses.
  • Bargaining is expected in the Grand Bazaar and souvenir stalls, not at fixed-price shops, restaurants, or grocery stores.
  • ATMs are common throughout Sultanahmet and Beyoğlu; withdraw larger amounts to avoid repeated fees.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎫

The Museum Pass Istanbul covers major sites

One pass bundles Topkapi Palace, the Basilica Cistern, and several other paid sites for less than buying tickets separately.

⛴️

Public ferries over paid Bosphorus tours

The regular commuter ferry to Üsküdar or Kadıköy costs a fraction of a tourist cruise and crosses the exact same strait.

🍽️

Eat where the lunch crowd is Turkish

Lokantas a few streets back from Sultanahmet's main strip serve the same home-style dishes for noticeably less.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🕌

The Süleymaniye Mosque over the Blue Mosque crowds

The Ottoman architect Sinan's masterpiece sits on a quieter hill above the Grand Bazaar, with fewer tour groups and equally striking views.

🏘️

Kadıköy for an evening away from the tourist trail

The Asian side's market streets and bars fill with locals rather than visitors, a genuinely different pace from Sultanahmet.

🛁

A neighborhood hammam over the tourist-priced ones

Local bathhouses away from Sultanahmet run the same ritual for a lower price, with a more everyday crowd.

✈️ Flights

  • Istanbul Airport (IST) is the main international gateway, about 60-75 minutes from Sultanahmet.
  • The HAVAIST shuttle bus runs directly into the old city; the metro plus tram is cheaper but involves a transfer with luggage.
  • Sabiha Gökçen Airport (SAW) on the Asian side serves some budget carriers and is a longer transfer into Sultanahmet.

🚊 Public Transit & the Istanbulkart

  • One rechargeable Istanbulkart works across trams, the metro, buses, and ferries, and is noticeably cheaper than single-ride tokens.
  • The T1 tram is the backbone of the old city, linking Sultanahmet, the Grand Bazaar area, and Galata Bridge.
  • Ferries are treated as ordinary public transit here, not a novelty: the same card taps onto a boat as onto a tram.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Metered yellow taxis are everywhere; insist the meter runs, since flat "tourist rates" are a common overcharge near Sultanahmet.
  • BiTaksi, a local ride-hailing app, is a reliable way to book a metered taxi without haggling over price upfront.
  • Traffic across the bridges at rush hour can be slow: the tram or a ferry is often faster for the same crossing.

⛴️ Ferries & Crossing Continents

  • Public ferries from Eminönü or Karaköy to Üsküdar or Kadıköy run every 15-20 minutes and cost about the same as a bus ride.
  • It's the simplest way to say you've been on two continents in one day: the crossing itself takes well under half an hour.
  • Dedicated Bosphorus sightseeing cruises run longer routes past the waterfront mansions and both suspension bridges, useful for sunset.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Sultanahmet: walkable to the Hagia Sophia, Blue Mosque, and Topkapi Palace, though also the most tourist-priced area.
  • Karaköy or Beyoğlu: closer to the Galata Tower, İstiklal Avenue, and a livelier restaurant and bar scene at night.
  • Kadıköy, on the Asian side: quieter and less touristy, a short ferry ride from all the main sights.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Many nationalities need an e-Visa for Turkey; apply online in advance rather than on arrival.
  • Travel insurance is worth it for the ferry crossing and the general amount of walking on uneven, historic streets.
  • Carry some Turkish Lira cash for bazaar bargaining and small tea houses that don't take cards.
