4 Days in Grindelwald: Eiger, Jungfraujoch & Alpine Trails

Four days based in a village under the Eiger's sheer north face are enough to ride two legendary mountain railways to the glaciers above, walk a cliffside trail without a single transfer, and still leave a slow afternoon for the valley below. Built around the June to September hiking season, though this same village swaps hiking boots for skis from December to March as one of the Bernese Oberland's classic ski resorts.

Ideal SeasonJun – Sep
Est. Budget$1,700-2,300
Duration4 Days
PaceActive
How many days do you have?

Morning

Scenic train from Interlaken Ost on the BOB line

The Berner Oberland Bahn climbs out of Interlaken Ost through the Lütschental valley, and the Eiger's north face and the Wetterhorn open up together as the train rounds the final bends into Grindelwald.

🚆 ~35 min direct train from Interlaken Ost 💵 ~CHF 10 one way 🕐 Trains run roughly every 30 minutes, no reservation needed
Afternoon

Check in and an easy valley walk

Grindelwald strings along a single valley road, so most hotels sit an easy walk or a short local bus ride from the station, with a flat path along the valley floor giving an easy first look at both peaks.

🚶 ~30 min flat walk along the valley floor 💵 Free to walk 🕐 Early afternoon, to settle in before dinner
Evening

Fondue dinner and a first look at the Eiger

A chalet-style restaurant along Dorfstrasse is the classic way to start a trip here, a pot of melted cheese shared at a wooden table while the last light catches the Eiger's summit ridge outside.

🚶 Flat walk along Dorfstrasse 💵 ~CHF 28 per person for fondue 🕐 Early evening, before restaurants fill up
Morning

Eiger Express to Eigergletscher, then the cogwheel train up

The Eiger Express gondola crosses high above the valley floor from Grindelwald Terminal to Eigergletscher in well under 15 minutes, cutting what used to be a much longer approach before the final climb up through the mountain.

🚡 ~15 min gondola, then ~35 min cogwheel train through the Eiger 💵 ~CHF 220 round trip from Grindelwald 🕐 First departure of the day, before the crowds and cloud build
Midday

Sphinx Observatory and the Aletsch Glacier

Jungfraujoch sits at 3,454 meters, Europe's highest-altitude railway station, and the Sphinx Observatory's terrace looks south over the Aletsch Glacier, the longest ice stream in the Alps, curling away toward the horizon.

🚶 Elevator up to the Sphinx terrace 💵 Included in the rail ticket 🕐 Late morning, while the Aletsch side is typically clearest
Afternoon

Ice Palace and the descent through Kleine Scheidegg

A short walk through the glacier's carved ice tunnels rounds out the summit before the train winds back down through Kleine Scheidegg, where the Eiger, Mönch, and Jungfrau line up in a single frame.

🚞 ~1 hr descent via Kleine Scheidegg 💵 No extra cost on the same ticket 🕐 Early-to-mid afternoon, before the last trains back get busy
Morning

Cable car up to Grindelwald-First and the Cliff Walk

A 25-minute gondola ride climbs to First at 2,168 meters, where the First Cliff Walk by Tissot juts out over the rock face on a steel walkway with the Bernese Alps spread out below.

🚡 ~25 min gondola from Grindelwald village 💵 ~CHF 75 round trip 🕐 Mid-morning, once the early cloud has burned off
Midday

First Flyer, mountain carts, or the walk to Bachalpsee

Thrill-seekers zip down the First Flyer or steer a mountain cart back toward the valley, while a gentler option is the roughly hour-long walk to Bachalpsee, a small lake that mirrors the Wetterhorn on a still day.

🥾 ~1 hr walk to Bachalpsee, or a few minutes on the First Flyer 💵 ~CHF 39 for the First Flyer, Bachalpsee is free 🕐 Midday, leaving the afternoon open either way
Afternoon

Alternative: the Eiger Trail beneath the north face

For a lower-key afternoon instead, the Wengernalpbahn train up to Alpiglen drops hikers at the start of the Eiger Trail, a quieter path tracing the base of the 1,800-meter north face back toward Eigergletscher.

🚞 ~15 min train to Alpiglen, then a ~3 hr hike 💵 ~CHF 14 one-way train fare 🕐 Early afternoon, to finish well before dinner
Morning

A slow morning at the Grindelwald Sports Centre

With the big mountain days behind you, the Sportzentrum Grindelwald's pools and wellness area are a mellow way to ease legs tired from two days of altitude and gondolas.

🚶 ~10 min walk from the village center 💵 ~CHF 10 pool entry 🕐 Late morning, once the pools open
Midday

Waterfalls in the Lauterbrunnen valley

A short train ride over to Lauterbrunnen puts you beneath Staubbach Falls, which drops almost 300 meters straight off the valley's cliff face right behind the village, with Trümmelbach Falls' glacier-fed cascades a short walk further on, or Wengen a quick cable car ride up the other side.

🚆 ~30 min train via Zweilütschinen 💵 ~CHF 12 round trip 🕐 Midday, leaving time to get back for departure
Afternoon

Train departure via Interlaken

Retrace the BOB line back to Interlaken Ost to connect onward to Zurich or Geneva airport, or add a short stop in Bern first if there's time to spare.

🚆 ~35 min train to Interlaken Ost, longer connections onward 💵 ~CHF 10 to Interlaken 🕐 Leave at least 4 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is included by law in Swiss restaurant and hotel bills, so tipping isn't expected the way it is in the US.
  • Rounding up the bill or leaving a few francs for good service is a nice gesture, common among locals but never required.
  • Mountain restaurants at First and Kleine Scheidegg follow the same rule, the price on the menu is the price you pay.

👗 Dress Code

  • Alpine weather shifts fast, so layer up: a moisture-wicking base layer, an insulating mid-layer, and a windproof or waterproof shell that packs down small.
  • Temperatures at Jungfraujoch and First can sit 15 to 20 degrees Celsius colder than the village, even in summer, so bring gloves and a warm hat regardless of season.
  • Visiting between December and March instead, this same village turns into a full ski resort, so trade the hiking layers for ski-specific gear.

🚡 Getting Around the Jungfrau Region

  • Trains, gondolas, and cogwheel railways connect nearly everything on this itinerary, from the Eiger Express to the Wengernalpbahn and the First Bahn.
  • The village itself is compact enough to walk end to end in about 20 minutes, with a free local bus for guests holding a Grindelwald guest card.
  • All mountain excursions leave from stations within walking distance of the center, no rental car is needed for this itinerary.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Altitude at Jungfraujoch, 3,454 meters, can bring on light-headedness or headaches, drink water and move slowly if you feel it.
  • Sun exposure is far stronger above 3,000 meters, wear high-SPF sunscreen and sunglasses even on overcast days, especially near snow and ice.
  • The Eiger Express and Jungfraujoch line occasionally close for high wind or fog, build a buffer day into your plans if the summit view matters most.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in German

  • Grüezi: hello (Swiss German greeting)
  • Vielen Dank: thank you very much
  • Die Rechnung, bitte: the bill, please
  • Locals speak Bernese German day to day, a distinct dialect within Swiss German, though standard German is understood everywhere in the valley.

💳 Money & Payments

  • Switzerland uses the Swiss franc (CHF), not the euro, though some shops near the station will accept euros at a mediocre exchange rate.
  • Cards and contactless payments are accepted almost everywhere, including gondola ticket counters and mountain restaurants.
  • Switzerland has one of the highest price levels in Europe, budget accordingly for lift tickets, meals, and lodging well above nearby Italy or France.

A multi-day regional pass covering several of the area's trains and gondolas often beats buying each ticket separately across four days.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌄

Bachalpsee at sunrise

The alpine lake above First mirrors the Wetterhorn and Schreckhorn on a calm morning, best reached before the late-morning gondola crowds arrive.

🛷

Pfingstegg over First for fewer crowds

The smaller gondola on the opposite side of the valley gives quieter Eiger views along with a toboggan run back down to the village.

🚡

Ride the Eiger Express early

The gondola to Eigergletscher gets you well ahead of the day's tour groups heading up to Jungfraujoch.

✈️ Flights

  • Grindelwald has no airport of its own, Zurich (ZRH) and Geneva (GVA) are the two real international gateways, both requiring a train transfer via Interlaken Ost.
  • From Zurich, the scenic rail journey to Grindelwald takes around 2.5 hours with a change in Interlaken; from Geneva it runs closer to 3.5 hours.
  • Book the final Interlaken to Grindelwald leg in advance during summer, seats can sell out on peak-season weekends.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Local taxis are easily flagged at the station or booked through your hotel, useful mainly for luggage on arrival and departure days.
  • Ride-hailing apps have limited presence in the valley; hotels and the taxi stand outside the station are the more reliable option.
  • Most everything on this itinerary is within walking distance of the village center or a short gondola ride away.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Unlike car-free Zermatt, Grindelwald allows private cars, and parking is available near the station and most hotels.
  • Still, a rental adds little value here since Jungfraujoch, First, and the Eiger Trail all run on trains and gondolas rather than roads.
  • A car becomes more useful if you plan to explore beyond this itinerary, say a wider loop around Interlaken and Lake Thun.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Village center near Dorfstrasse: closest to shops and restaurants, convenient for the Day 1 arrival and Day 4 departure.
  • Near Grindelwald Terminal: a short walk shaves time off the early start for the Eiger Express on Day 2.
  • Grund, lower in the valley: quieter and often better value, with a short train or bus ride back up to the main village.

🚞 Cable Cars & Lift Passes

  • The Eiger Express, First Bahn, and Wengernalpbahn are the backbone of this itinerary, each departing from stations within the village.
  • Multi-day lift passes covering several of these lines can be worth it if you plan to ride more than the two main excursions in this itinerary.
  • Some lines close in high wind or heavy snow even in summer at altitude, check the day's status before setting out early.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Switzerland is part of the Schengen area but not the EU, so some non-EU travelers should still double-check passport and visa rules before booking.
  • Remember the local currency is the Swiss franc (CHF), not the euro, when budgeting for this trip.
  • Travel insurance that covers altitude activities and cable car disruptions is worth adding, given the Day 2 and Day 3 mountain excursions.
