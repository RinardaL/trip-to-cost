4 Days in Budva: Old Town, Beaches & Sveti Stefan

The walled Old Town and Citadel, pebble beaches and a boat to Sveti Nikola, Sveti Stefan and Petrovac, then a mountain morning on Lovćen: four relaxed days on the Budva Riviera.

Ideal SeasonMay – Sep
Est. Budget$300-520
Duration4 Days
PaceRelaxed
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Tivat or Podgorica & the transfer to Budva

Tivat (TIV) is 20 to 25 km from Budva, Podgorica (TGD) about 65 km. From Tivat a taxi takes roughly 25 minutes and costs about €25 to 45, so agree the fare first. There is no bus stop at the terminal. From Podgorica the official taxi fare is about €65 (check current), or take a city bus.

🚕 ~25 min by taxi from Tivat, ~1 hr from Podgorica 💵 ~€25-45 taxi from Tivat, ~€65 from Podgorica (check current) 🕐 Plan around your landing time, then drop your bags
Afternoon

Old Town (Stari Grad) walls & the Citadel

Budva's Old Town is a walled peninsula of stone lanes, small churches and cafés, and walking it is free. The Citadel at the seaward end has ramparts, a small museum and wide sea views. Entry is about €3.50 to 5 (check current). Go in the late afternoon for softer light and fewer tour groups, and allow about 1.5 hours.

🚶 Walk in through the town gate, the lanes are traffic-free 💵 Old Town free, Citadel ~€3.50-5 (check current) 🕐 Late afternoon, the Citadel usually opens around 9:00 (check current)
Evening

The seafront promenade & a first fish dinner

The promenade runs from the marina past the Old Town and along Slovenska Plaža, free and well lit, with cafés and ice-cream stands. For dinner, walk a few streets back from the water. Grilled fish is sold by weight, so ask the price per kilo first. A main with a drink often costs €15 to 30 (check current).

🚶 Flat walk, ~20 min along Slovenska Plaža 💵 Promenade free, ~€15-30 for a fish dinner (check current) 🕐 Sunset to late, busiest July and August
Morning

Mogren beach

Mogren I and II are two pebble coves about 10 minutes on foot from the Old Town, along a cliff path. Go before 10:00 for space and calm water. A sunbed and parasol set costs roughly €10 to 18 in season, more in July and August, or use a free spot with your own towel (check current).

🚶 ~10 min on the cliff path from the Old Town 💵 ~€10-18 for sunbeds, or free with your own towel (check current) 🕐 Before 10:00, the sea is calmest early
Afternoon

Slovenska Plaža & a boat to Sveti Nikola

Slovenska Plaža is Budva's long, lively beach below the promenade. Small taxi boats run in season to Sveti Nikola, a wooded island about 1 to 2 km offshore, for roughly €5 to 10 return (check current). Bring water and shoes for the rocks, as shade is limited. Confirm the last boat back before you set off.

⛴️ ~15 min by taxi boat from the beach or marina 💵 ~€5-10 return boat (check current) 🕐 After lunch, and note the last boat back
Evening

Jaz Beach & the Przno coves

Jaz is a long pebble bay about 3 km west of Budva, a short taxi ride with the fare agreed first. It hosts big summer concerts, so check dates, as roads and prices change then. For something smaller, Przno, about 7 km south-east towards Sveti Stefan, is a quieter cove with beach restaurants. Both suit a late swim in summer.

🚕 ~10 min by taxi to Jaz, ~15 min to Przno 💵 ~€8-15 taxi each way, agree first (check current) 🕐 Early evening, when the heat and crowds ease
Morning

The Sveti Stefan viewpoint

Local buses run from Budva to Sveti Stefan in about 20 to 25 minutes for roughly €1.50 to 3 (check current). The islet is a private Aman resort and non-guests generally cannot walk around it, so treat it as a view. Free viewpoints are on the road above and by St Sava church.

🚌 ~20-25 min by local bus from Budva 💵 ~€1.50-3 by bus, viewpoints free (check current) 🕐 Morning light on the islet, before the beach fills
Afternoon

Petrovac

Petrovac is a small resort about 17 km from Budva, with a pebble beach, a promenade and a Venetian fort at one end. It is calmer than Budva and sunbeds are often cheaper. Take the local bus from Sveti Stefan, about 10 minutes, and check the last bus back to Budva.

🚌 ~10 min by local bus from Sveti Stefan 💵 ~€1.50-3 by bus, lunch ~€8-15 (check current) 🕐 A long afternoon, and check the last bus back
Evening

Dinner in Budva: fish, pršut & a glass of Vranac

Back in Budva, choose a konoba (small tavern) with a local crowd. Try black risotto, grilled sea bass, pršut (smoked ham) with cheese, or cevapi, with a glass of Vranac red or Krstač white. Old Town bars and clubs are loud in July and August and quiet outside the season. Expect about €20 to 35 each (check current).

🚌 ~30 min by local bus back from Petrovac 💵 ~€20-35 for dinner with wine (check current) 🕐 Dinner from 19:30, nightlife peaks July and August
Morning

Lovćen National Park & the Njegoš Mausoleum

The Mausoleum of Njegoš stands at about 1,660 m on Lovćen, up 461 steps. A tour from Budva costs roughly €30 to 60 per person, or agree a taxi price for a few hours (check current). Park entry is about €3 and the mausoleum about €8. It is cooler and often misty, so bring a layer.

🚗 ~1 hr by tour minibus or taxi via Cetinje 💵 ~€30-60 tour, ~€3 park + ~€8 mausoleum (check current) 🕐 Leave early, shorter hours and snow closures in winter
Afternoon

Njeguši village: pršut, cheese & the hairpins

Njeguši is a small mountain village known for smoked ham (pršut) and cheese. Family-run houses and taverns serve tasting plates for roughly €8 to 15 (check current), and many tours stop here. The road to Kotor descends 25 hairpin bends with views over the bay. For Kotor and Perast, see our Kotor page and use the bus.

🚗 ~25 min from the mausoleum by tour minibus or taxi 💵 ~€8-15 for a tasting plate (check current) 🕐 Lunchtime, many tours arrive around midday
Evening

Departure via Tivat or Podgorica

Reach Tivat or Podgorica about 2.5 to 3 hours before an international flight, because airports are busy in summer and coastal roads jam in July and August. A taxi to Tivat costs about €25 to 45 (agree it first). For an early flight, skip Lovćen, have a relaxed beach morning and book your taxi the night before.

🚕 ~25 min by taxi to Tivat, ~1 hr to Podgorica 💵 ~€25-45 to Tivat, ~€65 to Podgorica (check current) 🕐 Arrive 2.5 to 3 hours before an international flight

💶 Tipping, Coffee & Bills

  • Tipping is appreciated, not required. In restaurants, leaving about 5 to 10 percent or rounding up the bill for good service is normal. Check whether service is already added.
  • Tip taxi drivers by rounding up. In the busy season, ask for the total fare or the rate per kilometre before the trip starts.
  • Coffee is a social ritual, so a single espresso or Turkish-style coffee at a table can last an hour. At a bar counter it costs less than on the seafront terraces.

🍖 Eating & Drinking in Montenegro

  • Burek (flaky pastry with cheese or meat) is the cheap breakfast, sold at bakeries for a couple of euros, usually with yoghurt. Cevapi (grilled minced meat) and pršut (smoked ham with cheese) are common mains and starters.
  • Fish and shellfish are sold by weight, so ask the price per kilo before you order and ask to see the fish. Black risotto (crni rižoto) and grilled sea bass are the coastal classics.
  • Rakija (fruit brandy) is offered as a welcome, often homemade, and it is polite to accept a small glass. Wines to try are Vranac (red) and Krstač (white).
  • Lunch is usually between 12:00 and 15:00 and dinner starts around 19:30. Konobas are small taverns with simpler menus and lower prices than the seafront.

🏖️ Beaches, Dress & Local Habits

  • Most beaches are pebble, so water shoes help. Sunbed and parasol sets are rented by the day, but you may use the free areas with your own towel.
  • Swimwear belongs on the beach. Cover up in the Old Town, in shops and in restaurants, and cover your shoulders and knees in churches and monasteries.
  • Smoking is common in cafés and on terraces. Put cigarettes out fully on hills and dry grass, as wildfires are a risk from spring to autumn.
  • Many bars and clubs play loud music until late in July and August. Ask your host about noise before booking a room in the Old Town.

⚠️ Safety, Scams & Etiquette Warnings

  • Summer is crowded and expensive. Book rooms ahead for July and August, and expect beach clubs with minimum spends and drinks priced far above a normal bar. Check the menu and any minimum charge before sitting down.
  • Use only licensed taxis (a roof sign and a company name and number on the door) and agree the full fare before you set off. Ride-hailing apps are patchy on the coast, so check the app (for example MonteGO) rather than relying on it.
  • Mountain and coastal roads are narrow and busy in summer, and the Lovćen hairpins are demanding. The drink-driving limit is low, and fines are collected before you leave. Check the UK FCDO or US State Department advice.
  • Speedboats share the water with swimmers, and jellyfish and sea urchins are common. Swim in marked areas and call 112 in an emergency, or 129 for sea rescue. Keep bags in sight on beaches and use ATMs inside banks.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Montenegrin

  • "Dobar dan" (good day), "Dobro jutro" (good morning), "Dobro veče" (good evening), "Zdravo" (hi)
  • "Molim" (please, you're welcome), "Hvala" (thank you), "Izvinite" (excuse me, sorry), "Da / Ne" (yes / no)
  • "Jedan sto za dvoje, molim" (a table for two, please), "Račun, molim" (the bill, please), "Živjeli" (cheers)
  • "Govorite li engleski?" (do you speak English?), "Gdje je...?" (where is...?), "Koliko košta?" (how much is it?)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Montenegro uses the euro, although it is not in the EU or the eurozone. Cards and contactless work in most hotels and restaurants, but carry cash for buses, small bakeries, markets and taxis.
  • Use ATMs inside bank branches and avoid standalone exchange booths. If a terminal offers to charge you in your home currency, choose euros.
  • Your accommodation adds a small tourist tax per person per night and registers foreign guests with the authorities. Keep your passport with you, because you may be asked to show it.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🚌

Take the local bus, not a taxi

Buses between Budva, Sveti Stefan and Petrovac cost a few euros and run often in season, while a taxi on the same stretch costs several times more. Buy tickets from the driver in cash (check current fares).

🏖️

Bring your own towel and shade

Sunbed and parasol sets can cost €10 to 20 or more on the busiest beaches in summer. A towel, a light umbrella and a free pebble spot save a lot over a few days (check current prices).

🥐

Eat where the locals eat

Bakery burek and yoghurt for breakfast cost a couple of euros, and konobas away from the seafront serve a daily set meal at a lower price. Supermarkets such as Voli are good for fruit, picnic items and water.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

Visit in the shoulder season

Swimming is best from June to September, but May and late September are quieter and cheaper, with the sea still pleasant by September. In winter many beach cafés close and the town is very quiet.

🚶

Walk the coast to Bečići

The seafront promenade continues south-east from Slovenska Plaža to Bečići, about 2 km, with sea views, cafés and pebble beaches. It is flat and easy, and a pleasant evening walk.

⛰️

Add Kotor, Perast or Cetinje

Kotor and Perast are a short bus ride from Budva (about €4 to 5, 35 to 60 minutes, check current), and our Kotor page covers them. Cetinje, the old royal capital, is about 40 minutes inland and a good wet-weather option.

✈️ Flights

  • Tivat (TIV) is the closest airport, about 20 to 25 km from Budva, with many seasonal flights from Europe. Podgorica (TGD) is about 65 km away and has more year-round routes, including some budget airlines.
  • Dubrovnik (DBV) in Croatia is also an option, about 2 to 3 hours away by road including the border, but check coach times and queues in summer.
  • Compare both Montenegrin airports for your home city, since fares and seasons differ a lot. Airports are very busy in July and August, so arrive early.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Use licensed taxis with a roof sign and a company name and number on the door. Taxis are often unmetered, so agree the full fare before you set off.
  • Tivat to Budva is about €25 to 45, and the Podgorica Airport official fare to Budva is about €65 (check current). Pre-booked private transfers are an alternative.
  • Uber does not operate in Montenegro. Bolt is reported patchy and may not cover the coast, so check the app. Local apps such as MonteGO are worth installing.

🚌 Public Transport & Tickets

  • There are no trains to Budva. Buses run from the main bus station, about 1.5 km from the Old Town, to Kotor (about €4 to 5, 35 to 60 minutes), Tivat, Cetinje and Podgorica (check current).
  • Local buses along the coast to Bečići, Sveti Stefan and Petrovac run often in summer for a few euros. Pay the driver in cash, in small notes.
  • Check the last bus back before a day trip, as evening service is thin outside summer.
  • Timetables change by season, so check busticket4.me or the station for current times.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Old Town: atmospheric and car-free, but noisy in summer and the priciest.
  • Slovenska Plaža and the centre: close to beaches, the promenade and restaurants, with many apartments.
  • Bečići and Rafailovići: calmer, with long beaches, a promenade and frequent buses to Budva.
  • Petrovac or Sveti Stefan: quieter and more local, but you will rely on buses.
  • Book July and August well ahead. Apartments and guesthouses are cheaper than hotels, and rates drop sharply in May and September.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Montenegro is not in the EU, so EU roaming does not automatically apply. Check your operator, or use an eSIM or a local prepaid SIM (Crnogorski Telekom, One, m:tel).
  • Cards work at most hotels and restaurants, but carry cash for buses, bakeries, markets and small taxis.
  • Use ATMs inside banks and avoid standalone exchange booths.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Entry: citizens of the EU, UK, US, Canada and Australia can generally enter Montenegro visa-free for up to 90 days, but EU citizens entering on an ID card may be limited to 30 days. Check your nationality with your government or the Montenegrin embassy.
  • Montenegro is not in the Schengen area or the EU, and the euro is used without EU membership.
  • Emergency numbers: 112 for general emergencies and wildfires, 129 for maritime rescue.
  • You do not need a car. Parking in Budva in summer is hard, and buses and taxis cover this itinerary. If you drive, expect heavy summer traffic and narrow mountain roads.
