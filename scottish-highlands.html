4 Days in the Scottish Highlands: A Self-Drive Road Trip

Culloden and Loch Ness, the Glenfinnan Viaduct, Glen Coe and Eilean Donan: a four-day loop from Inverness by hire car, with driving times, single-track road rules and entry prices.

4-day Scottish Highlands itinerary

How many days do you have?

Morning

Inverness Airport & collecting the car

Inverness Airport (INV) is about 9 miles east of the city. Book your hire car weeks ahead, and reserve an automatic early if you do not drive manual. Photograph any damage first, set the sat-nav while parked, and remember: drive on the left. Bus 11 to town costs about £2 one way while the Highlands £2 single fare cap runs, until March 2027 (check current).

🚗 ~15 min by car to Culloden, ~20 min by taxi to the city 💵 Hire car from ~£45-70 a day, taxi to town ~£20-25 (check current) 🕐 Book the pick-up to match your landing time
Afternoon

Culloden Battlefield

Culloden, 5 miles east of Inverness, is where the last pitched battle on British soil was fought in April 1746. The National Trust for Scotland visitor centre costs £12.50 for adults and parking £5 (check current). The open battlefield is free to walk, and 2 hours is plenty. Clava Cairns, a free prehistoric burial site, is a few minutes away.

🚗 ~15 min from the airport, ~15 min on to Inverness 💵 Centre £12.50, car park £5, battlefield and Clava Cairns free 🕐 Centre open daily, shorter hours in winter (check current)
Evening

The River Ness & Ness Islands

Park at your accommodation and walk along the River Ness, where church spires rise above the water. The Ness Islands, a wooded footpath loop over small bridges, are about 20 minutes upstream from the centre. For dinner, a pub supper or fish and chips costs roughly £12 to £18. Book ahead at weekends and in busy months.

🚶 ~20 min on foot along the river 💵 Walk free, ~£12-18 for dinner 🕐 Golden hour, then dinner from 6 PM
Morning

Urquhart Castle

Urquhart Castle is a ruin on a headland over Loch Ness, about 30 minutes from Inverness on the A82. Historic Environment Scotland charges roughly £14 online or £16 at the gate (check current), with timed tickets. Climb the Grant Tower for the view along the loch. Arrive at opening to miss the coach parties, and allow about 1.5 hours.

🚗 ~30 min on the A82 from Inverness, about 16 miles 💵 ~£14 online, ~£16 on the day (check current) 🕐 First slot of the day, allow 1.5 hours
Afternoon

Fort Augustus & the Caledonian Canal locks

Fort Augustus sits at the south end of Loch Ness, about 35 minutes from Urquhart on the A82. Walk beside the Caledonian Canal locks, where boats step down to the loch. Parking is limited in summer, so check the signs. Buy a picnic or a pie here, and top up the tank if it is under half full.

🚗 ~35 min on the A82 from Urquhart, about 25 miles 💵 Free to watch, a pie lunch ~£8-12 🕐 Early afternoon, lock operation varies by season
Evening

Fort William & Neptune's Staircase

Fort William is about an hour on from Fort Augustus on the A82. Check in, then drive 10 minutes to Banavie for Neptune's Staircase, a flight of eight locks on the Caledonian Canal designed by Thomas Telford. The towpath is free to walk. Eat on the High Street, where pub mains cost about £14 to £20.

🚗 ~1 hr from Fort Augustus on the A82, then ~10 min to Banavie 💵 Staircase free, ~£14-20 for a pub dinner 🕐 Early evening light, dinner from 6 PM
Morning

Glenfinnan Viaduct & Monument

Glenfinnan is 25 minutes west of Fort William on the A830. The National Trust for Scotland car park costs £5 and can fill by mid-morning, so arrive before 9. A 2 km hillside path leads to the viaduct viewpoint. The Jacobite steam train runs April to October, with times posted at the visitor centre. The monument is £5.

🚗 ~25 min on the A830 from Fort William, then ~40 min on foot 💵 Car park £5, monument £5, viewpoint free (check current) 🕐 Arrive before 9 AM, check the steam train times
Afternoon

Glen Coe & the Three Sisters

From Fort William the A82 reaches Glen Coe in about 35 minutes. Stop at the National Trust for Scotland visitor centre (£4 per car, free for members) for walk maps, then use the marked Three Sisters viewpoint on the A82 to look up at the peaks. Never stop on the road itself, and keep to lay-bys and car parks.

🚗 ~35 min on the A82 from Fort William, about 17 miles 💵 Visitor centre car park £4, viewpoints free (check current) 🕐 Early afternoon, visitor centre closes earlier in winter
Evening

Glen Nevis & Steall Falls

Glen Nevis starts at the edge of Fort William and ends at a car park on a single-track road with passing places. The Steall Falls path through the Nevis Gorge takes about 1.5 to 2 hours return and is rocky and slippery. In April or October, check sunset time first, and carry a torch.

🚗 ~15 min from Fort William up Glen Nevis, then a rocky footpath 💵 Walk free, ~£14-20 for dinner in town (check current) 🕐 Set off at least 2.5 hours before dark
Morning

Eilean Donan Castle

Eilean Donan is about 1 hour 25 minutes from Fort William via the A82 and A87, so fill the tank first, as A87 petrol stations are few. Adult tickets cost about £13 and are sold only at the visitor centre; parking is charged in opening hours (check current). Allow 1 hour; skip it if your flight is before 5 PM.

🚗 ~1 hr 25 min via the A82 and A87, about 65 miles 💵 ~£13 adult, parking charged in opening hours (check current) 🕐 Leave Fort William by 7:30 AM, hours vary by season
Afternoon

Glen Shiel, Invermoriston & the A82 home

Take the A87 through Glen Shiel, with the Five Sisters ridge beside the road and lay-bys for photographs. At Invermoriston, join the A82 and stop at Thomas Telford's old stone bridge, where a short free path leads to waterfalls on the River Moriston. Refuel in Drumnadrochit, which is 17 miles on, before the final stretch.

🚗 ~1 hr 15 min to Invermoriston on the A87, then ~45 min to Inverness on the A82 💵 Stops free, fuel dearer at remote stations 🕐 Allow about 3 hours in total with stops
Evening

Refuelling, car return & departure

Refuel at an Inverness supermarket, since hire firms charge a premium for a part-empty tank. The airport is about 15 minutes east on the A96, away from the Kessock Bridge, which takes the A9 north. Allow 20 minutes at the return desk, and arrive 2 hours before a domestic flight or 3 before an international one.

🚗 ~15 min from Inverness to the airport on the A96 💵 Refill ~£20-30, check hire terms for late-return fees 🕐 Be at the airport 2 to 3 hours before your flight

Scottish Highlands: what to know before you go

💷 Tipping & Eating Out

  • Tipping is not required in the UK. In restaurants, 10 to 12.5 percent is usual for good service, and check the bill first, because some places add a service charge.
  • In pubs you order and pay at the bar, then take a table number or find your own seat. There is no need to tip for drinks, and you can round up taxi fares.
  • In small Highland villages, kitchens often stop serving around 8:30 or 9 PM, and some close for the winter. Book dinner ahead in busy months.

🥃 Highland Food, Drink & Whisky

  • Try a full Scottish breakfast, haggis with neeps and tatties (turnip and potato), cullen skink (smoked haddock soup) and fresh seafood. Venison and Highland beef are on many menus.
  • A whisky is a dram, served neat. Add a splash of water if you like, and ask the bar for a smaller measure if you are driving.
  • Scotland has a drink-drive limit of 50 mg per 100 ml of blood, lower than in England, and Police Scotland has a zero-tolerance approach. If you are driving, do not drink.

🚗 Driving Rules & Single-Track Etiquette

  • Drive on the left, give way to traffic from the right at roundabouts, and wear a seatbelt. The limits are 30 mph in built-up areas, 60 mph on single carriageways and 70 mph on dual carriageways, unless signs say otherwise (gov.uk).
  • On single-track roads, use the passing places and let oncoming traffic go by. Pull in on your left, or stop opposite a passing place on the right. Never park in one, and wave to say thanks.
  • If a queue builds up behind you, pull in and let it pass. Watch for deer, sheep, cyclists and campervans. Check Traffic Scotland before you set off, since the A82 and A87 can close in bad weather.

⚠️ Weather, Midges & Safety Warnings

  • Highland weather changes within an hour, even in May. Check the Met Office forecast daily, carry a waterproof layer, and for any hill walk check the Mountain Weather Information Service (MWIS) and tell someone your route. Emergency number: 999.
  • Midges bite mostly from late May to early September, most at dawn and dusk and in still, damp air. A breeze or sunshine keeps them away. Use repellent such as Smidge, and check the midge forecast.
  • Thieves target hire cars at busy car parks and viewpoints, so take valuables with you and leave nothing visible. Check your own government's travel advice for the UK before you go.
  • At Glen Coe and Glenfinnan, park only in marked lay-bys or car parks, never on verges, pavements or in passing places, because police monitor the areas and vehicles can be towed.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Scottish Gaelic

  • "Madainn mhath" (MAT-in va, good morning), "Feasgar math" (FESS-kar ma, good afternoon)
  • "Tapadh leibh" (TAH-pa lev, thank you), "Fàilte" (FAHL-cha, welcome)
  • "Slàinte mhath" (SLAHN-cha va, good health, cheers), "Beannachd leibh" (BYEN-achk lev, goodbye)
  • English is spoken everywhere, and you will also hear "aye" (yes), "wee" (small) and "cheers" (thanks)

💳 Money & Payments

  • The UK uses the pound sterling (£). Cards and contactless work almost everywhere, including most hostels and cafés, but carry a little cash for rural honesty-box car parks and small shops.
  • Scottish banknotes are legal in Scotland, but some shops in England refuse them, so spend or swap them before you leave. Use bank ATMs, which are scarce in small villages, and avoid foreign-currency booths.
  • If a card terminal offers to charge you in your home currency, choose pounds. The NTS car park machines at Glencoe accept both card and cash.

Insider tips for Scottish Highlands

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🚗

Hire smart and share the cost

Book the car weeks ahead for spring and autumn, compare the airport with an Inverness city branch, and check what the excess insurance covers. Automatics cost more than manuals. A car and a double room split between two people is the cheapest way to do this trip.

🚆

No car? Use the West Highland Line and Citylink

ScotRail trains run Glasgow to Fort William in about 3 hours 50 minutes and on to Glenfinnan and Mallaig, and Citylink buses link Inverness and Fort William in about 2 hours (check current). Show a rail or bus ticket at Glenfinnan for a free hot drink and a 2-for-1 monument voucher.

🏨

Hostels, B&Bs and supermarket picnics

Scottish Youth Hostels in Glen Nevis and Glencoe, and independent hostels in Fort William, are the cheapest beds, with B&Bs next. Book two or three months ahead for May to September. Buy lunch and fuel at supermarkets in Inverness or Fort William, which are usually cheaper than rural shops.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🅿️

Beat the Glenfinnan and Glencoe crowds

The Glenfinnan car park is £5 and fills early, and a neighbouring community car park is the back-up. Arrive before 9 AM or after 4 PM. At Glen Coe, use the visitor centre or the marked viewpoints rather than random verges.

🚂

See the viaduct without the steam train

The Jacobite steam train runs from April to October on dates that change each year. The regular ScotRail service also crosses the viaduct several times a day, and the walk to the viewpoint is free. Check the posted times at the visitor centre.

🛣️

Try Glen Etive on a clear day

This dead-end single-track road leaves the A82 at the head of Glen Coe and runs along the River Etive to the sea loch. Allow an extra hour, use the passing places, and note there are no shops or services at the end.

Travel services for Scottish Highlands

✈️ Flights

  • Inverness Airport (INV) is about 9 miles east of the city and has UK and some European links (check current). It is the best fit for this loop, because you can collect and return the car in the same place.
  • Glasgow (GLA) and Edinburgh (EDI) offer more long-haul and budget flights. From Glasgow allow about 3 hours to Fort William, and from Edinburgh about 3.5 hours to Inverness. Shorten Day 1 if you arrive there.
  • One-way car hires between airports usually add a fee, so compare it with flying into and out of Inverness.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Taxis wait outside the terminal at Inverness Airport, and a metered ride into the city costs about £20 to £25 (check current).
  • Stagecoach bus 11 links the airport and the centre in about 25 to 30 minutes, for about £2 one way under the Highlands £2 single fare cap, which runs until March 2027 (check current).
  • Outside Inverness and Fort William, taxis are scarce, so book a pick-up the day before, especially after a late hill walk.

🚌 Public Transport & Tickets

  • ScotRail runs the West Highland Line from Glasgow Queen Street to Fort William (about 3 hours 50 minutes) and Mallaig (about 5 hours 30 minutes). Services are limited, so check the timetable and book ahead.
  • Scottish Citylink coach 919 links Inverness and Fort William in about 2 hours, and Citylink 915 runs from Glasgow via Glen Coe and Fort William to Skye. Fares and times change, so check current.
  • The Jacobite steam train from Fort William to Mallaig runs from April to October, sells return tickets only, and needs advance booking from West Coast Railways.
  • Buses from Fort William stop outside the Glenfinnan visitor centre, and trains stop at Glenfinnan station, so a car-free day there is possible.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Inverness (night 1): handy for the airport and Culloden, with hostels, guesthouses and hotels close to the River Ness.
  • Fort William (nights 2 and 3): the best base for Glenfinnan, Glen Coe and Glen Nevis, with hostels, B&Bs and hotels. Dorm beds are often £25 to £40 and B&B doubles £90 to £140 in season (check current).
  • Glencoe village and Glen Nevis: SYHA and independent hostels sit right in the scenery, but dining options are fewer, so bring food.
  • Fort Augustus and Drumnadrochit: smaller alternatives on Loch Ness, quieter than Inverness, if you want to split the drive on Day 2.
  • Book two or three months ahead for May to September, and weekends fill first.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Check roaming rates with your network, or buy a UK eSIM or prepaid SIM. Mobile signal is patchy in Glen Coe, Glen Nevis and along the A87, so download offline maps.
  • Cards and contactless work at nearly all hotels, shops and ticket desks, but keep some coins for small car parks and public toilets, such as the £1 out-of-hours block at Glenfinnan.
  • Use bank-branded ATMs in Inverness or Fort William before heading into remote areas.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Check whether you need a UK visa or an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) before you book. Apply only through the official gov.uk service, and check the current fee.
  • Car hire: most firms want a full licence, a credit card in the driver's name and a minimum age of 21 to 25 with a young-driver fee. Many foreign licences are valid for visits, and EU, EEA and Swiss licences need no international permit, but confirm with the hire firm and the gov.uk licence checker.
  • Historic Environment Scotland and National Trust for Scotland sell passes, but this route has only two or three paid sights, so single tickets usually cost less. Members of either body get free entry to the matching sites.
  • Read the Highway Code section on single-track roads, and check Traffic Scotland and the Met Office each morning.

The Scottish Highlands self-drive route and driving times

This itinerary bases night 1 in Inverness, then spends nights 2 and 3 in Fort William before driving back via Eilean Donan. Times are typical driving without traffic and exclude stops, parking and photo breaks, so add time on single-track roads.

  • Inverness to Urquhart~25-30 min
  • Urquhart to Fort Augustus~35-40 min
  • Loch Ness to Fort William~45-60 min
  • Fort William to Glenfinnan~30 min
  • Fort William to Glen Coe~25-35 min
  • Fort William to Eilean D.1.25-1.5 hr
  • Eilean Donan to Inverness1.5-2 hr

Do you need a car in the Scottish Highlands?

Not strictly. ScotRail's West Highland Line runs Glasgow to Fort William (about 3 hours 50 minutes) and Glenfinnan, Citylink coaches link Inverness and Fort William in about 2 hours, and tours cover Loch Ness and Glen Coe. A car matters for Glen Nevis, Urquhart and Eilean Donan, where services are sparse (check current).

What do visitors get wrong about driving in the Highlands?

Drive on the left. On single-track roads, use passing places on your left or wait opposite one on the right, and never park in them. Fuel stations are sparse on the A87, so refill in Fort William. The A82 and A87 can close in winter; check Traffic Scotland. Speed limits vary from 30 to 60 mph, so follow signs.

Where should you base yourself in the Scottish Highlands?

This route uses Inverness for night 1, then Fort William for nights 2 and 3, about 65 miles and 1.5 hours apart. Fort William is 30 minutes from Glenfinnan, 25 to 35 from Glen Coe and 1.25 to 1.5 hours from Eilean Donan. Inverness suits the airport, 15 minutes away.
