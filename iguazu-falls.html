4 Days at Iguazu Falls: Devil's Throat, Boat Rides & Both Sides of the Border

Nearly 275 individual cascades spread across almost two miles of jungle river, spanning the border of Argentina and Brazil, one side puts you on walkways just meters from the water and inside a boat that drives underneath it, the other pulls back for the panoramic view that makes sense of the whole thing at once. Four days is enough to properly cover both national parks, cross the border between them, and still leave room for the coatis that own the walkways.

Morning

Arrival & check-in in Puerto Iguazú

Most visitors base on the Argentine side in Puerto Iguazú, a small jungle town a short drive from both the national park and the border, with a wider range of budget lodging than its Brazilian counterpart.

✈️ Fly into Iguazú International (IGR) 💵 ~$15-20 taxi from the airport 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time
Afternoon

Lower Circuit, Circuito Inferior

This looping walkway drops down toward river level and passes directly beneath several individual falls, including Salto Bossetti and Dos Hermanas, the most intimate perspective the Argentine side offers and a gentle introduction before the longer Devil's Throat walk tomorrow. Coatis, small raccoon-like mammals, are everywhere along this trail and thoroughly used to people, admire them but don't feed or approach them.

🚶 Enter through the Argentine park's Central Station 💵 Park ticket ~$34 (foreign adult, 2026 rate) 🕐 Early afternoon, allow 1.5 to 2 hrs
Evening

Dinner in Puerto Iguazú town

Avenida Córdoba is the main strip for parrilla-style grilled meats and river fish like surubí, a relaxed first night before two longer park days ahead.

🚶 A short walk or taxi from most hotels 💵 ~$15-25 dinner with a drink 🕐 Argentine dinners run late, 8:30 PM onward
Morning

The Eco-Train & Devil's Throat walkway

A small train carries you to Estación Garganta del Diablo, then a flat 1.1km metal walkway crosses the river straight to the edge of the Devil's Throat, a U-shaped chasm where roughly half the river's flow drops nearly 80 meters into a permanent cloud of spray. It's the single most dramatic view at either park, go early before the train queue and the crowds build.

🚂 Free Eco-Train, runs every ~20 min 💵 Included in park entry 🕐 Arrive by park opening, ~8 AM
Midday

Upper Circuit, Circuito Superior

Where the Lower Circuit goes underneath the falls, this walkway runs flat along the clifftop right at the lip of a dozen different cascades, looking straight down the drop rather than up at it, a completely different vantage on the same falls you saw from below yesterday.

🚶 Starts near Central Station 💵 Included in park entry 🕐 Late morning, allow ~1.5 hrs
Afternoon

Gran Aventura: the boat ride under the falls

A jeep transfer through the jungle drops you at the river for this speedboat trip, which finishes by driving straight into the spray at the base of San Martín and Tres Mosqueteros falls, everyone gets completely soaked and it's genuinely one of the trip's highlights. Bring a dry bag for phones and cameras, or leave them at the boat dock lockers.

🚤 ~12 min in the falls' spray 💵 ~$70-85 per person, book same-day at Central Station 🕐 Afternoon departures run continuously
Morning

Border crossing to Brazil

The Tancredo Neves International Bridge is the only road crossing, about 15km from Puerto Iguazú. Shared buses stop at both immigration posts, everyone gets off to have passports stamped exiting Argentina and entering Brazil, then reboards, budget a full hour for the crossing and don't lose your ticket. A valid passport is required for every nationality; depending on your passport, Brazil may also require an advance eVisa, so check requirements before you fly, not at the border.

🚌 Shared bus, ~40 min plus immigration stops 💵 ~$4-5 one-way bus fare 🕐 Depart by 8:30 AM to beat the queue
Midday

The Brazilian side's panoramic walkway

Brazil's park covers a fraction of the trail distance of Argentina's, but its single walkway is purpose-built for the wide shot, facing the full curtain of falls head-on from across the canyon, ending at a platform and elevator right beside the Devil's Throat's outer face. Where Argentina puts you inside the falls, this is the view that makes sense of the whole horseshoe at once.

🚶 Shuttle bus from the park entrance to the trailhead 💵 Brazilian park ticket ~$28 🕐 Late morning into early afternoon, allow ~2 hrs
Afternoon

Parque das Aves

Right next to the Brazilian park's entrance, this walk-through aviary houses toucans, macaws and other Atlantic Forest species in large enclosures you walk directly into, a good complement to whatever wildlife you've spotted on the trails and an easy stop before heading back into Foz do Iguaçu for the night.

🚶 Adjacent to the Brazilian park entrance 💵 ~$25 entry 🕐 Mid to late afternoon, allow ~1.5 hrs
Morning

Coatis of the subtropical rainforest

These ring-tailed relatives of the raccoon are the park's unofficial mascots, bold, constantly foraging, and completely unbothered by people, especially around the picnic and snack areas. They look harmless but they bite, and human food genuinely harms them, so admire from a couple of meters back and never hand-feed one, no matter how close it wanders.

🐾 Most common near Central Station and the picnic areas 💵 No extra cost 🕐 Any time, most active in the morning
Midday

Hito Tres Fronteras

Back in Puerto Iguazú, this riverside obelisk marks the exact point where Argentina, Brazil and Paraguay meet, with each country's matching landmark visible across the water, a quick, easy stop and a good last look at how much geography this trip has actually covered.

🚶 Short walk from central Puerto Iguazú 💵 Free 🕐 Late morning, before checkout
Afternoon

Departure

Iguazú International (IGR), a short drive from Puerto Iguazú, has the widest range of domestic connections; Foz do Iguaçu's airport (IGU) on the Brazilian side is the better choice if your onward flight routes through Brazil. Either way, decide before crossing back so you're not stuck re-crossing the border with a tight connection.

🚗 ~20 min to either airport 💵 ~$15-20 taxi 🕐 Leave at least 2.5 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • In Argentina, 10 percent at sit-down restaurants is standard and usually left in cash even if you paid by card.
  • In Brazil, a 10 percent service charge is often already added to the bill as "couvert" or "serviço", check before tipping again on top.
  • Tour guides and boat crews on both sides appreciate a small tip, it isn't obligatory but is common practice.

👗 Dress Code

  • Quick-dry clothing beats cotton everywhere here, between humidity, spray, and the boat ride, something is getting wet.
  • Sturdy, closed-toe walking shoes are worth it for the length of the Argentine circuits, sandals get old fast over several hours.
  • A swimsuit under your clothes makes the Gran Aventura boat ride far more comfortable than getting soaked in street clothes.

🚶 Getting Around Iguazu

  • Inside the Argentine park, the free Eco-Train and all three walkway circuits are covered by a single entrance ticket.
  • Between Puerto Iguazú, the Argentine park, and the border, taxis and shared vans are the standard way to get around, no rental car is really needed.
  • Inside the Brazilian park, a shuttle bus included with entry carries you from the gate out to the panoramic trailhead.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Never feed or approach the coatis, they bite when provoked and human food damages their health, keep bags zipped and food out of sight.
  • Walkway railings exist for a reason, the drops beside the Devil's Throat and Upper Circuit are real and the metal decking is slick with spray.
  • Keep both park tickets and your passport on you at all times if you're doing the border crossing as a day trip rather than switching hotels.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases

  • Spanish (Argentina): Buen día: good day/hello
  • Spanish (Argentina): La cuenta, por favor: the bill, please
  • Portuguese (Brazil): Obrigado/a: thank you

💳 Money & Payments

  • Argentina uses the peso and Brazil the real, both parks and most tour operators also quote and accept US dollars in cash.
  • Cards are widely accepted at hotels and larger restaurants on both sides, but carry small bills for the bird park, snacks, and tips.
  • ATM rates and fees vary a lot between the two countries, withdrawing a larger amount at once in Argentina is usually the better rate.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Buy a second-day discount ticket in Argentina

If you re-enter the Argentine park on consecutive days, a discounted second-day ticket is available at the gate, worth asking about if you want extra time on either circuit.

🚌

Take the public bus, not a private transfer, across the border

The shared Crucero del Norte / Rio Uruguay bus between Puerto Iguazú and Foz do Iguaçu runs hourly for a few dollars, a fraction of what private transfers charge for the same route.

🏨

Base in Puerto Iguazú, not Foz do Iguaçu

Lodging and meals both run noticeably cheaper on the Argentine side, and the border crossing is easy enough to do as a single day trip rather than switching hotels.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🚂

Skip the first Eco-Train and walk instead

The queue for the first morning train can run 30-45 minutes; the walking trail alongside the tracks to Cataratas Station is scenic, uncrowded, and gets you moving immediately.

🌙

Full-moon walks at the Argentine park

On nights around the full moon, the park opens for guided evening walks out to the Devil's Throat, an entirely different, near-empty experience of the same view, book well ahead as slots are limited.

🦜

Arrive at Parque das Aves right at opening

The bird enclosures are far more active in the cooler first hour after opening, before the afternoon heat sends most birds into the shade.

✈️ Flights

  • Iguazú International (IGR) on the Argentine side has frequent connections to Buenos Aires and is the most common gateway for this trip.
  • Foz do Iguaçu International (IGU) on the Brazilian side connects to São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro, useful if your itinerary continues into Brazil.
  • Both airports are about 20 minutes from their respective town centers, so either works as an arrival or departure point.

🛂 Border Crossing & Visas

  • A valid passport is required to cross between Argentina and Brazil at Iguazu, no exceptions, even for a same-day visit to the other park.
  • Visa requirements depend on nationality and change periodically, US, Canadian and Australian citizens have required a Brazilian eVisa since April 2025, apply well ahead of travel.
  • The Tancredo Neves International Bridge is the only road crossing between the two towns; budget a full hour each way for immigration stops.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Taxis are the standard way to reach either national park from its town center, agree on a fare or confirm the meter before departing.
  • Rideshare apps have limited or unreliable coverage on both sides, don't count on one being available at the airport or park exits.
  • Shared minivans run scheduled routes between Puerto Iguazú, the Argentine park, and the border, cheaper than a private taxi if your timing is flexible.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Puerto Iguazú, Argentina: the cheaper and more common base, walkable town center with easy access to the park, border, and airport.
  • Foz do Iguaçu, Brazil: a reasonable alternative if you're continuing into Brazil afterward, generally pricier than the Argentine side.
  • A handful of hotels sit inside or right at the edge of each national park itself, convenient but priced at a premium for the location.

🎟️ Park Tickets & the Boat Ride

  • The Argentine and Brazilian parks sell separate tickets, there's no combined pass since they're run by two different countries.
  • The Gran Aventura boat ride is a paid add-on booked separately at Central Station in the Argentine park, not included in general entry.
  • Book Argentine park tickets online in advance where possible, foreign-visitor pricing and queues can both be steep at the gate in high season.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Both national parks are UNESCO World Heritage Sites with rules against feeding wildlife, leaving the marked trails, and drone flights.
  • Travel insurance covering both countries is worth having given the border crossing, the boat ride, and the amount of walking involved.
  • Yellow fever vaccination isn't mandatory for this region but is sometimes recommended, check current guidance for your specific itinerary before you go.
