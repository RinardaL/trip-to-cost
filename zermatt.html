4 Days in Zermatt: Matterhorn Views & Alpine Trails

Four days in a car-free alpine village beneath the Matterhorn are enough to ride two of the world's great mountain railways, hike close to the glacier, and still leave time for a slow coffee on a car-free village street.

Ideal SeasonJun – Sep
Est. Budget$1,600-2,200
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate to Active
How many days do you have?

Morning

Train to Täsch, then the shuttle into Zermatt

Zermatt is entirely car-free, so drivers park in Täsch and finish the last stretch on the frequent shuttle train, while everyone else simply rides the main line straight in.

🚆 ~12 min shuttle train from Täsch, or direct rail from Visp/Brig 💵 ~CHF 17 round trip from Täsch 🕐 Trains run every 20 minutes, no need to time it precisely
Afternoon

Check in and an electric taxi to the hotel

Only electric taxis and small electric vehicles move luggage and guests around the village, a novelty that also means near-silent streets from the moment you arrive.

🔋 Electric taxi from the station 💵 ~CHF 15 taxi fare 🕐 Early afternoon, to settle in before the walk
Evening

First walk through the village with a Matterhorn view

Bahnhofstrasse and the old Hinterdorf quarter's weathered wooden barns make for an easy first stroll, with the Matterhorn's summit catching the last light straight ahead.

🚶 Flat walk through the village center 💵 Free to stroll 🕐 An hour before sunset, for alpenglow on the peak
Morning

Ride the Gornergrat Railway to the summit

The red cog train climbs out of Zermatt and above the treeline in about 33 minutes, ending at 3,089 meters with the Matterhorn framed dead ahead and the Gorner Glacier below.

🚞 ~33 min cog railway from Zermatt station 💵 ~CHF 100 round trip 🕐 First train at opening, before afternoon clouds build
Midday

Gornergrat viewpoint and the terrace

Twenty-nine peaks over 4,000 meters ring the horizon from the observation terrace, with the Matterhorn as the unmistakable centerpiece and the Monte Rosa massif filling the other side.

🚶 A few steps from the Gornergrat platform 💵 Included in the rail ticket 🕐 Late morning, the clearest window before clouds gather
Afternoon

Descend partway and hike back through Riffelalp

Hopping off at Rotenboden or Riffelberg for a downhill hike through wildflower meadows stretches the Matterhorn views out over a couple more relaxed hours before the train ride's over.

🥾 ~2 hr downhill hike, or ride the train the whole way 💵 No extra cost on the same ticket 🕐 Early afternoon, before the light gets flat
Morning

Cable car up to Matterhorn Glacier Paradise

A series of cable cars climbs to 3,883 meters, Europe's highest-altitude cable car station, where a glacier palace of ice tunnels sits beside a sweeping view over more than a dozen glaciers.

🚡 ~40 min in stages from Zermatt 💵 ~CHF 115 round trip 🕐 First departure, for clear visibility before afternoon cloud buildup
Midday

Glacier Palace and the summit viewing platform

The ice cave carved into the glacier and the outdoor platform both put the Matterhorn's sheer north face closer than any other easily reached viewpoint around the village.

🚶 On-site at the summit station 💵 Included with the lift ticket 🕐 Late morning, while skies are typically clearest
Afternoon

Alpine hike to the Matterhorn Glacier Trail

Back down at Schwarzsee or Furi, a well-marked trail winds past the glacier's old moraine lines with the Matterhorn looming close enough to feel genuinely enormous.

🥾 ~2.5 hr moderate hike from Schwarzsee 💵 Free once you're off the lift 🕐 Early afternoon, descending before evening cloud rolls in
Morning

Slow breakfast and Bahnhofstrasse shopping

With the mountains checked off, a last morning is best spent over coffee at a streetside table before browsing the watch shops and outdoor outfitters along the main street.

🚶 Flat walk along Bahnhofstrasse 💵 ~CHF 12 for coffee and pastry 🕐 Late morning, once shops open around 9 AM
Midday

Spa hour or a final short walk to Gorner Gorge

A hotel spa session is a fitting way to ease legs tired from two days of altitude and hiking, or trade it for a short flat walk to the dramatic Gorner Gorge just outside the village.

🚶 ~20 min walk to Gorner Gorge 💵 ~CHF 8 gorge entry 🕐 Midday, leaving the afternoon free to pack
Afternoon

Train departure via Täsch or Visp

Retrace the shuttle to Täsch for a parked car, or stay on the through train toward Visp and Brig to connect onward to Zurich or Geneva airport.

🚆 ~12 min shuttle to Täsch, longer connections onward 💵 ~CHF 17 to Täsch 🕐 Leave at least 4 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is included by law in Swiss restaurant and hotel bills, so tipping isn't expected the way it is in the US.
  • Rounding up the bill or leaving a few francs for good service is a nice gesture, common among locals but never required.
  • Mountain restaurants and cable car cafes follow the same rule, the price on the menu is the price you pay.

👗 Dress Code

  • Alpine weather shifts fast, so layer up: a moisture-wicking base layer, an insulating mid-layer, and a windproof or waterproof shell that packs down small.
  • Temperatures at Gornergrat and Matterhorn Glacier Paradise can sit 15 to 20 degrees Celsius colder than the village, even in summer, so bring gloves and a warm hat regardless of season.
  • Village restaurants are smart-casual at most; hiking boots at dinner are completely normal here.

🚡 Getting Around a Car-Free Village

  • Zermatt has banned combustion-engine vehicles for over a century, the only traffic is electric taxis, electric hotel shuttles, and the occasional horse-drawn carriage.
  • The village is compact enough to walk end to end in about 20 minutes, and most hotels offer a free electric shuttle from the station.
  • All mountain excursions leave from stations within walking distance of the center, no local transit pass is needed for this itinerary.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Altitude at Gornergrat and Matterhorn Glacier Paradise can bring on light-headedness or headaches, drink water and ascend gradually if you feel it.
  • Sun exposure is far stronger above 3,000 meters, wear high-SPF sunscreen and sunglasses even on overcast days, especially near snow.
  • Cable cars and the Gornergrat line occasionally close for high wind or fog, build a buffer day into your plans if the mountain views matter most.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in German

  • Grüezi: hello (Swiss German greeting)
  • Vielen Dank: thank you very much
  • Die Rechnung, bitte: the bill, please
  • Switzerland also has French and Italian as national languages, though the Zermatt region is German-speaking Swiss German territory.

💳 Money & Payments

  • Switzerland uses the Swiss franc (CHF), not the euro, though some shops near the station will accept euros at a mediocre exchange rate.
  • Cards and contactless payments are accepted almost everywhere, including cable car ticket counters and mountain restaurants.
  • Switzerland has one of the highest price levels in Europe, budget accordingly for meals, lift tickets, and lodging well above nearby Italy or France.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Get a multi-day Peak Pass

A 2 or 3-day Peak Pass covering both Gornergrat and Matterhorn Glacier Paradise costs meaningfully less per day than buying each lift ticket separately.

🥪

Picnic instead of the mountain restaurant

Buying sandwiches and snacks in the village before heading up saves considerably over the mountain-top restaurant prices at Gornergrat or Matterhorn Glacier Paradise.

🏨

Book a hotel with a half-board rate

Given how expensive standalone dinners run in Zermatt, a half-board hotel package often works out cheaper than paying for restaurant meals each night.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌄

Rothorn over Gornergrat for fewer crowds

The Rothorn cable car on the opposite side of the valley gives a nearly as impressive Matterhorn panorama with a fraction of the Gornergrat crowds.

🏔️

Five Lakes Trail for reflection photos

The Fünf-Seenweg links five small alpine lakes, several of which mirror the Matterhorn on a calm morning, without needing any lift ticket beyond the first one up.

🌲

Sunnegga at sunrise

The smaller Sunnegga funicular gets moving early and offers a quiet, near-empty viewpoint before the bigger Gornergrat and Matterhorn Glacier Paradise lines fill up.

✈️ Flights

  • Zermatt has no airport of its own, Zurich (ZRH) and Geneva (GVA) are the two real international gateways, both requiring a train transfer.
  • From Zurich, the scenic rail journey to Zermatt via Visp takes around 3.5 hours with one change; from Geneva it's a similar length via Visp or Brig.
  • Book the train connection in advance during summer, seats on the final Zermatt leg can sell out on peak-season weekends.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Only electric taxis operate within the car-free village, easily flagged at the station or booked through your hotel.
  • Ride-hailing apps have limited presence here; hotels and the taxi stand outside the station are the more reliable option.
  • Most everything on this itinerary is within walking distance, so taxis are mainly useful for luggage transfers on arrival and departure days.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • A rental car is genuinely useless for this trip, Zermatt bans private combustion vehicles entirely from the village.
  • Drivers must park in Täsch and finish the journey by shuttle train regardless, so there's no time or money saved by renting.
  • Skip the rental altogether and put that budget toward the Gornergrat and Matterhorn Glacier Paradise lift tickets instead.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Bahnhofstrasse and the village center: closest to the train station and shops, convenient for the Day 1 arrival and Day 4 departure.
  • Hinterdorf and the older quarter: quieter wooden-chalet streets a short walk from the center, good for a slower village mood.
  • Near the Gornergrat or Matterhorn Glacier Paradise base stations: a short walk shaves time off early starts on Day 2 and Day 3.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • Zermatt is a landlocked alpine village at over 1,600 meters elevation, so boat trips simply aren't applicable here.
  • The nearest lake excursions are hours away by train near Geneva or Lucerne, better suited to a separate add-on trip.
  • The alpine lakes on the Five Lakes Trail are small reflection pools for photos, not boating destinations.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Switzerland is part of the Schengen area but not the EU, so some non-EU travelers should still double-check passport and visa rules before booking.
  • Remember the local currency is the Swiss franc (CHF), not the euro, when budgeting for this trip.
  • Travel insurance that covers altitude activities and cable car disruptions is worth adding, given the Day 2 and Day 3 mountain excursions.
