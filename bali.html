💵 Tipping Etiquette

  • Not obligatory: round up or leave 10% at nicer restaurants.
  • Spa and massage staff appreciate a small tip directly, not always shared via the front desk.
  • No tipping expected at warungs (local eateries).

🙏 Dress Code & Temple Etiquette

  • A sarong and sash are required at temples: most rent them right at the entrance.
  • Cover shoulders and avoid temple visits during menstruation, per local custom.
  • Beachwear is fine on the coast, but cover up when moving inland.

🛵 Getting Around

  • Scooter rental is common but technically requires an International Driving Permit.
  • Grab and Gojek apps are the easiest, cheapest way to book rides and drivers.
  • Hiring a private driver for the day is affordable and popular for temple-hopping.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Never step on or over the small woven offerings (canang sari) placed on the ground.
  • Strong equatorial sun: dehydration and sunburn happen fast, even on cloudy days.
  • Use the right hand for giving/receiving items and eating, considered respectful local etiquette.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases

  • Selamat pagi: good morning
  • Terima kasih: thank you
  • Berapa harga ini? how much is this?

💳 Money & Payments

  • Indonesian Rupiah (IDR) cash needed for warungs, markets, and scooter rentals.
  • Cards accepted at hotels, resorts, and larger restaurants.
  • ATMs are common in Ubud and Seminyak, less so in rural areas.

✈️ Flights

  • Ngurah Rai International Airport (DPS) in Denpasar is the only gateway, right on the Bukit Peninsula near Kuta.
  • Ubud sits about 1h 15min inland, so arrange an airport transfer or private driver ahead of landing rather than hailing one cold.
  • Confirm your driver's name and plate number in advance; the arrivals area outside DPS gets chaotic.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Grab and Gojek apps are the easiest, cheapest way to book rides in the towns and along the coast.
  • Street taxis exist but agree a price before getting in if the meter isn't used, especially near tourist areas.
  • For longer legs like Ubud to Uluwatu or Uluwatu to Seminyak, hiring a private driver for the day beats stacking app rides.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Self-drive rental isn't the norm here: this itinerary runs on private drivers for longer legs and scooters for short local hops.
  • Scooter rental is common and cheap, but technically requires an International Driving Permit: pack one if you plan to ride.
  • Bali's traffic and road rules differ enough from home that a private driver is the easier, less stressful choice for day trips.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Ubud: rice terraces, the Monkey Forest, and yoga studios all within reach, ideal for the wellness-focused first days.
  • Uluwatu: clifftop and close to the temple and the Kecak fire dance, worth a night rather than driving back to Ubud after dark.
  • Seminyak: beach clubs and spa treatments, with an easy final transfer down to Kuta and the airport.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • Essential for this itinerary: the Nusa Penida day trip runs by fast speedboat from Bali's coast.
  • Book the earliest crossing: the water is calmest and Kelingking Beach and Broken Bay are quieter before the day-tour crowds arrive.
  • Confirm your return crossing time when booking; missing the last boat means an unplanned night on Nusa Penida.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Most nationalities get visa-on-arrival or visa-free entry to Indonesia for short stays; confirm the current rule for your passport before flying.
  • Pack an International Driving Permit if renting a scooter: checkpoints do happen and local insurance may not cover riders without one.
  • Travel insurance is worth it for scooter mishaps and the Nusa Penida boat crossing alone.
