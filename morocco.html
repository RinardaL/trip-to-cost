6 Days in Morocco: Fes, Chefchaouen & the Sahara Desert

A northern and eastern Morocco loop: the tanneries and ancient university of Fes el-Bali, the blue-painted lanes of Chefchaouen in the Rif Mountains, a canyon-and-oasis drive over the Atlas mountain passes, and a sunset camel trek into the Erg Chebbi dunes for a night under the stars.

Ideal SeasonMar – May, Sep – Nov
Est. Budget$750-1,050/person
Duration6 Days
PaceActive
How many days do you have?

Morning

Land at Fes-Saiss Airport & transfer into the Medina

Fes-Saiss sits about 15 minutes from the old city; petit taxis wait outside arrivals and cars cannot enter the Medina's alleys, so riad hosts typically meet guests at the nearest gate to walk them in.

🚕 ~15-20 min by petit taxi 💵 ~$8-10 🕐 Confirm check-in time with your riad
Afternoon

Through Bab Boujloud into the world's largest car-free urban area

The blue-and-green tiled Bab Boujloud gate opens onto Talaa Kebira, the medina's main artery. Fes el-Bali has no cars and barely any motorbikes, so donkeys and hand-carts still do the moving here, a genuinely different rhythm from Marrakech's souks.

🚶 steps from the Medina entrance 💵 Free to wander 🕐 Mid-afternoon, before dinner
Evening

First dinner in a Fes riad courtyard

Riads here favor Fassi cooking over Marrakech's rooftop-terrace scene: a quieter, indoor courtyard meal of pastilla or a slow-cooked lamb tagine is the classic first-night introduction to the city.

🚶 dinner at your riad 💵 ~$15-20 🕐 Early night before a full day tomorrow
Morning

Chouara Tannery viewing terrace

Leather has been hand-dyed in these honeycombed stone vats since the 11th century using the same methods; a leather-shop balcony above gives the classic overhead view, and a sprig of mint at the door helps with the smell.

🚶 ~10 min into the tanners' quarter 💵 Free viewing, tip expected 🕐 Morning, when the vats are most active
Afternoon

Al-Qarawiyyin, among the world's oldest universities

Founded in 859 CE and often cited as the oldest continuously operating institution of higher learning anywhere, Al-Qarawiyyin's mosque-university is still active; non-Muslims can't enter the prayer hall but the ornate exterior and Bab El Werd gate are open to view.

🚶 ~5 min from the tanneries 💵 Free to view the exterior 🕐 Early afternoon
Evening

Chicken tagine with preserved lemon

Fassi cuisine is considered Morocco's most refined, and a chicken tagine simmered with preserved lemon and olives is the dish to order here before an early night ahead of tomorrow's mountain drive.

🚶 Medina restaurant 💵 ~$10-15 🕐 Evening
Morning

Drive north through the Middle Atlas cedar forests

The road out of Fes climbs into cool cedar forest around Ifrane, a genuine change of scenery from the desert and medina landscapes bookending this trip; keep an eye out for Barbary macaques along the roadside.

🚗 ~4-4.5 hr drive to Chefchaouen 💵 ~$70-90/day rental car 🕐 Depart Fes by 8 AM
Afternoon

First look at Chefchaouen's blue medina

Founded in 1471 in a fold of the Rif Mountains, Chefchaouen's walls have been washed in shades of blue since the 1930s. Check into a guesthouse inside the old town and take a slow first wander before the light fades.

🚶 walkable hillside medina 💵 Free to wander 🕐 Late afternoon arrival
Evening

Wander the blue alleys at dusk

Chefchaouen's steep lanes are still a working town, not just a photo backdrop: donkeys hauling goods squeeze past cafe tables as the blue walls turn deep indigo under the streetlights.

🚶 the whole old town is walkable 💵 ~$8-12 dinner 🕐 After sunset
Morning

The Kasbah & its small ethnographic museum

Built in the 15th century and later used as a prison, the red-walled kasbah sits right off the main plaza; its garden and short tower climb give the first proper rooftop view over the blue town.

🚶 on the main plaza 💵 ~$1 🕐 Morning, right at opening
Afternoon

Plaza Uta el-Hammam & the weavers' souk

The main square is Chefchaouen's social hub, ringed by cafes and the Great Mosque's octagonal minaret. The compact souk nearby sells the wool blankets and rugs the Rif region is known for, at a far gentler bargaining pace than Marrakech.

🚶 central square 💵 Free to browse, bargain gently 🕐 Afternoon
Evening

Sunset at Ras el-Ma spring

The spring at the edge of town, the reason Chefchaouen was founded here in the first place, is where locals wash carpets and wool by hand; the walk up is an easy, scenic close to the day before packing for the desert.

🚶 ~10 min from the plaza 💵 Free 🕐 Late afternoon into sunset
Morning

South over the Tizi n'Talghomt pass & down into the Ziz Valley

The long drive climbs over the Tizi n'Talghomt mountain pass before dropping through the switchbacks of the Ziz Gorge into a valley of palm groves and kasbahs that runs unbroken for miles, a first glimpse of the pre-Sahara landscape ahead.

🚗 ~5 hr to the Ziz Valley 💵 Fuel and toll costs included in rental 🕐 Depart Chefchaouen by 7 AM
Afternoon

Detour through Todra Gorge, then on to Merzouga

Todra's canyon walls narrow to about 10 meters apart and rise over 160 meters straight up, a dramatic roadside stop before the final push across open hammada desert to the dune village of Merzouga.

🚗 ~3 hr from Todra to Merzouga 💵 ~$5 gorge-side cafe stop 🕐 Early afternoon
Evening

Sunset camel trek into the Erg Chebbi dunes

Leave the 4x4 at a fixed camp on the desert's edge and ride out by camel as the Erg Chebbi dunes, some rising over 150 meters, turn gold and then deep orange with the setting sun.

🐫 ~1 hr camel trek to camp 💵 Usually included in a desert-camp package 🕐 Depart camp roughly 90 min before sunset
Morning

Climb a dune for sunrise, mint tea at camp

After a night in a Berber-style tent, with the Sahara's total darkness making for genuinely spectacular stargazing, the ritual is a short pre-dawn climb up the nearest dune ridge to watch the light break over the sand.

🥾 ~15 min barefoot climb up the ridge 💵 Included in the camp package 🕐 Before sunrise, roughly 6 AM
Afternoon

Camel out, then the drive back north

The camels retrace the route out to the 4x4 waiting at the desert's edge, followed by the long drive back across the Atlas toward Fes, the loop's natural endpoint for a flight out.

🚗 ~6.5-7 hr drive back to Fes 💵 Fuel included in rental 🕐 Leave camp by 9 AM
Evening

Farewell dinner back in Fes

A late arrival back into Fes closes the loop with one last Fassi meal, before a flight out the next morning from the same Fes-Saiss airport this trip started at.

🚶 dinner in the Medina 💵 ~$15-20 🕐 Evening

💵 Tipping Etiquette

  • Not obligatory, but round up or add roughly 10% at sit-down restaurants.
  • A few dirhams for the tannery viewing terraces, desert-camp staff, and camel guides is customary and appreciated.
  • Riad and guesthouse staff who go out of their way often receive a modest tip at checkout.

👗 Dress Code

  • Cover shoulders and knees in the medinas of Fes and Chefchaouen and at the desert camp; Morocco is a conservative, Muslim-majority country outside resort areas.
  • Lightweight, loose layers work best for the medina heat, cool Rif Mountain evenings, and the desert's dramatic day-night temperature swing.
  • A scarf or shawl doubles as sun protection on the dunes and extra coverage near mosque exteriors.

📸 Photography Norms

  • Always ask before photographing people directly, especially in the tanneries, souks, and desert camps; a smile and a gesture toward the camera usually gets a clear yes or no.
  • Some vendors and guides expect a small tip in exchange for being photographed at their stall or with their camel; agree on it upfront if you want the shot.
  • Non-Muslims cannot enter Al-Qarawiyyin's prayer hall or most working mosques, so photograph the exteriors and courtyards instead.

🛍️ Bargaining in the Souks

  • Haggling is expected at souk stalls in both Fes and Chefchaouen, not in fixed-price shops.
  • Chefchaouen's souk moves at a noticeably gentler pace than Fes or the larger imperial cities; a firm, friendly counteroffer is usually enough.
  • Walking away is a normal negotiating tactic and often brings the price down further.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • The Fes el-Bali medina is a genuine maze with thousands of alleys; self-appointed "guides" may offer to walk you somewhere for a tip, a polite decline works if you'd rather navigate alone.
  • Desert nights get cold even after scorching days, pack real layers for the Erg Chebbi camp regardless of season.
  • Mountain roads between Chefchaouen and the desert have sharp switchbacks; a private driver is worth it if you're not confident on unfamiliar hairpin passes.

🕌 Prayer Times & Ramadan

  • The call to prayer sounds five times daily and carries clearly through both medinas; some shops pause briefly around prayer times, especially midday Friday.
  • During Ramadan, avoid eating, drinking, or smoking in public during daylight hours out of respect.
  • Restaurant hours shift around the sunset iftar meal during Ramadan; confirm opening times, including at the desert camp, if traveling then.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Darija/French

  • Salam: hello
  • Shukran: thank you
  • Bezaf: too much (useful when bargaining)

💳 Money & Payments

  • The Moroccan Dirham (MAD) is a closed currency and is difficult or impossible to buy before you arrive.
  • Cash is essential in the medinas, at the tannery terraces, and for the desert camp; cards are common only in larger hotels and Fes's newer riads.
  • ATMs are reliable in Fes and Chefchaouen but nonexistent in Merzouga, so withdraw enough before the desert leg.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🐫

Book the desert camp as a package

A one-night camel trek and camp package from a Merzouga-based operator is usually cheaper and simpler than arranging the camel, tent, and dinner separately.

🚗

Split a shared driver for the long legs

Splitting a private driver across the Fes-Chefchaouen and Chefchaouen-Merzouga legs with other travelers cuts the per-person cost well below a solo rental for this distance.

🛍️

Bargain in dirham, not dollars

Negotiating in the local currency keeps prices grounded and avoids inflated "tourist math" in both the Fes and Chefchaouen souks.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

Climb a dune away from camp for sunrise

Walking even five minutes further than the crowd gathers a private ridge for sunrise, since most camps funnel guests to the same nearby dune.

💧

Ras el-Ma over the crowded viewpoints

Chefchaouen's Instagram-famous stairs get crowded by mid-morning; the spring at Ras el-Ma stays quieter and gives a more local, working-town view of the same blue walls.

🏛️

Bab El Werd over the crowded tannery balconies

The ornate entrance gate to Al-Qarawiyyin draws far fewer visitors than the Chouara Tannery terraces, and its tilework rivals anything in the medina.

✈️ Flights

  • Fes-Saiss Airport (FEZ) works well for this loop, close to day one's start and day six's return.
  • Casablanca Mohammed V (CMN) has far more international routes; many travelers fly into Casablanca and connect on to Fes, or add a rail leg.
  • Most Western passport holders don't need a visa for short tourist stays; confirm the current rule for your nationality before booking.

🚗 Driver vs. Self-Driving

  • A private driver for the full loop removes the stress of mountain switchbacks, unfamiliar signage, and long unlit desert roads, and is the more common choice for this route.
  • Self-driving works for confident drivers comfortable with hairpin mountain passes and gives more flexibility for photo stops in the Ziz Valley and Todra Gorge.
  • Either way, book well ahead in spring and autumn shoulder season when this route is busiest.

🏕️ Choosing a Desert Camp Operator

  • Look for operators with genuine reviews mentioning the exact camp, since some list photos of a nicer "luxury" camp while placing guests elsewhere.
  • Fixed, permanent camps with proper toilets tend to be more reliable than pop-up tents pitched fresh each night.
  • Confirm whether the sunset camel trek and next-morning transfer back to your vehicle are included in the quoted price.

🏨 Riads & Where to Stay

  • Fes: a riad inside Fes el-Bali puts the tanneries, Al-Qarawiyyin, and Bab Boujloud all within walking distance.
  • Chefchaouen: guesthouses inside the blue old town cost little more than those outside it and are worth the short uphill walk with luggage.
  • Merzouga: skip a hotel entirely for night five, the desert camp itself is the overnight stay.

🧭 Medina Navigation

  • Fes el-Bali is widely considered even more labyrinthine than Marrakech's medina; GPS often loses the plot in its narrow, unnamed alleys, so screenshot directions and note nearby landmarks.
  • Save your riad's phone number in both cities: staff can often talk you in by phone if you get turned around.
  • Chefchaouen's hillside layout is smaller and easier to navigate by eye, since the blue walls make backtracking simpler than in a monochrome medina.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Travel insurance that covers the camel trek and desert camp night is worth adding for this itinerary.
  • This route works best in the spring and autumn shoulder seasons; summer brings extreme Sahara heat and winter brings genuinely cold desert nights.
  • Pack for real temperature swings: cool mountain mornings in Chefchaouen, hot midday medina walking in Fes, and a sharp day-to-night drop in the desert.
