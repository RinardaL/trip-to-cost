4 Days in Livigno: Budget Skiing, Duty Free & Snowshoes

Two ski days on Mottolino and Carosello 3000 with 2026/27 lift pass prices, a free snowshoe and cross-country day, duty-free limits, and the long winter road in from Milan.

4-day Livigno itinerary

How many days do you have?

Morning

Malpensa Airport & the Livigno Express

Livigno has no airport, so most visitors land at Milan Malpensa (MXP), Terminal 1. The Livigno Express coach runs daily from 13 November 2026 to 3 May 2027, from about €65 (check current), and takes roughly 4 hours 40 minutes plus a 30-minute stop. Bergamo has the same service, and Linate runs on Saturdays only.

🚌 ~4 h 40 min by Livigno Express, plus a ~30 min stop 💵 From ~€65 one way (check current) 🕐 Daily service, book ahead and avoid tight connections
Afternoon

Village walk & duty-free shopping

Livigno sits outside the EU customs and VAT area, so fuel, spirits, tobacco and perfume are cheaper than elsewhere in Italy. Walk the main street and compare prices before buying. Limits apply when you leave: for Italy, each adult may take 1 litre of spirits, 2 litres of drinks under 22%, 4 of still wine and 16 of beer.

🚶 On foot along the village centre 💵 Browsing free, allowances apply when you leave 🕐 After dropping your bags, before dinner
Evening

Dinner in the village & a night walk

Eat early after the long transfer. Look for Valtellina dishes such as pizzoccheri (buckwheat pasta with cheese and cabbage) and sciatt (fried cheese fritters), roughly €15 to 25 a main course (estimate). A self-catering apartment and a supermarket supper cut costs further. Check tomorrow's lift and bus notices, then sleep early.

🚶 Short walks inside the village 💵 ~€15-25 for a main course (estimate) 🕐 Early dinner, early night before a ski day
Morning

Mottolino: lift pass & first runs

For 2026/27, an adult day ticket costs €51.50 in promotional weeks, €66 in season and €74.50 in high season, and two days cost €99 to €143.50. Buy online or at a lift office. Take the new 10-seat Mottolino gondola up, and ask about the beginner Easy Lift. Lifts open around 8:30 (check current).

🚡 Mottolino gondola from the base station 💵 1-day pass €51.50 to €74.50, 2-day €99 to €143.50 🕐 First lifts around 8:30 (check current)
Afternoon

Monte Sponda & a mountain lunch

Use the eight-seat Monte Sponda chairlift, which has heated seats, for the upper runs. Mountain restaurants cost more than village delis, so pack a sandwich from a supermarket and eat outside. Helmets are compulsory on Italian pistes and third-party insurance is required, so check what your pass includes when you buy it.

🎿 Chairlifts and pistes inside the ski area 💵 Pass already bought, packed lunch is much cheaper than a hut 🕐 Ski until mid-afternoon, then check last lift times
Evening

Aquagranda spa & a quiet night

Aquagranda, in via Rasia, has three saunas, a steam bath, hot and cold circuits and six outdoor pools. Wellness and Relax entry is about €58, or about €46 with a Gold MyLivignoPass (check current). It is the biggest splurge on this page, so skip it if funds are tight and rest your legs at home.

🚌 Local bus or a short walk, depending on where you stay 💵 ~€58 entry, ~€46 with a Gold card (check current) 🕐 Evening, check Aquagranda hours before you go
Morning

Carosello 3000: the high runs

Carosello 3000 is the high side of the ski area. Ride the gondola from the village, then the six-seat Altavista chairlift, which reaches 2,794 m, the highest point you can reach on skis. Start early for firmer snow and shorter queues. The same Livigno pass covers both sides, which add up to 115 km of pistes.

🚡 Carosello 3000 gondola, then the Altavista chairlift 💵 Pass already bought (day tickets €51.50 to €74.50) 🕐 First lifts, around 8:30 (check current)
Afternoon

Costaccia & the run back to the village

Costaccia is a restaurant and lift area on the Carosello side, with long runs back toward town. The Carosello 3000 hut is self-service with a sandwich bar, usually the cheaper way to eat on snow. Many lifts start on the valley floor, so you can ski back to your door. Check piste closures on the lift map.

🎿 Chairlifts and pistes down toward Livigno 💵 Hut sandwich or packed lunch, table service costs more 🕐 Last lifts close mid-afternoon, check the board
Evening

Church square, dinner & a rest day plan

A pizzeria or village trattoria a street or two off the main road is usually cheaper than one on it, and daily specials are the best value. Tomorrow is a rest day for your legs. If your accommodation sells cross-country tickets, buy one there for a 30% discount, and pick up snowshoes from a village rental shop.

🚶 On foot inside the village 💵 ~€15-25 for dinner (estimate) 🕐 Dinner after skiing, then an early night
Morning

Cross-country or snowshoes on the valley floor

Livigno has about 30 km of cross-country trails. A day ticket for the tourist track is about €10 for adults, or 30% less through your accommodation (check current). Managed snowshoe trails run from the lake area toward Forcola, free to walk and marked with green and fuchsia posts. Check the avalanche bulletin first.

🚶 Trails start near the village and the lake 💵 Cross-country ~€10 a day, snowshoe trails free, gear extra 🕐 Morning, after the daily trail checks
Afternoon

Trepalle & Passo Eira

The free Livigno to Trepalle bus takes roughly 15 to 20 minutes. At Passo Eira, a snowshoe circuit tops the pass. Snowmobile rides from Trepalle start at about €30 for 15 minutes (2024/25 price, check current). Wear sunglasses and sunscreen, because the glare at 2,000 m and above is strong.

🚌 Free bus to Trepalle, ~15-20 min 💵 Bus and snowshoe circuit free, snowmobile from ~€30 🕐 Early afternoon, buses run about 7:30 to 20:00
Evening

Last duty-free stop & the road out

Shop last. Leaving for Italy, each adult may carry 200 cigarettes, 10 litres of spare fuel and €300 of goods; for Switzerland, CHF 150 of goods. Driving out? Winter tyres or chains are required, and the Munt la Schera tunnel costs about CHF 20 to 29 one way online (check current).

🚌 Next morning: Livigno Express back to Milan, ~4 h 40 min 💵 Tunnel ~CHF 20-29 one way for cars (check current) 🕐 Book a flight home after about 15:00 on departure day

Livigno: what to know before you go

💶 Tipping & Cover Charge

  • Tipping is not expected in Italy. Rounding up the bill or leaving a few euros for good service is plenty, and a service charge is sometimes already included.
  • Many restaurants add a small cover charge (coperto) per person. It is legal if it is shown on the menu, so check it before you sit down.
  • An espresso at the counter of a bar costs less than the same coffee at a table.

🧀 Mountain Food & Drink

  • Look for Valtellina and Alpine dishes: pizzoccheri (buckwheat pasta with cheese and cabbage), sciatt (fried cheese fritters), polenta and bresaola (air-dried beef).
  • Après-ski classics are hot chocolate and vin brulé (mulled wine). Bormio's bitter liqueur Braulio is a common digestif.
  • Self-service huts on the slopes cost less than table-service restaurants. A sandwich bought in the village and eaten on the terrace is cheaper still.

🎿 Slope Rules & Etiquette

  • Helmets are now compulsory on Italian pistes and skiers need third-party liability cover, so check what your lift pass includes (check current rules).
  • The skier ahead and below has priority. Stop at the edge or below a rise, not in the middle of a piste, and give way when you join a run.
  • Do not leave the marked runs without a guide and avalanche gear. Check the day's piste status on the lift map or the operator's board before heading up.
  • Queue calmly at the gondola and keep your skis tidy in racks so nobody trips.

⚠️ Safety, Weather & Customs Warnings

  • Avalanche risk is real off the marked runs. Read the avalanche bulletin on the Livigno tourism site before any snowshoe or backcountry outing, and stay on managed trails.
  • Do not walk on the frozen lake unless an operator or the tourist office says a marked circuit is open. Ice thickness is not guaranteed.
  • Goods above the duty-free allowance cannot be brought out of Livigno, even if you offer to pay duty. Check the official table or call the customs office in Tirano on +39 0342 701198.
  • Check your own government's Italy travel advice (such as the UK FCDO or the US State Department), and call 112 in an emergency.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Italian

  • "Buongiorno" (good morning), "Buonasera" (good evening), "Ciao" (hi, bye, informal)
  • "Per favore" (please), "Grazie" (thank you), "Prego" (you're welcome), "Scusi" (excuse me)
  • "Uno skipass giornaliero, per favore" (one day lift pass, please), "Il conto, per favore" (the bill, please)
  • "Parla inglese?" (do you speak English?), "Dov'è...?" (where is...?), "Quanto costa?" (how much is it?)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Livigno uses the euro, and cards and contactless work in most shops, hotels and lift offices. Keep a little cash for small bars and mountain huts.
  • Use bank-branded ATMs in the village and avoid standalone exchange booths. If a terminal offers to charge you in your home currency, choose euros.
  • Ask your accommodation whether a local tourist tax is charged and how it is paid, because it is usually due at the property.

Insider tips for Livigno

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

📅

Travel in the promotional weeks

For 2026/27, an adult day ticket costs €51.50 from 28 November to 18 December and from 10 April to 2 May, against €74.50 in the high-season weeks. A 2-day pass is €99 in the cheapest period (check current).

🎟️

Buy half-day or 2-day passes

A morning or afternoon pass costs €41 in promotional weeks and €59 in high season, and a 2-day pass costs less per day than a day ticket. Choose the shortest pass that fits your legs and your plan.

🏨

Ask about stay bundles and MyLivignoPass

Some hotels and apartments offer Skipass Free deals with longer stays, and MyLivignoPass is free with partner accommodation and covers local bus travel. Compare these before buying passes separately.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🧊

Ask about the lake circuit

Local operators list a snowshoe circuit on the frozen Lake Livigno and a second one at the top of Passo Eira. Ask the tourist office which circuits are open and marked, and keep off the ice otherwise.

🌅

Take the free bus to Trepalle

The Livigno to Trepalle line is free and reaches a high hamlet with a wide view of the valley. It is a cheap way to see the mountains without lifts, and a good place to catch late light.

🌙

Try an evening guided snowshoe

Local guides run evening snowshoe outings, listed at about €45 per person, or about €30 with MyLivignoPass (third-party listing, check current). They offer a quiet, lit-by-snow walk after dinner.

Travel services for Livigno

✈️ Flights

  • Livigno has no airport. The tourism site lists Milan Malpensa, Linate, Bergamo and Verona, plus Zurich, Innsbruck, Munich and Friedrichshafen. Malpensa and Bergamo have the most direct coaches.
  • The Livigno Express serves Malpensa T1 and Bergamo daily and Linate on Saturdays only, from about €65 one way (check current), in roughly 4 hours 40 minutes plus a stop.
  • From Zurich, you can take the train to Zernez and the bus to Livigno, a trip of roughly 4 hours or more (check SBB for current times).

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Livigno has a local taxi service, bookable through its website. Use it for late returns from dinner or for luggage-heavy hotel transfers.
  • Long-distance taxis from Milan cost far more than the coach, so book the shuttle if you can.
  • Rideshare coverage is thin in the mountains, so do not count on an app for the transfer.

🚇 Public Transport & Tickets

  • Local buses run roughly 7:30 to 20:00, hourly on three lines, with extra routes in high season. The Livigno to Trepalle line is free, and MyLivignoPass covers local buses (check current).
  • Winter coaches also run from Livigno to Bormio and Tirano (Perego) and to Zernez (Silvestri). Livigno to Zernez has about 6 daily runs in winter. Confirm the Tirano link, as sources differ on whether it runs in winter.
  • The train from Milano Centrale to Tirano takes about 2 hours 30 minutes and costs about €12.50 (check Trenord).
  • Buy bus and coach tickets from the operator, and keep the ticket until you arrive.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Livigno centre: the busiest area, with shops and restaurants close by, and usually the priciest rooms.
  • The edges of the village: quieter and usually cheaper, but you may need a bus or a longer walk to the lifts.
  • Self-catering apartments: a good way to save money on food, and many offer Skipass Free bundles with longer stays (check current).
  • Booking tip: promotional weeks (28 November to 18 December and 10 April to 2 May) are cheaper than 26 December to 8 January and the February peak.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Italy is in the EU, so EU SIM holders roam at home rates. Other visitors can use an eSIM or a local prepaid SIM, and most hotels offer Wi-Fi.
  • Near the Swiss border your phone may connect to a Swiss network, so check your roaming settings.
  • Cards work at most shops and lift offices, but keep some cash for huts and small bars.
  • Use bank-branded ATMs and avoid standalone exchange booths.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Check your passport and visa rules for the Schengen area. The EU Entry/Exit System is in use at the border, and ETIAS was not yet operating at the time of writing, so check the official ETIAS site.
  • If you drive, winter tyres or chains are compulsory from 15 October to 30 April on the Foscagno route and for the Munt la Schera tunnel. The Forcola pass is closed in winter.
  • The Munt la Schera tunnel is single-lane with alternating one-way traffic. It is normally open 24 hours, but check hours before you travel, especially on Saturdays.
  • Reduced and multi-day lift passes need a photo and official ID.
