7 Days on the Iceland Ring Road: Waterfalls, Glaciers & Hot Springs

Seven days and about 1,620 km clockwise around Route 1 from Keflavík: waterfalls, a glacier lagoon, the East Fjords, Dettifoss and Mývatn, with the car cost split for two.

7-day Iceland Ring Road itinerary

How many days do you have?

Morning

Keflavík Airport & collecting the car

Many hire firms run a free shuttle from the arrivals hall to a yard a few minutes away (check your voucher). Bring your driving licence and a credit card in the driver's name. Photograph every scratch, confirm petrol or diesel, and buy food at a Bónus before leaving. SafeTravel advises resting before driving after an overnight flight.

🚌 ~5-10 min hire-firm shuttle to the yard 💵 Supermarket stock-up ~$20-30 (check current) 🕐 Allow about 1 h for paperwork and the check-in photos
Afternoon

Seljalandsfoss & Gljúfrabúi

Take Route 41 and then Route 1 past Reykjavík and over the Hellisheiði pass. Seljalandsfoss falls about 60 m from a cliff, and the path behind it is wet and slippery. Parking is about ISK 1,000 (check current), paid by app or machine. Gljúfrabúi, partly hidden in a gorge a few minutes' walk north, needs waterproofs.

🚗 ~165 km, 2 h 15 min from Keflavík 💵 Falls free, parking ~ISK 1,000 (~$8, check current) 🕐 Afternoon, with time to walk behind the falls
Evening

Skógafoss & the night in Vík

Skógafoss, about 30 km on, is a 60 m curtain of water you can walk right up to. A staircase beside it climbs to a viewpoint above the lip. Parking is about ISK 1,000 by app (check current). Then drive 35 km to Vík, where beds are pricey, so book months ahead.

🚗 ~30 km, 25 min to Skógafoss, then ~35 km, 30 min to Vík 💵 Falls free, parking ~ISK 1,000 (check current) 🕐 Early evening, then check in at Vík
Morning

Reynisfjara black sand beach

Reynisfjara is 10 km from Vík. Sneaker waves reach far up the beach and have killed visitors, so follow the warning lights and signs, stay well back from the water and never turn your back on the sea. Parking fees may apply (check current). Arrive early, before the tour coaches.

🚗 ~10 km, 15 min from Vík 💵 Beach free, parking fee may apply (check current) 🕐 Early morning, and check the wave warning first
Afternoon

Skaftafell & the Svartifoss walk

Skaftafell is 140 km east on Route 1. Fill up at Kirkjubæjarklaustur, the last dependable station for about 205 km to Höfn. Park at the visitor centre for ISK 1,110 (national park fee, valid until midnight). The easy 5.8 km Svartifoss loop takes about 2 hours and ends at a waterfall framed by basalt columns.

🚗 ~140 km, 2 h from Vík 💵 Parking ISK 1,110 per car, trail free 🕐 Midday, allow 2 h for the loop
Evening

Jökulsárlón glacier lagoon & Diamond Beach

Jökulsárlón is 57 km on, where icebergs from the Breiðamerkurjökull glacier drift toward the sea. Viewing is free, and the park parking fee is half price (ISK 555) if you paid at Skaftafell the same day. Walk across the road to Diamond Beach, then drive 65 km to Höfn. Boat tours cost extra.

🚗 ~57 km, 50 min to Jökulsárlón, then ~65 km, 1 h to Höfn 💵 Parking ~ISK 555 after Skaftafell, boat tours extra (check current) 🕐 Early evening, when day-trip coaches have left
Morning

Stokksnes & Vestrahorn

Stokksnes is 17 km from Höfn, with the Vestrahorn peaks behind a black sand beach and dunes. The fee is about ISK 1,100 per person (check current), paid at the Viking Café. Fill the tank in Höfn before heading east, and pay the Hornafjörður bridge toll (ISK 1,500) at spolur.is within 24 hours.

🚗 ~17 km, 25 min each way from Höfn 💵 ~ISK 1,100 per person (check current), bridge toll ISK 1,500 🕐 Morning, when the light is soft and the car park quiet
Afternoon

Djúpivogur & the fjord road

Route 1 follows the coast fjord after fjord. At Gleðivík harbour in Djúpivogur, Eggin í Gleðivík is a row of 34 stone eggs, one for each bird species nesting in the area. It is free, and the village has a café for coffee. Stations are far apart here, so fill up whenever the tank is half empty.

🚗 ~100 km, 1 h 30 min from Höfn 💵 Sculptures free, coffee ~$5-7 🕐 Early afternoon, a leg-stretch stop
Evening

Egilsstaðir & the Seyðisfjörður rainbow street

From Djúpivogur, Route 1 runs about 160 km to Egilsstaðir via Breiðdalsvík and the Fáskrúðsfjörður tunnel. If light and energy allow, drive the 27 km pass road to Seyðisfjörður, a small town with a rainbow-painted path to its blue church. It is free. The pass can close in bad weather, so check road.is.

🚗 ~160 km, 2 h 15 min to Egilsstaðir, plus ~54 km round trip to Seyðisfjörður 💵 Sights free 🕐 Reach Egilsstaðir by about 6 PM to keep Seyðisfjörður
Morning

Route 1 over the Möðrudalur highlands

Route 1 climbs onto the bare Jökuldalsheiði and Möðrudalsöræfi moors, an exposed stretch. Fill up in Egilsstaðir first: there is no reliable station for about 165 km to Reykjahlíð. Möðrudalur's small church and farm café make a stop (check hours). Check road.is and vedur.is for wind before you go.

🚗 ~95 km, 1 h 30 min from Egilsstaðir to Möðrudalur 💵 Stop free, coffee ~$5-8 🕐 Leave Egilsstaðir by about 9 AM
Afternoon

Dettifoss on paved Route 862

Take paved Route 862 north from Route 1 to Dettifoss, 45 m high and 100 m wide, which the regional tourist board calls the most powerful waterfall in Europe. Avoid gravel Route 864 on the east bank. Route 862 is not in winter service from January to early April. Keep well back from the wet, slippery edges.

🚗 ~67 km, 1 h from Möðrudalur 💵 Free to visit (check for new parking fees) 🕐 Early afternoon, when sun brings out rainbows in the spray
Evening

Hverir & Lake Mývatn

Back on Route 1, Hverir at Námaskarð is a free field of mud pots and steam vents that smells of sulphur. Stay on the marked paths, because the crust is thin and the ground can scald. Reykjahlíð, on the lake, has a supermarket, fuel and guesthouses. The Earth Lagoon baths cost about ISK 7,900 (check current).

🚗 ~69 km, 1 h from Dettifoss to Reykjahlíð 💵 Hverir free, baths ~ISK 7,900 (~$64, check current) 🕐 Golden hour for the steam, and expect midges by the lake
Morning

Dimmuborgir & Skútustaðagígar

Dimmuborgir is a maze of lava pillars and arches with marked paths of different lengths. Skútustaðagígar, on the south shore, is a group of grassy pseudocraters with a short loop above the lake. Both are free. Midges can swarm around Mývatn in summer, so carry a head net, and keep to the paths.

🚗 ~36 km lake loop, about 45 min with stops between 💵 Both free 🕐 Morning, when it is calm and fewer midges are about
Afternoon

Goðafoss, the Waterfall of the Gods

Goðafoss is beside Route 1 with car parks on both banks. The horseshoe falls are about 12 m high and 30 m wide, and are named for the pagan idols said to have been thrown in when Iceland adopted Christianity around the year 1000. Entry is free, and the paths near the edge are slippery with spray.

🚗 ~49 km, 45 min from Mývatn 💵 Free 🕐 Afternoon, allow about 45 min
Evening

Akureyri via the Vaðlaheiðargöng toll tunnel

Route 1 reaches Akureyri through the 7.5 km Vaðlaheiðargöng tunnel, about ISK 2,216 per car. Pay at veggjald.is or tunnel.is up to 24 hours before or after, or at the kiosks. The free old road over Víkurskarð is an option in summer. Akureyri has a free botanical garden and a cheap town pool.

🚗 ~36 km, 40 min from Goðafoss 💵 Tunnel ~ISK 2,216 (~$18), pool ~ISK 1,400 (check current) 🕐 Arrive by early evening and check opening hours
Morning

Öxnadalur & the Glaumbær turf farm

Fill the tank in Akureyri, then follow Route 1 up the Öxnadalur valley and down into Skagafjörður. Glaumbær is a preserved turf farm that shows how most Icelanders lived until the 20th century. An entry fee applies, and hours are shorter outside summer (check current). Allow about 45 minutes.

🚗 ~100 km, 1 h 35 min from Akureyri 💵 Museum entry fee (check current) 🕐 Mid-morning, open daily in summer (check current)
Afternoon

Holtavörðuheiði & the Grábrók crater

Route 1 runs south past Blönduós and over the Holtavörðuheiði moor, where weather can turn fast. Staðarskáli is a classic roadside stop for fuel, hot dogs and coffee. Grábrók, a crater right beside the road in Norðurárdalur, has a stepped path to the rim: a short, free walk of roughly 20 to 30 minutes.

🚗 ~185 km, 2 h 30 min from Glaumbær 💵 Crater free, roadside lunch ~$10-15 🕐 Mid-afternoon, check the moor forecast before you cross
Evening

Borgarnes & a last self-catered dinner

Borgarnes, 38 km on, sits on a small peninsula in Borgarfjörður and is tied to Egil's Saga. It has supermarkets, fuel, a pool and guesthouses, and West Iceland hotel rates are among the lowest on the loop. Walk the shore path, then cook a final picnic-style dinner from the supermarket.

🚗 ~38 km, 30 min from Grábrók 💵 Self-catered dinner ~$15-20 🕐 Evening, with a relaxed stroll before dinner
Morning

Deildartunguhver & Hraunfossar

From Borgarnes take Route 50 inland. Deildartunguhver pours out about 180 litres a second of near-boiling water beside the road. Hraunfossar, 60 km from Borgarnes, is a long line of small falls streaming from the lava into the Hvítá, with Barnafoss a short walk upstream. Both are free (check for parking fees).

🚗 ~60 km, 50 min from Borgarnes to Hraunfossar 💵 Both free (check for parking fees) 🕐 Start by 8 AM so the day stays relaxed
Afternoon

Reykjavík: Hallgrímskirkja & the harbour

Return via Route 1 and the Hvalfjörður tunnel, free since 2018. Park once in Reykjavík (paid zones, pay by app or machine) and walk to Hallgrímskirkja: entering the church is free, the tower lift costs extra (check current). Then stroll down Skólavörðustígur to the harbour and Harpa.

🚗 ~120 km, 1 h 50 min from Hraunfossar, then on foot 💵 Paid parking by app, tower lift extra (check current) 🕐 Late afternoon, allow 1.5-2 h in town
Evening

Returning the car & flying home from Keflavík

Refuel before returning the car, since most firms want a full tank. Take Route 41 for 47 km to Keflavík. Hand the car back about 3.5 hours before your flight, to leave time for the inspection, the shuttle, check-in and security. Photograph the car again and keep the fuel receipt.

🚗 ~47 km, 45 min from Reykjavík 💵 Last fill-up, no one-way fee (same airport) 🕐 Return the car about 3.5 h before departure

Iceland Ring Road: what to know before you go

💶 Tipping, Pools & Tap Water

  • Tipping is not expected in Iceland. Service is built into prices, so round up only for exceptional service if you like.
  • Swimming pools are a daily habit and the cheapest way to soak. You must shower without your swimsuit before getting in, and the staff do check.
  • Tap water is cold and clean, and the hot water often smells of sulphur because it is geothermal. It is safe, so carry a refillable bottle and skip bottled water.

🌭 Eating & Drinking on the Road

  • Cheap local food: a hot dog "with everything" (eina með öllu) from a petrol station or a kiosk, lamb soup (kjötsúpa) and skyr from the supermarket.
  • Restaurant meals are expensive, so most budget travellers self-cater from Bónus or Krónan and treat themselves to one good dinner.
  • Alcohol is sold in state-run Vínbúðin shops and at licensed venues, not in supermarkets, and is costly. The drink-drive limit is very low.

🚗 Driving Etiquette on the Ring Road

  • Do not stop on the road to take photos. Use the pull-outs, because bends and rises hide oncoming traffic.
  • On single-lane bridges the car nearer the bridge has priority, but slow down and read what the other driver intends to do.
  • Let faster drivers pass at a safe place, and slow right down for sheep and lambs, which can bolt across the road.
  • In strong wind, park facing into it and hold the door with both hands when opening it.

⚠️ Safety, Weather & Nature Warnings

  • Check vedur.is for weather and wind warnings, road.is for road conditions and safetravel.is for alerts every morning. The emergency number is 112, and SafeTravel offers a free travel plan form.
  • Driving off roads and marked tracks is banned in Iceland and carries heavy fines. Stay on roads and paths, because moss and soil take decades to recover.
  • Sneaker waves at Reynisfjara have killed visitors. Obey the warning lights and signs, and never turn your back on the sea. Stay well back from the edges at Dettifoss and at geothermal areas, where the ground can scald.
  • Check the current rules on camping or sleeping in a car outside campsites with the Environment Agency of Iceland (ust.is) before you plan to do it.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Icelandic

  • "Halló" or "Hæ" (hello), "Góðan daginn" (GOH-thun DIE-in, good day), "Bless" (bless, bye)
  • "Takk" (tak, thanks), "Takk fyrir" (tak FIR-ir, thank you), "Já / Nei" (yow / nay, yes / no)
  • "Fyrirgefðu" (FIR-ir-gev-thu, sorry or excuse me), "Skál" (skowl, cheers)
  • "Talarðu ensku?" (TAL-ar-thu EN-skoo, do you speak English?), "Hvað kostar þetta?" (kvath KOS-tar THET-ta, how much is it?)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Iceland uses the króna (ISK). Cards and phone payments work almost everywhere, even for small purchases, so you rarely need cash.
  • Unmanned fuel pumps need a card with a PIN. Fuel stations may place a temporary hold on your card, so keep spare credit available.
  • If a card terminal offers to charge in your home currency, choose ISK. Tolls and some car parks are paid by plate number online or by app.

Insider tips for Iceland Ring Road

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🛒

Self-cater from Bónus and Krónan

Bónus is usually the cheapest supermarket chain, followed by Krónan. Buy bread, skyr, fruit and pasta, make a picnic lunch each day, and keep restaurants for one dinner. Hot dogs at petrol stations cover a quick, cheaper meal.

🏊

Soak in town pools, not spas

A municipal pool such as Akureyri's costs about ISK 1,400 (check current), against about ISK 7,900 for the Mývatn Earth Lagoon. Most towns have a geothermal pool with hot tubs, and entry includes the showers.

🅿️

Pay half for the second park fee

Skaftafell and Jökulsárlón share a parking fee of ISK 1,110 per car, valid until midnight. If you pay at one and visit the other the same date, the second is half price. Stop at both on Day 2.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

Use the long June evenings

Around midsummer the sun barely sets, so Vestrahorn, Hverir and Dettifoss can be visited late, when coaches have gone. In September, plan the big sights before late afternoon as daylight shrinks.

🥾

Add Hengifoss if you have time

Hengifoss, near Egilsstaðir in Fljótsdalur, is among the tallest waterfalls in Iceland, with striped red layers in the gorge walls. It is a walk of roughly an hour or two (check current), and a good swap if the weather closes in on the highlands.

🌋

Climb Hverfjall at Mývatn

Hverfjall is a large explosion crater beside Mývatn with a path up to its rim and views over the lake and lava fields. It is free, and best in clear weather, with a windproof layer for the top.

Travel services for Iceland Ring Road

✈️ Flights

  • Keflavík (KEF) is the international airport and the start and end of this loop, about 47 km from Reykjavík. Most hire firms are a short shuttle ride from the terminal.
  • A return into KEF is simplest: the car goes back where it started, so there is no one-way fee.
  • An open-jaw trip finishing at Akureyri (AEY) or Egilsstaðir would add a one-way hire fee and fewer flights (check the airlines).
  • Many transatlantic flights land early in the morning and leave in the afternoon, which matches Day 1 and Day 7.

🚗 Car Hire

  • Hire firms set their own minimum ages and licence rules, often 20 for small cars and 23 for 4x4s. Check licence rules, including whether an international permit is needed, with the Icelandic Transport Authority and your hire firm.
  • Bring a credit card in the main driver's name. The deposit is a hold, not a charge, and some firms need a card rather than a debit card.
  • Insurance: basic CDW leaves a large excess. Gravel protection covers windscreen, headlights and paint. Sand and ash protection (Hertz lists ISK 1,990 per day for small cars) covers wind-blown sand, which matters most on the South Coast.
  • Many credit-card policies exclude Iceland-specific risks such as gravel, F-roads and wind damage to doors, so confirm in writing.
  • A small manual or automatic is enough on Route 1 in summer. Compare road tax, extra drivers and fuel policy (full to full) before booking.

🛣️ Driving Rules & Roads

  • Drive on the right with headlights on at all times. Limits are 50 km/h in towns, 80 on gravel and 90 on paved rural roads unless signed. The alcohol limit is 0.02%, so even one drink is too much.
  • Route 1 still has about 26 single-lane bridges: slow to 50 km/h and give way if the other car is closer. Sheep roam freely and the road can switch to gravel without warning. Off-road driving is banned.
  • Fuel gaps: Kirkjubæjarklaustur to Höfn is about 205 km with no dependable station, and Egilsstaðir to Reykjahlíð about 165 km. Refuel at half a tank and check station hours.
  • Official sources: road.is (road conditions, phone 1777), safetravel.is (alerts, app) and vedur.is (weather and wind warnings). Winter conditions can occur in spring and autumn too.
  • F-roads are not part of this route and need a 4x4. In winter (roughly November to March) snow, wind and short daylight can close sections, so check road.is first.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Overnight stops: Vík, Höfn, Egilsstaðir, Mývatn (Reykjahlíð), Akureyri and Borgarnes. These towns have the most guesthouses, hostels and farm stays on the route.
  • A June 2026 guide puts summer mid-range doubles at about $280-420 on the South Coast, $300-500 around Höfn, $190-260 in the East, $200-300 in the North and $180-280 in the West. Guesthouses with shared bathrooms and hostel rooms cost less.
  • Shoulder months (late May, September) often trim 20 to 30 percent off summer rates. Vík and Höfn sell out first.
  • Book as early as you can: 6 to 12 months ahead for summer, and as soon as your flights are fixed for the shoulder months.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • EU and EEA SIM holders roam at home rates in Iceland. Others can use an eSIM or a local prepaid SIM. Coverage can be patchy on remote stretches, so download offline maps.
  • Install the SafeTravel app for alerts and the 112 emergency number.
  • Cards and phone pay work almost everywhere. Unmanned fuel pumps need a PIN card.
  • Tolls are paid by plate number online: Vaðlaheiðargöng at veggjald.is or tunnel.is (or kiosks), and the Hornafjörður bridge at spolur.is, each within 24 hours. Unpaid tolls are billed to the hire firm, which may add a fee.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Iceland is in the Schengen area. Check entry rules for your passport with the Icelandic Directorate of Immigration, and check the official EU ETIAS page for the start date of the new authorisation.
  • Take travel insurance that covers medical care and car-related risks, and check what your credit card does and does not cover.
  • Submit a travel plan on safetravel.is for any long walk, and tell your hotel your route.
  • In the car: water, snacks, a warm layer, a charger, a torch and a basic first-aid kit.

The Iceland Ring Road route, distances and driving times

The loop starts and ends at Keflavík Airport (KEF) and follows the full Route 1 Ring Road (about 1,320 km), roughly 1,620 km of driving over 7 days with detours. It runs clockwise, South Coast first: you reach the waterfalls on day 1, the long empty East Fjords and highlands fall mid-trip when you have slack for weather, and Reykjavík comes last, 47 km from the airport. It suits late May to mid-June or September, when cars and beds cost less than in July and August.

Is 7 days enough for the Iceland Ring Road?

Seven days works as a driving-focused loop with 3 to 4 hours behind the wheel on most days and a few short walks. It leaves little slack for bad weather or long hikes. If you can add days, spend them at Mývatn, in the East Fjords or on the Snæfellsnes peninsula rather than driving faster.

Do I need a 4x4, and can I do this in winter?

A small 2WD car is enough on paved Route 1 in summer, and F-roads are not part of this route. In winter, snow, wind and short daylight can close sections, and Route 862 to Dettifoss is not in winter service from January to early April. Check road.is and safetravel.is, and see the 5-day page for a winter-based option.

Does my credit card cover a hire car in Iceland?

Often not for Iceland-specific risks. Hire firms say card cover can exclude gravel damage, F-roads, 4x4s, tyres, windscreens and campervans. Confirm the cover in writing before you decline the hire firm's insurance, ask about sand and ash damage, and photograph the car at pick-up and drop-off.
