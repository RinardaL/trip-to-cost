💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Not obligatory: round up or leave 5–10% at sit-down restaurants.
  • No tipping expected at tapas bars or cafes.
  • Rounding up for taxis is common but not required.

👗 Dress Code

  • Casual everywhere, but cover shoulders and knees for the Cathedral and Sagrada Família.
  • Beachwear stays at the beach, so cover up when heading back into town.
  • Comfortable shoes for the Gothic Quarter's uneven, centuries-old paving.

🚇 Transit Nuances

  • A T-Casual card (10 rides) is far cheaper than single metro tickets.
  • Validate your ticket at the turnstile every time.
  • The Aerobús is the easiest airport connection: faster than city buses.

⚠️ Safety & Scam Warnings

  • Pickpocketing is a real risk on La Rambla and the metro, so keep bags zipped and in front.
  • Be wary of "spilled" ketchup or bird-poop scams used as a distraction to pick pockets.
  • Use only official taxis (black and yellow) or a licensed rideshare app.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases

  • Hola: hello
  • Gracias: thank you (Spanish) / Gràcies (Catalan)
  • La cuenta, por favor: the bill, please

💳 Money & Payments

  • Cards widely accepted, including most tapas bars.
  • Some small, traditional bars are cash-only, so carry some euros.
  • ATMs are common throughout the city center.

✈️ Flights

  • Barcelona-El Prat Airport (BCN) is the main gateway, about 15 km southwest of the city center.
  • The Aerobús and R2 Nord train both run directly into town, no need to book an airport transfer in advance.
  • Terminal 1 handles most international carriers; confirm your terminal before booking a pickup.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Free Now and Cabify both operate reliably in Barcelona; Uber is more limited than in other major cities.
  • Official black-and-yellow taxis are metered and easy to hail on the street, especially near Plaça Catalunya.
  • Agree on a fixed fare only for the flat-rate airport run; everything else should run on the meter.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Not needed for this itinerary: the metro, buses, and walking cover every stop comfortably.
  • Parking in the Gothic Quarter and Eixample is scarce and expensive, so a car would be a liability, not a convenience.
  • If extending the trip to Costa Brava or Montserrat afterward, rent on your way out of the city rather than for the Barcelona days.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Eixample: central, well-connected by metro, and close to Casa Batlló and Sagrada Família.
  • Gothic Quarter: walkable to almost everything on this itinerary, though rooms can be smaller and noisier at night.
  • Barceloneta: best if a beach morning matters most to you, a short walk or bus ride from the old town.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • Not required for this 3-day plan, but a short Golondrinas harbor cruise from Port Vell is an easy add-on if you have a spare hour.
  • Day trips to Costa Brava fishing towns typically go by rental car or organized tour rather than ferry.
  • No inter-island or coastal ferry legs are part of this itinerary.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • EU/Schengen entry rules apply; non-EU visitors should confirm visa requirements before booking flights.
  • Travel insurance is worth it mainly for the Gaudí site tickets and any flight delays, since most days involve minimal risk.
  • A T-Casual transit card (10 rides) covers metro and bus for the whole trip more cheaply than single tickets.
