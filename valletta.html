3 Days in Valletta, Malta: Fortifications, Harbours & Baroque Streets

Malta's fortress capital packs a gilded Caravaggio-holding cathedral, a harbour ringed by bastion walls, and a silent walled hilltop city into a grid barely a kilometer wide. Three days is enough time to walk it all, cross the Grand Harbour by traditional boat, and still make it out to Mdina before you fly home.

Ideal SeasonApr – Jun, Sep – Oct
Est. Budget$550-750
Duration3 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival & check-in in the capital

Malta International Airport (MLA) sits about 8 km south of Valletta in Luqa, and the X4 public bus reaches the capital's bus terminus just outside City Gate in around 30 to 40 minutes, or a taxi covers the same run in about 20.

🚌 ~35 min X4 airport bus, or ~20 min taxi 💵 ~€3 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time
Afternoon

Walking the Baroque grid & St. John's Co-Cathedral

Laid out by the Knights of St. John after they survived the Great Siege of 1565, Valletta's streets run in a tight, fortified grid you can cross end to end in twenty minutes. Behind the plain façade of St. John's Co-Cathedral sits a gilded Baroque interior, a floor inlaid with knights' marble tombstones, and Caravaggio's The Beheading of Saint John the Baptist, his largest work and the only one he signed.

🚶 Cathedral sits mid-grid, a 5 min walk from City Gate 💵 ~€15 cathedral entry 🕐 Early afternoon, before closing at 4:30 PM
Evening

Upper Barrakka Gardens & the Saluting Battery

The Upper Barrakka Gardens frame the best view in Valletta, a sweeping look across the Grand Harbour to the Three Cities' bastions, and the Saluting Battery just below fires a ceremonial cannon salute at noon and again at 4 PM. Stay on for a harbourside dinner as the fortifications light up.

🚶 A few minutes' walk from the cathedral 💵 Free gardens, ~€3 battery re-enactment 🕐 Late afternoon into sunset
Morning

A dgħajsa cruise around the Grand Harbour

A dgħajsa, Malta's brightly painted, gondola-like harbour boat, has ferried people and goods across the Grand Harbour for centuries. A short cruise from the Valletta Waterfront threads beneath the bastion walls for a view of the fortifications that's impossible to get from land.

⛴️ Boats board at the Valletta Waterfront, below the city walls 💵 ~€10 to €15 per person 🕐 Morning, before the cruise-ship crowds arrive
Midday

Fort St. Elmo & the National War Museum

The star-shaped fort at Valletta's tip held out for weeks against Ottoman forces during the Great Siege of 1565, and today houses the National War Museum, tracing Malta's military history from the Knights through the WWII Siege of Malta and the island's George Cross.

🚶 ~10 min walk from City Gate, at the peninsula's tip 💵 ~€10 entry 🕐 Midday, once the boat trip wraps up
Afternoon

Across the harbour to the Three Cities

Vittoriosa (Birgu) was the Knights' first fortified home in Malta, well before Valletta existed, and its quiet bastion walks, the Inquisitor's Palace, and a small marina lined with yachts feel far removed from the capital, a few minutes away by ferry.

⛴️ ~10 min passenger ferry from the Valletta Waterfront to Birgu 💵 ~€3.50 return 🕐 Late afternoon, before the last ferry back
Morning

Out to Mdina, the Silent City

The X3 bus runs directly from Valletta's bus terminus to Mdina's gate in about 40 minutes, or a taxi makes the same trip in roughly 25. Mdina was Malta's capital for over a thousand years before the Knights built Valletta, and cars are barred from its walled core today, hence the nickname.

🚌 ~40 min X3 bus, or ~25 min taxi 💵 ~€2.20 bus fare 🕐 Mid-morning, ahead of tour-group buses
Midday

Wandering Mdina's walls & cathedral

Honey-colored bastions, silent alleyways, and palazzo courtyards make Mdina feel frozen in time. St. Paul's Cathedral, its Baroque interior rebuilt after a 1693 earthquake, sits on the site traditionally linked to the Roman governor Publius, said to have met the shipwrecked St. Paul here.

🚶 The entire walled city on foot in under an hour 💵 ~€5 cathedral entry 🕐 Late morning, before the midday heat builds
Afternoon

Rabat's catacombs & the trip back to Valletta

Just outside Mdina's walls, Rabat holds St. Paul's Catacombs, a sprawling underground Roman-era burial complex, and St. Paul's Church on the traditional site of the saint's grotto. Ride the X3 bus back into Valletta for a final harbourside dinner before departure.

🚶 Rabat adjoins Mdina's gate; 🚌 ~40 min X3 bus back 💵 ~€6 catacombs entry 🕐 Early afternoon, heading back by early evening

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Tipping isn't obligatory in Malta, but 10% at a sit-down restaurant is appreciated when service isn't already included.
  • Rounding up taxi and dgħajsa boat fares to the nearest euro is common and welcomed.
  • Bar and café tabs rarely see more than loose change, if anything at all.

👗 Dress Code

  • St. John's Co-Cathedral and Mdina's St. Paul's Cathedral expect shoulders and knees covered, so pack a light scarf or wrap.
  • Comfortable, sturdy shoes matter more than style: Valletta and Mdina are built on steps, ramps, and uneven cobblestones.
  • Smart-casual works for harbourside dinners, though breathable fabrics matter most given the midday sun.

🚲 Getting Around Valletta

  • Valletta's entire old city is walkable end to end in about 20 minutes, though its grid hides a lot of stairs between the ridge streets and the harbour.
  • Malta Public Transport buses hub at the terminus just outside City Gate, with a single ticket costing about €2.20 and covering the whole island, including Mdina and the airport.
  • Passenger ferries connect the Valletta Waterfront to Sliema and to Birgu in the Three Cities, a scenic and cheap alternative to the bus.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Steep staircases and worn limestone cobbles are the main everyday hazard in Valletta and Mdina, so proper shoes matter more here than in most cities.
  • Summer heat regularly hits 32 to 35°C (90 to 95°F) in July and August, so front-load sightseeing into the morning and evening.
  • Malta drives on the left, which catches out visitors crossing streets as much as those renting a car.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Maltese

  • Bonġu: good morning/hello
  • Grazzi: thank you
  • Il-kont, jekk jogħġbok: the bill, please

💳 Money & Payments

  • Malta uses the euro, and English is a co-official language alongside Maltese, so there's rarely a communication barrier.
  • Contactless cards are accepted almost everywhere, from museum ticket counters to harbourside restaurants.
  • Carry some small cash for dgħajsa boatmen, bus fares, and smaller kiosks in Mdina and Rabat.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Look into a Heritage Malta multi-site pass

A combined pass covering Fort St. Elmo's War Museum, St. Paul's Catacombs, and other state-run sites costs less than paying for each one separately.

🚶

Walk Valletta instead of taking a taxi

The entire old city fits within a twenty-minute walk end to end, so save the ride-hailing budget for the airport transfer and the Mdina day trip.

⛴️

Take the public ferry instead of a paid harbour tour

The Valletta-to-Sliema and Valletta-to-Birgu passenger ferries cost a few euros and deliver much of the same Grand Harbour view as a paid cruise.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌳

Lower Barrakka Gardens over the Upper

Quieter than its postcard-famous neighbor, with the Siege Bell War Memorial and a different, less crowded angle on the harbour.

🏰

Mdina after the day-trip buses leave

Coach groups clear out by late afternoon, when the Silent City finally lives up to its name and the light on the honey-colored stone turns gold.

🎭

The Malta Experience for the midday heat

An air-conditioned audiovisual history show near Fort St. Elmo makes a good midday escape from the sun that most first-timers skip entirely.

✈️ Flights

  • Malta International Airport (MLA) in Luqa is the country's only airport, about 8 km south of Valletta.
  • The X4 public bus reaches Valletta's bus terminus in about 30 to 40 minutes for roughly €2.20.
  • A taxi or pre-booked transfer covers the same run in around 20 minutes, a good option late at night or with heavy luggage.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Bolt is the dominant ride-hailing app across Malta and tends to be cheaper and more transparently priced than a street taxi.
  • eCabs is a local metered taxi operator with its own booking app, a solid backup if Bolt is scarce.
  • Street taxis exist, but confirm the fare or meter before setting off.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • A car isn't useful inside Valletta itself, whose walled core is fully walkable and largely closed to through-traffic.
  • Malta drives on the left, and the roundabouts and narrow lanes around the capital can be confusing for first-time visitors.
  • A rental could help for reaching Mdina and Rabat independently, though the X3 bus or a taxi is simpler for a single half-day trip.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Valletta itself: boutique hotels in restored townhouses put Days 1 and 2 within walking distance, at a price premium.
  • Sliema or St. Julian's: across Marsamxett Harbour, cheaper and livelier, with a short ferry or bus ride into the capital.
  • The Three Cities (Vittoriosa): a quieter, more local base with harbour views and easy ferry access, a good fit if Day 2's side trip appeals.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • A short dgħajsa or motor-launch cruise around the Grand Harbour is Valletta's signature boat trip and the best way to see the bastions from the water.
  • Passenger ferries link Valletta to Sliema and to Birgu in the Three Cities year-round, cheap and scenic alongside being practical.
  • Full-day boat trips to Gozo or the Blue Lagoon on Comino run from Sliema or Bugibba, worth adding only with extra days beyond this itinerary.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Malta is a full Schengen member, so standard Schengen entry rules apply for non-EU visitors.
  • Travel insurance is worth adding given pre-booked cathedral and museum tickets, which are usually non-refundable.
  • Tap water is safe to drink island-wide, though many locals still prefer bottled for taste.
