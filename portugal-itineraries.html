A city week, an island or a south coast?

Portugal's three itineraries are really three different kinds of trip. Lisbon is the longest at seven days and the cheapest per day at $135, built around old neighbourhoods, trams and day trips to Sintra and Cascais. Madeira runs for five days at $190 a day and centres on Funchal's gardens, a levada walk and mountain paths. Algarve is four days at $205 a day and is about cliffs, sea caves and beaches.

The daily rate and the total tell different stories. The Algarve has the highest rate but the lowest total, $700-950 per person, because the trip is short. Lisbon spreads $750-1,150 over a full week, so it gives you the most days for the money.

First-timers who like walking and food will be happiest in Lisbon. Beach-minded couples and groups of friends should look at the Algarve, while hikers and garden lovers will find more to do on Madeira, where days three to five go on a levada walk, a summit trail and a north coast drive.

Which months suit each trip?

Lisbon lists March to May and September, the Algarve May to September, and Madeira April to October. May and September are the two months all three share, so they are the safest choice if you are undecided or want to do more than one trip. Lisbon is the only one that lists March.

Day-to-day timing matters as much as the month. On the Lisbon trip, the Feira da Ladra flea market runs on Tuesdays and Saturdays only and the National Pantheon is closed on Mondays, so line up your days to match. On Madeira, the Pico do Arieiro to Pico Ruivo day begins before dawn because clouds often gather later in the morning, and the page advises checking trail conditions before you set out.

None of the three lists the months from November to February, so a winter break in Portugal means checking the weather for your dates before you book anything.

How do you combine them and save money?

Lisbon and the Algarve combine most easily, because both are on the mainland. Fly into Faro, which is the Algarve's only international airport, or travel overland between the two. Madeira is an Atlantic island, so it needs its own flight and works best as a separate trip or a final leg. Lisbon plus the Algarve gives you eleven days.

Lisbon is the easiest place to travel cheaply. The Lisbon page puts the metro ride from Baixa-Chiado to the airport at about 25 minutes with one change, and a reusable Navegante card covers public transport. The Algarve and Madeira lean on cars, taxis and transfers, and on Madeira the levada and summit days use a taxi or tour transfer, so travelling with others spreads the cost.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a trip to Portugal cost?

Our 3 Portugal itineraries cost from $700 to $1,150 per person for the whole trip, which is about $135 to $205 a day. Prices cover lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and exclude international flights.

Which Portugal itinerary is the cheapest?

Algarve ($700-950), Lisbon ($750-1,150), Madeira ($800-1,100) are the three lowest, per person and excluding flights.

How many days do I need in Portugal?

Our Portugal itineraries run from 4 to 7 days, and the most common length is 4 days. Each page has a day picker with a price for every length from one day up to the full plan.

When is the best time to visit Portugal?

It depends on the trip, and each itinerary lists its ideal season. For example: Lisbon, Mar – May, Sep; Madeira, Apr – Oct; Algarve, May – Sep. The table above shows the ideal season for every Portugal trip.

Do I need a car in Portugal?

Not in Lisbon, where the trip relies on the metro, trams and day trips by train to Sintra and Cascais. On the Algarve and Madeira a rental car or transfer helps with trailheads, beaches and early mountain starts, and the pages quote taxi or tour transfers for several days. Decide trip by trip, and compare rental and transfer prices for your dates.

Is Madeira a beach trip?

Not mainly. The Madeira itinerary is labelled Beach & Coastal, but its five days centre on Funchal, a levada walk, the climb from Pico do Arieiro to Pico Ruivo and a north coast drive that ends at the volcanic pools of Porto Moniz. For classic beach time, the Algarve trip is the closer fit.
