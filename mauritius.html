5 Days in Mauritius: Le Morne, Chamarel & the Seven Colored Earths

A basaltic mountain with a hard history, sand dunes in seven natural colors, and a lagoon so calm it barely needs a shoreline. Five days across Mauritius's southwest and east coasts, closing with a full day in Port Louis and the multicultural food that ties the whole island together.

Ideal SeasonMay – Dec
Est. Budget$1,200-1,600
Duration5 Days
PaceRelaxed
How many days do you have?

Morning

Land at SSR International & transfer to Le Morne

The airport sits on the southeast coast, so the drive to Le Morne's resort strip cuts across the island.

🚗 ~1h drive 💵 ~$35 private transfer 🕐 Confirm pickup time
Afternoon

Le Morne Brabant, up close

This basaltic monolith isn't just a dramatic backdrop: 19th-century maroons hid in its caves and forests to escape recapture, and the UNESCO listing honors that history, not just the scenery.

🚶 base trail 💵 Free (guide required past the first checkpoint) 🕐 Late afternoon, cooler
Evening

Sunset at the foot of the mountain

Settle in with a Creole dinner of fresh catch and rougaille, the tomato-based sauce that shows up at almost every Mauritian table.

🚶 beachfront 💵 ~$20 🕐 Sunset
Morning

The Seven Colored Earths

Cooled volcanic ash weathered into ridges of red, brown, violet, and gold, a dune landscape that looks digitally altered until you're standing on the boardwalk above it.

🚗 ~45 min from Le Morne 💵 ~$7 🕐 Morning, best light
Afternoon

Chamarel Waterfall

Mauritius's tallest single-drop waterfall plunges roughly 100 meters into a forested gorge, viewable from a signposted lookout a short walk from the parking area.

🚶 5 min from the viewpoint 💵 Included with the Seven Colored Earths ticket 🕐 Early afternoon
Evening

Rum tasting at Rhumerie de Chamarel

A working sugarcane distillery with a tasting room and restaurant, a good excuse to slow down before the drive back down to the coast.

🚗 on site 💵 ~$15 tasting flight 🕐 Late afternoon
Morning

Into Black River Gorges

Mauritius's largest national park protects the island's last significant stretch of native rainforest; the observatory viewpoint and the trails leading off it reward the walk with gorge views down to the coast.

🥾 ~2-3h round trip 💵 Free entry 🕐 Start early, cooler
Afternoon

Pink pigeons & a conservation story

The park's breeding aviaries are part of a program that pulled the pink pigeon back from fewer than 20 wild birds in the 1990s to a stable wild population today, one of conservation's genuine comeback stories.

🚶 short walk from the visitor center 💵 ~$5 🕐 Afternoon
Evening

Trou aux Cerfs at sunset

A dormant volcanic crater on the edge of Curepipe, with a walkable rim path and views back down toward the coast as the light turns gold.

🚗 ~30 min from the park 💵 Free 🕐 Sunset
Morning

Board the catamaran

Most day trips leave from the east coast and sail past smaller islets before anchoring for a swim stop in open lagoon water.

⛴️ full-day charter 💵 ~$65 pp 🕐 Morning departure
Afternoon

Île aux Cerfs

Powder-white sand and shallow turquoise water on an islet with no permanent residents; most catamaran tours build in two or three hours here plus a barbecue lunch on board.

🏖️ free time on the island 💵 Lunch usually included 🕐 Midday
Evening

Sail back at golden hour

The return leg is the slow part of the day on purpose, rum punch included on most tours, landing back on the east coast as the light turns amber.

⛴️ ~1h return sail 💵 Included 🕐 Late afternoon
Morning

Port Louis Central Market

Spice stalls, textiles, and produce spread over two crowded floors; arrive early for the freshest produce and the quietest aisles.

🚗 ~1h from the east coast 💵 Free to browse 🕐 Morning, before the heat
Afternoon

Dholl puri & a street food lunch

Thin split-pea flatbreads folded around bean curry and chutney, sold from carts across the city and considered by many locals the island's true national dish, a legacy of Mauritius's Indian heritage.

🚶 street stalls citywide 💵 ~$1-2 per wrap 🕐 Lunchtime
Evening

Caudan Waterfront & the flight home

A last stroll along the harbor before the transfer back to the airport: Mauritius's mix of Creole, Indian, Chinese, and French influence is as visible in the shopfronts here as it was on the plate all week.

🚗 ~45 min to the airport 💵 ~$25 transfer 🕐 Allow extra time

💵 Tipping Etiquette

  • Not obligatory: 10% at resorts and nicer restaurants is appreciated, not expected.
  • Many all-inclusive resorts already add a service charge, check the bill before tipping twice.
  • Small tips for catamaran crews and tour guides are customary, not required.

🕌 A Multicultural, Multi-Faith Island

  • Hindu, Muslim, Christian, and Chinese traditions coexist closely; temples, mosques, and churches often sit within blocks of each other.
  • Public holidays draw from several faiths at once, including Diwali, Eid, Christmas, and Chinese New Year.
  • Dress modestly and remove shoes where asked when visiting any place of worship, regardless of faith.

🚗 Getting Around

  • Most visitors stay resort-based and book excursions through their hotel; taxis and private drivers are easy to arrange but not cheap.
  • Renting a car is affordable and worth it for exploring beyond the resort at your own pace; driving is on the left.
  • Local buses connect the main towns but run on a limited, hard-to-plan schedule.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Le Morne Brabant is a place of real historical weight: approach the mountain and its story of escaped slaves with the same care you'd bring to any site of historical trauma, not just as a scenic backdrop.
  • The Indian Ocean sun is strong even on breezy days; reef-safe sunscreen protects both you and the lagoons.
  • Currents can be strong outside the reef-protected lagoons, and coral should never be touched or stood on.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases

  • Bonzour: good morning (Mauritian Creole)
  • Mersi boukou: thank you very much (Mauritian Creole)
  • Combien ça coûte? how much is this? (French, widely understood)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Mauritian Rupee (MUR) is the local currency; cash is useful for markets and small vendors.
  • Cards are widely accepted at resorts, restaurants, and larger shops.
  • ATMs are common around Port Louis and resort towns, less so inland.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🐦

Île aux Aigrettes

A coral islet nature reserve with more pink pigeons and giant tortoises, and none of Île aux Cerfs's crowds.

🍵

Bois Chéri Tea Plantation

One of Africa's oldest tea estates, with tastings and views over the southern hills.

🌅

Early-morning Chamarel

Arrive at opening to see the Seven Colored Earths without the midday tour-bus rush.

✈️ Flights

  • Sir Seewoosagur Ramgoolam International Airport (MRU), near Plaisance on the southeast coast, is the only gateway.
  • It's a long-haul flight from most of Europe, North America, and Asia; a stopover is worth considering from farther afield.
  • Cyclone season runs roughly January-March, the highest-risk window for flight and excursion disruption, one reason this itinerary favors May-December.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Metered taxis exist, but agreeing a fare upfront is standard practice, especially for airport transfers.
  • Ride-hailing apps have limited coverage outside Port Louis; most visitors rely on hotel-arranged transfers or private drivers.
  • A private driver for the day is a common, affordable way to cover Chamarel, Black River Gorges, and Le Morne without renting a car.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Resort-based transit covers beach days fine, but a rental car is worth it for exploring the highlands and west coast at your own pace.
  • Driving is on the left; a valid driving license from your home country is generally accepted for short stays.
  • Roads inland through Black River Gorges are narrow and winding, budget more time than the map suggests.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Le Morne: dramatic scenery, plus some of the best kitesurfing and lagoon access on the island.
  • Belle Mare / east coast: calmer water and closer to the Île aux Cerfs catamaran departures.
  • Grand Baie: the liveliest base in the north, with more restaurants and nightlife outside the resort gates.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • The Île aux Cerfs catamaran is the signature boat trip, usually a full day with a barbecue lunch included.
  • Book directly with a local operator for a better price than the same tour sold through a resort concierge.
  • Smaller sunset cruises run from most west-coast resort towns, a lower-key alternative if a full-day sail isn't your pace.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Most nationalities get visa-free entry to Mauritius for tourism; confirm the current allowance for your passport before flying.
  • Cyclone season (roughly January-March) brings the highest risk of disrupted flights and excursions, this itinerary's May-December window avoids it.
  • Travel insurance is worth it for the catamaran crossing and any inland hiking in Black River Gorges.
