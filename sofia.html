4 Days in Sofia, Bulgaria

Gold-domed cathedrals, a mountain you can hike straight from the city center, and a UNESCO monastery two hours away, all on one of Europe's smallest budgets.

Ideal SeasonMay – Jun, Sep
Est. Budget$450-600
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Sofia Airport & transfer to the center

Metro Line 1 runs directly from the airport terminal into the city center, one of the cheapest and most reliable airport connections in Europe.

🚇 ~20 min metro from the airport 💵 ~€1 🕐 Trains run every 10 min, plan around your flight
Afternoon

Alexander Nevsky Cathedral

Sofia's landmark, gold-domed cathedral is one of the largest Orthodox churches in the world and the city's most recognizable sight, right in the historic core.

🚶 Central, walkable from most hotels 💵 Free entry, crypt ~€6 🕐 Midday for the best light on the gold domes
Evening

First walk through the historic center

The Largo, the ancient Serdica ruins beneath the underpass, and the tiny St. George Rotunda sit within a few minutes of each other, a compact intro to layers of Sofia's history.

🚶 ~5 min from the cathedral 💵 Free 🕐 Early evening before dinner
Morning

Vitosha Boulevard & the National Palace of Culture

Sofia's main pedestrian shopping street runs straight toward the NDK, the vast Communist-era cultural complex that anchors the south end of downtown.

🚶 Pedestrian boulevard, easy walking 💵 Free to wander 🕐 Late morning, before the lunch crowds
Afternoon

Sofia's religious tolerance square

The Sofia Synagogue, Banya Bashi Mosque, and St. Nedelya Church sit within a few blocks of each other, a genuinely rare cluster of active Jewish, Muslim, and Orthodox sites side by side.

🚶 ~10 min from Serdica metro station 💵 ~€3 synagogue entry 🕐 Early afternoon, before mosque prayer times
Evening

Dinner at a traditional mehana

A tavern-style dinner of shopska salad and banitsa, with live folk music at some of the older spots near the center, costs a fraction of a Western European meal.

🚶 Central Sofia, walkable 💵 ~€12 with drinks 🕐 Dinner around 7 to 8 PM
Morning

Cable car up from Simeonovo

Few European capitals let you ride a cable car onto a mountain within 30 minutes of downtown. The Simeonovo line climbs straight into the Vitosha Nature Park.

🚌 ~30 min bus + cable car from the center 💵 ~€10 round trip 🕐 Early morning, cable car opens around 9:30 AM
Afternoon

Hike toward Cherni Vrah or the Golden Bridges

From the Aleko hut area, a network of marked trails leads to the Golden Bridges rock river or, for a longer day, up to Cherni Vrah, Vitosha's 2,290-meter summit.

🚶 ~2 to 3 hr moderate trail from Aleko 💵 Free, no trail fees 🕐 Midday, allow a full stretch of daylight
Evening

Descend for dinner at a mountain hut

Several hizhas near the cable car base serve simple, hearty Bulgarian food at mountain-hut prices, a relaxed way to end a hiking day before heading back down.

🚌 Confirm the last cable car down 💵 ~€10 hut meal 🕐 Before dusk, the last car fills up
Morning

Depart Sofia for Rila Monastery

Bulgaria's most famous monastery and a UNESCO World Heritage Site sits about two hours south of Sofia in the Rila mountains, easiest reached by an organized day tour.

🚗 ~2 hr drive from Sofia 💵 ~€25 organized tour seat 🕐 Depart by 8 AM to beat the tour buses
Afternoon

Explore the monastery complex

The striped arches, vivid frescoes, and small museum inside make Rila's courtyard one of the most photographed spots in the country.

🚶 Self-guided within the complex 💵 Free entry, museum ~€3 🕐 Late morning, before the midday crowds peak
Evening

Return to Sofia & departure

Back in the city by early evening, with the same direct metro line covering the last leg out to the airport for a departing flight.

🚇 ~20 min metro to the airport 💵 ~€1 🕐 Leave 2 hrs before your flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • A tip of around 10% is common at sit-down restaurants, though it isn't strictly obligatory.
  • Rounding up the fare for taxis and casual cafes is normal and appreciated.
  • Tour guides and free walking tours run on tips, so bring small lev notes for them.

👗 Dress Code

  • Cover shoulders and knees to enter Alexander Nevsky Cathedral and Rila Monastery, both active places of worship.
  • Some churches offer wraps at the entrance for visitors who arrive uncovered, but it's easier to plan ahead.
  • Pack real layers for Day 3, temperatures on Vitosha's summit run noticeably colder than downtown Sofia.

🚇 Getting Around

  • Sofia's metro is modern, clean, and cheap, with two lines that cover the airport, the center, and Vitosha's cable car access points.
  • Trams and buses fill in the gaps the metro doesn't reach, all on the same ticket system.
  • A single ticket costs around €1, and multi-ride cards bring the price down further for a multi-day stay.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Sofia is generally very safe, though pickpocketing does occur around crowded tourist sites and on public transport.
  • Airport taxi overcharging is a known issue, covered in the Travel Services section below.
  • Sidewalks and cobblestones in the historic center can be uneven, worth watching for at night.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Bulgarian

  • Zdravey: hello
  • Blagodarya: thank you
  • Famously, Bulgarians reverse the usual gesture: a nod of the head can mean "no," and a side-to-side shake can mean "yes," so it's safer to listen for a verbal да (yes) or не (no) than to trust the gesture alone.

💳 Money & Payments

  • Bulgaria uses the Bulgarian lev (BGN), not the euro, despite being an EU member, a point worth flagging clearly before you land.
  • Cards are widely accepted in Sofia's center, but small vendors, mountain huts, and the Women's Market run on cash.
  • ATMs are common downtown; withdraw lev rather than paying in euros at inflated tourist exchange rates.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🍽️

Eat a block off the main boulevard

Mehanas just off Vitosha Boulevard serve the same dishes as the boulevard-front cafes for noticeably less.

🚇

Buy a multi-ride metro card

A short-term transit card beats individual tickets and cuts out any need for taxis on the city days.

🚶

Join a free walking tour

Sofia's tip-based walking tours cover the historic center in under two hours and cost only what you choose to tip.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

⛪

Boyana Church's medieval frescoes

A tiny UNESCO-listed church on the way to Vitosha, with frescoes older and quieter than anything at Alexander Nevsky.

🥾

Start the hike from Dragalevtsi instead

The Dragalevtsi monastery trailhead sees far fewer hikers than the Simeonovo cable car route, for the same mountain.

🥕

Browse the Women's Market

Zhenski Pazar is Sofia's real produce market, cheap, unpolished, and a genuine slice of neighborhood life away from the tourist center.

✈️ Flights

  • Sofia Airport (SOF) is small and easy to navigate, with Metro Line 1 running directly into the city center.
  • The metro ride into downtown takes about 20 minutes and costs roughly €1, no transfer needed.
  • Given the trip's low overall budget, the metro is the obvious choice over a taxi or private transfer for arrival and departure.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Bolt operates well throughout Sofia and is the easiest way to book a fair-priced ride from a phone.
  • Airport taxi overcharging is a well-known issue, stick to official taxi stands or an app-booked ride rather than drivers who approach you in the arrivals hall.
  • Always confirm the fare or use the app's estimate before getting in, since unmetered "special" rates target visitors.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • A car isn't necessary for any of the three city-based days, the metro, trams, and Vitosha cable car cover everything.
  • It becomes relevant only for Day 4, if you'd rather self-drive to Rila Monastery than join an organized tour.
  • Mountain roads into Rila are well-paved but winding, so a tour bus or driver is the lower-stress option for most visitors.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • City Center, near Alexander Nevsky Cathedral: puts Day 1 and Day 2's sights within easy walking distance.
  • Vitosha Boulevard: central, walkable, and lined with affordable places to eat for the Day 2 dinner.
  • Lozenets: a quieter residential district south of the center, with easier access to the Day 3 bus and cable car up Vitosha.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • Sofia is landlocked, sitting inland at the base of Vitosha Mountain, so no boat or ferry trips apply to this itinerary.
  • The closest thing on this trip to a scenic ride over water is the view of the reservoirs from the Vitosha trails on Day 3.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Bulgaria is an EU member but has historically sat outside the Schengen area, so confirm current border and passport-control rules before you fly, since this has been changing in recent years.
  • The currency is the Bulgarian lev (BGN), not the euro, worth double-checking again here since it trips up first-time visitors.
  • Travel insurance is cheap relative to the trip's overall cost and worth it for the Rila Monastery day trip and the Vitosha hike alike.
