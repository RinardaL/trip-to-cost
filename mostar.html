4 Days in Mostar: The Old Bridge, Kravica & Blagaj

The rebuilt Old Bridge and Ottoman bazaar, a day at Kravica Waterfalls and the cliff-side monastery at Blagaj, reached by the scenic Sarajevo train: four budget days in Herzegovina.

Ideal SeasonApr – Jun, Sep – Oct
Est. Budget$220-420
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Sarajevo Airport & the transfer to town

Mostar's own airport has very few scheduled flights, so most visitors land at Sarajevo (SJJ), about 12 km from the centre. The airport bus costs 5 KM (about €2.50) one way, and a taxi is roughly 20 KM, usually cash only. Withdraw Bosnian marks from an ATM, since the ticket office and small shops prefer cash.

🚌 ~20-30 min by airport bus or taxi to the centre 💵 Airport bus 5 KM, taxi ~20 KM (check current) 🕐 Plan around your landing time, airport open 05:00 to 23:00
Afternoon

The Neretva train or bus to Mostar

Two daily trains run from Sarajevo to Mostar along the Neretva canyon in about two hours. The afternoon one leaves around 16:46 and arrives about 18:50. A second-class seat costs about 14 KM (€7), with no seat reservations. Buses take about 2.5 hours and cost roughly 20 to 28 KM. Confirm times at zfbh.ba.

🚆 ~2 hr by train, ~2.5 hr by bus 💵 Train ~14 KM, bus ~20-28 KM (check current) 🕐 Train at ~16:46 (check), buses run through the day
Evening

Check-in & the Old Bridge at dusk

The Old Bridge (Stari Most) dates from 1566, was destroyed in 1993 during the war, and was rebuilt to the original design, reopening in 2004. It is part of a UNESCO World Heritage area. Cross the polished, slippery stones slowly, then watch the lit bridge from the river terraces. The station is 15 to 20 minutes' walk away.

🚶 ~15-20 min on foot from the train station to the Old Town 💵 Free, ~15-25 KM for a simple dinner 🕐 Dusk or after dark, when the day-trippers have left
Morning

The Old Bridge & the bridge divers

Members of the Mostar Divers Club jump from around 20 m, mostly between about 11:00 and 15:00 from May to October, once enough donations are collected. Stand on the riverbank steps for the best view and add a few marks to the diver's bucket first. Tourists should not attempt the jump: the river is cold and fast.

🚶 Everything in the Old Town is within a 10-minute walk 💵 Free to watch, tip ~5-10 KM per person (check current) 🕐 Jumps are usually late morning to mid-afternoon, May to October
Afternoon

Kujundžiluk, Koski Mehmed Pasha Mosque & an Ottoman house

Kujundžiluk is the cobbled bazaar street of copper, ceramics and souvenirs. Koski Mehmed Pasha Mosque, dating from 1618, costs about 10 KM (check current) for the prayer hall and minaret climb, and you cover up and remove shoes. Bišćević House shows Ottoman home life for about 4 KM. Try a Bosnian coffee on a terrace.

🚶 ~5-10 min on foot between the stops 💵 Mosque ~10 KM, Bišćević House ~4 KM, coffee ~2-3 KM 🕐 After the first tour buses arrive, with a coffee break at 15:00
Evening

The Crooked Bridge & ćevapi by the river

The Crooked Bridge (Kriva Ćuprija) is a small arched bridge over the Radobolja stream, rebuilt after a flood in 2000. It is free to cross and photograph. For dinner, order ćevapi, grilled minced-meat sausages served with somun flatbread, onion and kajmak, usually 10 to 14 KM for a portion. Cheaper grills sit a few streets back from the bridge.

🚶 ~5 min on foot from the Old Bridge 💵 Free, ćevapi ~10-14 KM, ~20-30 KM with a drink 🕐 Dinner from about 19:00, when the Old Town is calm
Morning

Kravica Waterfalls

Kravica is about 40 km from Mostar with no regular public bus, so choose a shared tour (roughly €25 to 45, check current) or a taxi at about 50 to 70 KM each way. Entry is 20 KM, and swimming suits about June to September. Open 07:00 to 22:00 in summer, earlier closing in winter.

🚐 ~45 min by tour van or taxi, no regular bus 💵 Entry 20 KM, tour ~€25-45 (check current) 🕐 Arrive by 09:00 before the crowds, open 07:00 daily
Afternoon

Počitelj, the Ottoman fortress village

Počitelj is a stepped Ottoman village with a hilltop fort, a clock tower and the Hadži-Alija mosque, about 25 to 30 minutes south of Mostar. Walking the village is free, though the mosque may charge a small fee. The cobbled climb is steep and exposed, so bring water and a hat. Most tours combine it with Kravica.

🚐 ~20-30 min by tour van, taxi or local bus 💵 Village free (mosque may charge), bus ~6 KM (check current) 🕐 Early afternoon, avoid the midday heat in summer
Evening

Blagaj Tekke at the source of the Buna

Blagaj Tekke is a Sufi monastery under a cliff, where the Buna river rises from a cave. Local bus 11 runs from Španski Trg in Mostar in about 25 to 30 minutes for roughly 3 KM. Interior entry is about 5 to 10 KM (check current), with covered shoulders and knees. It closes around 17:00 in winter.

🚌 ~25-30 min by bus 11 from Španski Trg 💵 Bus ~3 KM, entry ~5-10 KM (check current) 🕐 Check closing time, around 17:00 in winter, later in summer
Morning

Karađoz-bey Mosque

Karađoz-bey Mosque dates from 1557 and is quieter than the bridge area. Entry to the courtyard is usually free, and a small fee or donation (check current) may apply inside. Visit outside prayer times, especially around midday on Fridays, and cover your shoulders and knees. Remove shoes before the carpet.

🚶 ~10 min on foot from the Old Bridge 💵 Free or small donation (check current) 🕐 Mid-morning, avoid prayer times and Friday midday
Afternoon

The Partisan Memorial Cemetery & a last ćevapi lunch

The Partisan Memorial Cemetery, designed by Bogdan Bogdanović and opened in 1965, is a terraced stone memorial on the hillside above the west bank. It is a 20 to 25 minute walk from the Old Town. The site is weathered and partly overgrown, and the view over Mostar is the reward. Afterwards, eat a last portion of ćevapi.

🚶 ~20-25 min on foot from the Old Town, uphill 💵 Free, lunch ~10-15 KM 🕐 Late morning to afternoon, in daylight
Evening

Departing by train or bus

The Mostar train to Sarajevo leaves at about 17:09 and arrives around 19:07, a fare of about 14 KM (check zfbh.ba for current times). The bus station is a 25-minute walk north of the Old Town, or a short taxi ride. Buses run to Dubrovnik and Split, with possible long border checks. Allow extra time for passport control.

🚆 ~2 hr to Sarajevo, ~3-4 hr by bus to Dubrovnik or Split 💵 Train ~14 KM, Dubrovnik/Split bus ~€15-25 (check current) 🕐 Train ~17:09 (check), leave town 30 min earlier

💶 Tipping & Everyday Payments

  • Tipping is appreciated, not required. In restaurants, leave about 10 per cent or round up. For taxis, round up to the nearest mark or two.
  • Tip bridge divers before the jump, not after. Spectators usually put a few marks in the bucket, and a few more if you film or photograph the dive.
  • Many small cafés, grills and bus drivers prefer cash, so keep small notes and coins. Ask for the bill with "Račun, molim".

🍖 Eating & Drinking in Herzegovina

  • Try ćevapi (grilled minced-meat sausages in somun flatbread with onion and kajmak), burek (filled pastry, usually meat; sirnica has cheese) and baklava or tufahija (a baked apple stuffed with walnuts) for dessert.
  • Bosnian coffee comes in a copper džezva with a cup and a sugar cube. Let the grounds settle, sip slowly and do not stir the bottom of the cup.
  • Local wines include the white Žilavka and the red Blatina from Herzegovina. Rakija (fruit brandy, often plum) is offered as a welcome, and declining politely is fine.

🕌 Mosque & Church Etiquette

  • Mosques are working places of worship. Cover your shoulders and knees, remove shoes before the prayer hall and keep your voice low. Women may be asked to cover their hair, and scarves are often lent.
  • Avoid visiting during prayer times, especially around midday on Fridays. Ask before photographing worshippers.
  • Mostar has Muslim, Catholic and Orthodox communities, and the city was divided during the 1990s war. Stay respectful when discussing it, and let locals raise the subject themselves.

⚠️ Safety, Heat & Cautions

  • Landmines from the 1990s war remain in parts of the country. Stay on paved or marked paths, especially on hillsides outside town, and never touch suspicious objects. Check the official travel advice for your country before you go.
  • Do not jump from the Old Bridge or dive at the river. The drop is about 20 m, the water is cold and fast, and only trained club members jump. Polished bridge stones are slippery when wet.
  • July and August can be very hot (35°C plus). Drink water, wear a hat, and plan outdoor sights for early or late in the day.
  • Agree a taxi price first or use a taxi app. If you rent a car, confirm Green Card insurance for the border, expect checks from Croatia and keep dipped headlights on.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Bosnian/Croatian

  • "Dobar dan" (good day), "Dobro jutro" (good morning), "Zdravo" (hi), "Doviđenja" (goodbye)
  • "Molim" (please, you're welcome), "Hvala" (thank you), "Izvinite" (excuse me), "Oprostite" (sorry)
  • "Jedna kafa, molim" (one coffee, please), "Račun, molim" (the bill, please), "Pet ćevapa, molim" (five ćevapi, please)
  • "Govorite li engleski?" (do you speak English?), "Koliko košta?" (how much does it cost?), "Gdje je...?" (where is...?)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Bosnia and Herzegovina uses the convertible mark (KM or BAM), fixed at about 1.96 KM per euro. Roughly, 2 KM is €1. Some Mostar shops accept euros, but the exchange rate is often poor, so pay in KM.
  • Cards are accepted at most hotels, larger restaurants and some tour desks, but small cafés, grills, buses and bazaar stalls often want cash. ATMs are easy to find in Mostar, so withdraw KM as you need it.
  • Choose to be charged in KM, not your home currency, if a terminal offers the choice. Avoid exchange kiosks in tourist areas and compare rates at a bank.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🚆

Take the train, not the taxi

The Sarajevo to Mostar train costs about 14 KM (€7) one way, with a discount for round trips, against 120 to 160 KM for a private taxi. Buy at zfbh.ba or at the station counter, and confirm the timetable first.

🚐

Share the Kravica trip

A taxi to Kravica is about 50 to 70 KM each way, but a shared tour or a group of four splitting the fare is cheaper per person. Entry to Kravica is a flat 20 KM, so check whether a tour price includes it.

🥙

Eat at the grills, not on the bridge

A portion of ćevapi, a burek slice and a Bosnian coffee cost a fraction of the menu prices right by the Old Bridge. Walk three or four streets back, and look for places busy with locals at lunch.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

Visit early or late

Tour groups from the coast tend to arrive from about 10:00 and leave by late afternoon. Before 09:00 and after dusk the Old Town is quiet, and the lit bridge looks better after dark.

📷

Photograph the bridge from the river

The eastern riverbank steps and the area near the Crooked Bridge give the best views of the bridge and the cliff. The top of the bridge shows you only the back of a diver.

🏘️

Walk up to the Partisan Cemetery

Few day-trippers climb the hillside to Bogdan Bogdanović's memorial, so you will often have the terraces and the view over Mostar to yourself. Go in daylight and keep to the paved paths.

✈️ Flights

  • Sarajevo (SJJ) is the main airport for Mostar, about 2 hours away by train or 2.5 by bus. Mostar airport (OMO) has few scheduled flights, so check current routes.
  • Dubrovnik (DBV) and Split (SPU) in Croatia are alternatives by bus, about 3 to 4 hours, with border checks. Compare flight prices to all three.
  • Sarajevo airport is about 12 km from the centre. The airport bus is 5 KM and a taxi is around 20 KM (check current).

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Taxis from Mostar station to the Old Town cost about €5 (10 KM). Ask for the meter or agree a price first, and keep small notes.
  • Hotel-arranged taxis and local taxi apps such as TaxiHaron are the easiest ways to avoid haggling (check current coverage). Do not count on ride-hailing apps.
  • A private taxi Sarajevo to Mostar costs about 120 to 160 KM, usually only worth it when sharing between four people.

🚌 Public Transport & Tickets

  • The Sarajevo to Mostar train (ŽFBH) takes under 2 hours, costs about 14 KM and runs twice a day. Buy at zfbh.ba (booking opens about 2 weeks ahead) or at the station, and confirm times.
  • Buses connect Mostar with Sarajevo, Dubrovnik, Split and Čapljina. The Mostar bus station is about a 25-minute walk north of the Old Town.
  • City bus 11 to Blagaj leaves from Španski Trg. The fare is about 3 KM, paid in cash to the driver.
  • The Old Town is walkable. Use a taxi for the cemetery hill in hot weather.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Old Town (Stari Grad): the most atmospheric base, with guesthouses a few minutes from the bridge, though lanes get noisy in summer.
  • Near the Bulevar or Španski Trg: calmer and cheaper, about 10 to 15 minutes on foot from the bridge.
  • Near the railway station: practical for early trains, but plan a short walk or taxi to the Old Town.
  • Book summer weekends early, and ask the guesthouse about breakfast and a station pickup.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Bosnia is outside the EU, so EU roaming rules do not apply. Check your plan, or buy a local prepaid SIM or an eSIM before you arrive.
  • Wi-Fi is common in hotels, cafés and restaurants.
  • Withdraw KM from bank ATMs in the Old Town, and keep small notes for buses, coffee and tips.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Visa rules depend on your passport: many nationalities, including EU, UK and US citizens, can visit for up to 90 days without a visa. Check the official rules for yours before booking.
  • Carry your passport at all times. Hotels normally register foreign guests with the police for you.
  • From Croatia you pass border control on the bus, which can add an hour or more in summer. Rental cars need Green Card insurance and dipped headlights on.
  • You do not need a car in Mostar. Parking in the Old Town is limited, and tours, buses and trains cover every stop here.
