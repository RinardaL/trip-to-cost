4 Days in St. Moritz: Ski Slopes, the Cresta Run & Frozen-Lake Glamour

Four days in Switzerland's original winter resort are enough to ski two legendary mountains, take a supervised run down the world's oldest natural ice track, walk out onto a frozen lake built for horse racing, and still catch the Bernina Express through the snow before you go.

Ideal SeasonDec – Mar
Est. Budget$1,900-2,600
Duration4 Days
PaceActive & Luxury
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival by the Rhaetian Railway

St. Moritz has no airport of its own, so the last leg in is a scenic narrow-gauge ride through a snowbound alpine pass, one of the most dramatic rail approaches to any resort in the Alps.

🚆 ~3.5 hr scenic train from Chur, or ~4 hr from Milan via Tirano 💵 ~CHF 60-90 second class from Chur 🕐 Book window seats ahead, they sell out in ski season
Afternoon

A first stroll down Via Serlas

The world's highest-altitude luxury shopping street runs just a few hundred meters past Badrutt's Palace, its window displays lit early against the winter dusk that settles in well before 5pm.

🚶 Flat walk through St. Moritz Dorf 💵 Free to browse, window-shopping only for most 🕐 Late afternoon, as the shop lights come on
Evening

A first fondue in the Dorf

A pot of melted cheese, a basket of bread, and a glass of local Veltliner wine is the standard opening night in the Engadin, and the easiest way to warm back up after a day of travel through the snow.

🧀 Reserve ahead in peak weeks 💵 ~CHF 35-45 per person 🕐 Dinner from 7pm
Morning

Funicular up to Corviglia

The St. Moritz–Corviglia funicular climbs out of the village in minutes, opening onto 155 kilometers of groomed pistes across sun-drenched, mostly treeless terrain that made this the birthplace of Alpine winter tourism.

🚡 ~8 min funicular from St. Moritz Dorf 💵 ~CHF 89 day pass, Corviglia-Corvatsch combined 🕐 First cabin up, before the pistes fill in
Midday

Lunch on a sun terrace above the Engadin

Corviglia's slopeside restaurants are as much a St. Moritz tradition as the skiing itself, sun loungers and a glass of something cold on the snow with the Engadin's chain of lakes laid out far below.

🍽️ On-mountain, ski-in ski-out 💵 ~CHF 45-70 for lunch 🕐 Midday, when the terraces are at their sunniest
Afternoon

Cable car to the Corvatsch glacier station

A second cable car system climbs to 3,303 meters on Piz Corvatsch, where the Bernina Range unfolds in full, Piz Bernina and Piz Roseg rising over the Tschierva glacier in one of the widest panoramas in the Engadin.

🚡 ~20 min in two stages from Surlej 💵 Included on the combined lift pass 🕐 Early afternoon, before the light flattens
Morning

Watch or ride the Cresta Run

Rebuilt from scratch in ice every winter since 1884, the Cresta Run between St. Moritz and Celerina is the birthplace of skeleton sledding, and its clubhouse welcomes first-timers for a supervised taster run most winter mornings.

🛷 Taster runs by reservation, members first on race days 💵 ~CHF 675 for a first-timer's package of runs 🕐 Morning sessions, before the ice softens
Midday

Walk out onto the frozen lake

From January into March, Lake St. Moritz freezes thick enough to host the White Turf horse races and the Snow Polo World Cup, and on non-event days it's simply the widest, flattest place in town for a walk with the whole ring of peaks around you.

🥾 Flat lakeside walk from the Dorf 💵 Free to walk; event tickets extra if White Turf or polo is on 🕐 Midday, when the ice is busiest with walkers
Afternoon

Cross-country skiing on the Engadin's valley trails

More than 240 kilometers of groomed loipe link St. Moritz to Pontresina and Silvaplana across open valley floor and frozen lake edges, the largest cross-country network in Switzerland and a completely different pace from the morning's ice run.

🎿 Rental gear available at the Dorf sports shops 💵 ~CHF 30-40 half-day rental 🕐 Afternoon, tracks are groomed fresh overnight
Morning

Ride the Bernina Express toward the glaciers

St. Moritz is the northern terminus of the UNESCO-listed Bernina line, and even a half-day out-and-back toward Alp Grüm crosses the open Bernina Pass past frozen waterfalls and the Morteratsch glacier without a single tunnel shortcut.

🚆 ~2 hr each way to Alp Grüm and back 💵 ~CHF 74 round trip plus seat reservation 🕐 Morning departure, for the clearest glacier light
Midday

Afternoon tea in the grand-hotel style

Badrutt's Palace has anchored this end of town since 1896, and its lobby and terrace are where St. Moritz's winter high season has always been seen and been seen in, worth an hour even for non-guests over coffee or afternoon tea.

🎩 Smart-casual minimum, some rooms request more 💵 ~CHF 25-35 for coffee and pastry 🕐 Early afternoon, between lunch and the last packing
Afternoon

A last snowy walk to the station

St. Moritz's compact center means the walk back to the station is short even with luggage, leaving time for one last look at the lake before the train pulls out toward Chur, or south toward Tirano and Milan.

🚶 ~10 min walk from the Dorf center 💵 Free 🕐 Leave at least 3 hrs before an international flight from Zurich or Milan

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is included by law in Swiss restaurant and hotel bills, so tipping isn't expected the way it is in the US.
  • Rounding up the bill or leaving a few francs for good service is a nice gesture at mountain restaurants and lift-base bars, never required.
  • Grand hotels sometimes add a discretionary service charge for event weekends like White Turf, check the bill before adding more.

👗 Dress Code

  • Pack a real down jacket, not just a shell, mornings routinely sit well below -10°C on the mountain, and this is one of the colder resorts in the Alps.
  • Corvatsch and Corviglia's summits run colder and windier than the village, bring proper insulated ski gloves, not just liner gloves.
  • Badrutt's Palace and the grand-hotel bars keep a smart-casual-to-formal dress code in the evenings, ski gear at dinner is not the local norm here the way it is in some other Alpine towns.

🚡 Getting Around

  • The village is compact between the Dorf and Bad districts, most of this itinerary is walkable or a short funicular ride away.
  • A free ski bus links the lift bases, and the regional Engadin bus network covers Celerina, Pontresina, and Silvaplana for the Cresta Run and cross-country trails.
  • Slip-on ice grips for your boots are worth packing, village pavements ice over hard overnight even where they're cleared.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • The Cresta Run and any off-piste terrain off Corvatsch or Corviglia carry real injury risk, stick to marked runs and book the Cresta taster through the club, not informally.
  • Altitude at Corvatsch's 3,303m summit station can bring on light-headedness, especially combined with alcohol at the mountain restaurants.
  • Lake ice thickness is measured and managed by the town through the White Turf and polo season, only walk on the sections marked open to the public.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in German & Romansh

  • Grüezi: hello (Swiss German greeting)
  • Vielen Dank: thank you very much
  • Die Rechnung, bitte: the bill, please
  • Allegra: hello in Romansh, Switzerland's fourth national language, still spoken through the Engadin valley.

💳 Money & Payments

  • Switzerland uses the Swiss franc (CHF), not the euro, though some shops near the station will accept euros at a mediocre exchange rate.
  • Cards and contactless payments are accepted almost everywhere, including lift ticket counters and the Cresta Run clubhouse.
  • St. Moritz is priced at the very top of an already expensive country, budget well above nearby Austrian or Italian resorts for lift passes, dinners, and lodging.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Book the Corviglia-Corvatsch combined pass

A multi-day pass covering both ski areas costs meaningfully less per day than separate single-mountain tickets bought one at a time.

🥪

Picnic on the mountain

Buying lunch in the Dorf before heading up saves considerably over the sun-terrace restaurants at Corviglia or Corvatsch.

🚆

Skip the panorama-car surcharge on the Bernina Express

A standard Rhaetian Railway seat on the same train costs far less than the tourist panorama car for essentially the same views.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🛷

Watch the Cresta Run for free

Grandstand viewing at the Shuttlecock corner is free and open to the public most race mornings, no taster booking required just to watch.

🌄

Muottas Muragl over Corviglia for fewer crowds

The funicular up to Muottas Muragl gives a classic view over the Engadin's chain of lakes with a fraction of the ski-area crowds.

🎿

Ski Corviglia's back side toward Marguns

Most visitors stay on the Piz Nair side, the Marguns slopes see noticeably fewer skiers with equally good snow.

✈️ Flights

  • St. Moritz has no airport of its own, Zurich (ZRH) is the main gateway, with Milan Malpensa (MXP) a genuine alternative via the Bernina Express itself.
  • From Zurich, the scenic rail journey via Chur takes around 3.5 hours; from Milan, the Bernina Express route via Tirano takes roughly 4 hours.
  • Book the final rail leg well ahead in ski season, St. Moritz-bound trains sell out on peak weekends.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Taxis and hotel shuttles handle the short hops between the Dorf, Bad, and the lift bases.
  • Ride-hailing apps have limited presence here; hotels and the station taxi rank are the more reliable option.
  • Most of this itinerary is walkable once you're in the village center.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • A rental car adds little for a St. Moritz-based trip, the lifts, the Cresta Run, and the lake are all within walking distance or a short bus ride.
  • Winter tires and snow chains are mandatory on Graubünden's mountain passes if you do drive in, factor that into any rental.
  • Put the budget toward lift passes or the Bernina Express instead.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • St. Moritz Dorf: closest to Via Serlas, the Corviglia funicular, and the grand hotels, the most central base for this itinerary.
  • St. Moritz Bad: quieter and slightly lower priced, still a short walk or bus ride from the lake and the Dorf.
  • Celerina: right by the Cresta Run, a cheaper alternative a few minutes from the center by bus or train.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • Lake St. Moritz is frozen solid through this itinerary's entire winter season, so boat trips aren't applicable here.
  • Summer rowing and paddleboarding return to the same lake by July, a completely different trip worth its own visit.
  • The lake's role on this itinerary is entirely as an ice surface, for walking, White Turf, and snow polo.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Switzerland is part of the Schengen area but not the EU, so some non-EU travelers should still double-check passport and visa rules before booking.
  • Travel insurance that covers winter sports and altitude is worth adding, given the skiing, Cresta Run, and cable car days.
  • Remember the local currency is the Swiss franc (CHF); this is one of the most expensive resorts in the world, budget accordingly.
