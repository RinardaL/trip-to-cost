4 Days in Málaga: The Alcazaba, Picasso & Winter Sun

A Moorish fortress, two art museums, tapas bars and sardines on the beach, plus the Caminito del Rey: four days in Spain's sunniest mild winter, with honest weather and prices.

Ideal SeasonYear-round (mild winters, Mar – May and Sep – Nov best)
Est. Budget$300-520
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Málaga Airport & the C1 Train

Málaga-Costa del Sol Airport (AGP) is about 8 km from the centre. The Cercanías C1 train from the airport station reaches Málaga Centro-Alameda in roughly 12 to 15 minutes for about €1.80, with trains every 20 minutes or so. The EMT Line A bus costs about €4 (check current). Buy tickets at the machines.

🚆 ~12-15 min by Cercanías C1 to Centro-Alameda 💵 ~€1.80 train, ~€4 Line A bus (check current) 🕐 Plan around your landing time, then drop your bags
Afternoon

Calle Larios & Plaza de la Constitución

Calle Marqués de Larios is Málaga's pedestrian main street and runs straight into Plaza de la Constitución, the old town's central square. Both are free and flat. In winter, daytime highs are typically 16 to 18°C and the sun is strong, so a light jacket is enough. Skip the café terraces on the square, which charge the most.

🚶 ~5-10 min on foot from Centro-Alameda station 💵 Free, ~€2-3 for a coffee away from the square 🕐 After lunch, when the street is lively but not packed
Evening

Tapas Crawl & the Christmas Lights

From late November to about 6 January, Calle Larios is covered in lights, with a music show at set times each evening (check current times). Then eat around Calle Granada and Calle Císter. Tapas cost about €3 to €6 each and are not free with a drink, so order a few at a time. Finish with sweet Málaga wine.

🚶 ~5-10 min on foot between the street and the tapas lanes 💵 Lights free, ~€20-30 for a tapas dinner with drinks 🕐 Dinner from about 8:30 PM, when locals start to eat
Morning

The Roman Theatre & the Alcazaba

The Roman Theatre at the foot of the hill is free to see (check its current days and hours). Above it, the Alcazaba costs about €7, or about €10 combined with Gibralfaro (check current). In winter it opens 9:00 to 18:00, last entry 17:00, and entry is free from 14:00 on Sundays, with queues. Go at opening for quiet courtyards.

🚶 ~10 min on foot from Plaza de la Constitución 💵 Theatre free, Alcazaba ~€7 or ~€10 combined (check current) 🕐 Opens at 9:00, so arrive at the gate then
Afternoon

Gibralfaro Castle & the Views

Gibralfaro Castle crowns the hill above the Alcazaba, and its ramparts give the best views over the port, the bullring and the old town. The walk up takes about 20 minutes and is steep, so wear grippy shoes. Your combined ticket covers it. In winter the last entry is at 17:00, so start by about 14:30.

🚶 ~20 min uphill on foot from the Alcazaba 💵 Included in the ~€10 combined ticket 🕐 Start by about 14:30, last entry 17:00 in winter
Evening

The Cathedral (La Manquita) & Tapas

The cathedral is nicknamed La Manquita, the one-armed lady, because its second tower was never finished. Entry is about €10 with an audioguide, but winter hours vary (about 10:30 to 18:30 on weekdays, afternoons only on Sundays). Roof tours were suspended from April 2024 for repairs, so check whether they have reopened. Then try tapas nearby.

🚶 ~10 min on foot downhill from Gibralfaro, then 5 min to the tapas lanes 💵 Cathedral ~€10 (check current), ~€20-30 for dinner 🕐 Late afternoon, last entry 45 minutes before closing
Morning

The Picasso Museum

Málaga is Picasso's birthplace, and the museum sits in a 16th-century palace in the old town. The standard ticket is about €13 (check current). From November to February it opens 10:00 to 18:00, with last entry at 17:30, and entry is free on Sundays in the last two hours, with queues. Book online and allow about two hours.

🚶 ~5 min on foot from the cathedral 💵 ~€13 (check current), free Sunday late afternoon 🕐 Right at opening, 10:00, or a Sunday after 16:00 for free entry
Afternoon

Centre Pompidou at El Cubo & Muelle Uno

The Centre Pompidou Málaga lives under a glass cube on the quay. The permanent collection costs about €7, or about €9 with the temporary exhibition (check current). It is open 9:30 to 20:00 and closed on Tuesdays, with free entry on Sundays from 16:00. Afterwards, walk the free Muelle Uno promenade past the yachts and the lighthouse.

🚶 ~15 min on foot from the old town 💵 ~€7 permanent collection, Muelle Uno free (check current) 🕐 Closed Tuesdays, free Sundays from 16:00
Evening

Espetos de Sardinas at La Malagueta

Espetos are sardines skewered on cane and grilled over a wood fire in an old boat filled with sand. Try them at a beach bar on La Malagueta or in Pedregalejo, a short bus ride east. A portion costs about €10 to €14 (check current). In winter the sea is about 15°C, so paddle rather than swim.

🚶 ~15 min on foot from Muelle Uno, or a short bus ride to Pedregalejo 💵 ~€10-14 for a portion of espetos (check current) 🕐 Sunset is around 18:00 in December, and some beach bars close early
Morning

Train to El Chorro

The Caminito del Rey is a boardwalk fixed to the wall of a gorge. Tickets are named, so book your slot on the official site caminitodelrey.info before you travel. Take the train from María Zambrano to El Chorro, about 45 minutes for under €4 with a few departures a day (check timetables). The route is closed on Mondays.

🚆 ~45 min by train from María Zambrano to El Chorro 💵 Train under ~€4 each way (check current) 🕐 Choose a slot that leaves time after your train arrives
Afternoon

Walking the Caminito del Rey

The 7.7 km walk takes three to four hours. A shuttle bus (about €2.50, check current) takes you to the start, and the helmet comes with the ticket of about €10. Under-eights are not allowed, and the path can close in heavy rain or wind. If it is closed or sold out, take a bus to Nerja instead.

🚌 Shuttle bus to the start, then ~3-4 hours on foot 💵 ~€10 entry plus ~€2.50 shuttle (check current) 🕐 Closed Mondays, bring ID and water
Evening

Back to Málaga & the Airport

Take the train back to María Zambrano, then the C1 to the airport, about 10 minutes for around €1.80. Leave at least 2 hours before your flight. This day ends late, so book a late evening or next-morning flight, or ask your hotel to hold your bag.

🚆 ~45 min to María Zambrano, then ~10 min on the C1 to the airport 💵 Train under ~€4 plus ~€1.80 on the C1 (check current) 🕐 Check last trains, and be at the airport 2 hours before your flight

💶 Tipping, Bread & Coffee

  • Tipping is not expected in Spain and service is included in menu prices. For good table service, rounding up or leaving 5 to 10 per cent is plenty, and nothing is expected at a bar counter.
  • Some restaurants charge a small amount per person for bread or a cover (pan or cubierto). It should be on the menu, so check it before you sit down.
  • Málaga has its own coffee code: ask for a "mitad" (half coffee, half milk), a "sombra" (more milk) or a "nube" (mostly milk). A coffee at the bar costs less than one at a table.

🐟 Eating & Drinking in Málaga

  • Try espetos de sardinas (grilled sardines), pescaíto frito (a mixed plate of fried fish), boquerones (anchovies), ajoblanco (a cold almond and garlic soup, mostly served in warm weather) and porra antequerana, a thick tomato soup.
  • Málaga is known for sweet wine, such as Moscatel and Pedro Ximénez, often served from barrels in old bars. A small glass costs a couple of euros. Beer is ordered as a caña (small) or a tubo (taller).
  • Tapas are usually paid for in Málaga, not given free with a drink. Order a tapa, a media ración (half) or a ración (full plate) to share. Lunch is 14:00 to 16:00 and dinner from about 21:00, and many places offer a weekday menú del día for roughly €12 to €16.

🕐 Hours, Siesta & Sundays

  • Some small shops close for a midday break, roughly 14:00 to 17:00, but big shops, museums and the main tourist sights stay open through the day.
  • The Centre Pompidou is closed on Tuesdays, the Caminito del Rey on Mondays, and the Roman Theatre on some days (check current). On 25 December and 1 January the cathedral, the Picasso Museum and the Pompidou are closed.
  • Sunday afternoon free entry (Alcazaba from 14:00, Pompidou from 16:00, Picasso in the last two hours) brings queues, so arrive early or pay and skip the wait.
  • Locals eat late. A restaurant that is empty at 19:00 is not a bad sign, it is just early.

⚠️ Safety, Scams & Etiquette Warnings

  • Pickpockets work crowded places, especially Calle Larios (more so at Christmas), the Alameda, Muelle Uno, the airport bus and the beach. Use a zipped bag, keep your phone away from table edges and never leave bags on the sand.
  • Restaurants right on Plaza de la Constitución and Calle Larios, and any with a tout outside or a photo menu, are priced for passers-by. Walk two or three streets away and check the price list first.
  • Winter sun is still strong at midday, and the sea can be rough or cold. Use sunscreen, obey the flag colours on the beach and avoid swimming alone.
  • Be wary of strangers who offer sprigs of rosemary or "fortunes" and then ask for money, and use only licensed taxis. Check your government's travel advice for Spain (for example the UK FCDO or the US State Department) before you go. The emergency number is 112.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Spanish

  • "Buenos días" (good morning), "Buenas tardes" (good afternoon), "Hola" (hello), "Adiós" (goodbye)
  • "Por favor" (please), "Gracias" (thank you), "De nada" (you're welcome), "Perdone" (excuse me)
  • "Una mesa para dos, por favor" (a table for two, please), "La cuenta, por favor" (the bill, please)
  • "¿Habla inglés?" (do you speak English?), "¿Dónde está...?" (where is...?), "¿Cuánto cuesta?" (how much is it?)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Spain uses the euro. Cards and contactless work almost everywhere, including bars and most beach restaurants, but carry a little cash for small kiosks, markets and some tapas bars.
  • Use bank-branded ATMs and avoid standalone exchange booths. If a terminal offers to charge you in your home currency, choose euros.
  • Check whether any local tourist tax applies to your hotel or apartment, since it is usually paid on top of the room rate at the property.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Buy the combined Alcazaba and Gibralfaro ticket

The combined ticket costs about €10, against about €7 for one monument (check current). It covers both on the same visit, which is the cheapest way to see them both.

🍽️

Eat the menú del día at lunch

Many bars and restaurants offer a weekday set lunch of two courses, a drink and bread for roughly €12 to €16. It costs far less than the same food in the evening, so make lunch your main meal.

🚆

Take the train, not a taxi, from the airport

The C1 costs about €1.80 and takes about 12 to 15 minutes to the centre. A taxi costs roughly ten times that, so use the train unless you arrive very late or very early (check timetables).

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🥬

Visit the Atarazanas market in the morning

The Mercado de Atarazanas has a medieval Moorish gate and stalls of fish, ham and fruit. It is busiest in the morning, and many stalls close by mid-afternoon, so go early and check its days.

🌅

Watch sunset from the Alcazaba or Gibralfaro

The views over the port and the old town are at their best near sunset, but the sites close at 18:00 in winter. The beach at La Malagueta is a free alternative after you finish.

🏖️

Swap the Caminito for Nerja or Ronda

If the Caminito is closed or sold out, Nerja (about an hour by bus) has clifftop viewpoints and beaches. Ronda has a dramatic gorge and bridge but is a longer trip. Check current timetables.

✈️ Flights

  • Málaga-Costa del Sol Airport (AGP) is the main airport for the Costa del Sol, about 8 km from the centre, with many low-cost and full-service flights from across Europe.
  • The Cercanías C1 train from the airport station to Málaga Centro-Alameda takes about 12 to 15 minutes and costs about €1.80, with trains every 20 minutes or so (check current).
  • The EMT Line A bus runs to Paseo del Parque for about €4 and takes roughly 20 minutes (check current).

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Licensed taxis are white with a blue stripe and use a meter. Airport rides to the centre cost roughly €20 to €25 (check current), and there are ranks outside arrivals.
  • Apps such as Free Now, Cabify and Uber operate in Málaga. Check the app for current coverage and prices.
  • The old town is largely pedestrianised, so taxis drop you at the edge. Walk the rest.

🚌 Public Transport & Tickets

  • EMT runs the city buses. A single ticket costs about €1.40 to €1.50 (check current) and you can pay the driver, but a rechargeable Bonobús card gives cheaper rides.
  • Cercanías trains link the airport, the centre, María Zambrano station and the coast. The C1 runs to Fuengirola, and a single in the central zones is about €1.80.
  • Most of this itinerary is walkable. Add the El Chorro train for the Caminito del Rey, which costs under about €4 each way (check current).
  • Buses and the airport train are a pickpocket hotspot, so keep your bag zipped and in front of you.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Centro Histórico: the most walkable base for the Alcazaba, the cathedral and the tapas lanes, but the priciest.
  • Soho and the Alameda area: lively, close to the Centre Pompidou and Muelle Uno, with good transport links.
  • La Malagueta: near the beach and bullring, quieter in winter, with a 20-minute walk to the old town.
  • Near María Zambrano station: practical for trains to El Chorro, the airport and Seville, but less atmosphere.
  • Pedregalejo or El Palo: cheaper and local, with fish restaurants, but a bus ride from the sights.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Spain is in the EU, so EU SIM holders roam at home rates. Other visitors can use an eSIM or a local prepaid SIM, and many cafés and hotels offer Wi-Fi.
  • Cards work almost everywhere, but keep some cash for small kiosks and a few tapas bars.
  • Use bank-branded ATMs and avoid standalone exchange booths.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Check your passport's Schengen rules before booking, including whether ETIAS applies on your travel dates.
  • Book the Caminito del Rey well ahead on the official site, because slots sell out several weeks ahead in busy periods, and winter maintenance closures happen.
  • In winter, plan the sunny hours for outdoor sights and keep museums for rainy days. Daytime highs are typically 16 to 18°C, with about 5 to 6 hours of sun a day.
  • You do not need a car. Trains, buses and walking cover this itinerary, and parking in the old town is difficult.

About $300-520 per person for 4 days, roughly $75-130 a day, excluding international flights. The estimate covers lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and real costs vary with the season and your spending habits.
