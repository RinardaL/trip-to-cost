4 Days in Strasbourg: Petite France, Cathedral & the Alsace Wine Route

From the canal-side half-timbered houses of Petite France to a pink sandstone cathedral and a day among the Alsace vineyards, Strasbourg packs a distinctly Franco-German character into four unhurried days.

Ideal SeasonMay – Jun, Sep
Est. Budget$650-850
Duration4 Days
PaceRelaxed to Moderate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival and check-in near the Grande Île

Strasbourg's train station sits a short tram ride from the old town, and most travelers connect here by a fast TGV from Paris or a train from Frankfurt or Basel-Mulhouse airport.

🚊 ~10 min tram from Gare de Strasbourg 💵 ~€1.80 🕐 Plan around your train or transfer's arrival time
Afternoon

Wandering the canals of Petite France

Half-timbered tanners' and millers' houses lean over the Ill's canals in this old quarter, still the single most photographed corner of the city and an easy afternoon to lose yourself in.

🚶 Steps from Place Gutenberg 💵 Free to wander 🕐 Mid-afternoon, then linger for the light to shift
Evening

Golden hour on the Ponts Couverts and a first winstub dinner

The covered bridges and the Barrage Vauban terrace catch the best light of the day, then settle into a traditional winstub for tarte flambée and a glass of local Riesling.

🚶 Along the Ponts Couverts and Barrage Vauban 💵 ~€22 dinner at a winstub 🕐 Golden hour, about an hour before sunset
Morning

Strasbourg Cathedral's pink sandstone facade

Notre-Dame de Strasbourg's single spire once made it the tallest building in the world, and the platform climb up its tower rewards you with rooftops stretching to the Black Forest on a clear day.

🚶 ~5 min walk from Place Gutenberg 💵 ~€8 platform climb 🕐 Right at the 8:30 AM opening, before tour groups
Midday

The astronomical clock's mechanical show

Inside the cathedral, the 16th-century astronomical clock puts on a brief mechanical parade of the apostles once a day, a genuine piece of Renaissance engineering still keeping time.

🚶 Inside the cathedral, south transept 💵 ~€3 clock viewing ticket 🕐 12:30 PM sharp, the one daily show
Afternoon

Place Kléber, Place Gutenberg & Maison Kammerzell

These three landmarks anchor the old town: a broad central square, a quieter one named for the printing pioneer, and one of the finest carved half-timbered facades in France.

🚶 Connected by a 10-minute walk 💵 Free to stroll 🕐 Mid-to-late afternoon
Morning

Day trip to Colmar by train

A direct regional train covers the distance in well under an hour, dropping you close enough to walk into Colmar's old town without any further transfers.

🚆 ~35 min direct train from Strasbourg 💵 ~€17 round-trip 🕐 Catch an early train, before 9 AM
Midday

Colmar's Petite Venise

Colmar's own canal quarter, nicknamed Little Venice, is smaller and calmer than Strasbourg's Petite France, with the same half-timbered charm and far fewer crowds before noon.

🚶 ~10 min walk from Colmar station 💵 Free to wander 🕐 Late morning into lunch
Afternoon

Wine tasting in a nearby Alsace village

A short bus or taxi ride reaches Riquewihr or Eguisheim, two of the prettiest stops on the Alsace Wine Route, where small family cellars pour Riesling and Gewürztraminer flights on request.

🚌 ~20 min bus or taxi from Colmar to Riquewihr 💵 ~€15 per tasting flight 🕐 Mid-afternoon, before the last train back
Morning

A slow bike ride through the old town

Strasbourg is one of France's most bike-friendly cities, and an early ride through the quiet Grande Île streets is a relaxed way to close out the trip before the day-trippers arrive.

🚲 Rented from a stand near Place Gutenberg 💵 ~€10 for a half-day rental 🕐 Early morning, while the streets are quiet
Midday

The European Quarter and European Parliament

Strasbourg is one of only a handful of cities that hosts a genuine seat of European governance, and the European Parliament's glass-and-steel hemicycle is an easy tram ride from the medieval old town.

🚊 Tram Line E or C to Droits de l'Homme 💵 Free to view from outside 🕐 Late morning on a weekday, when Parliament is in session
Afternoon

Transfer to the station or airport for departure

Budget extra time if you're catching a connecting train onward to Frankfurt or Basel-Mulhouse airport, since Strasbourg's own airport handles only a limited set of routes.

🚆 TGV to Paris, or train to Frankfurt/Basel-Mulhouse airport 💵 Varies by route 🕐 Leave at least 3 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is included by law (service compris) in French restaurants and cafes, so tipping is a small gesture rather than an expectation.
  • Rounding up the bill or leaving a euro or two for good service is common practice, especially at a winstub or wine tasting.
  • Taxi drivers and hotel staff appreciate a small tip, but no one will chase you down for skipping it.

👗 Dress Code

  • Strasbourg Cathedral asks for shoulders to be covered inside, though the rule is more relaxed than in Italy's grand churches.
  • Smart-casual suits most winstubs and restaurants, and Alsace's village tastings are informal enough for daytime walking clothes.
  • Layers matter more than style here: a warm one for the cathedral's stone interior, a light one for the vineyards' open sun.

🚊 Getting Around Strasbourg & Alsace

  • The CTS tram network has six lines covering the old town and the European Quarter, with a single ticket costing around €1.80 and valid for an hour of travel.
  • The Grande Île and Petite France are compact enough to walk end to end, so save the tram for the European Quarter or the train station.
  • Alsace wine villages like Colmar, Riquewihr, and Eguisheim are reached by regional train plus a short local bus or taxi, or by rental car if you want full flexibility for the day.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Strasbourg is generally low-risk, though bag-snatching around the train station and busy tram stops is worth a moment's caution.
  • Bike lanes are everywhere and locals ride fast, so look both ways before stepping off a curb, even on quiet-looking streets.
  • Validate any paper tram ticket before boarding; inspectors do check, and unvalidated tickets carry an on-the-spot fine.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in French

  • Bonjour: good morning/hello
  • Merci beaucoup: thank you very much
  • L'addition, s'il vous plaît: the bill, please
  • Alsace itself has a distinct regional flavor: the local Alsatian dialect and much of the region's food, architecture, and surnames carry a clear German influence, a legacy of the border shifting back and forth over the centuries.

💳 Money & Payments

  • The euro is the currency, and contactless cards are accepted almost everywhere, including tram ticket machines and small wine cellars.
  • Carry some small cash for village tastings and market stalls, where card minimums or spotty connectivity can be an issue.
  • ATMs are common in both Strasbourg and Colmar, but bank-branded machines avoid the higher fees some standalone kiosks charge.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🚆

Book the Colmar train early

Regional TER fares to Colmar rise closer to departure, so booking a day or two ahead can cut the round-trip cost noticeably.

🥖

Lunch at a boulangerie instead of a sit-down winstub

A sandwich or quiche from a good bakery costs a third of a restaurant lunch and tastes just as regional.

🍷

Ask for a tasting flight, not a full bottle

Most Alsace cellars will pour a small flight of three or four wines for a modest fee, cheaper than committing to a bottle at every stop.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🏡

Riquewihr or Eguisheim over Colmar's busiest lanes

Both villages have the same half-timbered, flower-box charm as central Colmar with a fraction of the tour buses, especially outside midday.

🌳

Parc de l'Orangerie for a quiet break

A short tram ride from the old town, this park has quiet lakeside paths and a small storks' enclosure, a good pause between sightseeing days.

🛶

Petite France at sunrise

The canals are nearly empty before 8 AM, a completely different mood from the crowds that fill the same lanes by midday.

✈️ Flights

  • Strasbourg Airport (SXB) is small and handles a limited set of mostly domestic and regional routes, so it rarely works as a main international gateway.
  • Most travelers fly into Frankfurt or Basel-Mulhouse-Freiburg (EuroAirport) and connect by train, both around an hour and a half from the city center.
  • Flying into Paris CDG and taking the TGV is also a strong option: the high-speed train covers the distance in under two hours, often faster than a connecting flight.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Uber operates in Strasbourg alongside licensed taxis, both easy to book from a phone at any hour.
  • The compact, walkable old town means you'll rarely need either for sightseeing, they're mainly useful for the train station or late-night returns.
  • Taxis are the simplest option for the short bus-free hop between Colmar and a wine village if the timing doesn't line up.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • A car is genuinely useful for only one part of this itinerary: Day 3's Wine Route trip, where a car lets you stop at several villages in one afternoon instead of relying on Colmar's local bus schedule.
  • Inside Strasbourg itself, skip it entirely: the old town is largely pedestrianized, parking is limited, and the tram covers everything else.
  • If you do rent for the wine route, pick it up the morning of Day 3 and return it that evening rather than carrying it for the whole trip.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Grande Île / Petite France: puts Day 1's canal walk and Day 2's cathedral within easy walking distance, the most convenient base for this itinerary.
  • Near Place Gutenberg or the Cathedral: central and quiet at night, a good middle-ground option close to the old town squares.
  • Near the train station: a practical choice if you want the fastest possible access to Day 3's Colmar train and Day 4's onward travel.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • This itinerary has no boat leg built in, Strasbourg is a city best covered on foot, by tram, and by train to the wine villages.
  • That said, a short guided boat tour along the Petite France canals and the Ill exists as an optional add-on if you want to see the half-timbered houses from the water rather than the towpath.
  • Treat it as a nice-to-have on Day 1 or Day 2, not a necessity, since the same views are free from the canal-side streets.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Standard Schengen entry rules apply, so non-EU visitors should confirm visa requirements before booking flights.
  • Travel insurance is worth adding given the pre-booked cathedral platform and wine tasting slots, which are usually non-refundable.
  • Strasbourg sits right on the French-German border, and that dual identity shows up everywhere, from choucroute and flammekueche on menus to the German-style step gables on half-timbered houses.
