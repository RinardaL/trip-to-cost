4 Days in Colmar: Christmas Markets & the Alsace Wine Route

Based entirely in Colmar rather than passing through on a day trip, this four-day itinerary lingers in Alsace's self-declared "Capitale de Noël" during its most festive weeks: illuminated market squares, a canal quarter strung with lights, and full days among the wine villages of Riquewihr, Kaysersberg, and Eguisheim, each with its own Christmas market.

Ideal SeasonNov – Dec (Christmas Markets)
Est. Budget$750-1,000
Duration4 Days
PaceRelaxed
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Gare de Colmar and check-in near the old town

Colmar's station connects directly to Strasbourg, and onward to Basel-Mulhouse airport or Paris with a single change, with the market squares only a fifteen-minute walk from the platform.

🚶 ~15 min walk from Gare de Colmar to the old town 💵 ~€10-15 taxi if you're carrying luggage 🕐 Plan around your train or transfer's arrival time
Afternoon

Place de l'Ancienne Douane and the Koifhus market

Colmar calls itself Alsace's Capitale de Noël, and this market wraps wooden chalets around the 15th-century Koifhus customs house, selling vin chaud, roasted chestnuts, and hand-carved ornaments from late November through December.

🚶 Steps from Place de l'Ancienne Douane 💵 Free to browse, ~€4-6 per vin chaud 🕐 Markets open around 10 AM
Evening

Petite Venise strung with lights, and a first cup of vin chaud

Colmar's own canal quarter trades its summer flower boxes for strings of white lights this time of year, and the reflections on the Lauch make for a quieter, more atmospheric scene than the crowded central squares.

🚶 ~10 min walk from the Koifhus 💵 ~€4 for a cup of mulled wine (plus a refundable mug deposit) 🕐 After dark, once the lights come on around 5 PM
Morning

Maison Pfister and the carved facades of the old town

Built in 1537 for a wealthy hatmaker, Maison Pfister's painted medallions, carved wooden gallery, and octagonal corner turret make it the single most photographed building in Colmar, and mornings here are quiet before the day-trip buses arrive.

🚶 On Rue des Marchands, ~5 min from the Koifhus 💵 Free to view from outside 🕐 Before 10 AM, ahead of the crowds
Midday

Wandering Rue des Marchands and the Grand'Rue

These two streets string together most of Colmar's finest half-timbered facades, small ateliers, and Alsatian bakeries, an easy midday walk between the morning's sights and the afternoon's markets.

🚶 Connected on foot, ~15 min end to end 💵 Free to stroll 🕐 Late morning into lunch
Afternoon

Place Jeanne-d'Arc's market and the Marché Couvert

A second, smaller market fills Place Jeanne-d'Arc with crafts and food stalls, and the 19th-century covered market nearby is the best stop for a sit-down Alsatian lunch or to pick up foie gras and kougelhopf to take home.

🚶 ~10 min walk from Rue des Marchands 💵 ~€12-18 lunch at the covered market 🕐 Early-to-mid afternoon, before it closes at 6 PM
Morning

Riquewihr's walled village market

A short drive or bus ride from Colmar, Riquewihr's Christmas market sets up along a single cobbled main street inside intact medieval ramparts, with the Dolder belfry lit up above the wooden chalets.

🚗 ~30 min drive or bus from Colmar 💵 Free to browse, ~€15 per wine tasting flight 🕐 Late morning, before tour groups arrive
Midday

Kaysersberg, voted France's favorite village

Kaysersberg's market spreads along the Weiss River below a ruined hilltop castle, and the village's 2017 title as the French people's favorite has made its market one of the most talked-about in Alsace without yet drawing Riquewihr's crowds.

🚗 ~15 min drive from Riquewihr 💵 Free to browse, ~€5-8 for lunch from a stall 🕐 Midday into early afternoon
Evening

Back to Colmar for a second evening among the market lights

Returning to Colmar by early evening gives you a second, more relaxed pass through the old town's markets, this time with a dinner reservation at a winstub instead of a schedule to keep.

🚗 ~20 min drive back to Colmar 💵 ~€20-25 dinner at a winstub 🕐 Early evening, before dinner reservations
Morning

Eguisheim's circular village market

Eguisheim's medieval streets curl in tight concentric rings around its central church, officially named one of France's most beautiful villages, and its compact Christmas market fills the square with local producers and craft stalls.

🚗 ~15 min drive or bus from Colmar 💵 Free to browse 🕐 Morning, for the quietest walk through the rings
Midday

Last-minute shopping at the Marché Couvert

Back in Colmar, the covered market is the easiest single stop for last-minute gifts, from Alsace's Christmas-spiced pain d'épices to bottles of Gewürztraminer packed for travel.

🚗 ~20 min drive back from Eguisheim, then on foot 💵 Varies by what you pack home 🕐 Late morning into early afternoon
Afternoon

Transfer to Gare de Colmar for departure

Regional trains connect Colmar back to Strasbourg, on to Basel-Mulhouse airport, or onward to Paris with a change, so budget extra time if market crowds are thick between your hotel and the station.

🚆 Regional train to Strasbourg, Basel-Mulhouse, or Paris 💵 Varies by route 🕐 Leave at least 2 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is included by law (service compris) in French restaurants and cafes, so tipping is a small gesture rather than an expectation.
  • Market stall vendors don't expect tips, though rounding up for a cup of vin chaud is common and appreciated.
  • At a winstub dinner, rounding up the bill or leaving a euro or two for good service is standard practice.

🧥 What to Wear in Winter Alsace

  • Layer heavily: evening temperatures at the markets regularly sit near or below freezing through December.
  • Waterproof, grippy boots matter more than style, cobblestones turn slick with winter rain, frost, or the occasional snow flurry.
  • Bring gloves, a scarf, and a warm hat, most of each day here is spent outdoors moving between market squares and villages.

🚗 Getting Around Colmar & the Wine Route

  • Colmar's old town is compact and fully walkable, so save transport for the trips out to the wine villages.
  • A rental car is the most flexible way to reach Riquewihr, Kaysersberg, and Eguisheim in a single day, especially since local bus schedules thin out in winter.
  • Regional trains link Colmar to Strasbourg in about 35 minutes, with onward connections to Basel-Mulhouse airport or Paris.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Cobblestones get slick with winter rain, frost, or occasional snow, so grippy, waterproof boots aren't optional.
  • Christmas market crowds, especially on December weekends, are prime pickpocket territory, keep bags zipped and worn to the front.
  • Most vin chaud stalls charge a small deposit (usually €1-2) for the ceramic mug, refunded when you return it, don't walk off with it by accident.

🎄 Alsace's Claim as the Christmas Market's Birthplace

  • Alsace, and the area around Strasbourg and Colmar in particular, points to written records of Christmas markets dating back to the 1570s, and locals take the "Capitale de Noël" title seriously.
  • The blend of French and German traditions shows up on the stalls themselves: Alsatian bredele cookies and kougelhopf sit alongside German-style glühwein and stollen.
  • Colmar's market sites, spread across Place Jeanne-d'Arc, Place de l'Ancienne Douane, and elsewhere, typically run from late November through December 30th, each square with a slightly different theme.

💳 Money & Payments

  • The euro is the currency, and contactless cards are accepted almost everywhere, including most market stalls.
  • Carry small notes and coins regardless, some individual stalls skip card readers or set a minimum purchase.
  • ATMs are common throughout Colmar's old town, and bank-branded machines avoid the higher fees some standalone kiosks charge.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🚗

Split a rental car for Day 3

A single day's rental divided among a group costs a fraction of separate taxis or tours to Riquewihr and Kaysersberg.

🍷

Ask for a tasting flight, not a full bottle

Wine cellars along the route will pour a small flight of three or four wines for a modest fee, cheaper than committing to a bottle at every village stop.

🥐

Lunch at the covered market instead of a sit-down restaurant

A plate from one of the Marché Couvert's stalls costs a fraction of a winstub lunch and tastes just as regional.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌙

Petite Venise after dark, not at midday

The canal quarter's lights and reflections are best appreciated once the day-trip buses have already left for the evening.

🏘️

Kaysersberg over Riquewihr's busiest hours

Kaysersberg's market has the same half-timbered charm with noticeably thinner crowds, especially before noon.

🕯️

Weekday mornings beat December weekends

Early-season weekday visits in late November are far calmer than the packed weekends once December arrives.

✈️ Flights

  • Basel-Mulhouse-Freiburg (EuroAirport) is the closest major airport, about 40 minutes from Colmar by car or train with a connection.
  • Strasbourg and Frankfurt also work as connecting hubs, both reachable by regional train in under two hours.
  • Flying into Paris CDG and taking the TGV to Strasbourg, then a short regional train to Colmar, is a strong option for a single-connection trip.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Uber and local taxis both operate in Colmar, easy to book from a phone at any hour.
  • The old town itself is fully walkable, so you'll mainly need either for the train station or a village day trip if you skip renting a car.
  • A taxi to Eguisheim or Riquewihr is workable for a single village, but adds up quickly if you're covering more than one in a day.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • A car earns its keep on Day 3, letting you reach both Riquewihr and Kaysersberg in one day instead of waiting on winter's reduced local bus schedule.
  • Keep it through Day 4 morning for Eguisheim, then return it once you're back in Colmar for the afternoon.
  • Inside Colmar itself, skip it entirely: the old town is largely pedestrianized during market season and parking near the squares is limited.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Old Town, near Place de l'Ancienne Douane: puts Days 1 and 2's markets and half-timbered streets within easy walking distance.
  • Near Petite Venise: quieter at night, with the canal's Christmas lights just outside the door.
  • Near the train station: practical if you're renting a car for Day 3 and want a fast pickup, or want the shortest walk for Day 4's departure.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • Petite Venise's flat-bottomed punt tours run through the warmer months but pause or cut back sharply in winter, so don't count on one for this itinerary.
  • The canal is just as photogenic from its towpaths and footbridges after dark, with none of the wait for a boat.
  • If you're building a return trip for the warm season instead, the boat tour is worth adding back in then.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Standard Schengen entry rules apply, so non-EU visitors should confirm visa requirements before booking flights.
  • Travel insurance is worth adding given the pre-booked rental car and wine tasting slots, which are usually non-refundable.
  • Markets typically close or reduce hours on December 24th evening and December 25th itself, worth checking exact dates before locking in which days go where.
