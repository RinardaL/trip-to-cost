3 Days in Bruges: Canals, Belfry & Medieval Streets

A tightly packed medieval core of stepped gables, quiet canals, and cobbled lanes, Bruges rewards three unhurried days with a belfry climb, a Michelangelo sculpture, and more chocolate and waffles than is strictly reasonable.

Ideal SeasonApr – Jun, Sep – Oct
Est. Budget$500-700/person
Duration3 Days
PaceRelaxed to Moderate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival and check-in near the Markt

Bruges has no airport of its own, so most travelers arrive by train into Bruges Station, a short hop from the historic center, and settle in before the old town's cobbles and canals take over the rest of the trip.

🚶 ~20 min walk or ~7 min bus from Bruges Station 💵 ~€3 bus into the center 🕐 Plan around your train's arrival time
Afternoon

The Belfry tower and the Burg's Basilica of the Holy Blood

The 83-meter Belfry rises straight out of the Markt, and the 366-step climb up its narrow stone stairs earns a rooftop panorama over the old town, just around the corner in the Burg square, the Basilica of the Holy Blood shelters a relic venerated for centuries.

🚶 Steps from the Markt to the Burg 💵 ~€14 Belfry climb, free to enter the Basilica 🕐 Early afternoon, before the climb's timed-entry slots fill
Evening

A canal boat tour at golden hour

A half-hour boat tour from one of the docks along the Dijver threads under low stone bridges and past the same brick facades painters have been sketching for centuries, and the light softens everything nicely by early evening.

🚤 Boarding docks along the Dijver, near the Burg 💵 ~€12 per adult for a 30-min tour 🕐 About an hour before sunset
Morning

The Church of Our Lady's Michelangelo Madonna

At 115.6 meters, this brick spire is one of the tallest in the world, and inside, a small marble Madonna and Child by Michelangelo, the only one of his sculptures to leave Italy in his lifetime, draws a steady stream of quiet visitors.

🚶 ~5 min walk from the Burg 💵 ~€8 for the museum section with the Madonna 🕐 Right at the 9:30 AM opening
Midday

Flemish Primitives at the Groeninge Museum

This compact fine arts museum punches well above its size, holding a concentrated collection of Flemish Primitive masters like Jan van Eyck and Hans Memling, painted with a level of detail that rewards standing closer than feels polite.

🚶 ~5 min walk along the Dijver 💵 ~€14 admission 🕐 Late morning, allow at least an hour
Afternoon

The Béguinage and a chocolate and waffle tasting walk

The whitewashed courtyard of the Princely Beguinage Ten Wijngaerde is one of the calmest corners in the city, and the walk back into town is the natural excuse for a proper chocolate shop stop and a warm waffle from a street stall.

🚶 ~10 min walk from the Groeninge Museum 💵 ~€2 entry to the Béguinage grounds, ~€15 for tastings 🕐 Mid-to-late afternoon
Morning

A bike ride along the canal to Damme

A flat, poplar-lined towpath follows the Damse Vaart canal out to Damme, a tiny fortified town with a Gothic town hall and a literary link to the legend of Till Eulenspiegel, an easy and scenic half-day round trip by bike.

🚲 ~25 min ride each way from the center 💵 ~€15 for a half-day bike rental 🕐 Start by 9 AM to beat the midday heat
Midday

The windmills along the old city ramparts

Riding back into Bruges along the Kruisvest, the grassy stretch of the old fortifications, passes four surviving windmills, including the still-operational Sint-Janshuismolen, which is open to climb for a small fee.

🚲 Along the Kruisvest ramparts path 💵 ~€4 to climb Sint-Janshuismolen 🕐 Late morning into midday
Afternoon

Transfer to the station for departure

Return the bike, collect any last-minute chocolate from a shop near the station, and budget extra time if you're connecting onward through Brussels for an international flight.

🚆 Train to Brussels Airport (BRU) or onward city 💵 Varies by route 🕐 Leave at least 3 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is included by law in Belgian restaurants and cafes, so tipping is a small gesture rather than an expectation.
  • Rounding up the bill or leaving a euro or two for good service is common practice, especially at a chocolate shop tasting or a canal boat tour.
  • Taxi drivers and hotel staff appreciate a small tip, but no one will chase you down for skipping it.

👗 Dress Code

  • Both the Church of Our Lady and the Basilica of the Holy Blood ask for shoulders to be covered inside, though the rule is casually enforced.
  • Smart-casual suits most restaurants, and the Béguinage and canal boat dock are informal enough for daytime walking clothes.
  • Comfortable, flat shoes matter more than style here, the old town's cobblestones are charming but unforgiving underfoot.

🚲 Getting Around Bruges

  • The historic center is compact enough to cover almost entirely on foot, with the Markt to the Béguinage taking about 15 minutes end to end.
  • Bicycles are the local default and can be rented by the hour or the day from shops near the station and the Markt, ideal for the Damme ride.
  • A local bus network connects the station to the center, useful on arrival or with luggage, but rarely needed once you're settled in.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Bruges is generally very low-risk, though pickpocketing around the Markt and busy canal-boat queues is worth a moment's caution.
  • Cyclists have the right of way on many streets and move quickly, so look both ways before stepping into a bike lane, even a quiet-looking one.
  • The cobblestones are slick when wet, and Belgium's weather turns quickly, so watch your footing after a rain shower.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Dutch (Flemish)

  • Goedendag: good day/hello
  • Dank u wel: thank you very much
  • De rekening, alstublieft: the bill, please
  • Bruges sits in Flanders, Belgium's Dutch-speaking region, though English and French are both widely understood in tourist areas.

💳 Money & Payments

  • The euro is the currency, and contactless cards are accepted almost everywhere, including canal boat docks and chocolate shops.
  • Carry some small cash for the Béguinage's entry box and smaller waffle stalls, where card minimums can be an issue.
  • ATMs are common throughout the center, but bank-branded machines avoid the higher fees some standalone kiosks charge.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Combine the Belfry and Groeninge Museum on a museum pass

A city museum pass covering several sights, including the Groeninge and Gruuthuse, works out cheaper than paying separate entry fees if you're visiting more than two.

🧇

Buy waffles from a stall, not a sit-down cafe

A waffle from a street window costs a fraction of the same thing served at a table with a tourist-menu markup, and it tastes just as good.

🚲

Rent bikes for a half day, not a full one

The Damme ride and ramparts loop together take under four hours, so a half-day rental covers the whole route without paying for unused time.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

The Markt at sunrise

The square is nearly empty before 8 AM, a completely different mood from the crowds and tour groups that fill it by midday.

🏞️

Minnewater Park over the busiest canal corners

Nicknamed the "Lake of Love," this quiet park just south of the Béguinage has the same canal-and-swan scenery with far fewer people.

🍫

Skip the chocolate shops directly on the Markt

Small family chocolatiers a few streets back, like those near Sint-Jakobsstraat, often have better prices and fresher batches than the main-square storefronts.

✈️ Flights

  • Bruges has no airport of its own, so nearly every visitor flies into Brussels Airport (BRU) and connects by a direct train, about an hour and fifteen minutes to Bruges Station.
  • Brussels South Charleroi Airport (CRL) is a common budget-carrier alternative, though it needs a shuttle bus into Brussels before the onward train.
  • Flying into Amsterdam Schiphol and taking a train south is also workable, at roughly three hours with one change, if fares line up better.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Uber has limited presence in Bruges, so licensed taxis, easily found at the station or called by phone, are the more reliable option.
  • The compact, largely pedestrianized old town means you'll rarely need either for sightseeing, mainly useful for the station or late-night returns.
  • Taxis are the simplest backup if you'd rather skip the bike ride out to Damme and still see it, though a bike is the more scenic way there.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Skip a car entirely for this itinerary, the old town is largely pedestrianized and car-free, parking is scarce, and everything on Days 1 and 2 is walkable.
  • Even Day 3's Damme trip is better and more scenic by bike along the canal towpath than by car.
  • A rental only makes sense if you're extending the trip to Ghent or Ypres afterward, in which case pick it up on departure day rather than carrying it through the whole stay.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Markt / Burg area: puts Day 1's Belfry climb and Basilica within a two-minute walk, the most convenient and liveliest base.
  • Near the Béguinage or Minnewater: quieter at night, still an easy walk to Day 2's museums and the Markt.
  • Near Bruges Station: a practical choice if you want the fastest possible access to Day 3's onward train, though a bit further from the historic core.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • The canal boat tour is one of Bruges' signature experiences and is built directly into Day 1 of this itinerary, not just an optional add-on.
  • Boats run frequently from several docks along the Dijver and Rozenhoedkaai, no advance booking needed outside of peak summer weekends.
  • For something longer, a seasonal boat also runs between Bruges and Damme along the Damse Vaart canal as an alternative to the bike ride.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Standard Schengen entry rules apply, so non-EU visitors should confirm visa requirements before booking flights.
  • Travel insurance is worth adding given the pre-booked Belfry climb slot, which is usually non-refundable.
  • Bruges is compact enough that most of the old town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in its own right, so tread lightly on the cobblestones and canal edges.
