4 Days in Strasbourg: Christmas Markets, Cathedral & Colmar

Vin chaud and bredele at the Christkindelsmärik, the giant Place Kléber tree, a lit-up cathedral and Colmar's markets: four days in Strasbourg, with tips on crowds and cold.

Ideal SeasonNov – Dec (Christmas Markets), May – Jun, Sep
Est. Budget$650-850
Duration4 Days
PaceRelaxed to Moderate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival, security checks and check-in on the Grande Île

Strasbourg's station is about 10 minutes on foot from the edge of the old town, and most visitors arrive on a TGV from Paris (under two hours) or by train from Frankfurt or Basel-Mulhouse airport. During the Christmas markets, security checks are set up at the entrances to the city centre, and some tram stops on the Grande Île are closed or not served, so check the CTS app and drop large bags at your hotel before you head into the crowds. Book trains and hotels as early as you can, because both fill up for market season.

🚊 Tram from the station to the old town, or about 10 min on foot 💵 €1.90 single tram ticket (check current) 🕐 Plan around your train's arrival time and allow extra time at the security checks
Afternoon

Petite France and its canal-side Christmas market

Half-timbered tanners' and millers' houses lean over the Ill's canals in this old quarter, and in the Christmas season stalls set up on its squares too. The latest programme listed a small market on Place Saint-Thomas and one on Place Benjamin-Zix selling glass baubles from Meisenthal, so check this year's map for the current list. The canals look best in daylight and the sun sets before 5 PM in December, so start early and stay while the lights come on.

🚶 A short walk from Place Kléber 💵 Free to wander, vin chaud about €3-7 a cup (check current) 🕐 Early afternoon, while there is daylight on the canals
Evening

Christmas lights, the Ponts Couverts and a winstub dinner

In recent years the streets around the cathedral, such as rue Mercière, have been hung with angel-shaped lights after dark. If there is still daylight, cross the Ponts Couverts and the Barrage Vauban terrace first (check the terrace's winter hours), then settle into a traditional winstub for tarte flambée, choucroute and a glass of local Riesling. Popular winstubs fill up in market season, so reserve if you can.

🚶 On foot between rue Mercière and the Ponts Couverts 💵 ~€22 dinner at a winstub 🕐 From dusk, before 5 PM in December
Morning

Strasbourg Cathedral's pink sandstone facade and the tower platform

Notre-Dame de Strasbourg's single spire made it the tallest building in the world from 1647 to 1874. According to the cathedral's website, the nave is open Monday to Saturday from 8:30 and stays open through the day to 17:45 during the Christmas market, with Sunday and holiday hours of 14:00 to 17:15. From 1 October to 31 March the tower platform opens at 10:00 and closes at 18:00, with the last climb at 17:15. Enter by the south portal, where bags are checked, and check current hours before you go: in 2025 the cathedral closed on the afternoon of 24 December.

🚶 ~5 min walk from Place Gutenberg 💵 Nave free, platform climb about €6.50-8 (check current) 🕐 Nave from 8:30, platform from 10:00 in winter
Midday

Place de la Cathédrale market, bredele & vin chaud

The chalets on Place de la Cathédrale and the neighbouring Place du Château are the closest market to the cathedral, with pottery, crafts, mulled wine and bredele, the small spiced and buttery Christmas biscuits of Alsace. Vin chaud comes in an official deposit cup (gobelet): guides report about €1 for the deposit, and you can return the cup or keep it as a souvenir. One change from other seasons: the cathedral's website says the 12:30 astronomical clock show is not held during the Christmas market, so check current before you plan around it.

🚶 Next to the cathedral, on Place de la Cathédrale and Place du Château 💵 Vin chaud ~€3-7 a cup, plus ~€1 deposit (check current) 🕐 Around 11:30, when the chalets open, for a calmer visit
Afternoon

Place Kléber's tree, Place Broglie & Maison Kammerzell

Place Kléber, the central square, holds the Grand Sapin, a tree of roughly 30 metres, and chalets run by associations. A short walk away, Place Broglie has the Christkindelsmärik, the "Christ child market" said to date back to 1570, which has stood on Place Broglie since 1871. Place Gutenberg, on the way to the cathedral, is a quieter square, and Maison Kammerzell beside the cathedral has one of the finest carved half-timbered facades in France. Guides list the markets as running about 27 November to 24 December 2026, closing at 18:00 on the 24th, so check the official dates, and stay for the tree after dark.

🚶 Linked by 5 to 10 minute walks 💵 Free to stroll 🕐 From mid-afternoon into the evening, to see the tree after dark
Morning

Day trip to Colmar by train

A direct regional train (TER) runs from Strasbourg to Colmar in roughly 30 minutes, with one to three trains an hour depending on the time of day. Colmar's old town is about a 15-minute walk from the station. The city's Christmas site lists its markets as running about 23 November to 29 December 2026, so check the official dates. Trains and markets are busier at weekends, so a weekday suits this day trip better.

🚆 ~30 min direct train from Strasbourg 💵 ~€15 each way for a same-day ticket (check current) 🕐 Catch an early train, before 9 AM
Midday

Colmar's Petite Venise and its Christmas markets

Colmar's canal quarter, nicknamed Little Venice, is smaller and calmer than Strasbourg's Petite France, and in the season it has its own market and decorations. The markets are spread across the old town, including Place des Dominicains, Place de l'Ancienne Douane, Petite Venise and the Koïfhus, where craft makers set up. Reported hours are 11:00 to 19:00 Monday to Thursday and 10:00 to 20:00 Friday to Sunday, so check current.

🚶 ~15 min walk from Colmar station 💵 Free to wander (the markets are free to enter) 🕐 Late morning into lunch, on a weekday if you can
Afternoon

A Christmas market in an Alsace wine village

Eguisheim is a village on the Alsace Wine Route a short ride from Colmar, and the Colmar region's Christmas site lists its market as running about 27 November to 30 December 2026 (in 2025 it closed on 24, 25 and 26 December, so check). Riquewihr's market is listed for 27 November to 20 December 2026. Alsace cellars pour Riesling and Gewürztraminer, but winter opening hours vary, so check before you go. Reach Eguisheim by local bus or taxi, and check the last train back to Strasbourg first.

🚌 ~10-15 min by local bus or taxi from Colmar to Eguisheim 💵 Free to enter the market, tasting prices vary by cellar 🕐 Mid-afternoon, before the last train back
Morning

A last market loop for bredele and gifts

Walk the old town once more before the chalets open, which recent guides put at about 11:30 daily, then shop for bredele, gingerbread, Meisenthal glass baubles and Soufflenheim pottery, all listed in recent market guides. Return your vin chaud cups for the deposit if you are not keeping them, and carry a padded bag for fragile gifts.

🚶 On foot between Place Kléber, Place Broglie and the cathedral 💵 Prices vary by stall, so compare before you buy 🕐 From about 11:30, once the chalets open
Midday

The European Quarter and European Parliament

Strasbourg is one of only a handful of cities that hosts a genuine seat of European governance, and the European Parliament's glass-and-steel hemicycle is an easy tram ride from the medieval old town. It is free to see from outside, and the Parliament's calendar shows whether a plenary week falls on your dates.

🚊 Tram B or C to République, then E to Droits de l'Homme 💵 Free to view from outside 🕐 Late morning, and check the Parliament calendar for plenary weeks
Afternoon

Transfer to the station or airport for departure

Budget extra time if you're catching a connecting train onward to Frankfurt or Basel-Mulhouse airport, since Strasbourg's own airport handles only a limited set of routes. Trains are busy around the holidays, so reserve seats ahead and check SNCF for holiday timetables.

🚆 TGV to Paris, or train to Frankfurt/Basel-Mulhouse airport 💵 Varies by route 🕐 Leave at least 3 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping & Market Etiquette

  • Service is included by law (service compris) in French restaurants and cafes, so tipping is a small gesture rather than an expectation.
  • Rounding up the bill or leaving a euro or two for good service is common practice, especially at a winstub.
  • Market lanes are narrow and get packed in the late afternoon and evening, so step aside to look at stalls or take photos, and keep large bags at your hotel because of the security checks.

🧥 Dress Code & Cold Weather

  • Strasbourg Cathedral asks for shoulders to be covered inside, though the rule is more relaxed than in Italy's grand churches.
  • Smart-casual suits most winstubs and restaurants, so one decent outfit covers your dinners.
  • Standing at a stall is colder than walking. Wear a warm coat, hat, gloves, a thermal layer and waterproof shoes with grip for wet cobbles, and remember that the sun sets before 5 PM in December, so evenings are the coldest hours.
  • The cathedral's stone interior is cool while winstubs and trains are warm, so layers you can add and remove work better than one heavy coat.

🚊 Getting Around Strasbourg & Alsace

  • The CTS tram network has six lines covering the old town and the European Quarter. On the CTS site a single ticket is from €1.90 and valid for an hour, a 24h Solo pass is from €4.60 and a 72h Solo pass from €10.20 (check current).
  • The Grande Île and Petite France are compact enough to walk end to end. During the markets some Grande Île tram stops are not served or close at busy times, so check CTS and walk the last stretch.
  • Colmar is a regional train ride away, and villages like Eguisheim and Riquewihr add a local bus or taxi. Check the timetable and the last train back, since winter days end early.

⚠️ Crowds, Security & Safety Warnings

  • Security checks are set up at the entrances to the city centre while the markets run, and bags are checked at the cathedral, so allow extra time and travel light.
  • Weekends and mid-December are the busiest. Guides advise a weekday, ideally in late November or early December, and one guide reports Tuesday and Thursday as the quietest days.
  • Pickpockets are reported at the busiest stalls, around the cathedral and near the train station. Keep bags zipped and in front of you, and look both ways at bike lanes, since locals ride fast on wet or frosty streets.
  • Validate any paper tram ticket before boarding; inspectors do check, and unvalidated tickets carry an on-the-spot fine.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in French

  • Bonjour: good morning/hello
  • Merci beaucoup: thank you very much
  • Un vin chaud, s'il vous plaît: a mulled wine, please
  • L'addition, s'il vous plaît: the bill, please
  • Joyeux Noël: Merry Christmas
  • Alsace itself has a distinct regional flavor: the local Alsatian dialect and much of the region's food, architecture, and surnames carry a clear German influence, and the market's name, Christkindelsmärik, is Alsatian for the Christ child market.

💳 Money, Mugs & Hours

  • The euro is the currency, and contactless cards are accepted almost everywhere. Carry coins and small notes for market stalls, where card minimums or spotty connectivity can be an issue.
  • Vin chaud is served in a deposit cup (gobelet). Guides report about €1 for the deposit and about €3 to €7 a cup, so ask at the stall where to return it (check current).
  • Guides citing the city list the Strasbourg markets as running 27 November to 24 December 2026, daily 11:30 to 21:00, opening day from 14:00 and closing at 18:00 on 24 December. Sources disagree on 25 December (closed or reduced hours), so check the official dates.
  • 26 December is an extra public holiday in Alsace, so expect many shops and some services to close. In 2025 the cathedral closed on the afternoon of 24 December, so check the 2026 schedule.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🚆

Check the Colmar train fare for your date

Fares for the Strasbourg to Colmar regional train differ by date and train: listings ranged from about €6 to €16 one way on the days sampled, and aggregator sites quote roughly €15 for a same-day ticket. Check the price for your day before you decide when to go.

🏨

Book the hotel early, or sleep a short walk off the centre

Guides say the most sought-after central hotels fill months ahead and that prices in Advent can be well above January rates. One guide reports hotels a few minutes on foot from the centre costing 30 to 40% less in Advent, so compare a base near the station or a tram stop with one on the Grande Île.

🥖

Lunch at a boulangerie instead of a sit-down winstub

A sandwich or quiche from a good bakery costs a third of a restaurant lunch and tastes just as regional, which leaves room in the budget for vin chaud and bredele.

🍷

Compare vin chaud prices and keep one cup

Guides report vin chaud from about €3 to €7 a cup, so look at a few stalls before you buy. The deposit is reported at about €1 (check current), so return your cups for a refund or keep one official cup as a souvenir.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🏡

The smaller markets for a quieter evening

Guides suggest Square Louise Weiss and Place Grimmeissen for a calmer visit than the cathedral and Place Kléber. Square Louise Weiss has producers' stalls, and Place Grimmeissen has the Marché OFF with second-hand and eco-responsible stalls.

⏰

Go on a weekday at 11:30 or after 20:30

Guides say the chalets open at 11:30 and that a weekday visit at opening is the calmest, and that the illuminations are quieter after 20:30. Weekend crowds are the biggest, and the city can set up one-way pedestrian routes on weekends.

🛶

Petite France before the chalets open

The canals are nearly empty in the early morning, a completely different mood from the crowds that fill the same lanes in the afternoon, and the market stalls on the nearby squares are not open yet.

✈️ Flights

  • Strasbourg Airport (SXB) is small and handles a limited set of mostly domestic and regional routes, so it rarely works as a main international gateway.
  • Most travelers fly into Frankfurt or Basel-Mulhouse-Freiburg (EuroAirport) and connect by train. From Frankfurt the fastest direct trains take under two hours, but most journeys need a change, so check the DB or SNCF timetable for your date.
  • Flying into Paris CDG and taking the TGV is also a strong option: the high-speed train covers the distance in under two hours. December trains fill up, so book your seats early.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Uber lists taxis and VTC cars in Strasbourg alongside the licensed taxis at stands near the station and the cathedral.
  • The compact, walkable old town means you will rarely need either for sightseeing. They are mainly useful for the train station or a late return when the trams are crowded.
  • Taxis are the simplest option for the short hop between Colmar and Eguisheim if the bus timing does not line up.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • A car is not needed for this itinerary: the train reaches Colmar, and a local bus or taxi covers the short hop to Eguisheim.
  • Inside Strasbourg itself, skip it entirely: the old town is largely pedestrianized, parking is limited, and the tram covers everything else.
  • If you rent anyway for the Alsace villages, pick it up the morning of Day 3 and return it that evening, and check current road and winter-driving conditions before you set out.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Book as early as you can: guides say central hotels fill months ahead for the markets, and prices in Advent can be far above January rates.
  • Grande Île / Petite France: puts Day 1's canal walk and Day 2's cathedral and markets within easy walking distance, the most convenient base for this itinerary and the most expensive in market season.
  • Near Place Gutenberg or the Cathedral: central, with the market crowds right outside, so ask about street noise in the evening.
  • Near the train station: a practical choice for Day 3's Colmar train and Day 4's onward travel, and one guide reports lower Advent prices a few minutes' walk from the centre.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • This itinerary has no boat leg built in, since Strasbourg is a city best covered on foot, by tram, and by train to Colmar.
  • A guided boat tour along the Petite France canals and the Ill exists as an optional add-on, but check whether tours run in winter and at what times before you plan around one.
  • In Colmar, the city's Christmas site lists children singing carols on boats in Petite Venise as an activity, so check the dates if that appeals.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Standard Schengen entry rules apply, so non-EU visitors should confirm visa requirements before booking flights.
  • Travel insurance is worth adding given the pre-booked hotels and trains for market season, which are often non-refundable.
  • Plan around the holiday closures: the markets close on 24 December (at 18:00 on the current guides), sources disagree on 25 December, and 26 December is a public holiday in Alsace. Check the official dates and opening hours for the days you travel.
  • Strasbourg sits right on the French-German border, and that dual identity shows up everywhere, from choucroute and flammekueche on menus to the German-style step gables on half-timbered houses.
