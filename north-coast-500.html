6 Days on the North Coast 500: Mountain Passes, Sea Lochs & the Far North

Hire a car at Inverness, cross the Bealach na Bà, follow the west coast to Durness and Smoo Cave, reach Dunnet Head and John o' Groats, then finish at Dunrobin Castle and Dornoch.

Ideal SeasonLate May to June, or September
Est. Budget$845-1185
Duration6 Days
PaceActive

Last updated: November 2026

6-day North Coast 500 itinerary

How many days do you have?

Morning

Inverness Airport & collecting the car

Inverness Airport (INV) is about 8 miles north-east of the city, and the main hire firms have desks in or beside the terminal. Bring your licence, passport and a credit card in the driver's name for the deposit hold. Photograph every scratch, check the fuel level, then drive 40 km to the Black Isle.

🚗 ~40 km, 40 min from the airport to Chanonry Point 💵 Card deposit hold, size set by the hire firm (check current) 🕐 Allow 45 to 60 min for the desk and the car check
Afternoon

Chanonry Point & Fortrose

Chanonry Point is a shingle spit on the Black Isle with a small lighthouse. Bottlenose dolphins often feed in the narrow channel between spring and late summer, but sightings are never guaranteed. Local guides suggest the rising tide, from about an hour after low tide, so check tide times.

🚗 ~5 km, 10 min between the point, Fortrose and Rosemarkie 💵 Free, parking at the point (check current) 🕐 Check the tide table: rising tide from about 1 hour after low tide
Evening

Inverness: castle, river & supplies

Drive back over the Kessock Bridge to Inverness, about 40 km. Walk the River Ness path below the castle, which stands on a mound above the water. Before bed, buy water, snacks and a picnic lunch, and fill the tank at an Inverness supermarket station, which a route guide lists as the cheapest fuel on the loop.

🚗 ~40 km, 40 min from Chanonry Point to Inverness 💵 Picnic supplies ~£10-15, dinner ~£15-25 (check current) 🕐 Early night: day 2 is a long drive
Morning

Inverness to Lochcarron by Strath Bran

Leave early through Beauly and Muir of Ord on the A862, then the A832 across Strath Bran to Achnasheen and down Glen Carron to Lochcarron, about 105 km. Parts are single-track with passing places. Fill the tank at Lochcarron if you did not in Inverness, because the Applecross peninsula has very few fuel stations.

🚗 ~105 km, 1 h 50 min to Lochcarron 💵 Fuel at Lochcarron (check current); coffee ~£4 🕐 Leave Inverness by 08:30 to cross the pass in good light
Afternoon

The Bealach na Bà

The road climbs from Tornapress to about 626 m, with gradients near 20 per cent, tight hairpins and single-track stretches. The official NC500 FAQ says it is not suitable for large motorhomes, caravans or inexperienced drivers, and signs say it is unlikely to be open in winter. The A896 coast road via Shieldaig is the alternative.

🚗 ~35 km, 1 h from Lochcarron to Applecross over the pass 💵 Free road, fuel only 🕐 Dry daylight; avoid ice, snow or low cloud
Evening

Applecross Bay & the coast road to Torridon

Applecross sits on a sheltered bay facing Raasay and Skye. The Applecross Inn is the village pub, so check meal times, which vary by season. Then take the single-track coast road north through Shieldaig to Torridon, about 45 km. Drive slowly at dusk, because deer and sheep graze beside the road, and use passing places for oncoming cars.

🚗 ~45 km, 1 h from Applecross to Torridon via Shieldaig 💵 Dinner ~£15-25 (check current) 🕐 Eat in Applecross by about 19:00, then drive in the long evening light
Morning

Glen Torridon, Beinn Eighe & Loch Maree

From Torridon take the A896 through Glen Torridon, under Beinn Eighe, to Kinlochewe, then the A832 beside Loch Maree to Gairloch, about 70 km. Use lay-bys, not passing places, for photos. Kinlochewe and Gairloch have fuel, and a route guide lists only Kinlochewe between Applecross and Gairloch, so top up at either.

🚗 ~70 km, 1 h 15 min from Torridon to Gairloch 💵 Free scenery; fuel at Kinlochewe or Gairloch 🕐 Mid-morning, before the lay-bys fill
Afternoon

Inverewe Garden, Poolewe

Inverewe Garden at Poolewe is run by the National Trust for Scotland and looks over Loch Ewe. The NTS lists it open daily 09:30 to 18:00 from May to September, with shorter hours in spring and autumn and closed days in winter. Adult entry is about £15 (check current; members free). Allow 1.5 to 2 hours.

🚗 ~10 km, 15 min from Gairloch to Poolewe 💵 ~£15 adult, NTS members free (check current) 🕐 Open daily 09:30-18:00, May to September
Evening

Corrieshalloch Gorge & Ullapool

From Poolewe, drive about 80 km via Dundonnell to Braemore Junction, where Corrieshalloch Gorge has a short path to a suspension bridge (parking about £5 for non-members; check the bridge status and hours first). Then continue to Ullapool, the last large shopping town before Thurso: buy food, withdraw cash and fill the tank.

🚗 ~80 km, 1 h 20 min from Poolewe to Ullapool 💵 Gorge parking ~£5 non-members, dinner ~£15-25 (check current) 🕐 Gorge early evening, harbour for sunset
Morning

Knockan Crag National Nature Reserve

Knockan Crag is about 50 km north of Ullapool on the A835 and lies in the North West Highlands Geopark. A short Thrust Trail from the Rock Room shows where older rocks were pushed over younger ones. NatureScot says the reserve is open all day, all year, and I found no admission charge (check current). Allow 45 to 60 minutes.

🚗 ~50 km, 55 min from Ullapool 💵 No admission charge found (check current) 🕐 Mid-morning, open all day all year
Afternoon

Lochinver & the B869 to Kylesku

Take the A835 and A837 to Lochinver, about 35 km, then the narrow B869 coast road to Kylesku, about 40 km of tight single-track. The official NC500 FAQ calls the B869 tricky for large vehicles and suggests motorhomes over about 16 to 18 ft avoid it. Allow nearly 2 hours. Lochinver has fuel and a good lunch stop.

🚗 ~75 km, 2 h from Knockan Crag to Kylesku via Lochinver 💵 Free road; lunch ~£8-14, fuel at Lochinver 🕐 Leave Knockan by 12:00 so you reach Durness before dark
Evening

Kylesku to Durness & Smoo Cave

From Kylesku drive about 55 km north via Scourie and Rhiconich to Durness. Smoo Cave is just east of the village, and the outer cave is free to enter. Boat trips into the inner cave run roughly April to October and are weather dependent (about £15 adult, one listing says cash only; check current). Durness has fuel and an ATM.

🚗 ~55 km, 1 h 10 min from Kylesku to Durness 💵 Cave free, boat trip ~£15 (check current) 🕐 Boat trips in the day, outer cave at any hour in summer light
Morning

Durness to Tongue & Castle Varrich

The A838 runs 58 km east to the Kyle of Tongue, crossing it on a causeway built in 1971. From Tongue a signposted path leads to Castle Varrich, a ruined Mackay tower, about 2 miles round trip and 1 to 1.5 hours; start opposite the Ben Loyal Hotel. Fill up at Tongue: the next pumps are at Bettyhill.

🚗 ~58 km, 1 h from Durness to Tongue 💵 Castle and path free; fuel at Tongue (check current) 🕐 Mid-morning
Afternoon

Bettyhill, Thurso & Dunnet Head

Follow the A836 east via Bettyhill to Thurso, about 80 km, then take the B855 north to Dunnet Head, the most northerly point of mainland Britain. The RSPB reserve has a lighthouse and sea cliffs, with puffins from about April to July. The large car park is free, and the viewpoint is exposed, so bring a windproof layer.

🚗 ~105 km, 2 h 10 min from Tongue to Dunnet Head 💵 Free to visit (check current); fuel at Bettyhill or Thurso 🕐 Afternoon; puffins April to July
Evening

Duncansby Head, John o' Groats & Wick

Drive 40 km to John o' Groats, where the village car park charges from April to October (reports of £2 to £3.50, check current). Then go 5 km to Duncansby Head, where parking is free, and walk to the Duncansby Stacks. Finish with the 27 km drive to Wick for the night.

🚗 ~75 km, 1 h 30 min via Duncansby Head to Wick 💵 John o' Groats parking ~£2-3.50 (check current); stacks free 🕐 Evening light on the stacks, May to September
Morning

Whaligoe Steps

The Whaligoe Steps are about 17 km south of Wick off the A99, a stair cut down a cliff to a tiny harbour. Sources give 330 steps, with one saying 365. They are steep, uneven and slippery when wet, so wear grippy shoes. Free to visit, with a small car park at the top. Allow 45 to 60 minutes.

🚗 ~17 km, 20 min from Wick to Whaligoe 💵 Free 🕐 Morning in dry weather; leave Wick by 08:45
Afternoon

Dunrobin Castle, Golspie

Dunrobin Castle is open daily 1 April to 31 October: 10:00 to 17:30 from April to September (last entry 16:30). Adult entry is £16 for the castle, museum and gardens (check current). Falconry displays run in season. From Whaligoe it is about 68 km on the A9, which has steep hills such as the Berriedale Braes.

🚗 ~68 km, 1 h 5 min from Whaligoe to Golspie 💵 £16 adult (check current), lunch in Golspie ~£8-12 🕐 Arrive about 11:00; closed November to March
Evening

Dornoch & the car back at Inverness Airport

Dornoch is a small cathedral town about 20 km from Dunrobin, with a sandy beach and a compact square. Allow an hour, then drive about 85 km to Inverness Airport via the Dornoch Firth Bridge. Refill the tank at an Inverness supermarket, return the car, and aim to reach check-in 2 hours before an evening flight.

🚗 ~105 km, about 1 h 40 min from Golspie via Dornoch to the airport 💵 Refill ~£20-30; Dornoch sights free (check current) 🕐 Car back by about 17:30 for a flight after 20:00

North Coast 500: what to know before you go

💷 Tipping, Cards & Cash

  • Tipping is not compulsory in the UK. Restaurants sometimes add a service charge (check the bill), and 10 per cent for good table service is a normal extra if it is not included.
  • Cards and contactless work almost everywhere, but carry some cash for small car parks, honesty boxes, village shops and boat trips (one listing says the Smoo Cave inner-cave tours are cash only; check current).
  • Some small fuel stations are pay-at-pump or card-only or cash-only, so carry a debit and a credit card.

🍽️ Food & Drink Etiquette

  • Highland menus lean on seafood, venison, Cullen skink (smoked haddock soup) and haggis. Order and pay at the bar in many pubs, and ask whether table service is used.
  • Kitchens in remote villages can close early and many places shut in winter, so check opening hours and book dinner ahead in busy months.
  • Whisky is a local pride: a dram is usually sipped, with a little water if you like. Do not drink and drive: see the warning card.

🚙 Single-Track Road Etiquette

  • Highway Code rule 155: if you meet oncoming traffic, pull into a passing place on your left, or wait opposite one on your right. Where you can, give way to uphill traffic. You may need to reverse to reach a passing place.
  • The official NC500 FAQ says that if you cannot accurately reverse your vehicle several hundred yards on a narrow single track you should not drive it.
  • Never park in a passing place, and pull in to let faster vehicles behind you pass. A raised hand is the usual thank-you.
  • Watch for sheep and deer on unfenced roads, and slow right down at blind summits and bends.

⚠️ Safety, Weather & Driving Warnings

  • Drive on the left, and take the first day slowly if you have just flown in. Scotland's drink-drive limit is 50 mg of alcohol per 100 ml of blood, and the Scottish Government says you cannot safely drink any alcohol when driving.
  • The official FAQ says it cannot guarantee good weather from November to February, and some attractions close in winter. In summer, midges bite most from May to September, especially early mornings and warm evenings; repellent and a midge hat help, and they cannot be found indoors.
  • Check your own government's travel advice for the UK before you go. In an emergency call 999 or 112.
  • Wild camping rules do not apply to motorised vehicles such as campervans, says the official FAQ. Use campsites or booked pitches.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Scottish Gaelic

  • English is used everywhere, but road signs are often bilingual. "Madainn mhath" (MAT-in VA), good morning
  • "Tapadh leibh" (TA-pa LEV), thank you; "Tapadh leat" (TA-pa LET), thank you to one friend
  • "Slàinte mhath" (SLAN-cha VA), good health, the usual toast
  • Place-name words: "loch" (lake or sea inlet), "glen" (valley), "ben" (mountain), "bealach" (BYAL-ach, a pass)

💳 Money & Payments

  • The UK uses the pound sterling (£). This page uses about $1.32 to £1 (a mid-market rate for 16 November 2026; the recent range was about 1.32 to 1.37).
  • There are no tolls on roads or bridges in Scotland, according to VisitScotland, and no ferry is needed to complete the loop.
  • Use bank ATMs in Inverness and Ullapool, because cash machines are few in the far north-west. Durness has an ATM, according to one listing (check current).

Insider tips for North Coast 500

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

⛽

Fill up at Inverness supermarkets

A route guide names the Inverness supermarket-linked stations as the cheapest fuel on the loop. Fill up before leaving and again whenever you pass Ullapool, Lochinver, Durness, Tongue or Thurso. Remote pumps tend to cost more (check current).

🥪

Picnic lunches

Buy bread, cheese and fruit at the Inverness and Ullapool supermarkets and eat at a viewpoint. A picnic costs a fraction of a pub lunch, and you do not lose time looking for open kitchens.

🛏️

Book early or go hostel

Late bookings cost much more, and one older official-site post saw prices rise sharply for same-day bookings. Its figures were £70-100 a night for two in a double and £17-25 per person in hostels or campsites with hostels (2018, so expect more now).

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

Duncansby Stacks at the end of the day

The stacks are a short walk from the free Duncansby Head car park, and the evening light on the red cliffs is better than the signpost photo at John o' Groats.

🪨

Whaligoe Steps before Dunrobin

Most visitors skip the steps. They are free, take under an hour and fit between Wick and Golspie on the A99.

🌊

The Drumbeg road in a small car

The B869 is a slow, twisting coast road that suits a compact car and rewards you with empty beaches and sea views. Large vehicles should stay on the main road.

Travel services for North Coast 500

✈️ Flights

  • Inverness Airport (INV) is the best choice: it is about 8 miles north-east of the city, and the loop starts and ends there.
  • Edinburgh and Glasgow have more flights and often cheaper fares, but the drive to Inverness takes roughly 3 to 3.5 hours each way (check a route planner), which would eat a full day.
  • Book flights into and out of INV to return the car where you collected it and avoid a one-way fee. For an evening departure, aim to leave the hire park about 2 hours before.
  • Check which airlines serve INV from your country; many visitors connect via London or another UK airport.

🚗 Car Hire

  • Visitors can drive small vehicles on a full, valid non-GB licence for 12 months after arriving in Great Britain (gov.uk). An International Driving Permit is not required by that rule, but a hire firm may ask for one if your licence is not in English, so check with the firm.
  • Hire firms set their own minimum age and a young-driver fee. Check both before booking. Expect a deposit hold on a credit card in the driver's name.
  • Check the excess (deductible) on the included insurance, and ask what an excess waiver costs. Take photos of the car, and ask whether it carries a spare wheel or only a repair kit.
  • Manual cars are cheaper and more common. Book an automatic early if you need one. A compact car is easier on single-track roads.
  • Choose full-to-full fuel. There is no one-way fee if you return to INV.

🛣️ Driving Rules & Roads

  • Drive on the left. National speed limits for cars are 30 mph built-up, 60 mph on single carriageways and 70 mph on dual carriageways and motorways (gov.uk), but you should drive far slower on single-track and hairpin roads.
  • Bealach na Bà: the official NC500 FAQ says it is not suitable for large motorhomes, caravans or inexperienced drivers; signs say it is unlikely to be open in winter. The A896 via Shieldaig is the alternative. Check Traffic Scotland and Highland Council road news before you go.
  • Fuel gaps (route guide): Thurso to Durness is about 80 miles with only Bettyhill and Tongue; Scourie to Ullapool is about 50 to 60 miles; Ullapool to Gairloch about 56 miles with only Laide; Gairloch to Applecross about 50 miles with only Kinlochewe. Small stations keep short hours, so fill up early.
  • A route is not a single road: the NC500 is a signed loop of A and B roads, with no tolls and no ferry needed.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Night 1 Inverness, night 2 Torridon or Shieldaig (or Applecross), night 3 Ullapool, night 4 Durness, night 5 Wick (or John o' Groats).
  • The official FAQ strongly advises booking ahead because providers may book up and some close at certain times of year. An older official-site post said popular hotels and B&Bs can fill 3 to 6 months ahead in summer.
  • Budget guide: a B&B or guest house double around £95-125 a night (my assumption; check current), hostels or campsites with hostels around £17-25 per person in the 2018 official-site post. July and August cost more.
  • Applecross, Durness and Tongue are small and sell out early (a third-party guide). Book those first.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • The UK is not in the EU, so check roaming with your home network or buy an eSIM or UK prepaid SIM before you arrive.
  • Download offline maps, because signal can drop in the glens and on single-track stretches.
  • There are no road tolls in Scotland (VisitScotland). Parking is mostly free, though John o' Groats charges April to October and Corrieshalloch Gorge about £5 for non-members (check current).
  • Carry cash and a card: some small pumps and boat trips take one only.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Check entry rules on the UK government site (gov.uk/eta). Many visitors from Europe, the USA, Australia and Canada need an ETA, which costs £20 and lasts up to 6 months; British and Irish passport holders do not need one.
  • Buy travel insurance covering medical care and cancellations. Ask whether your policy or credit card covers the car-hire excess.
  • Keep a full tank, water, snacks, waterproofs and a torch in the car on the west and north coasts.
  • Wild camping rules do not cover campervans, according to the official FAQ. Use campsites.

The North Coast 500 route, distances and driving times

This trip starts and ends at Inverness Airport and covers about 1,025 km (637 miles) in 6 days, including the airport transfers and side trips. The official loop is 516 miles (830 km). It goes west first: the Bealach na Bà and the Atlantic side come while you are fresh, and the east coast makes an easy final day.

Is 6 days enough for the North Coast 500?

It is possible but active. VisitScotland says non-stop driving takes 11 to 13 hours and most visitors spend 5 to 7 days; the official NC500 site says allow at least 5 days. At 6 days, expect 3 to 4.5 hours of driving most days and short stops. Add a 7th day if you want to hike or linger.

Which way round, and can I skip the Bealach na Bà?

VisitScotland says you can drive the loop in either direction. This plan goes west first so the pass and the west coast come while you are fresh. If the pass is closed or you are nervous, the A896 via Shieldaig bypasses it, as the official FAQ suggests. You miss one highlight but save time.

Does my credit card cover the car, and what should I check at pick-up?

Card cover varies: ask your issuer in writing whether it applies to UK hire and what the excess is. At pick-up, photograph every panel, wheel and the windscreen, check the fuel level and policy, and ask whether the car has a spare wheel or only a repair kit, because garages are far apart.
