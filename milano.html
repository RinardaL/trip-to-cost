4 Days in Milan: Duomo, Navigli & Brera

From the marble spires of the Duomo to aperitivo along the Navigli canals, four days is enough time to see Milan's grand piazzas, its fashion quarter, and its quieter art district without feeling rushed.

Ideal SeasonApr – Jun, Sep – Oct
Est. Budget$650-850
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival & check-in near the city center

The Malpensa Express train drops you at Cadorna or Centrale in under an hour, or Linate connects into town by bus in about 25 minutes.

🚆 ~50 min Malpensa Express, or ~25 min bus from Linate 💵 ~€13 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time
Afternoon

First look at Piazza del Duomo

Step out of the metro into the piazza and let the cathedral's marble spires do the introducing, then queue for a walk around the roof terraces if the light is good.

🚇 M1/M3 to Duomo station 💵 Free to admire the piazza 🕐 Mid-afternoon, before the queues build
Evening

Evening aperitivo near the Duomo

Settle into a rooftop or terrace bar just off the piazza for Milan's signature aperitivo, a drink that comes with a spread of snacks included in the price.

🚶 A few minutes' walk from the piazza 💵 ~€12 per drink with snacks 🕐 7 to 9 PM, the local aperitivo window
Morning

Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II

Walk the glass-domed arcade connecting Piazza del Duomo to La Scala, one of the world's oldest shopping galleries, and spin a heel on the bull mosaic for luck.

🚶 Steps from Piazza del Duomo 💵 Free to walk through 🕐 Right at opening, before the crowds fill the arcade
Midday

Quadrilatero della Moda shopping district

Milan's fashion quadrilateral, a few blocks of flagship boutiques between Via Montenapoleone and Via della Spiga, is worth a wander even on a browsing-only budget.

🚶 ~10 min walk from the Galleria 💵 Free to window-shop 🕐 Late morning, once the boutiques open at 10 AM
Afternoon

Castello Sforzesco & Parco Sempione

The Sforza family's Renaissance castle holds several city museums behind its brick ramparts, and the park just behind it is a good spot to slow down before dinner.

🚇 M1 to Cairoli or Cadorna 💵 ~€5 museum entry, courtyards free 🕐 Mid-afternoon, then stay for the park at golden hour
Morning

Daytime walk along the Naviglio Grande

These canals once moved the marble for the Duomo itself; today the towpaths are lined with artisan workshops and, on the last Sunday of the month, an antiques market.

🚇 M2 to Porta Genova 💵 Free to stroll 🕐 Mid-morning, when the towpaths are quiet
Afternoon

Aperitivo culture along the water

The Navigli is where Milan's aperitivo ritual runs deepest, a canal-side table, a Negroni or Spritz, and a buffet of small plates that can double as an early dinner.

🚶 Along the canal-front bars 💵 ~€10 per drink with buffet access 🕐 From 6 PM, before the after-work crowd arrives
Evening

Evening along the canals

Once the sun drops, the towpaths fill with locals moving between bars and small live-music spots, easily Milan's liveliest few blocks after dark.

🚶 Naviglio Grande and Naviglio Pavese towpaths 💵 ~€25 dinner nearby 🕐 After 9 PM, when the canal lights come on
Morning

Pinacoteca di Brera & the antique quarter

Milan's finest art collection hangs in a quiet former convent, and the surrounding Brera streets are packed with antique dealers, small galleries, and quiet courtyard cafes.

🚶 ~15 min walk from Piazza del Duomo 💵 ~€15 gallery entry 🕐 Right at the 8:30 AM opening, before tour groups
Afternoon

Final visit to the Duomo rooftop terraces

Save the stairs (or the lift) up to the cathedral's roof for a clear afternoon, when you can see the spires up close and the Alps on the horizon on a good day.

🚇 M1/M3 back to Duomo station 💵 ~€20 rooftop access with lift 🕐 Early afternoon, for the clearest visibility
Evening

Transfer to Malpensa or Linate for departure

Budget extra time for the Malpensa Express if your flight leaves from there, since it's the longer of the two airport connections.

🚆 ~50 min Malpensa Express, or ~25 min bus from Linate 💵 ~€13 🕐 Leave at least 3 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is usually already included (servizio compreso), so tipping isn't expected the way it is in the US.
  • Rounding up the bill or leaving a euro or two in cafes and bars is a nice gesture, not a requirement.
  • A "coperto" (cover charge) of €2 to €4 per person is standard at sit-down restaurants and is separate from any tip.

👗 Dress Code

  • The Duomo enforces a strict dress code: shoulders and knees must be covered for both the cathedral interior and the rooftop terraces.
  • Smart-casual works for most restaurants, and Milan's fashion-forward locals tend to dress a notch up even for daytime errands.
  • Comfortable shoes matter more than style on Days 2 and 4, given the cobblestones around Brera and the Quadrilatero della Moda.

🚇 Getting Around Milan

  • The ATM metro network (four lines) covers every stop on this itinerary, and a single ticket costs about €2.20 and covers 90 minutes of travel.
  • A rechargeable ATM transit card or a 24-hour pass (~€7) is worth it if you're making more than four trips a day.
  • Central Milan is compact enough to walk between the Duomo, the Galleria, and Brera, saving the metro for Navigli and Sforza Castle.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Pickpocketing is the main risk around the Duomo metro station and on crowded trams, especially during peak tourist hours.
  • Validate paper metro and tram tickets at the machine before boarding; inspectors do fine riders for unvalidated tickets.
  • Jaywalking across tram tracks is both dangerous and mildly frowned upon, wait for the crossing signal like the locals do.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Italian

  • Buongiorno: good morning/hello
  • Grazie mille: thank you very much
  • Il conto, per favore: the bill, please

💳 Money & Payments

  • The euro is the currency, and contactless cards are accepted almost everywhere, including small cafes and metro ticket machines.
  • Carry some small cash for market stalls in Navigli and for the coperto at traditional restaurants that still prefer it.
  • ATMs are widely available, but withdraw from bank-branded machines to avoid the steep fees some standalone kiosks charge.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🥂

Treat aperitivo as dinner

A €10 to €12 aperitivo in Navigli or near the Duomo usually comes with enough small plates to skip a separate sit-down dinner entirely.

🎟️

Buy a 24-hour ATM pass on arrival

If you're taking more than four metro or tram rides in a day, the ~€7 day pass beats paying per ride almost every time.

🖼️

First Sunday museum discounts

State-run sites, including some Sforza Castle museums, offer free or reduced entry on the first Sunday of the month.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

📚

The courtyard of Biblioteca Ambrosiana

A quiet Renaissance library and picture gallery a few minutes from the Duomo, with a fraction of the crowds and one of Leonardo's own notebooks on display.

🕍

Basilica di Sant'Ambrogio over the Duomo queue

Milan's oldest major church, a short metro ride from the center, offers the same medieval atmosphere without the line.

🛶

Darsena at sunrise

The old Navigli basin is calm and nearly empty before 9 AM, a completely different mood from its busy aperitivo evenings.

✈️ Flights

  • Milan Malpensa (MXP) is the main international gateway, connected to the city center by the Malpensa Express train in around 50 minutes.
  • Milan Linate (LIN) is smaller and much closer in, with a bus link into the center in about 25 minutes, convenient for short-haul European flights.
  • A third airport, Bergamo Orio al Serio, serves budget carriers but adds close to an hour of transfer time, worth checking before booking a cheap fare.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Uber operates in Milan alongside licensed white taxis, both easy to book from a phone at any hour.
  • Taxis charge fixed fares from Malpensa and Linate into the city center, worth confirming with the driver before setting off.
  • Within the center, walking or the metro is usually faster than a taxi once you factor in traffic around the Duomo.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • A car isn't needed for this itinerary; every stop from the Duomo to Navigli sits on the compact metro network or within walking distance.
  • Central Milan has a restricted traffic zone (Area C) with a daily entry fee, which makes driving downtown more hassle than it's worth.
  • Parking is scarce and expensive near the center, so skip the rental and save the budget for aperitivo and museum tickets instead.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Around the Duomo: puts Piazza del Duomo, the Galleria, and the Day 1 evening aperitivo within a five-minute walk.
  • Brera: a quieter, art-and-antiques base for Day 4, with easy walking access back to the center.
  • Navigli: a livelier choice with the canal-side aperitivo scene right outside the door, a short metro ride from the main sights.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • Milan is landlocked, so boat trips aren't part of this itinerary and there's no practical boat excursion to add.
  • The closest thing on offer is a short guided boat ride along the historic Navigli canals themselves, a novelty rather than a necessity.
  • If you want real water time, that's better saved for a coastal add-on trip before or after Milan, such as the Italian Lakes or the Amalfi Coast.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Standard Schengen entry rules apply, so non-EU visitors should confirm visa requirements before booking flights.
  • Pick up a rechargeable ATM transit card on arrival at any metro station to avoid buying single tickets for four straight days.
  • Travel insurance is a smart add-on given the pre-booked Duomo rooftop and Pinacoteca di Brera tickets, which are usually non-refundable.
