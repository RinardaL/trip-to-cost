What do the three North Africa trips have in common?

The three trips sit at opposite ends of Africa's northern edge. Two are in Morocco, in the far northwest, and one is in Egypt, in the northeast. What they share is a historic old town or bazaar in the plan: the medinas of Marrakech, Fes and Chefchaouen, and Khan el-Khalili in Cairo.

Marrakech is the simplest to run: five days from one base, with palaces, souks, a hammam, Jardin Majorelle and a day trip into the Atlas Mountains and the Ourika Valley. At $115 a day and $440-700 per person it has the lowest total of the three, and it suits anyone who dislikes repacking each morning.

Fes costs the same $115 a day, but over six days, so $540-840 per person, and it moves every day or two. The plan covers Fes, Chefchaouen and a camel trek into Erg Chebbi, and it is a road trip, with four to four and a half hours allowed between Fes and Chefchaouen on the trip page. Cairo is the longest and dearest, at seven days, $155 a day and $850-1,300 per person, with Cairo, an overnight sleeper train to Luxor and a Nile cruise to Aswan.

When does each trip work best?

The Moroccan trips favour spring and autumn: March to May and October to November for Marrakech, March to May and September to November for Fes. Cairo runs from October to April. Only four months fall inside all three windows: March, April, October and November.

Summer is the reason for the gaps. July highs are roughly 36 to 40°C in Marrakech and about 41°C in Luxor and Aswan, and the Sahara dunes are hotter still. Desert nights can be cold even after a hot day, so pack a warm layer for the Erg Chebbi camp.

If you can only travel in winter, Cairo is the better match, because its season runs through to April. If you can only travel in late spring or early autumn, the two Moroccan plans fit and the Egyptian one does not.

Combining the trips and saving money

Choose one Moroccan trip rather than both, because they overlap on medina and souk time. Fes and Marrakech are separate bases, so doing both also means an extra internal journey that neither itinerary covers. Take Marrakech if you want a single base, and Fes if you want the blue town and the dunes.

Treat Cairo as its own holiday, in its own season. It already fills seven days with a Nile cruise, and the Moroccan trips lie at the far side of the region. Book a timed slot online for the Grand Egyptian Museum on day two, since the trip page says on-site ticket sales have ended.

The easiest saving is length. Marrakech has the lowest total because it is the shortest plan at five days, even though its daily rate matches Fes. Compare total costs rather than daily figures before you choose.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a trip to North Africa cost?

Our 3 North Africa itineraries cost from $440 to $1,300 per person for the whole trip, which is about $115 to $155 a day. Prices cover lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and exclude international flights.

Which North Africa itinerary is the cheapest?

Marrakech ($440-700), Fes ($540-840), Cairo ($850-1,300) are the three lowest, per person and excluding flights.

How many days do I need in North Africa?

Our North Africa itineraries run from 5 to 7 days, and the most common length is 5 days. Each page has a day picker with a price for every length from one day up to the full plan.

When is the best time to visit North Africa?

It depends on the trip, and each itinerary lists its ideal season. For example: Marrakech, Mar – May, Oct – Nov; Fes, Mar – May, Sep – Nov; Cairo, Oct – Apr. The table above shows the ideal season for every North Africa trip.

Is Marrakech or the Fes and Sahara route better for a first visit?

Start with the Marrakech plan if you want one hotel, short transfers and a gentle pace. Choose the Fes, Chefchaouen and Sahara route if you want contrast: a medina maze, a blue town and a night in the desert. The second involves much more driving, and the trip page allows four to four and a half hours for the Fes to Chefchaouen leg alone.

Can I fit Egypt and Morocco into one trip?

Not comfortably. The Egypt plan already uses seven days for Cairo, Luxor and a Nile cruise, and Morocco lies at the opposite end of the region. Plan them as two separate holidays, with Egypt from October to April and Morocco in spring or autumn.
