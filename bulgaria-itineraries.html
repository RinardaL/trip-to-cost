How do Bulgaria's three trips differ?

All three Bulgaria itineraries last four days and begin at Sofia airport, so the choice is about what you want to do with the days. Sofia is a city trip at $115 a day, with Alexander Nevsky Cathedral, Vitosha Boulevard and a hike on Vitosha Mountain. Bansko is a summer mountain trip at $120 a day, and Borovets is a ski trip at $150 a day.

Rila Monastery appears in all three plans: as a day trip from Sofia, a day trip from Bansko, and paired with Samokov on the Borovets itinerary. Whichever you pick, you see it once, so choose by what surrounds it. Sofia and Bansko cost about the same in total, at $360-560 and $370-590, while Borovets runs to $480-700.

City-break fans and first-timers should start with Sofia. Hikers and anyone who prefers cooler mountain air in summer should look at Bansko, with its gondola and Pirin National Park. Skiers should choose Borovets, where the plan includes a day on the pistes, a floodlit evening and a last morning in Sofia.

When should you go?

Sofia lists May to June and September, Bansko June to September, and Borovets December to March. Late spring and early autumn suit the city, summer suits the Pirin hiking, and winter is for skiing. June and September are shared by Sofia and Bansko, so they are the easiest months for combining the two.

Skiing has more moving parts than the other two. The Borovets page lists floodlit skiing on only some evenings of the week and group lessons on limited days, and it says snow can change the schedule, so check the current timetable before you choose an arrival day.

In Bansko, the page advises going up the gondola early because cloud tends to build over the peaks by early afternoon, and it notes that the top station can be 10 to 15 degrees colder than the town, so pack layers even in summer.

Can you combine them, and how do you save money?

Sofia works as the hub. Borovets is about 70 km from the airport, roughly an hour and twenty to an hour and a half by road, and Bansko is about two and a half to three hours by shuttle or bus. A summer plan of Sofia then Bansko gives eight days, and since both include Rila Monastery, swap one of those days for something else.

The metro links Sofia airport with the centre in roughly 20 to 30 minutes, which keeps the first and last day cheap. For Rila, an organised day tour is the easiest option from Sofia or Bansko, according to the pages. For Borovets, a shared shuttle is usually the cheaper door-to-door option for one or two people, while a private car is priced per vehicle and can match it for a group of three or four. Quotes vary by operator, so ask for several and give your flight number. Bulgaria switched to the euro in January 2026, so you no longer need lev.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a trip to Bulgaria cost?

Our 3 Bulgaria itineraries cost from $360 to $700 per person for the whole trip, which is about $115 to $150 a day. Prices cover lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and exclude international flights.

Which Bulgaria itinerary is the cheapest?

Sofia ($360-560), Bansko ($370-590), Borovets ($480-700) are the three lowest, per person and excluding flights.

How many days do I need in Bulgaria?

Our Bulgaria itineraries run from 4 to 4 days, and the most common length is 4 days. Each page has a day picker with a price for every length from one day up to the full plan.

When is the best time to visit Bulgaria?

It depends on the trip, and each itinerary lists its ideal season. For example: Sofia, May – Jun, Sep; Bansko, Jun – Sep; Borovets, Dec – Mar. The table above shows the ideal season for every Bulgaria trip.

Is Rila Monastery included in all three Bulgaria trips?

Yes. It is a day trip from Sofia, a day trip from Bansko, and part of day three at Borovets, paired with Samokov. If you combine two trips, replace one Rila visit with extra time in the city or the mountains.

Do I need a car in Bulgaria?

The itineraries are built around the metro, shuttles, buses and organised day tours, so a car is not required. Sofia's airport has a metro link, Bansko is reached by shuttle or bus, and Borovets by shared or private transfer. Ask for quotes that match your flight, as the Borovets page notes that the road can take longer in snow.
