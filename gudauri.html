4 Days in Tbilisi & Gudauri: Baths, Mountain Road & Skiing

Sulphur baths and khachapuri in Old Tbilisi, the Military Road past Ananuri, then two days of cheap skiing at Gudauri, and Kazbegi as an optional last-day trip if the road is open.

4-day Tbilisi & Gudauri itinerary

How many days do you have?

Morning

Tbilisi Airport (TBS) & into the city

Tbilisi airport (TBS) is about 17 km from the centre. Bus 337 runs to Station Square for roughly 1 GEL (about €0.30) in 45 to 60 minutes, from about 7 AM to 11 PM. A Bolt or Yandex Go ride is about 15 to 35 GEL (€5-12, check current). Skip rank drivers who quote 50 GEL or more.

🚌 ~45-60 min by bus 337, ~25-35 min by taxi 💵 Bus ~1 GEL (~€0.30), app taxi ~15-35 GEL (check current) 🕐 Bus runs about 7 AM to 11 PM, taxis all day and night
Afternoon

Old Tbilisi & the sulphur baths

Walk the Old Town from Meidan Square to Abanotubani, the bath district with brick domes. Private sulphur rooms cost roughly 50 to 150 GEL (€17-50) an hour per room, not per person (check current). The water smells of sulphur, so shower afterwards, drink water and book ahead at weekends. Sioni Cathedral and Narikala fortress are a short walk away.

🚶 ~5-10 min on foot from Meidan Square to the baths 💵 Private room ~50-150 GEL an hour (~€17-50), walking free 🕐 Late afternoon, after check-in; book weekend rooms ahead
Evening

Khachapuri, khinkali & Georgian wine

Try Adjarian khachapuri, a cheese boat with egg, at about 10 to 14 GEL (€3.50-5), and khinkali dumplings at about 1 to 1.50 GEL each in local places (check current). Eat khinkali by hand: hold the top knob, bite, sip the broth and leave the knob. Eat a few streets from the main squares for lower prices.

🚶 Short walk back to your hotel 💵 ~25-40 GEL (~€8-13) for dinner and a glass of wine 🕐 Dinner from about 7 PM, then an early night for the morning van
Morning

Didube to Ananuri Fortress

Marshrutkas to Gudauri leave Didube station when full, from about 9 AM: roughly 15 GEL (€5), cash, about 2.5 hours, fewer in winter (check current). Ananuri, a free fortress on the Zhinvali reservoir about 70 km out, is not an automatic stop: ask the driver, or share a taxi (40 to 60 GEL a seat) and agree one.

🚐 ~1.5 h from Didube to Ananuri, then ~1 h to Gudauri 💵 Marshrutka ~15 GEL, shared taxi ~40-60 GEL a seat (check current) 🕐 Leave Didube by 8 to 9 AM to arrive in daylight
Afternoon

The Jvari Pass road, ski rental & the Pirveli slope

Gudauri is about 120 km from Tbilisi, just below the Jvari Pass at roughly 2,400 m. Winter tyres are compulsory and snow can close the road. Rent skis, boots and a helmet in the village for about 50 to 120 GEL a day (€17-40), less for older kit. The gentle Pirveli slope in Old Gudauri has its own chairlift.

🚐 ~2-2.5 h from Tbilisi in normal conditions 💵 Rental ~50-120 GEL a day (~€17-40), day pass ~70 GEL (check current) 🕐 Lifts usually run about 10 AM to 5 PM in winter (check current)
Evening

Dinner in the village & budget stays

Gudauri restaurants cost roughly 40 to 60% more than Tbilisi: khinkali about 2.50 GEL each, khachapuri about 25 GEL and draft beer about 11 GEL (check current). Supermarket snacks and bottled water are far cheaper. On Fridays and Saturdays the Pirveli lift runs night skiing from 8 to 10 PM, about 30 GEL extra (check current).

🚶 Most hotels and cafés are within a short walk or a short taxi hop 💵 ~35-60 GEL (~€12-20) for dinner and a drink 🕐 Early dinner, as the day started early and the next one is a full ski day
Morning

Beginner runs & a first lesson

Buy the rechargeable lift card (5 GEL deposit, refundable) and a day pass of about 70 GEL (€23); 2026/27 prices are not confirmed (check current). Stay on the green runs by the Pirveli lift and the carpets. A private two-hour lesson starts around 220 GEL with one operator (check current). Flat light is common above the treeline.

🚶 Walk or short taxi to the Pirveli lift in Old Gudauri 💵 Day pass ~70 GEL (~€23), lesson from ~220 GEL (check current) 🕐 First lifts about 10 AM, when the snow is best groomed
Afternoon

Upper runs & the Kobi gondola

Gudauri has about 21 marked runs, over half intermediate, between roughly 2,000 and 3,270 m. Once confident, ride the chairlifts above New Gudauri. The Kobi gondola is included in the pass (45 GEL on its own, check current). Stay on marked runs: off-piste avalanche risk is serious and standard insurance may exclude it.

🚡 Chairlifts and gondola from the village base 💵 Covered by the lift pass, gondola alone ~45 GEL (check current) 🕐 Last lift about 5 PM in winter, so finish with the sun
Evening

Après-ski & a budget dinner

Mountain lunches cost about 30 to 40 GEL (€10-13) with a drink, so save money by buying picnic food at a supermarket. In the evening try a hot Georgian stew such as ojakhuri, about 26 GEL, with a glass of wine (check current). Hang boots and gloves to dry, and ask about tomorrow's road.

🚶 Short walk around the village 💵 ~35-60 GEL (~€12-20) for dinner 🕐 Dinner from 6 PM, and ask your hotel about the road status
Morning

Over the Jvari Pass to Stepantsminda

Ask your hotel about the road first: the Gudauri to Kazbegi stretch is avalanche-prone in winter, and closures can last hours or days. If open, flag a passing Kazbegi marshrutka (if a seat is free) and pay the driver in cash (check current). Stepantsminda is about 30 km on. If it is shut, ski instead.

🚐 ~30 km over the Jvari Pass, past the Friendship Monument 💵 Fare paid in cash to the driver (check current) 🕐 Leave about 8:30 AM to be back in Tbilisi by evening
Afternoon

Gergeti Trinity Church

Gergeti Trinity Church stands on a hill above Stepantsminda. In winter a 4x4 taxi is often needed, at roughly 70 to 100 GEL (€23-33) per vehicle, or walk about 1 to 1.5 hours uphill if the track is packed (check current). Snow can block the track, so go only in clear weather and agree the price first.

🚕 4x4 taxi from the main square, or ~1-1.5 h on foot uphill 💵 4x4 taxi ~70-100 GEL per vehicle (~€23-33, check current) 🕐 Midday, with time to catch an early afternoon van
Evening

Back to Tbilisi & the airport

Marshrutkas from Stepantsminda to Tbilisi cost about 20 GEL (€7) and take 2.5 to 4 hours; winter timetables are shorter, so leave by about 2 PM (check current). From Didube take a Bolt or Yandex Go to the airport for about 15 to 35 GEL, and arrive about 3 hours before an international flight. Book a late or night departure.

🚐 ~3.5 h to Didube, then ~25-40 min to the airport 💵 Marshrutka ~20 GEL, taxi ~15-35 GEL (check current) 🕐 Leave Kazbegi by about 2 PM for a late evening flight

Tbilisi & Gudauri: what to know before you go

💶 Tipping & Service

  • Tipping about 10% is normal in Georgian restaurants. Some add a 10% service charge to the bill, so check it before adding more.
  • Round up app taxi fares to the next lari or two. Small tips of a few lari suit ski instructors, bag handlers and hotel staff.
  • Marshrutka and bus fares are not tipped, but have small notes ready for the driver.

🍷 Eating, Drinking & Toasts

  • Georgian meals are shared. Khachapuri, khinkali, pkhali, lobio and grilled meats arrive for the table, and bread comes with everything.
  • At a supra (feast) a tamada leads the toasts, and glasses are drunk in turn. If you do not drink, say so politely and take a sip.
  • Eat khinkali with your hands, holding the top knob. Leave the knob on the plate and do not use a knife or fork.
  • House wine, often amber or red from a local qvevri, is cheap and good value. Chacha is a strong grape spirit, so sip slowly.

⛷️ Ski Slope Etiquette & Mountain Safety

  • Give way to skiers below you, stop at the edge of a run and check uphill before joining. Helmets are strongly advised.
  • Gudauri is above the treeline and flat light is common. Stay on marked runs and check the lift status before you go up.
  • The lift card turnstiles read the physical card only, not a phone QR code, and passes are non-refundable, including for weather closures.
  • Off-piste skiing carries serious avalanche risk. Ski with a certified guide and avalanche gear, and check that your insurance covers it.

⚠️ Safety, Scams & Etiquette Warnings

  • Check your government travel advice before you go, such as the UK FCDO or US State Department pages on Georgia. They advise against travel to Abkhazia and South Ossetia, which are far from this route. Stay out of border zones near Kazbegi.
  • Taxi drivers outside the airport quote high fares. Use Bolt or Yandex Go, or agree a price before you get in, and avoid unmetered street taxis.
  • Georgian mountain roads are fast, with overtaking on bends. In winter, trucks queue on the Military Road and closures happen, so allow slack before a flight.
  • Health and accident insurance is reported to be required for foreign visitors since 1 January 2026. Confirm the rule on the Georgian foreign ministry site and take cover that includes skiing.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Georgian

  • "Gamarjoba" (hello), "Nakhvamdis" (goodbye), "Madloba" (thank you)
  • "Ki" (yes), "Ara" (no), "Gtkhovt" (please), "Bodishi" (sorry, excuse me)
  • "Ra ghirs?" (how much is it?), "Angarishi, gtkhovt" (the bill, please)
  • "Gaumarjos!" (cheers), "Gemrielia!" (it is delicious)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Georgia uses the lari (GEL). Around 1 GEL is about €0.33 or $0.36, so 100 GEL is about €33 (check current rates).
  • Cards work in most Tbilisi restaurants, hotels and ski hire shops, but carry cash for marshrutkas, small cafés, taxis in the mountains and village shops.
  • Use bank ATMs, which are easy to find in Tbilisi and fewer in Gudauri, so withdraw cash before the mountain. Decline conversion to your home currency and pay in lari.

Insider tips for Tbilisi & Gudauri

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🚌

Take bus 337 and the metro in Tbilisi

Bus 337 from the airport and a metro or bus ride cost about 1 GEL each. Taxis from the airport cost 15 to 35 GEL by app, so the bus saves a few euros each way if you have little luggage.

🎿

Rent older kit and buy a multi-day pass

Newer ski sets cost up to about 120 GEL a day, while older kit in smaller shops costs 60 to 70 GEL. The day pass is about 70 GEL, and 2 days is about 140 GEL (check current).

🥘

Eat local and self-cater on the mountain

Tbilisi lunches cost 15 to 25 GEL at local cafés, but the ski village costs 40 to 60% more. Buy bread, cheese and fruit at a supermarket for picnic lunches and save for a hot dinner.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌆

Climb to Narikala at golden hour

Walk up from the baths to the Narikala fortress for a free view over the Old Town, the Kura river and the city rooftops. Late afternoon light turns the brick domes warm.

🏔️

Stop at the Friendship Monument

The painted Russia-Georgia Friendship Monument overlooks a steep gorge near Gudauri on the road to Kazbegi. It is a good photo stop on a clear morning, but dress warmly in the wind.

🍬

Try churchkhela and Borjomi water

Churchkhela, a string of walnuts dipped in grape juice, is a cheap Georgian snack from street stalls. Borjomi mineral water is a local favourite, though the taste is salty and sulphurous.

Travel services for Tbilisi & Gudauri

✈️ Flights

  • Tbilisi International Airport (TBS) is the main gateway, about 17 km from the centre. Several low-cost airlines and Turkish carriers connect via Istanbul, so compare routes and dates.
  • Kutaisi (KUT) is a second airport with budget flights, about 230 km from Tbilisi. A winter shuttle to Gudauri is reported (check current), but Tbilisi is easier for this plan.
  • Many arrivals and departures are at night, so check that your hotel and the first marshrutka line up with your landing time.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Bolt and Yandex Go work in Tbilisi and show the price before you ride. Cross-town trips are usually a few lari (about 4 to 8 GEL), and the airport is about 15 to 35 GEL.
  • A private transfer from Tbilisi to Gudauri starts around 190 to 200 GEL a car (about 206 GEL from the airport, check current). This is the best option for a group of three or four.
  • Shared taxis from Didube cost about 40 to 60 GEL a seat and leave when full. Taxis in the mountain villages are unmetered, so agree the price first.

🚇 Public Transport & Tickets

  • Tbilisi has a metro, buses and marshrutkas. A single journey costs about 1 GEL with a reloadable card or contactless payment (check current).
  • Marshrutkas to Gudauri and Kazbegi leave from Didube station in northern Tbilisi. Fares are about 15 GEL to Gudauri and about 20 GEL to Kazbegi, and cash is needed. Departures in winter are fewer.
  • Lift cards in Gudauri are rechargeable, with a 5 GEL refundable deposit. Keep the card to get the deposit back.
  • The Kobi gondola links New Gudauri with Kobi and runs through the winter season.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Tbilisi Old Town and Sololaki: the most walkable base for the baths and restaurants, with guesthouse rooms from about 60 to 100 GEL (check current).
  • Marjanishvili or Rustaveli: central and lively, with good metro links and a wide choice of mid-range hotels.
  • Old Gudauri (near the Pirveli lift): best for beginners, with ski-in access to the gentle slope and small guesthouses.
  • New Gudauri: modern apartments and hotels closer to the main gondola and the upper runs, but a longer walk to beginner lifts.
  • Prices at Gudauri rise sharply in January and at weekends, from about €50 a night in quieter periods. Book early for peak weeks.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Local SIM and eSIM plans from Magti, Silknet and Cellfie are cheap and sold at the airport. Check your roaming rates first if you are using an EU plan.
  • Mobile signal is generally good along the Military Road, but can drop in the narrow valleys. Download offline maps before you leave.
  • Withdraw lari at a bank ATM in Tbilisi before heading up, because machines in the mountains can be empty or out of order.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Check the entry rules for your passport, including visa-free stays and the reported mandatory travel insurance (check current).
  • The official Gudauri resort site is gudauri.travel, with lift prices, piste maps and status. The 2026/27 season is expected to start around 20 December (check current).
  • Check the road status with your hotel or driver before leaving. Chains or winter tyres are mandatory and closures at the Jvari Pass happen.
  • You do not need a car. Marshrutkas and shared taxis cover the route, and mountain driving in snow needs experience.
