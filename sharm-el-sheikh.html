4 Days in Sharm el-Sheikh: Reefs, Ras Mohammed & the Old Market

Sun and reefs while Europe is cold: Naama Bay, Ras Mohammed snorkelling, a Tiran boat trip and the Old Market, with honest winter sea temperatures and a summer heat warning.

Ideal SeasonOct – Apr
Est. Budget$300-500
Duration4 Days
PaceRelaxed
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Sharm el-Sheikh Airport & transfer to Naama Bay

Sharm el-Sheikh International (SSH) lies about 10 to 12 km from Naama Bay. Many UK, EU and US passport holders get a free Sinai-only entry stamp (resort area, up to 15 days); a full visa costs about US$25 to 30 (check current). A taxi to Naama Bay is roughly 150 to 250 EGP: agree the fare before getting in.

🚕 ~15 to 25 min by taxi to Naama Bay 💵 ~150-250 EGP taxi (~$3-5), Sinai stamp free for many nationalities (check current) 🕐 Plan around your landing time, then drop your bags
Afternoon

Naama Bay beach & a first swim

Naama Bay is the original Sharm resort strip: a sheltered bay with a pedestrian promenade, cafés and dive shops. Many beaches belong to hotels, so expect a day fee of roughly 150 to 300 EGP (check current) for sunbeds and reef access. In winter the sea is about 21 to 24°C, so a thin wetsuit helps.

🚶 Walk the promenade from the hotel strip 💵 ~150-300 EGP beach day fee (check current) 🕐 Midday to 4 PM, when winter sun is warmest
Evening

The Naama Bay promenade after dark

After dark the promenade fills with lights, shisha cafés and restaurants. Eat a street back from the front, where koshary, grilled fish and shawarma cost far less than hotel-front menus (roughly 250 to 500 EGP, check current). Touts offer menus and tours: a polite 'la, shukran' (no, thank you) is enough. Winter evenings drop to about 14°C.

🚶 On foot along the promenade 💵 ~250-500 EGP (~$5-10) for dinner (check current) 🕐 From 6 PM, when it cools and the lights come on
Morning

Ras Mohammed: the park & the Mangrove Channel

Ras Mohammed is a national park at the southern tip of Sinai, about an hour from Naama Bay. Book a shared bus or boat tour with hotel pickup (roughly US$30 to 70, check current). Entry is about US$5, plus a US$10 marine fee on boat trips, paid in cash. Stay on marked paths and never collect shells or coral.

🚌 ~1 hour by tour bus or boat from Naama Bay 💵 ~$30-70 tour + ~$5 park fee, ~$10 marine fee on boats (check current) 🕐 Pickup is usually early, around 7 to 8 AM
Afternoon

Snorkelling the Ras Mohammed reefs

Snorkelling stops vary by operator, often Yolanda Reef or the Shark Observatory area. The water is clear but about 21 to 24°C in winter, so rent or bring a shorty wetsuit (about US$5 to 10, check current) and wear a vest if you are a weak swimmer. Never stand on or touch coral, and follow your guide.

🚤 Included in the tour, by boat or bus 💵 Wetsuit ~$5-10, usually lunch included (check current) 🕐 Late morning to mid-afternoon, 2 to 3 hours in the water
Evening

Sunset at Ras Um Sid

Back in town, take a taxi to Ras Um Sid, a headland near Hadaba with a lighthouse, a viewing terrace and a popular reef. The El Fanar beach area charges entry (roughly 150 to 200 EGP, check current). The winter sun sets around 5 PM, so arrive by 4:30. Agree the taxi fare first.

🚕 ~15 min by taxi from Naama Bay (agree the fare) 💵 ~150-200 EGP entry (check current) 🕐 Arrive by 4:30 PM in winter for the sunset
Morning

Boat trip to the Straits of Tiran

Full-day boat trips to the Straits of Tiran leave the Sharm marinas around 8 to 9 AM, with pickup. Shared trips with lunch run about US$30 to 75, plus roughly US$10 to 15 in cash for marine and marina fees (check current). In winter the sea is breezy and choppy: wear a windproof layer and take seasickness tablets if prone.

🚤 ~1 hour by boat from the marina to the straits 💵 ~$30-75 trip + ~$10-15 cash fees (check current) 🕐 Pickup around 7 to 8 AM, back about 4 PM
Afternoon

Gordon Reef, the Loullia wreck & Tiran snorkelling

The boat anchors at reefs around Tiran Island, including Gordon Reef, where the rusting wreck of the cargo ship Loullia, which ran aground in 1981, stands above the water. Each snorkelling stop is usually under an hour, and lunch on board is normally included. These are protected reefs: look, do not touch, and do not feed the fish.

🚤 Included in the boat trip 💵 Included (mask and fins may cost ~$5 extra, check current) 🕐 Midday to mid-afternoon, with lunch on board
Evening

The Old Market in Sharm el-Maya

The Old Market in Sharm el-Maya is about 5 km from Naama Bay by taxi (agree the fare first). Stalls sell spices, cotton, shisha pipes and papyrus until late. Bargain: start at a third to half of the first price and be ready to walk away. Supermarkets and pharmacies have fixed prices. Pay in Egyptian pounds for a better rate.

🚕 ~15 min by taxi from Naama Bay 💵 Entry free, souvenirs from ~50 EGP, dinner ~200-400 EGP (check current) 🕐 After 6 PM, open until late
Morning

A morning on the Naama Bay house reef

Spend a slow morning at a day-use beach in Naama Bay, with a mask and snorkel for the house reef, where coral starts close to shore. Day passes at mid-range hotels are often about US$15 to 30, while luxury resorts charge far more (check current). Go before 10 AM, when the water is usually calmer and the beach is quieter.

🚶 Short walk or taxi within Naama Bay 💵 ~$15-30 mid-range day pass (check current) 🕐 Before 10 AM, when it is calmer and quieter
Afternoon

Sharm el-Maya Bay & Al Sahaba Mosque

Take a taxi back to Sharm el-Maya for last souvenirs and a look at Al Sahaba Mosque, a large landmark with twin minarets near the Old Market. Dress modestly, and check before going inside, as access for non-Muslims varies. Buy gifts such as spices, scarves or papyrus here, and bargain the same way as the night before.

🚕 ~15 min by taxi from Naama Bay 💵 Free to look, gifts from ~50 EGP (check current) 🕐 After lunch, before the evening crowds
Evening

Transfer to Sharm el-Sheikh Airport

Leave your hotel at least 3 hours before your flight: security checks at SSH are thorough and queues build at peak charter times. A taxi to the airport costs about 150 to 250 EGP (check current). Spend leftover pounds before you go, and keep your boarding documents and any visa paperwork to hand at the checkpoints.

🚕 ~15 to 25 min by taxi 💵 ~150-250 EGP (check current) 🕐 Leave at least 3 hours before your flight

👗 Dress, Conduct & Religion

  • Resort beaches are relaxed, and swimwear is fine on hotel beaches. In the Old Market, mosques and on the street away from the water, cover your shoulders and knees. Topless sunbathing is not accepted.
  • Keep public displays of affection modest, and ask before photographing local people. Do not photograph police, soldiers or checkpoints.
  • Friday midday is the main prayer time, and some shops close briefly. During Ramadan (the next one starts around February 2027, check the dates) avoid eating, drinking or smoking in the street in daylight; hotels and many restaurants still serve visitors.

🤝 Tipping, Baksheesh & Bargaining

  • Baksheesh (a small tip) is part of daily life. About 50 EGP per bag for a porter and a few pounds for small favours is plenty, and rounding up for a taxi driver is welcome (check current norms).
  • Restaurants often add a service charge of 10 to 12 percent, which may not reach the waiter. Leave an extra 5 to 10 percent in cash on the table for good service. On tours, about US$5 to 10 per person per day for the guide and crew is typical.
  • Bargain in markets and for souvenirs: start at a third to half of the first price, stay friendly and walk away if it is too high. Supermarkets, pharmacies and metered services have fixed prices.

🍽️ Food & Drink Etiquette

  • Try koshary (rice, lentils and pasta with tomato sauce), ful (stewed fava beans), ta'amiya (Egyptian falafel), grilled fish and fresh juice. Street-style places are far cheaper than hotel restaurants.
  • Eat with your right hand when sharing bread or food, and accept tea or coffee when offered. Alcohol is served in hotels and licensed bars but is expensive and not sold in most local shops.
  • Drink bottled water, and avoid ice from unknown places. Ramadan changes opening hours, and many local restaurants open only after sunset.

⚠️ Safety, Marine Rules & Touts

  • Government advice on Sinai differs by area. At the last check (27 October 2026) the UK FCDO advised against all travel to North Sinai and all but essential travel to the northern part of South Sinai, with coastal areas excluded, and did not name Sharm el-Sheikh. Read your own government's current advice before booking and again before flying.
  • Never stand on or touch coral, never feed fish, and follow your guide. Use a buoyancy vest if unsure, watch for currents at reef edges, and swim only where permitted. Wear reef shoes, as sea urchins and stonefish live on shallow reefs.
  • Touts push "free" tours, perfume shops and rides. Book through your hotel or an operator with reviews, and agree taxi fares in EGP before you set off. Check Uber or Careem coverage in the app.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Arabic

  • "Marhaba" (hello), "Sabah el-kheir" (good morning), "Masa el-kheir" (good evening)
  • "Min fadlak" (please, to a man), "Shukran" (thank you), "Afwan" (you're welcome), "La, shukran" (no, thank you)
  • "Bikam da?" (how much is this?), "Ghali awi" (too expensive), "Mish aawiz" (I do not want it)
  • "Fein...?" (where is...?), "El-hesab, min fadlak" (the bill, please), "Yalla" (let's go)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Egypt uses the Egyptian pound (EGP). It was around 50 to 53 EGP to the US dollar in October 2026, but the rate moves, so check it before you travel.
  • Cards work at hotels, larger restaurants and some shops, but cash is needed for taxis, markets, tips and park or marine fees quoted in US dollars or euros. Carry small notes of 10, 20 and 50 EGP for baksheesh.
  • Use bank ATMs inside hotels or banks, withdraw in EGP, and decline conversion to your home currency. Official bank exchange counters give better rates than street changers.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🤿

Bring your own snorkel kit

A mask, snorkel and fins can cost US$5 to 10 per day to rent on tours and at beaches. Your own set pays for itself in two days and fits better, which means fewer leaks and less stress in the water.

🚕

Agree every fare in advance

Taxis are not metered in most resort areas, so agree the fare in Egyptian pounds before you get in. Ask your hotel what locals pay for the same ride, and use a ride app where it works to see the price upfront.

🏷️

Book tours with a reputable local operator

Hotel-desk tours often cost more than the same boat booked with a licensed local operator. Compare two or three prices, check what is included, and keep US dollars or pounds ready for park and marine fees paid in cash.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

Snorkel the house reef at dawn

The reef off the beach is calmer and quieter before the tour boats leave, and fish are active in early light. Go before 9 AM, check conditions with the lifeguard, and wear reef shoes for the entry over rocks.

⛰️

Mount Sinai sunrise, only if advice allows

Overnight tours climb Mount Sinai for sunrise, then visit St Catherine's Monastery, and cost roughly US$25 to 75 (check current). Check your government's advice and ask whether your entry stamp covers the area first. It is cold at the summit in winter.

🌇

Plan sunsets for Hadaba and Ras Um Sid

The headlands near Hadaba look west to the Sinai mountains, and the winter sun sets about 5 PM. A short taxi ride, a drink and the walk back along the cliff-top beat a crowded sunset on the main promenade.

✈️ Flights

  • Sharm el-Sheikh International (SSH) is the only airport for the resort area, about 10 to 12 km from Naama Bay. Many European charter and scheduled flights run in winter, with the best fares outside school holidays.
  • Compare several airlines and nearby dates, since fares to SSH jump around school holidays, Christmas and New Year.
  • Book hand luggage carefully: charter airlines often have strict weight limits.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Taxis in Sharm are commonly blue and white and are rarely metered, so agree the fare in Egyptian pounds before you set off. The airport to Naama Bay is roughly 150 to 250 EGP (check current).
  • Uber and Careem are reported in Sharm, but coverage varies, so check the apps. They show the fare upfront, which helps avoid arguments.
  • Hotel taxi desks and airport touts usually charge more than a street taxi, so ask your hotel what locals pay.

🚌 Public Transport & Tickets

  • There is no metro or tram. Local minibuses and an e-bus network (such as line SH4 to El Fanar beach, check current) run on set routes but are hard to use on a short stay.
  • For short stays, shared tours with hotel pickup and taxis cover most journeys. Ras Mohammed and Tiran are best done on a tour.
  • Pay all fares in cash, ideally with small notes, and keep your tour voucher on your phone.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Naama Bay: central, walkable and lively, with the promenade, dive shops and restaurants on your doorstep.
  • Hadaba and Ras Um Sid: quieter, with good house reefs and cheaper hotels, but you will need taxis for evenings.
  • Sharm el-Maya (Old Market): cheaper and more local, close to the market and the harbour, with fewer beaches.
  • Nabq and Shark's Bay: large beach resorts further out, with good reefs but long, costly taxi rides to the centre.
  • All-inclusive deals are common and can beat paying per meal, but compare prices and check reviews.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Buy a local SIM or eSIM at the airport or online, and check roaming charges with your home operator. Most hotels and many cafés offer Wi-Fi.
  • Carry Egyptian pounds in small notes for taxis, tips and markets, and cash dollars or euros for fees quoted in those currencies.
  • Use ATMs inside banks or hotels, and decline currency conversion.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Check entry rules for your passport before booking. A free Sinai-only stamp covers the Sharm area for many nationalities, but anywhere else in Egypt needs a paid visa or e-visa (check current fees and your nationality).
  • Winter (October to April) is the season: sunny, dry and mild at 22 to 24°C, with the sea at 21 to 24°C and some wind. In summer, highs of 38 to 40°C and over make it extremely hot, with little shade.
  • Read your own government's current travel advice for Sinai before booking and before you fly.
  • Take out insurance that covers snorkelling and boat trips, and keep your hotel's phone number on your phone.
