4 Days in Gran Canaria: Las Palmas, Roque Nublo & the Maspalomas Dunes

Las Canteras beach and old Las Palmas, a mountain day to Roque Nublo and Teror, then the Maspalomas Dunes and Puerto de Mogán: four days of mild winter sun, mostly by bus.

Ideal SeasonYear-round (Nov – Apr for winter sun)
Est. Budget$300-500
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Gran Canaria Airport & the bus to Las Palmas

Gran Canaria Airport (LPA) is about 20 km south of Las Palmas. Global line 60 leaves roughly twice an hour, takes 25 to 30 minutes and costs about €2.30 to San Telmo or €2.95 to Santa Catalina, near Las Canteras (check current). Pay by card or cash on board. Line 5 is the night bus.

🚌 ~25-30 min by Global line 60 to San Telmo or Santa Catalina 💵 ~€2.30-2.95 by bus, taxi ~€25-35 (check current) 🕐 Buses roughly every 30 min through the day, then drop your bags
Afternoon

Las Canteras: the city beach & its natural reef

Las Canteras is a curving city beach of about 3 km. A natural reef, La Barra, breaks the Atlantic swell along the central stretch, so the water there is calmer. In winter the sea is around 19 to 21°C and the air about 21°C, so swimming is brisk. Always check the flag colour: red means stay out.

🚶 Walk the promenade end to end, about 45 min 💵 Free, ~€12-15 for a set lunch (check current) 🕐 Best from midday to 4 PM, the warmest hours in winter
Evening

Sunset on the promenade & tapas

Winter sunset is around 18:00 to 18:30, so walk south along the promenade towards the Auditorio Alfredo Kraus, the concert hall at the end of the bay. Eat in the streets behind the beach, not on the front. Order papas arrugadas with mojo, grilled local fish and queso asado, and expect about €15 to €25.

🚶 ~20 min on foot from Santa Catalina to the Auditorio 💵 Free walk, ~€15-25 for dinner 🕐 Leave 30 min before sunset, dinner from 19:30
Morning

Mercado de Vegueta & Casa de Colón

Vegueta, the old quarter, is about 3.5 km from Las Canteras: a 45-minute walk or short bus or taxi ride. The Mercado de Vegueta sells fruit, cheese and fish, usually from 7:00 to 14:00 and closed on Sundays (check current). Nearby, Casa de Colón is a museum of the Atlantic voyages, about €4 and free on Sundays (check current).

🚌 Short guagua or taxi ride, or ~45 min on foot from Las Canteras 💵 Market free, Casa de Colón ~€4 (check current) 🕐 Market is best before 11:00, museum hours vary
Afternoon

Santa Ana Cathedral & the Triana shopping street

Santa Ana Cathedral stands on Plaza de Santa Ana. Entry is about €8 and covers the interior, the sacred art museum and the tower, with views over the old town (check current). It is open Monday to Friday and shorter on Saturday, with limited visits on Sunday. Then walk 10 minutes to Calle Mayor de Triana, a pedestrian shopping street.

🚶 ~10 min on foot from Casa de Colón, ~10 min more to Triana 💵 Cathedral ~€8, Triana free (check current) 🕐 Cathedral from 10:00, last entry about 17:30 on weekdays
Evening

Parque Santa Catalina & the port quarter

Parque Santa Catalina is the lively square by the port, with cafés, street performers and the start of Las Canteras beach just across the isthmus. Walk Calle Albareda and its side streets for tapas bars and cheap set dinners. Prices rise right on the square, so choose a place a street back. It can feel rough late at night.

🚌 ~15 min by Global or city bus from Vegueta, or taxi ~€6-8 (check current) 💵 Free, ~€15-25 for dinner 🕐 Dinner from 19:30, best before 23:00
Morning

Teror & its Sunday market

Teror is a hill town about 25 km from Las Palmas, reached by Global line 216 from San Telmo (about €2.30, 25 to 30 minutes, check current) or by hire car. The market around the Basilica del Pino runs on Sundays, roughly 8:00 to 15:00. Try spreadable Teror chorizo and look at the wooden balconies on Calle Real.

🚌 ~25-30 min by Global line 216, or ~30 min by car 💵 Bus ~€2.30, hire car ~€30-45 per day (check current) 🕐 Arrive before 10:00, the market is Sunday only
Afternoon

Roque Nublo (reservation required)

Roque Nublo is a 1,813 m basalt monolith. Access from the Degollada de La Goleta needs a free online booking, roughly 9:00 to 17:00 (official site, check current). The trailhead car park is closed: park at Cruz de los Llanos and take the Global line 18 shuttle, about 10 minutes. The walk takes roughly 1.5 to 2 hours return.

🚗 About 1 hr by car from Teror to Cruz de los Llanos (check route), then the shuttle 💵 Free booking, shuttle fare (check current) 🕐 Choose an early-afternoon slot and be off the trail before dusk
Evening

Tejeda village & the drive back

Tejeda is a terraced mountain village, about 15 minutes by road from Cruz de los Llanos. Its almond sweets are the local speciality, usually a few euros a box. Almond trees may blossom from late January. It is cold after dark, about 10°C, and the winding road back to Las Palmas takes roughly 1.25 hours.

🚗 ~15 min by car from Cruz de los Llanos, ~1.25 hr back to Las Palmas 💵 ~€5 for almond sweets and a coffee, fuel ~€10-15 🕐 Leave by 18:00, as the roads are dark and winding
Morning

Maspalomas Dunes, the lighthouse & Playa del Inglés

Take Global line 30 from San Telmo or Santa Catalina to Faro de Maspalomas: about 70 minutes, around €6.15 to €6.80 (check current). The dunes are a protected nature reserve, so stay on marked paths. Fenced areas are closed and fines start at €150. Allow 1.5 hours, then walk the beach towards Playa del Inglés.

🚌 ~70 min by Global line 30 to Faro de Maspalomas 💵 Bus ~€6.15-6.80 each way, camel ride ~€18 (check current) 🕐 Go by 10:00, the wind and sand strengthen after midday
Afternoon

Puerto de Mogán

Puerto de Mogán is a small harbour village of white houses and bougainvillea. From the Faro de Maspalomas bus station, Global line 1 is slow, roughly an hour, and costs around €5 to €6 (check current). Fridays bring a market, usually 9:00 to 15:30. Have a fish lunch by the harbour, about €15 to €25, then stroll the canals.

🚌 ~1 hr by Global line 1 from Maspalomas, ~30 min by taxi 💵 Bus ~€5-6, fish lunch ~€15-25 (check current) 🕐 Arrive by 13:00, Friday for the market
Evening

Bus to the airport

Global line 91 runs from Puerto de Mogán to the airport for about €6.80, roughly an hour, and skips Maspalomas (check current). Check the last bus and leave a long buffer, because mountain roads and traffic can slow the trip. A taxi from Mogán is faster but costs several times more.

🚌 ~1 hr by Global line 91 to the airport 💵 ~€6.80 by bus (check current) 🕐 Arrive at the airport 2 to 3 hours before your flight

💶 Tipping, Hours & Siesta

  • Tipping is not expected in Spain. Rounding up the bill or leaving 5 to 10 percent for good table service is generous, and nothing is added by default.
  • Many small shops and offices close for a long lunch break, often from about 14:00 to 17:00, and shut on Sundays. Tourist areas and large shopping centres stay open.
  • Dinner starts later than in northern Europe. Kitchens in tourist areas open from about 18:30 to 19:30, but locals eat around 21:00.

🥔 Local Food & Drink

  • Papas arrugadas are small salty wrinkled potatoes, served with mojo, a red or green sauce. Queso asado is grilled local cheese, often with mojo or palm honey. Gofio is toasted flour used in stews and desserts.
  • Try ron miel, a honey rum, and the barraquito, a layered coffee with condensed milk, liqueur and lemon. Teror chorizo is a spreadable red sausage.
  • A set menu del día at lunch is the cheapest way to eat well, often around €10 to €15. Bread and sauces on the table may be charged, so check the menu.

🌤️ Winter Weather: What to Expect

  • Las Palmas is mild in winter, with highs around 21°C and lows around 15°C. The sea is about 19 to 21°C, so swimming is brisk rather than warm. There are about 6 to 7 hours of sun a day.
  • The south coast around Maspalomas is usually sunnier and a little warmer, while the north can be cloudier. Trade winds are common, and sand gets blown around on dune days.
  • The mountains are much cooler. Tejeda and Roque Nublo can be 8 to 12°C with wind and cloud, so bring layers. Showers are few, but expect some in the interior.

⚠️ Safety, Waves, Sun & Dune Etiquette

  • Atlantic waves and currents can be strong. Obey the lifeguard flags: green is fine, yellow means be careful, red means do not swim. Many open beaches have undertow, so swim in the sheltered section behind La Barra at Las Canteras.
  • The sun is strong even in winter, and the wind hides it. Use SPF 30 to 50 and a hat, especially on the dunes.
  • The Maspalomas Dunes are a protected reserve. Stay on marked paths, respect fences, do not fly a drone without permission and do not build stone messages. Fines start at €150.
  • Rental cars are targeted at viewpoints and trailheads. Leave nothing visible and never leave passports or bags in the car. Check the UK FCDO or US State Department travel advice for Spain, and call 112 in an emergency.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Spanish

  • "Hola" (hello), "Buenos días" (good morning), "Buenas tardes" (good afternoon or evening), "Adiós" (goodbye)
  • "Por favor" (please), "Gracias" (thank you), "De nada" (you're welcome), "Perdone" (excuse me)
  • "Una mesa para dos, por favor" (a table for two, please), "La cuenta, por favor" (the bill, please)
  • "¿Habla inglés?" (do you speak English?), "¿Dónde está...?" (where is...?), "¿Cuánto cuesta?" (how much is it?)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Spain uses the euro. Cards and contactless work in most hotels, shops and restaurants, and on Global buses, but carry some cash for markets, small bars and kiosks.
  • Use a bank-branded ATM and avoid standalone exchange booths. If a terminal offers to charge you in your home currency, choose euros.
  • The Canary Islands charge IGIC, a lower indirect tax than mainland VAT, and the price on the menu or tag normally includes it.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🍽️

Eat the menu del día

Many local restaurants serve a set lunch of a starter, main and drink for around €10 to €15, far less than the same dishes in the evening. Make lunch your main meal and keep dinner to tapas.

🚌

Pay by card on the bus, and watch the fare

Global buses accept cards and contactless payments, but tickets are charged by distance between fare stops. The TransGC rechargeable card gives a bonus on the top-up (check current terms) and pays off if you ride a lot.

🚗

Hire a car for one day only

The coast is easy by bus, but the mountains are not. A one-day hire for Teror, Roque Nublo and Tejeda is usually about €30 to €45 plus fuel (check current), far cheaper than a week with a car you barely use.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

Go early to the dunes

The Maspalomas Dunes are quietest and the light is softest early in the morning. By midday the wind picks up, and the paths fill with tour groups.

🌸

Time Tejeda for the almonds

Late January to February can bring almond blossom around Tejeda, though the exact peak changes each year. Ask at the tourist office or check local news before you go.

🥾

Book the Roque Nublo slot early

Access is limited per hour, and slots in dry weather fill up. Book a day or two ahead on the official site, and choose early afternoon so that you are off the trail before dusk.

✈️ Flights

  • Gran Canaria Airport (LPA) is on the east coast, about 20 km south of Las Palmas and about 25 km from Maspalomas, with direct flights from many European cities in winter.
  • Global line 60 goes to Las Palmas for about €2.30 to €2.95 (check current), and line 66 serves Playa del Inglés and Maspalomas for about €3.50 to €4.05.
  • Winter is high season for northern Europeans, so book flights and Las Palmas rooms a month or two ahead for the best price.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Official taxis are white and use a meter in town, with fixed fares from the airport to some destinations. Ask for the price before you set off.
  • A taxi from the airport to Maspalomas costs about €45 (check current), and ride-hailing apps such as Cabify and Uber may work in Las Palmas (check current coverage).
  • Taxis help for late arrivals and for Roque Nublo hikers who miss a shuttle, but they are expensive for long trips.

🚌 Public Transport & Tickets

  • Global runs island buses (guaguas), and the city has its own Guaguas Municipales. Pay by card, contactless or cash on board, and ask for exact change (notes over €20 are not accepted).
  • Useful lines: 60 airport to Las Palmas, 30 Las Palmas to Maspalomas, 216 to Teror, 18 for the Roque Nublo shuttle and 91 for Puerto de Mogán and the airport (check timetables).
  • The TransGC card is a rechargeable prepaid card that gives a bonus on top-ups. Check Global's fares page for current terms.
  • Services are thinner on Sundays and holidays, so check timetables before an early flight or a day in the mountains.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Las Canteras and Santa Catalina: the best base for beach, restaurants and buses to the airport and Maspalomas, and fine in winter.
  • Vegueta and Triana: calm, historic and good for old-town days, but a longer walk to the beach.
  • Playa del Inglés and Maspalomas: warm and sunny with resort hotels, but a long bus ride from the old town and quiet in the evenings.
  • Puerto de Mogán: small and pretty, but expensive and remote for a short trip.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Spain is in the EU, so EU SIM holders roam at home rates. Other visitors can use an eSIM or a prepaid SIM, and many cafés and hotels offer Wi-Fi.
  • Mountain roads, especially around Tejeda and Roque Nublo, can have weak signal, so download offline maps.
  • Cards work almost everywhere, but carry cash for markets and small bars. Use bank-branded ATMs.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Check your passport's Schengen rules before booking, including whether ETIAS applies on your travel dates.
  • Book Roque Nublo on the official site and check parking and shuttle rules again before you go, since they have changed recently.
  • Hire cars are cheapest booked ahead, especially in December, and local firms such as Cicar are often good value (check current terms).
  • If you can choose your dates, make Day 3 a Sunday, when the Teror market runs. Mountain roads are narrow and winding, so allow extra time and fill the tank before leaving the coast.

How much does a 4-day trip to Gran Canaria cost?

About $300-500 per person for 4 days, roughly $75-125 a day, excluding international flights. The estimate covers lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and real costs vary with the season and your spending habits.
