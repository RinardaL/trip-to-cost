4 Days in Quebec City: German Christmas Market, Château Frontenac & Montmorency Falls

Mulled wine at the German Christmas Market, a toboggan run on Dufferin Terrace, Petit-Champlain lights and snow at Montmorency Falls: four winter days in Quebec City.

Ideal SeasonNov – Dec (Christmas Markets), Jun – Sep
Est. Budget$700-1,000
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival & check-in in Vieux-Québec

Québec City Jean Lesage International Airport is about 20 minutes west of the Old Town, with no rail link. The tourism office lists a flat taxi fare of $41.40 CAD to the city centre, and RTC bus 80 runs every 30 minutes to Saint-Roch. In December allow extra time for snow and check your flight status.

🚕 ~20 min taxi/rideshare from YQB, longer in snow 💵 $41.40 CAD flat taxi fare (~$30 USD), or $4.00 CAD on RTC bus 80 (exact change) 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time, and allow extra for snow
Afternoon

Château Frontenac, Dufferin Terrace & the toboggan slide

The Fairmont Le Château Frontenac has defined this skyline since 1893; step into the lobby, then walk the Dufferin Terrace boardwalk for views over the St. Lawrence River. The terrace also holds the Au 1884 toboggan slide, listed from 4 December 2026 to 21 March 2027 (dates to be confirmed). Sunset comes before 4:30 PM in December.

🚶 Central, inside the Old Town walls 💵 Free to walk the terrace, slide tickets sold separately (check au1884.ca) 🕐 Early afternoon, while there is daylight
Evening

German Christmas Market & Rue du Trésor

The Marché de Noël allemand fills Place de l'Hôtel-de-Ville, Place d'Armes and Rue Sainte-Anne in Vieux-Québec, with free entry. The 2026 season is listed as 19 November to 23 December: Thursday to Saturday 11:00 to 21:00 and Sunday 11:00 to 18:00. One listing still shows dates as to be confirmed, so check. Then browse Rue du Trésor.

🚶 On Rue Sainte-Anne and Place d'Armes, inside the walls 💵 Free entry, pay per drink or snack 🕐 After dark for the lights; closes 21:00 Thursday to Saturday, 18:00 on Sunday
Morning

The Fortifications & the Citadelle in winter

Walk a stretch of the 4.6 km of walls that make Old Quebec the only fortified city north of Mexico, then continue to the star-shaped Citadelle. It can only be seen on a guided tour of about 60 minutes, mostly outdoors, so dress for the cold. Fall-winter hours are listed as 10:00 to 17:30. Stairs and ramparts can be icy.

🚶 ~10 min walk from the Upper Town 💵 $22 CAD adult admission, includes the guided tour and the Royal 22e Régiment Museum 🕐 Morning; open 10:00 to 17:30, closed 25 December
Afternoon

Funicular & Breakneck Stairs down to Petit-Champlain

The Old Quebec Funicular, running year-round, drops you into the Lower Town in about a minute. The 59 steps of the Casse-Cou Breakneck Stairs are free and look best in snow, but take the funicular instead when they are icy. At the bottom, Quartier Petit-Champlain is a cobblestone shopping street decorated with lights in December.

🚡 Funicular ~1 min, or 59 free steps 💵 $7 CAD one-way funicular (cash only), stairs free 🕐 Early-to-mid afternoon, while the daylight and the choirs last
Evening

Place Royale's tree, tourtière & hot chocolate

After dark, the giant decorated tree on Place Royale is the centre of the Lower Town. For dinner, order tourtière, the spiced meat pie, or ragoût de boulettes, a meatball stew. Aux Anciens Canadiens serves Québécois classics, so check holiday menus and book ahead. Finish with hot chocolate at Le Cochon Dingue in Petit-Champlain.

🚶 Central within Petit-Champlain and Place Royale 💵 ~$30-40 CAD dinner (check menus) 🕐 After dark, and book ahead in December
Morning

The Plains of Abraham in the snow

The Plains of Abraham is a free snow park in winter, with a skating rink, about 13.9 km of groomed cross-country ski trails, snowshoe and walking trails, and sliding hills, with rentals at the skaters' chalet. Opening dates depend on snow and ice, so check current conditions. The Musée national des beaux-arts du Québec is the indoor backup.

🚶 ~10 min walk from the Old Town walls 💵 Free access to the park, trails and rink; skate, ski and snowshoe rental extra (check current) 🕐 Morning, then the falls while there is still daylight
Afternoon

Montmorency Falls in winter

Montmorency Falls, just 15 minutes from downtown, drops 83 metres into the St. Lawrence. In winter the spray freezes into an ice cone at the base, usually biggest from January to March. Paid services such as the cable car run on seasonal hours, so check Sépaq. Take bus 800 toward Beauport to the des Rapides stop.

🚌 RTC bus 800 toward Beauport (des Rapides stop), or ~15 min by car 💵 Outdoor parking free; check Sépaq for cable car and activity fares 🕐 Early afternoon, before the early sunset
Evening

Skating at Place d'Youville

Place d'Youville, a square backed by the fortifications, becomes an outdoor skating rink in winter. Skating is free, skates can be rented on site, and the rink is listed as open from mid-November to mid-March, with hours that change on holidays, so check the city's page first. Afterwards, have a hot chocolate in the Old Town.

🚶 Beside the fortifications, a short walk from the Upper Town 💵 Free skating, skate rental extra (check current) 🕐 Early evening; holiday hours vary
Morning

Crossing to Île d'Orléans in winter

Île d'Orléans is about 15 minutes by car over the Pont de l'Île bridge, with six small villages along a rural island. There is no public transit, so you need a car, a taxi day rate or a guided tour. Go slowly on Chemin Royal in snow. Quebec-registered vehicles, rentals included, need winter tires from 1 December to 15 March.

🚗 ~15 min drive from Old Quebec, longer after snow 💵 Rental car or guided tour needed 🕐 Morning, to fit several stops before the early dusk
Afternoon

Ice cider, chocolate & a village Christmas market

Ice cider, made from frozen apples or juice, is the Québec winter specialty. Cidrerie Verger Bilodeau in Saint-Pierre offers tastings and is listed as open 9:00 to 18:00 daily, with variable hours from January and closures on 25 and 26 December. The Chocolaterie de l'Île d'Orléans in Sainte-Pétronille sells truffles and maple butter. Call ahead.

🚗 Self-drive along Chemin Royal 💵 Tasting and shop prices vary (check current) 🕐 Early-to-mid afternoon, and call ahead for winter hours
Evening

Transfer to Jean Lesage Airport for departure

Return your rental car or catch a pre-booked taxi, budgeting the same 20 minutes from the Old Town as on arrival, plus extra time for snow and the car drop-off. Winter storms can delay flights and de-icing adds waiting time, so check your flight status the night before and leave a buffer.

🚕 ~20 min taxi/rideshare to YQB, longer in snow 💵 $41.40 CAD flat taxi fare (check current) 🕐 Leave at least 2.5 hrs before a domestic flight, 3 hrs for international, more in a storm

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Unlike much of Europe, tipping is expected in Canada: 15-20% at sit-down restaurants is standard, not optional.
  • Card machines are everywhere and prompt for a tip percentage automatically when you pay.
  • A few dollars is appropriate for a Citadelle tour guide or a farm tasting, though it isn't strictly required.

🧥 Winter Dress, Footwear & Safety

  • Dress for well below freezing, with wind chill off the St. Lawrence: a warm, windproof coat, thermal layers, hat, gloves and a scarf. Daylight is short, with sunset before 4:30 PM in December.
  • Old Quebec is genuinely hilly, and cobblestones, sidewalks, the Casse-Cou Stairs and the ramparts can be icy. Wear insulated boots with deep grip, and consider slip-on ice cleats.
  • Notre-Dame-des-Victoires and other active churches expect modest, covered dress, though there's no strict enforcement.

🚶 Getting Around Quebec City

  • The Old Town is compact enough to walk end to end in under 30 minutes, but allow extra time on snowy sidewalks. The funicular runs year-round.
  • RTC city buses cover Montmorency Falls and the wider city for $4.00 CAD a ride, exact change, and check live departure times because snow can slow service.
  • Île d'Orléans has no public transit, so a rental car, taxi day-rate, or guided tour is the only way to see the island. Vehicles registered in Quebec must have winter tires from 1 December to 15 March.

🎄 Christmas Season: Book Early, Check Dates

  • The German Christmas Market is listed for 19 November to 23 December 2026, Thursday to Sunday plus 21, 22 and 23 December (check the official dates). It is closed from 24 December, and street parking near it fills fast on weekends and evenings, so walk or take the bus.
  • Most indoor attractions and museums are closed on 25 December and 1 January. The Citadelle and Érico are closed on 25 December, Cidrerie Verger Bilodeau on 25 and 26 December, and Montmorency Falls offers no services on 25 December. Restaurants and hotels fill up around Christmas, so book early.
  • The Winter Carnival and the Hôtel de Glace belong to the new year: the ice hotel is listed to reopen on 4 January 2027, so neither is part of a December trip.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in French

  • Bonjour: hello (the standard Quebec greeting, day or night)
  • Merci: thank you
  • L'addition, s'il vous plaît: the bill, please
  • Un vin chaud, s'il vous plaît: a mulled wine, please
  • Joyeux Noël: Merry Christmas

💳 Money & Payments

  • The Canadian dollar (CAD) is the currency; contactless and tap cards are accepted almost everywhere. Carry some cash, because the funicular takes cash only and some market stalls or farm stops may too.
  • Withdraw CAD from bank-branded ATMs rather than standalone tourist machines, which carry steep fees.
  • Sales tax (about 15% combined) is added at the register, not included in listed prices, so budget a bit above the sticker total.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🪜

Take the free Casse-Cou stairs instead of the funicular

The "Breakneck Stairs" run parallel to the funicular between Upper and Lower Town and cost nothing, a five-minute walk that saves the $7 CAD one-way fare. If they are icy, pay for the funicular instead.

🚗

Rent a car for one day only

The Old Town needs no vehicle at all, so book a rental car just for the Île d'Orléans day rather than for the whole trip, and use taxis or your own feet the rest of the time. Confirm winter tires when you book.

🍽️

Eat your big Québécois meal at lunch

Many Old Town restaurants run a table d'hôte lunch menu at a noticeably lower price than the identical dinner plates, so make lunch the splurge and dinner the light meal.

⛸️

Skate for free, rent only the skates

The rinks at Place d'Youville and on the Plains of Abraham let you skate for free, so you only pay for skate rental (check current prices). The German Christmas Market is free to enter, too, and bringing your own mug is encouraged.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

Petit-Champlain on a weekday morning

The roaming choirs and circus acts run in the afternoon (1:00 to 4:30 PM on listed days in 2025), so the street is calmer in the morning before they start. Check the 2026 programme.

🏰

The Martello towers on the Plains

Four squat 19th-century defense towers sit scattered across the Plains of Abraham park, largely ignored by visitors headed straight for the museum or the battlefield markers.

🚶

Walk the ramparts near Porte Saint-Jean

The stretch of fortification wall near this gate gets a fraction of the foot traffic of Dufferin Terrace, with the same views over the rooftops of the Upper Town. Watch for ice on the steps.

🎄

Visit the market on a Thursday or Friday

The German Christmas Market opens Thursday to Sunday, and street parking fills fastest on weekends and evenings, a good sign that these are the busiest times. A Thursday or Friday afternoon is the easier visit.

✈️ Flights

  • Québec City Jean Lesage International Airport (YQB) is small and about 20 minutes from the Old Town, but direct international routes are limited.
  • Connecting through Montréal-Trudeau (YUL) and continuing by a short flight, train, or roughly 3-hour drive (longer in snow) is often cheaper than flying direct into YQB from outside North America.
  • Winter storms can delay or cancel flights and de-icing adds waiting time, so keep a buffer day if you have a tight connection, and check flight status before you leave for the airport.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Uber operates in Quebec City alongside licensed taxis, both reliable for the airport transfer and evenings out. The tourism office lists a flat taxi fare of $41.40 CAD between the airport and the city centre (check current).
  • RTC bus 80 runs from the airport to Saint-Roch every 30 minutes, with connections to Old Québec; the fare is $4.00 CAD, exact change.
  • Inside the walls, you'll rarely need either; the Old Town is walkable end to end.

🚌 Day-Trip Transport

  • Montmorency Falls: RTC bus 800 reaches the park (des Rapides stop), no car needed.
  • Île d'Orléans: no public transit crosses the bridge, so a rental car, private driver, or guided tour is required for Day 4. Rentals in Quebec must have winter tires from 1 December to 15 March, so confirm that when you book.
  • Guided Île d'Orléans tours are a good option if you'd rather not drive on snowy roads yourself, but check which tours run in winter.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Upper Town (Vieux-Québec intra-muros): puts Château Frontenac, Dufferin Terrace, the German Christmas Market sites and Day 1's evening within a five-minute walk.
  • Petit-Champlain / Lower Town: closer to Day 2's afternoon, with a livelier, slightly less formal feel.
  • Grande Allée or outside the walls: a short walk or bus ride from the Old Town, generally at a lower nightly rate. Book early for the weeks around Christmas.

🎫 Guided Tours

  • The Citadelle can only be visited by guided tour (about 60 minutes, mostly outdoors, $22 CAD for adults including the regimental museum), run on a fixed schedule through the day.
  • Walking tours of the Old Town may run on a reduced schedule in winter, so check which departures operate before you plan Day 1 around one.
  • Île d'Orléans cidery and farm tours bundle several stops with tastings included, useful if you don't want to plan the route yourself.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Standard Canadian entry rules apply; confirm visa or eTA requirements for your passport before booking flights.
  • Remember the Canadian dollar (CAD), not the US dollar, is the local currency, and combined sales tax adds about 15% at checkout.
  • Travel insurance is a smart add-on in winter, since storms can disrupt flights and some tours and tickets are non-refundable. Most indoor attractions are closed on 25 December and 1 January.
