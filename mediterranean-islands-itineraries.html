What do these five islands have in common?

This hub covers five trips in three countries: Malta, Greece and Spain. They share a sea, but the holidays themselves are very different. Valletta is a fortified capital of harbours and Baroque streets, Crete is a larger island of ruins, plateaus and gorge hiking, and Milos, Mykonos and Formentera are beach and boat trips.

Cost per day is the quickest filter. Formentera is the cheapest at $135 a day, with four days for $430-650 per person. Valletta and Crete both run at $165 a day, but Valletta is three days at $380-600 per person while Crete is six days at $850-1,150 per person. Milos at $265 a day and Mykonos at $260 a day sit at the top, at $900-1,200 per person for four days and $1,100-1,500 per person for five.

Choose Valletta for history and a short break, Crete for variety and walking, Formentera for a quiet stay with a bike, and Milos or Mykonos if sea caves, beach clubs and boat days matter more than the budget.

When is the best time to go?

The five trips split into two groups. Valletta, Formentera and Crete are listed for the shoulder months: Valletta from April to June and September to October, Formentera from May to June and September to October, and Crete from May to June and September. Milos and Mykonos are summer trips, planned for June to September.

Crete's window skips July and August, the hottest part of the summer. Day 5 of that trip is the Samaria Gorge, a 16-kilometre full-day hike. The gorge is open from about May to October, depending on conditions, so the listed months fit it well. June and September are the two months that fall inside the ideal season of all five trips, so they are the safest choice if you are undecided. If you can only travel in midsummer, Milos and Mykonos are the two itineraries built for it.

How do you get there and plan the budget?

Islands add a transport leg, so plan the arrival first. Formentera has no airport: you fly to Ibiza, take a bus to the port and then a fast ferry of about 30 minutes to La Savina, and the trip advises booking that ferry ahead in July and August. Milos is reached by ferry from Piraeus, which takes roughly 2.5 to 3.5 hours on a high-speed boat and four hours or more on a conventional sailing.

Milos and Mykonos are both in the Cyclades. Valletta, Crete and Formentera are in different countries and are best taken as separate trips. The Formentera itinerary is built around bike hire at La Savina rather than a car, and with the lowest daily figures in this hub, Formentera and then Valletta are the places to start if your budget is tight.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a trip to the Mediterranean islands cost?

Our 5 Mediterranean Islands itineraries cost from $380 to $1,500 per person for the whole trip, which is about $135 to $265 a day. Prices cover lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and exclude international flights.

Which Mediterranean Islands itinerary is the cheapest?

Valletta ($380-600), Formentera ($430-650), Crete ($850-1,150) are the three lowest, per person and excluding flights.

How many days do I need in the Mediterranean islands?

Our Mediterranean Islands itineraries run from 3 to 6 days, and the most common length is 4 days. Each page has a day picker with a price for every length from one day up to the full plan.

When is the best time to visit the Mediterranean islands?

It depends on the trip, and each itinerary lists its ideal season. For example: Valletta, Apr – Jun, Sep – Oct; Crete, May – Jun, Sep; Milos, Jun – Sep. The table above shows the ideal season for every Mediterranean Islands trip.

Do I need to book ferries in advance?

For Formentera, the trip advises booking the crossing from Ibiza ahead in July and August. Milos is reached by ferry from Piraeus, and journey times vary by vessel and operator. Timetables change by season, so check current sailings before you book flights and accommodation.

Which islands suit sightseeing more than beaches?

Valletta and Crete. Valletta offers fortifications, harbours and a day out to Mdina and Rabat, while Crete combines Knossos, the Lasithi Plateau, Rethymno's old town and the Samaria Gorge hike. The other three trips are built mainly around beaches and boat days.
