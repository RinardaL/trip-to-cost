4 Days in Zhangye: Rainbow Mountains, Silk Road Oasis & Giant Buddha

Striped red, orange and yellow sandstone ridges ripple across the high desert of Gansu province at the Zhangye Danxia Rainbow Mountains, the centerpiece of a four-day loop through this old Silk Road oasis town. Four days is enough to catch the Danxia colors at both sunrise and sunset, wander the walled old town and its Silk Road-era guild halls, see Asia's largest reclining Buddha at the Giant Buddha Temple, cool off in an unexpected wetland park, and take a half-day trip out to the cliff-carved grottoes of Mati Temple.

Ideal SeasonJun – Sep
Est. Budget$500-700
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival by rail into the Hexi Corridor

Zhangye sits midway along the Hexi Corridor, the historic Silk Road passage between Lanzhou and Dunhuang, and is easiest to reach by high-speed rail rather than by air. Trains on the Lanzhou–Xinjiang line reach Zhangye in about two hours from Lanzhou, with connections onward from Xi'an; the small local airport handles only a handful of domestic routes, so most travelers fly into Lanzhou first and continue by train.

🚄 ~2 hr high-speed train from Lanzhou 💵 ~¥100-150 second-class ticket 🕐 Aim to arrive by early afternoon
Afternoon

The Drum Tower and the walled old town

The Ming-dynasty Drum Tower, known locally as Zhenyuan Lou, stands at the exact crossroads where the old city's four main streets meet, each once used by camel caravans passing through on the Silk Road. Climb up for the view down all four streets, then wander the surrounding lanes past preserved merchant guild halls built by traders who settled here centuries ago.

🚶 Walkable from most old-town hotels 💵 Free to view from outside 🕐 Late afternoon, before dinner
Evening

Dinner: Zhangye's own noodle culture

Gansu's hand-pulled noodle culture runs deep here: look for niurou xiaofan, a Zhangye specialty of diced beef and noodle squares in a peppery broth, or malatang, the spicy hot-pot noodle soup some food historians trace back to this stretch of the Hexi Corridor.

🚶 Old-town noodle houses 💵 ~¥20-35 per bowl 🕐 Most kitchens open until around 9 PM
Morning

Sunrise at the Colorful Sea of Clouds Platform

The park's shuttle buses start running before dawn for a reason: low-angle morning light brings out the mineral banding best, and Platform 1, nicknamed the Colorful Sea of Clouds Platform, gives the widest panorama of the striped hills with the snow-capped Qilian Mountains often visible on the horizon. Private cars aren't allowed past the gate, so the whole park is seen by hopping the official shuttle between numbered platforms.

🚌 Shuttle-bus-only inside the park 💵 Entry ~¥74, valid 2 days 🕐 Gates open around 5:30 AM in summer
Midday

Boardwalks across Platforms 2 and 3

Wooden boardwalks link the middle platforms, keeping foot traffic off the soft sandstone that took millions of years of mineral deposits and erosion to form its stripes. Midday sun flattens the colors compared to the golden hours, so treat this stretch as a chance to cover ground and scout the next platform for sunset rather than a color peak.

🚌 Free shuttle between platforms 💵 Included in park entry 🕐 Late morning into early afternoon
Evening

Sunset at Platform 4

Platform 4 is generally regarded as the park's best sunset viewpoint, where the reds and oranges deepen to almost crimson as the sun drops. Budget time to catch one of the last shuttles back to the gate, since service tapers off shortly after closing.

🚌 Last shuttles run near closing 💵 Included in park entry 🕐 The hour before sunset, roughly 7-8 PM in summer
Morning

The Giant Buddha Temple complex

Built in 1098 during the Western Xia dynasty, the Giant Buddha Temple, or Dafo Si, is the largest surviving Western Xia-era building in Gansu and one of the oldest continuously used religious sites on this stretch of the Silk Road. Marco Polo is said to have stayed in Zhangye for over a year and to have been struck by the temple's scale, according to accounts drawn from his travel writing.

🚶 Central, walkable from the old town 💵 Entry ~¥41 🕐 Morning, before tour groups arrive
Midday

Asia's largest reclining Buddha

The main hall houses a 34.5-meter clay-and-wood reclining Buddha, the largest of its kind in Asia, its shoulders alone spanning some 7.5 meters. Photography is generally restricted inside the hall itself, so take time to look rather than shoot before moving on to the temple's smaller side halls and its Ming-dynasty sutra library.

🚶 Inside the main temple hall 💵 Included in entry 🕐 Allow 45 min to an hour
Evening

An afternoon in Zhangye Wetland Park

An unexpected patch of green fed by underground springs sits just outside the city center, its reed marshes and channels laced with boardwalks that make for an easy, cooler counterpoint to a day spent among desert sandstone and temple halls. Birdwatchers can spot herons and other wetland species, especially toward dusk.

🚕 Short taxi ride from downtown 💵 Entry ~¥30 🕐 Late afternoon into early evening
Morning

The drive out to Mati Temple

About 65 kilometers south of the city, Mati Temple's Buddhist grottoes are carved directly into a sheer cliff face, some dating back over 1,600 years, with the Qilian Mountains rising behind. The complex is named for a hoofprint-shaped indentation in the rock, said in local legend to belong to a heavenly horse.

🚗 ~1.5 hr drive south of Zhangye 💵 Entry ~¥60 🕐 Depart by mid-morning
Midday

Climbing the grottoes

Narrow stairways and tunnels carved straight into the rock link the different grotto levels, opening onto small shrine chambers and, near the top, sweeping views back down the valley. Some passages are tight and low-ceilinged, so this stop rewards a bit of scrambling over a leisurely stroll.

🚶 Steep stairs, moderate fitness required 💵 Included in entry 🕐 Late morning, allow 1.5-2 hrs
Afternoon

Return to Zhangye & departure

Head back into the city in the early afternoon with time to spare before an onward train; Zhangye's station connects west toward Jiayuguan and Dunhuang or east back toward Lanzhou and Xi'an, making it an easy hinge point for a longer Gansu or wider Silk Road trip.

🚗 ~1.5 hr drive back to Zhangye 💵 ~¥100-150 onward train ticket 🕐 Leave at least 2 hrs before departure

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Tipping isn't customary anywhere in mainland China, including Zhangye, and can occasionally cause confusion if offered.
  • Restaurant bills are settled as printed, with no service charge or gratuity line to fill in.
  • Guides and drivers on private day trips, like the run out to Mati Temple, don't expect a tip, though it isn't refused for exceptional service.

👗 Dress Code

  • Modest, practical clothing works best at the temple complexes, cover shoulders and knees out of respect when entering the Giant Buddha Temple's halls.
  • Photography of the reclining Buddha itself is generally prohibited inside the main hall, save the camera for the temple's courtyards and exterior.
  • Sun-protective clothing matters more than fashion at the Danxia platforms, there's no shade anywhere across the geopark.

🚶 Getting Around Zhangye

  • Private cars, including rental cars and tour vans, aren't allowed inside the Danxia Geopark itself, everyone moves between platforms on the park's own shuttle buses.
  • Didi, China's main rideshare app, covers Zhangye's city center reliably, though a China-registered phone number is usually needed to book.
  • Taxis are inexpensive and easy to flag for short hops; few drivers speak English, so having your destination written in Chinese characters helps.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Stick to marked boardwalks and platforms inside the geopark, the sandstone is soft and prone to erosion, and climbing off-trail damages formations rangers actively work to protect.
  • Sun exposure is the main real risk here, the high-desert sun at the shadeless viewing platforms burns faster than it feels.
  • Gansu has a significant Hui Muslim population and visible mosque architecture in and around Zhangye's old town, dress and behave respectfully around religious sites of any faith you pass.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Mandarin

  • Ni hao: hello
  • Xiexie: thank you
  • Duoshao qian: how much does this cost?

💳 Money & Payments

  • China runs on mobile payments, Alipay and WeChat Pay dominate even small noodle stalls, and both now offer simplified setups for foreign tourist cards.
  • Cash in RMB is still worth carrying as backup, especially for the Mati Temple day trip where signal and card readers can be patchy.
  • Foreign credit cards are accepted at larger hotels but rarely at small family-run restaurants, plan accordingly.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Buy the 2-day Danxia ticket and use both days

Park entry is valid for two consecutive days, so a Day 2 spent entirely on sunrise-to-sunset chasing doesn't cost extra even if you pop back in briefly the next morning.

🍜

Eat where locals eat

Old-town noodle houses near the Drum Tower serve a full, filling bowl for a fraction of what a hotel restaurant charges for a similar dish.

🚗

Share a car-with-driver for Mati Temple

Splitting a private car and driver for the half-day Mati Temple round trip works out cheaper per person than a solo taxi charter or a packaged group tour.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌇

Platform 4 over Platform 1 for sunset

Most tour groups cluster at the main viewpoint for sunrise, Platform 4 at sunset draws a noticeably thinner crowd for arguably the better light.

🏮

Wander past the Drum Tower crossroads

A couple of streets beyond the main intersection, Zhangye's old town turns quieter, lined with older, less-restored buildings most day-trippers never reach.

🌧️

Chase the rain for saturated color

If a summer shower passes through, head into the geopark shortly after, wet sandstone saturates the reds and oranges beyond anything dry conditions show.

✈️ Flights

  • Zhangye Ganzhou Airport (YZY) has limited domestic connections, mainly to regional hubs within Gansu and a few larger cities.
  • Lanzhou Zhongchuan International Airport (LHW) is the more reliable gateway, with far more domestic routes and some international ones.
  • From Lanzhou, continue to Zhangye by high-speed rail rather than a connecting flight, it's usually faster door to door.

🚄 Trains

  • The Lanzhou–Xinjiang high-speed line is the easiest way in and out, with Zhangye about two hours from Lanzhou.
  • Onward connections run west toward Jiayuguan and Dunhuang or east toward Lanzhou, Xi'an, and beyond, useful for extending into a wider Gansu or Silk Road trip.
  • Book tickets a few days ahead in peak summer, popular routes and times can sell out.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Didi covers Zhangye's city center reliably, though setup usually requires a China-registered phone number.
  • Metered taxis are plentiful and inexpensive for short hops around town.
  • Both the Danxia Geopark and Mati Temple sit outside the city, so plan a pre-arranged driver or the geopark's own shuttle rather than relying on a street-hailed taxi.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Self-drive in China requires a Chinese driving license or a translated permit that isn't universally accepted, so it's rarely practical for foreign visitors here.
  • Most travelers hire a car-with-driver for the Mati Temple day trip and any Danxia Geopark transfers instead.
  • No car is needed once inside the Danxia Geopark itself, the shuttle bus system handles everything from there.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • The old town near the Drum Tower: central, walkable to the Giant Buddha Temple, and close to noodle houses for dinner.
  • Near Zhangye Railway Station or Zhangye West Station: convenient for an early Day 2 start toward the Danxia Geopark's shuttle pickup points.
  • Foreign visitors need to register with their passport at check-in, standard practice at Chinese hotels and quick to complete.

🎟️ Park Tickets & Shuttle System

  • Danxia Geopark entry is valid for two consecutive days and includes unlimited rides on the internal shuttle buses.
  • Private vehicles, including rental cars and tour vans, are barred from driving inside the park itself.
  • Ticket prices and gate hours shift slightly by season, check the current schedule a day or two before visiting.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • A VPN set up before arrival is worth having if you need access to apps or sites blocked within mainland China.
  • Travel insurance is a sensible add-on given the amount of sun exposure and stair-climbing on Days 2 and 4.
  • Alipay or WeChat Pay's tourist versions are worth setting up ahead, cash-only workarounds are increasingly rare even outside big cities.
