4 Days in Dhërmi & Palasë: Beaches on the Albanian Riviera

Four slow beach days on the northern Albanian Riviera: the Llogara Pass, Dhërmi, the walk down to Gjipe, Jale and Palasë, then Porto Palermo castle, with bus fares and cash tips.

Ideal SeasonJun – Sep
Est. Budget$280-480
Duration4 Days
PaceRelaxed
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Tirana Airport & the Rinas Express

Tirana (TIA, Rinas) is the realistic gateway: Vlorë airport had no scheduled passenger flights at the last check (check current). The Rinas Express bus reaches the centre in about 25 to 30 minutes for 400 lek (about €4). Southbound buses leave from a separate terminal, about €12 to 15 by taxi.

🚌 ~25-30 min by Rinas Express to the centre 💵 ~400 lek (~€4) bus, ~€12-15 taxi to the south terminal (check current) 🕐 Land by about midday, because the last coastal buses leave late afternoon
Afternoon

Bus south & the Llogara Pass viewpoint

Tirana to Himarë buses stop at Dhërmi: expect departures from about 07:00 to 18:00, roughly 2,000 to 2,500 lek and 3.5 to 4 hours via Vlorë (check current). The road crosses the Llogara Pass at 1,027 m, with views over the coast and Corfu. Buses rarely wait, so sit on the sea side and tell the driver "Dhërmi".

🚌 ~3.5-4 h by bus from the Tirana south terminal 💵 ~2,000-2,500 lek (~€20-25), cash (check current) 🕐 A 13:00 or 15:30 departure reaches Dhërmi by early evening
Evening

Settling into Dhërmi village

The bus stops on the SH8 road above the coast. The village is a short walk, while the beach is a steep 15 to 20 minute descent or about €3 by taxi (check current). Withdraw cash in Tirana or Vlorë, since Dhërmi ATMs can run empty. Eat in the village rather than on the sand, where meals cost more.

🚶 ~10 min on foot from the SH8 stop to the village 💵 ~€12-20 for dinner, rooms ~€40-60 off-peak, more in July and August 🕐 Check in before dark, since the descent is steep and poorly lit
Morning

The old village & Panagia monastery church

The old village climbs the hillside above the coast, with stone houses and small churches. The Panagia monastery church is reportedly 13th to 14th century (check) and has old frescoes. Walk up in the cool of the morning, about 30 to 45 minutes. It is often locked outside services, so ask in the village, and cover your shoulders and knees.

🚶 ~30-45 min on foot uphill from the village centre 💵 Free, a small donation is welcome 🕐 Before 10:00 to beat the heat, and ask about opening
Afternoon

Dhërmi main beach & sunbed prices

The main beach is a long strip of fine pebbles and clear water, backed by beach bars. Two sunbeds and an umbrella cost about 1,500 to 3,000 lek (€15 to 30) in July and August, and roughly half that in early June or September (check current). Some bars set a minimum spend instead. Ask first, and pay cash.

🚕 ~€3 taxi or a steep 15-20 min walk down from the road 💵 Sunbeds ~€10-30 per set, cheaper off-peak (check current) 🕐 Mid-morning to late afternoon, with shade only from your own umbrella
Evening

Drymades & a taverna dinner

Drymades is the Greek name of Dhërmi, also used for the beach area below the village. Choose a taverna and order grilled fish by weight: about 2,000 to 3,500 lek per kg is typical on this coast (check current). Ask the price per kilo and have it weighed at the table. One shared fish with salad suits two.

🚶 Short walk or ~€3 taxi back up to the village 💵 ~€15-25 per person for fish, salad and a drink 🕐 From about 19:30, and book in July and August
Morning

Gjipe Canyon & beach

Gjipe is a pebble and sand beach at the mouth of a canyon, about 15 minutes by car from Dhërmi. Parking at the track end costs about 300 to 500 lek (check current). Then it is a 1.6 km dirt track, about 30 minutes down and slower back up, with almost no shade. Go early with sturdy shoes and water.

🚕 ~15 min by taxi, then ~30 min on foot downhill 💵 Parking ~300-500 lek, sunbed ~€10 (check current) 🕐 Arrive by 09:00, before the heat and the day boats
Afternoon

Jale beach

Jale is a curved bay of sand and small pebbles between Dhërmi and Himarë, with beach clubs and calm water. From the SH8 take a furgon towards Himarë for about €2 to 3 (check current) and ask for Jale, then walk down. Sunbeds cost about the same as at Dhërmi, and some clubs set a minimum spend.

🚐 ~20 min by furgon on the SH8, then a short walk down 💵 Furgon ~€2-3, sunbeds ~€10-30 (check current) 🕐 Afternoon, with return furgons thinning out after about 18:00
Evening

Palasë beach at sunset

Palasë is both a small village on the hillside below the Llogara Pass and the beach beneath it, about 1.5 km long and facing west. It lies several kilometres north of Dhërmi. Take a taxi (agree about €10 to 15 first, check current), swim at sunset and eat nearby. Furgons are rare in the evening, so book the return ride.

🚕 ~10-15 min by taxi north from Dhërmi 💵 Taxi ~€10-15 each way, dinner ~€12-20 (check current) 🕐 Sunset swim, then arrange the return taxi before dark
Morning

Porto Palermo castle

Porto Palermo castle, built by Ali Pasha of Tepelena around 1804 to 1814, sits on a small peninsula about 10 km south of Himarë. Entry is about 300 lek, cash (check current; some sources say free). Take a furgon from Dhërmi towards Himarë and on, about €3 to 5, or a taxi. It opens around 09:00.

🚐 ~40 min by furgon or taxi south of Dhërmi 💵 Castle ~300 lek (~€3), furgon ~€3-5 (check current) 🕐 From about 09:00, and bring small notes
Afternoon

Himarë town & lunch

Himarë is the main town of the coast, with a seafront, Spile beach and the old castle quarter on the hill above. It has banks and ATMs, so top up on cash here. Lunch is your best chance to try local olive oil, goat dishes and fresh fish. A furgon back to Dhërmi costs about €2 to 3 (check current).

🚐 ~30 min by furgon from Himarë back to Dhërmi 💵 ~€10-18 for lunch, furgon ~€2-3 (check current) 🕐 Lunch from about 13:00, then collect your bags
Evening

The road north over Llogara to Tirana

Tirana-bound coastal buses pass the SH8 stops in the afternoon and evening, so ask your guesthouse for the day's times (check current). The journey takes about 4 hours and costs roughly €15 to 25. Do not book a flight for the same evening: sleep in Tirana or fly the next day. By car, cross Llogara in daylight.

🚌 ~4 h by bus from Dhërmi to Tirana 💵 ~€15-25 by bus, cash (check current) 🕐 Leave Dhërmi by mid-afternoon to reach Tirana before dark

💶 Tipping & Service

  • Tipping is appreciated but not compulsory. Rounding up the bill or leaving about 10% for good service in a taverna is normal, and a service charge is not usually added.
  • Give small tips in cash, as card terminals rarely have a tip option. Taxi drivers do not expect one, but rounding up is welcome.
  • Beach staff who carry drinks or set up your sunbed are happy with a few hundred lek in cash.

🐟 Food & Drink on the Riviera

  • Byrek (flaky pastry with cheese, spinach or meat) is the cheap breakfast or lunch, usually costing a few hundred lek from a bakery. Order a yoghurt drink with it.
  • Fresh fish is sold by weight, so ask the price per kilo and have it weighed. Himarë and the coast are known for olive oil, goat dishes and honey from the hills. Dhërmi holds a honey fair each year.
  • Rakia (raki) is the local fruit brandy, offered as a welcome drink. Sip it slowly and accept one small glass, then say "faleminderit" if you do not want more.

🌍 Villages, Churches & Local Customs

  • Dhërmi and nearby villages are part Greek-speaking. Many locals say "Kaliméra" or "Efharistó" as well as Albanian, and English is common in tourist businesses.
  • Cover shoulders and knees at the old churches, and ask before you photograph inside. Panagia and other chapels may be locked outside services.
  • Albanians nod and shake the head the opposite way round to many visitors: a nod can mean no, so confirm prices out loud.
  • In July and August, nightlife and beach clubs are loud until late near Dhërmi and Jale, so book a room in the village or in Palasë for quiet.

⚠️ Safety, Heat, Sea & Mountain Roads

  • Summer heat is strong, with little shade at Gjipe and on the hill walks. Carry water, a hat and sun protection, and avoid the middle of the day.
  • Few beaches have lifeguards. The sea can be rough on windy days, and pebble shores shelve steeply with currents around rocks, so stay near the shore.
  • Beach bars often have no printed price list. Ask for the price of sunbeds, drinks and food before you sit, and check the bill. Taxis have no meter: agree the fare first.
  • The Llogara road has hairpins, fog and heavy summer traffic, and parking in Dhërmi and at Gjipe fills up. Drive in daylight, never leave valuables in a car, and check your government travel advice (for example the UK FCDO or the US State Department).

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Albanian

  • "Mirëdita" (meer-DEE-tah, good day), "Mirëmëngjes" (good morning), "Përshëndetje" (hello)
  • "Faleminderit" (fah-leh-meen-DEH-reet, thank you), "Ju lutem" (yoo LOO-tem, please), "Gëzuar" (cheers)
  • "Sa kushton?" (sah koosh-TOHN, how much is it?), "Llogarinë, ju lutem" (the bill, please)
  • "Ku është plazhi?" (where is the beach?), "A flisni anglisht?" (do you speak English?)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Albania uses the lek (ALL), roughly 100 lek to €1 (check the current rate). Euros are widely accepted on the Riviera, but change is often given in lek at a poor rate.
  • Cash is important: buses, furgons, beach parking, Gjipe, Porto Palermo and many beach bars are cash only, while hotels and bigger restaurants usually take cards.
  • ATMs are in Vlorë, Himarë and Tirana, and a few in Dhërmi that can run empty. Foreign cards often pay about 500 to 800 lek per withdrawal, so take out larger amounts less often and choose to be charged in lek.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

📅

Travel in the shoulder season

Rooms and sunbeds drop sharply in early June and mid-September, often by a third or more compared with July and August. The sea is still warm and the beaches are far calmer.

🥙

Eat bakery lunches and village dinners

Byrek, souvlaki and a yoghurt drink make a lunch for a few euros. Dinner in the hillside village costs less than on the beachfront, where you pay for the view.

🚐

Use furgons, not taxis, between villages

A furgon along the SH8 costs about €2 to 3 per ride, against €10 to 20 for a taxi. Wave one down at the road, carry small notes and pay the driver in cash.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

Go to Gjipe at opening and Palasë at sunset

Gjipe is quietest before 10:00, before the day boats arrive. Palasë beach faces west and is calm in the evening, with room to spread out even in August.

⛪

Climb to Panagia in the cool of the day

The hilltop church above Dhërmi has wide views over the village and the sea. Go early or in late afternoon, and ask in the village whether someone has the key.

🌲

Add Llogara National Park

With a spare half day, the pine forest around the pass, at about 1,000 m, is much cooler than the coast. Take a taxi or drive, and walk the short marked paths near the road.

✈️ Flights

  • Tirana (TIA) is the only practical airport, about 4 to 5 hours by road from the Riviera. Vlorë airport had no scheduled passenger flights at the last check, so look at the latest news before relying on it.
  • The Rinas Express to central Tirana costs about 400 lek (about €4, check current). From there, buses to Himarë stop at Dhërmi.
  • Another route is the ferry from Corfu to Sarandë, then a coastal bus north for about €6 to 12 (check current). The Sarandë page covers the town.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Taxis on the Riviera have no meters, so agree the fare before you get in. A short hop from the road down to a beach is about €3, and Sarandë to Dhërmi is often €70 to 90 (check current).
  • A private transfer from Tirana to Dhërmi costs about €130 to 185 for a whole car, or about €25 to 35 per person in a shared minivan (check current).
  • Ride-hailing apps are not reliable on the coast, so ask your guesthouse to call a driver.

🚌 Public Transport & Tickets

  • Intercity buses and shared minivans (furgons) run along the SH8 coastal road. There is no fixed timetable at roadside stops, so ask your guesthouse for the day's times.
  • Tirana to Dhërmi is about 2,000 to 2,500 lek and 3.5 to 4 hours. Vlorë to Dhërmi is roughly 1 hour for about €10 to 12, and Himarë to Dhërmi about €2 to 3 and 30 minutes (check current).
  • Pay the driver in cash. Buses and furgons drop you on the SH8, so the beaches are a steep walk down, about 15 to 40 minutes depending on the beach.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Dhërmi village: family guesthouses on the hill, quieter nights and cheaper rooms, with a steep walk to the beach.
  • Drymades and Dhërmi beachfront: resorts and clubs near the sand, but noisier and pricier in July and August.
  • Palasë: calmer, with long beach access, and fewer restaurants.
  • Himarë town: the biggest choice, shops, ATMs and year-round services, and a base for Porto Palermo.
  • Rooms cost about €30 to 250 per night depending on the season, and July and August need booking 2 to 3 months ahead (check current).

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Albania is not in the EU, so check your roaming plan. A local prepaid SIM or an eSIM is cheap, and most guesthouses offer Wi-Fi.
  • Mobile signal is patchy in canyons and on parts of the mountain road, so download offline maps.
  • Carry plenty of lek in small notes, because buses, parking and many beach bars do not take cards. ATMs in Vlorë and Himarë are the safest bet.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Check entry rules for your passport before booking: many EU, UK and US visitors enter visa-free for short stays, but check yours on your government travel site.
  • Swimming runs from about June to September. July and August are the busiest and dearest, May and early October are quiet, and in winter many places on the coast are closed.
  • A rental car gives freedom for Gjipe and Porto Palermo, but the Llogara road and summer parking are demanding. Buses and taxis work if you stay put.
  • Take out travel insurance, and carry any medicine you need, since pharmacies are sparse on the coast.
