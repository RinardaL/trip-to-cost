4 Days in Niseko: Powder Skiing, Onsen & Ramen on a Budget

Four days of Hokkaido powder: the Resort Liner from New Chitose, one pass across four resorts, an onsen soak, and cheap ramen and izakaya dinners in Kutchan.

4-day Niseko itinerary

How many days do you have?

Morning

New Chitose Airport & the Hokkaido Resort Liner

New Chitose (CTS) is about 2 hours 45 minutes from Hirafu by the direct Resort Liner coach, around ¥6,000 one way (check current). Winter coaches run from about 10:00 to 19:30 and need a reservation, so book before you fly. Allow at least 90 minutes after an international landing, 50 after a domestic one.

🚌 ~2 h 45 min by direct coach to Hirafu 💵 ~¥6,000 (~€34, ~$38) one way (check current) 🕐 First coach ~10:00 from the domestic terminal, last ~19:30
Afternoon

Hirafu village: lift pass, rental & supplies

The coach stops at the Hirafu Welcome Centre. Buy your Niseko United pass online and collect it at a base ticket counter, then hire skis or a board the same day: a standard set with boots and helmet is about ¥7,000 to 10,000 a day, jacket and pants extra (check current). Book rental online for a discount.

🚶 Welcome Centre is the coach stop; a free village shuttle loops the streets 💵 Rental ~¥7,000-10,000/day (~€40-57, ~$45-64) (check current) 🕐 Collect gear on arrival day, before shops close for the evening
Evening

Ramen & izakaya in Hirafu

Hirafu has ramen counters and izakaya within walking distance of most lodgings. A bowl of ramen is about ¥1,100 to 1,800, and a casual izakaya dinner runs ¥3,000 to 5,000 a head. Kutchan, about 15 minutes away, is cheaper but needs a bus. Izakaya last orders are typically around 23:00 (check current).

🚶 Short walk, or the free village shuttle 💵 Ramen ~¥1,100-1,800, izakaya ~¥3,000-5,000 (check current) 🕐 Dinner from about 18:00, last orders ~23:00
Morning

Grand Hirafu first lifts

Be at the base for the first lifts, usually early morning (check current). The 2026/27 All Mountain Pass covers Hirafu, Hanazono, Niseko Village and Annupuri. A 1-day adult ticket is ¥12,600 in the regular season and ¥13,500 at peak, 24 December to 28 February. Fresh snow is best before 11:00.

🚡 Gondola and lifts from the village base 💵 1-day ¥12,600-13,500; 3-day ¥36,800-39,500 (~€70-76, ~$80-86 a day) (check current) 🕐 First lifts, fresh tracks before about 11:00
Afternoon

Niseko Village & Annupuri

Niseko Village and Annupuri sit on the same mountain as Hirafu and are reached by lifts or the Niseko United shuttle, free with the pass or about ¥500 cash. Annupuri is usually the least crowded of the four. Pack onigiri from a convenience store, since mountain restaurants cost more. Obey closed-gate signs.

🚡 Lifts across the mountain, or the Niseko United shuttle 💵 Included in the pass; shuttle ~¥500 without one (check current) 🕐 Early afternoon, back on the lifts before they close
Evening

An onsen soak: Goshiki, Kira no Yu or Iroha

Soak sore legs in an onsen. Goshiki Onsen is about ¥1,000, Kira no Yu by Niseko Station about ¥800 and Iroha at Annupuri about ¥1,200 (2026 listing, check current). Wash first, no swimsuits, and ask about tattoo rules at the door. A taxi is about ¥2,500 per 10 minutes.

🚕 Taxi from Hirafu, about ¥2,500 per 10 min (check current) 💵 Entry ~¥800-1,200 (~€4.5-7, ~$5-7.5) (check current) 🕐 After the lifts close, check each onsen's evening hours
Morning

Annupuri: quiet runs & a lesson option

Take the Niseko United shuttle to Annupuri, which feels quieter than Hirafu. A half-day group lesson of about 2.5 hours costs ¥15,000 to 22,000 depending on the season, with pass and rental extra (Niseko Central 2026/27, check current). If you already ski, skip it and spend the money on powder days instead.

🚌 Niseko United shuttle from Hirafu (free with the pass) 💵 Group lesson ~¥15,000-22,000 (~€85-125, ~$95-140) (check current) 🕐 Lessons start mid-morning; free skiing from the first lifts
Afternoon

Hanazono powder & the free shuttle

Hanazono has its own base, linked to Hirafu by a free shuttle about every 20 minutes from the Welcome Centre (check current). The pass covers it, and it is open to 11 April 2027. Powder holds best on the tree runs after a storm, but read the Niseko Avalanche Information before leaving marked runs.

🚌 Free Hanazono shuttle every ~20 min from Hirafu (check current) 💵 Included in the pass, shuttle free 🕐 Afternoon, after a snowfall for the best powder
Evening

Night skiing & dinner in Kutchan

Night skiing runs 12 December to 22 March on the pass, and last season the Hanazono shuttle ran to about 19:30 on night-ski days (check current). Then ride the Kutchan Night Go Bus from the Welcome Centre, about ¥100 for the first ride (check current). Kutchan izakaya dishes start around ¥700, and a bowl of ramen is about ¥1,050.

🚌 Kutchan Night Go Bus from Hirafu Welcome Centre (check current) 💵 Bus ~¥100, dinner ~¥1,500-3,000 (check current) 🕐 Night skiing to ~19:30, dinner from ~20:00
Morning

Last runs or a cheap morning in Hirafu

Ride the early lifts on the last day of your 3-day pass, then leave the hill by about 11:00 to return rental gear and check out. Lighter skiers can buy a 12-point ticket for ¥7,500 (check current) instead of a day pass. Ask your lodging to hold your bags until the coach.

🚡 Lifts from the village base, then a short walk 💵 3-day pass already paid; 12-point ticket ~¥7,500 (check current) 🕐 Off the hill by ~11:00 to return gear
Afternoon

Resort Liner back to New Chitose

Coaches leave Hirafu Welcome Centre at 7:50, 9:50, 13:50, 14:40 and 15:50 and take about 2 hours 50 minutes (check current). Reserve a seat. Be at the airport 60 minutes before a domestic flight or 120 before an international one, so take the 13:50 or 14:40 for an evening departure.

🚌 ~2 h 50 min by Resort Liner to New Chitose 💵 ~¥6,000 (~€34, ~$38) (check current) 🕐 Leave Hirafu by 13:50 or 14:40 for an evening flight
Evening

Ramen at the airport before your flight

New Chitose has the Hokkaido Ramen Dojo on the third floor of the domestic terminal, with about ten ramen shops. Hours vary and several close around 20:00 to 21:00, so check before you go. A bowl is roughly ¥1,000 to 1,500 (check current). Spend leftover yen on Hokkaido sweets and keep your boarding pass handy.

🚶 Short walk from the check-in halls (check current) 💵 Ramen ~¥1,000-1,500 (~€6-8.5, ~$6.5-9.5) (check current) 🕐 Eat before 20:00, when several shops close

Niseko: what to know before you go

💴 Tipping, Table Charges & Service

  • Tipping is not expected in Japan, and leaving money on the table can cause confusion. Good service is part of the price, so a thank-you ("arigato gozaimasu") is enough.
  • Many izakaya add a small table charge (otoshi) with the first drink, often a few hundred yen, and it comes with a small appetiser. It is normal, so do not argue it.
  • Ramen shops often use a ticket machine at the door: pay first, then hand the ticket to the staff. Small places may take cash only, so carry some yen.

🍜 Eating & Drinking Etiquette

  • Say "itadakimasu" before eating and "gochisosama deshita" when you leave. Slurping noodles is normal and shows you are enjoying them.
  • Never stick chopsticks upright in rice or pass food from chopstick to chopstick. Rest them on the holder or across the bowl.
  • Hokkaido specialities include miso ramen, soup curry, crab, scallops, Genghis Khan lamb grill and Sapporo beer. Order drinks first at an izakaya, then share a few plates.
  • Most places have English or picture menus in the resort, but Kutchan menus may be Japanese only, so a translation app helps.

♨️ Onsen & Slope Manners

  • In an onsen, wash and rinse seated at the shower stool before entering the bath, no swimwear, hair tied up and the small towel kept out of the water. Tattoo rules vary, so ask at the door.
  • On the mountain, follow the Niseko Rules: ski in control, give way to skiers below you, and never duck under ropes.
  • Leave skis and boards in the racks outside restaurants, and keep a leash or strap on them on windy days.
  • Take your rubbish with you or use the bins, and keep your voice down at night in lodgings, as walls are thin.

⚠️ Safety, Avalanches & Etiquette Warnings

  • Backcountry gates open and close daily at patrol discretion. A closed gate means no access, and violations can lead to your pass being taken. Rescue costs outside the boundary are charged to you, so check the daily Niseko Avalanche Information.
  • Heading out a gate needs a helmet, beacon, shovel and probe plus training, ideally with a licensed guide. Tree wells (soft snow around trunks) can trap a skier alone, so ride with a partner.
  • Roads and pavements are icy and taxis are scarce between about 18:00 and 21:00, and after 21:00 waits can reach 30 to 60 minutes, so plan your evening.
  • Emergency numbers: 110 for police and 119 for ambulance and fire. The JNTO Japan Visitor Hotline is 050-3816-2787. Check your government's travel advice for Japan and carry insurance that covers winter sports and rescue.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Japanese

  • "Konnichiwa" (hello), "Arigato gozaimasu" (thank you), "Sumimasen" (excuse me, sorry)
  • "Onegaishimasu" (please, or "I ask a favour"), "Kore o kudasai" (this one, please)
  • "Ikura desu ka?" (how much is it?), "O-kanjo o onegaishimasu" (the bill, please)
  • "Eigo o hanasemasu ka?" (do you speak English?), "Onsen wa doko desu ka?" (where is the onsen?)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Japan uses the yen. In early November 2026 the rate was about ¥157 to the US dollar and ¥177 to the euro, so ¥1,000 is roughly $6.4 or €5.7 (check current).
  • Cards and contactless work at the larger resort hotels, rental shops and lift offices, but small ramen counters and izakaya may take cash only.
  • Seven-Eleven and post office ATMs usually accept foreign cards. Avoid dynamic currency conversion: if a terminal offers your home currency, choose yen.
  • Some towns charge a small accommodation tax per person per night, usually paid at the property, so check it when you book.

Insider tips for Niseko

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

📅

Ski the shoulder weeks if you can

In 2026/27 a 1-day adult ticket is ¥13,500 at peak (24 December to 28 February), ¥12,600 in the regular weeks and ¥8,800 in the early (28 November to 11 December) and spring (23 March to 4 April) seasons. Rooms are cheaper then too (check current).

🚆

Take the JR train and a local bus

The train via Otaru to Kutchan costs about ¥3,000 and takes roughly 3 to 3.5 hours, then a local bus to Hirafu is about ¥400. It saves a few thousand yen each way against the Resort Liner, but trains are infrequent and need a change, so check the timetable (check current).

🍙

Self-cater and eat in Kutchan

Convenience-store breakfast and onigiri lunches cost a fraction of mountain restaurants. Kutchan dinners are often 20 to 30 percent cheaper than Hirafu, and the Night Go Bus makes the trip easy (check current).

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🏔️

Use Annupuri for fewer crowds

Annupuri is generally the quietest of the four resorts and is on the same pass, so it is a good place to relax or ride after a busy morning in Hirafu. Niseko Moiwa is a smaller separate area with its own ticket (check current prices).

☁️

Catch Mount Yotei on a clear morning

The cone of Mount Yotei is often hidden by cloud and snow. Early starts on cold, clear mornings give the best views from the lifts and from roads around Hirafu and Kutchan.

🥾

Swap one lift day for a free one

If your legs need a rest or the weather turns, walk the village, soak in an onsen and eat in Kutchan instead. Snowshoe tours are a paid, gentler alternative (check current).

Travel services for Niseko

✈️ Flights

  • New Chitose Airport (CTS) near Sapporo is the gateway to Niseko. Direct flights arrive from Tokyo and other Japanese cities, and a growing number of international routes serve it (check current).
  • The Resort Liner coach to Hirafu takes about 2 hours 45 minutes and costs about ¥6,000 one way (check current). Reserve a seat before you fly.
  • Allow at least 90 minutes after an international landing and 50 after a domestic one before your coach, and 120 or 60 minutes before departing flights.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Taxis in Hirafu cost about ¥2,500 for a 10-minute ride, with a surcharge of about ¥900 when booked through the GO app (check current).
  • Demand peaks between about 18:00 and 21:00, and after 21:00 in January and February waits of 30 to 60 minutes are realistic.
  • Book a taxi through your lodging for onsen trips, or use the Night Go Bus and shuttles instead.

🚇 Public Transport & Tickets

  • The Niseko United shuttle is free with the All Mountain Pass and costs about ¥500 for adults without one (check current). The Hirafu village shuttle and the Hanazono shuttle are free.
  • The JR line runs between Sapporo, Otaru and Kutchan roughly every 1 to 2 hours. From Kutchan Station a local bus to Hirafu costs about ¥400 (check current).
  • The Kutchan Night Go Bus runs in the evening between the Hirafu Welcome Centre and Kutchan Station, with the first ride about ¥100 and later rides that evening free (check current).
  • Check timetables on the day, since winter schedules and weather can change them.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Hirafu: the liveliest base, with the most restaurants and the Welcome Centre coach stop, and the highest prices in peak weeks.
  • Kutchan: a short bus ride away, with cheaper food and lodging and a local feel, but you rely on buses in the evenings.
  • Hanazono: modern, quiet and close to its own slopes, with a free shuttle to Hirafu, though dining is more limited.
  • Niseko Village and Annupuri: ski-in lodges and onsen hotels, calmer evenings and higher prices for the larger hotels.
  • Budget dorms and guesthouse rooms can start around ¥5,000 a bed in winter, but January and February sell out early (check current).

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Install an eSIM or rent a pocket Wi-Fi at the airport for maps and translation. Most lodgings and many cafés offer Wi-Fi.
  • Cards work in the resort, but carry yen for small izakaya, ramen shops and onsen.
  • Seven-Eleven and post office ATMs usually accept foreign cards.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Check Japan's entry rules for your passport on the Ministry of Foreign Affairs site before booking.
  • Niseko United is planned to open on 28 November 2026 and close on 5 May 2027, with Niseko Village ending on 4 April and Hanazono on 11 April. Dates depend on conditions, so check the official site, niseko.ne.jp.
  • Insurance should cover winter sports, and off-piste rescue is charged to you. Read the Niseko Rules and Avalanche Information before you ride.
  • You do not need a car: the Resort Liner, shuttles and the village layout cover this plan, and roads are icy.
