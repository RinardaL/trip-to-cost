Christmas Markets in Europe

Complete day-by-day itineraries for 12 Christmas market cities, from German and Alsace market towns to Vienna, Prague and Quebec City, with the 2026 dates and the price up front.

Last updated: November 2026

Most European Christmas markets open in the last week of November and close on or just before 24 December, and a few run into early January. Opening dates, hours and entry fees change every year, so each itinerary lists the dates we could confirm and marks the rest as "check current".

Every guide is built for a short trip: you land at the airport, get to the centre, see the main market in the evening and the sights by day. Prices are estimates per person for the whole stay, covering lodging, meals, local transport and activities. International flights are not included.

How to choose between twelve Christmas market trips

Eleven of these trips last four days and Budapest runs to five, so length hardly separates them. What does is cost, setting and what you do between markets. Cost per day is the quickest filter: Budapest works out at $140 per day, Prague and Dresden at $150, while Colmar reaches $220 and Copenhagen $265.

Match the city to the traveller. Vienna pairs the Rathausplatz market with palaces and coffeehouses, and Colmar and Strasbourg offer half-timbered streets and Alsace markets. For a German market, Nuremberg centres on the Christkindlesmarkt, Dresden on the Striezelmarkt and Munich on the Christkindlmarkt at Marienplatz. Berlin and Copenhagen are the two Modern Cities trips, with Museum Island and Wall history in one and Danish design and Tivoli in the other. Quebec City is the odd one out: a transatlantic option with a German Christmas market and snow at Montmorency Falls.

When to go and what the season changes

Every trip lists November or late November through December as its Christmas window. Luxembourg City and Munich are listed from late November, and Munich stops at 24 December, so a trip between Christmas and New Year needs checking against the current market dates. Weekdays are quieter than weekends, which the pages note for Vienna, Luxembourg City and the Alsace markets.

Daylight is short. Sunset in Prague comes at around 4 PM in early December, so use midday for castles and museums and keep the markets for dusk. Plan around 24 to 26 December as well: the pages for Copenhagen, Prague and Budapest all warn about holiday closures or changed transport timetables.

Several cities also work outside December. Luxembourg City and Copenhagen list May to September, Quebec City lists June to September, and Berlin, Strasbourg and Budapest list spring or early-autumn windows, which makes them easier to justify if you may want to return.

Combining cities and keeping costs down

Some trips overlap on purpose. The Strasbourg plan includes a day in Colmar by regional train, and the Colmar plan visits the village markets of Riquewihr, Kaysersberg and Eguisheim, so choose one as your base rather than booking both. Nuremberg adds a day trip to Bamberg, Dresden one to Saxon Switzerland, and Luxembourg City one to Vianden Castle, using Luxembourg's free public transport.

Direct trains link neighbouring pairs such as Vienna with Budapest or Dresden with Prague. Each plan has its own arrival and departure day, so a combined trip may let you trim a day: price it as a single itinerary.

To save money, book hotels early: the Colmar page says old-town hotels fill early in market season, and the Strasbourg and Munich pages also advise booking ahead. The Berlin page suggests eating dinner on a side street near Gendarmenmarkt. Ask about the deposit on mugs before you buy mulled wine. If budget is tight, Budapest gives five days for $600-800 per person, and Dresden four days for $460-720 per person.

Frequently asked questions

When do Christmas markets open in 2026?

In 2026 the earliest open on 13 November (Vienna, Budapest and Tivoli in Copenhagen), most others open between 19 and 28 November, and the latest to open is Prague on 28 November. Most close on or just before 24 December, while Berlin, Budapest, Colmar, Copenhagen, Luxembourg and Prague run into late December or January. The table above lists each city, and some cities had not published a final programme in early November.

How much does a Christmas market trip cost?

Our 12 itineraries range from about $460 to $1,300 per person for the whole trip, depending on the city and the length. Prices cover lodging, meals, local transport and activities; flights are not included.

Which Christmas trip is the cheapest?

Dresden ($460-720), Prague ($460-730), Nuremberg ($480-740) are the three lowest on this list, per person and excluding flights.

How many days do I need?

Most of these itineraries are 4 or 5 days, enough for the main market, the sights and a day trip. Each page has a day picker with a price for every trip length from one day up to the full plan.

Can I use these guides outside December?

Mostly yes. Each itinerary lists its ideal season, and for most cities the day-by-day plan also works in other months when the markets are closed.

Can I visit two Christmas market cities in one trip?

Strasbourg and Colmar are the easiest pair: the Strasbourg itinerary includes a day trip by regional train, which takes about half an hour, and the Colmar itinerary covers the village markets nearby. Vienna with Budapest, or Dresden with Prague, are also close neighbours linked by direct trains, but check timetables and market dates before you commit.

What is the mug deposit at Christmas markets?

Many of these markets serve mulled wine or punch in a deposit mug. You pay a deposit on top of the drink and get it back when you return the mug, or you can keep the mug as a souvenir. Amounts and rules differ between markets, and the Vienna page notes that deposits vary from market to market, so ask at the stall before you pay.
