What is Nordic travel, and how do these ten trips differ?

The Nordic countries are Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway and Sweden, and every one of them appears here. The ten trips fall into four groups: city breaks in Copenhagen, Stockholm, Oslo and Bergen; the Icelandic trips Iceland and Vatnajökull; Arctic winter trips in Tromsø, Abisko and Finnish Lapland; and a summer road trip to Lofoten Islands.

The cost gap between groups is the first thing to weigh. The four cities run from $220 to $265 per day. Iceland is $340 and Lofoten Islands is $380. Tromsø is $410, Abisko and Vatnajökull are $415 and Finnish Lapland is $420, and their days are built around guided activities such as husky sledding, reindeer rides and ice cave tours.

For a first Nordic visit on a tighter budget, Oslo and Bergen have the lowest totals. Families heading for Christmas will look at Finnish Lapland, which is built around Santa Claus Village, husky safaris and a snow hotel. City trips such as Stockholm suit travellers who prefer museums, island boat trips and cafés to outdoor activities in the cold.

Which season suits which trip?

Summer and winter pull in opposite directions. The four city trips list May to September, and Lofoten Islands is listed for June to August, with the midnight sun on its fourth day. Everything built around snow, ice and aurora sits at the other end of the year.

Abisko and Iceland list September to March, Vatnajökull lists November to March, Tromsø lists November to March with a Christmas and New Year peak, and Finnish Lapland lists December to March. Copenhagen is the exception, with a November and December Christmas option as well as May to September. The northern lights need dark, clear skies, so no itinerary can promise them. Tromsø, Abisko and Finnish Lapland each include more than one aurora outing, which improves the odds.

How do I combine trips and save money?

Do not stack trips that cover the same ground. Iceland reaches Jökulsárlón and a blue ice cave on day four, while Vatnajökull spends all four days around that glacier, so choose one. Tromsø and Lofoten Islands are both in northern Norway but list opposite seasons, and Abisko and Finnish Lapland overlap in what they offer, from dog sledding to reindeer.

Some of the cities connect by rail. Rail sites list a direct train between Stockholm and Oslo of roughly six hours, and the Bergen Line runs from Oslo to Bergen in about seven. The pages do not cover those legs, so check current timetables. To cut costs, pick a city trip over an Arctic one and travel in the months an itinerary lists. For Tromsø, the Christmas and New Year weeks are flagged as the peak, so a date outside them is the sensible place to start.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a trip to the Nordic countries cost?

Our 10 Nordic Countries itineraries cost from $720 to $2,400 per person for the whole trip, which is about $220 to $420 a day. Prices cover lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and exclude international flights.

Which Nordic Countries itinerary is the cheapest?

Oslo ($720-1,040), Bergen ($760-1,150), Copenhagen ($830-1,300) are the three lowest, per person and excluding flights.

How many days do I need in the Nordic countries?

Our Nordic Countries itineraries run from 4 to 5 days, and the most common length is 4 days. Each page has a day picker with a price for every length from one day up to the full plan.

When is the best time to visit the Nordic countries?

It depends on the trip, and each itinerary lists its ideal season. For example: Copenhagen, Nov – Dec (Christmas Season), May – Sep; Stockholm, May – Sep; Abisko, Sep – Mar. The table above shows the ideal season for every Nordic Countries trip.

Is Iceland part of Scandinavia?

Strictly, no. Scandinavia usually means Denmark, Norway and Sweden, while the Nordic countries also include Finland and Iceland. Usage varies, and English speakers sometimes use Scandinavia more widely. This hub uses the wider Nordic grouping, which is why Finnish Lapland and both Icelandic trips sit alongside the Scandinavian cities.

What is the difference between the Iceland trip and the Vatnajökull trip?

The five-day Iceland trip is a road trip from Reykjavik covering the Golden Circle, the south coast and Jökulsárlón. The four-day Vatnajökull trip stays around Höfn and the glacier, with a guided ice cave and Skaftafell hiking. Choose the first for range and the second for depth.
