4 Days at Salar de Uyuni: Mirror Reflections, Incahuasi Island & the Colored Lagoons

The world's largest salt flat, a blinding white expanse of over 10,000 square kilometers at nearly 3,700 meters in the Bolivian Andes, and, for a few months a year, the largest natural mirror on Earth. Four days based out of Uyuni town covers the rusting train cemetery, a 4x4 crossing of the flat itself with a stop on the cactus-studded Incahuasi Island, sunrise and sunset sessions chasing the famous sky-on-earth reflection, and an optional full-day detour south to the flamingo-filled Laguna Colorada and the Sol de Mañana geysers.

Ideal SeasonDec – Apr
Est. Budget$600-850
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Fly in and settle into Uyuni town

There's no direct international service to Uyuni's small airport, so almost every trip starts with a short domestic hop from La Paz or Sucre, a dramatic descent over the Altiplano that gives the first glimpse of white salt glinting on the horizon, followed by a quick ride into the compact, dusty grid of Uyuni town.

✈️ ~50-70 min flight from La Paz 💵 ~$100-150 round-trip flight 🕐 Aim for a late-morning arrival
Afternoon

The Great Train Cemetery

A short taxi ride outside town, dozens of rusting 19th and early 20th-century steam locomotives sit abandoned on open tracks, relics of a failed mining export boom, now a favorite spot for climbing, photos, and graffiti art against the empty desert backdrop.

🚕 ~10 min from the town center 💵 Free to enter 🕐 Late afternoon light works best for photos
Evening

First Bolivian meal and tour briefing

Most Uyuni operators run a briefing the evening before departure, a good excuse to sample local specialties like salteñas or a hearty quinoa soup while confirming pickup times and gear for the next three days on the salt.

🚶 A short walk around the town center 💵 ~$5-8 dinner 🕐 Early evening, most tours brief around 6-7 PM
Morning

Onto the salt flat: the Dakar monument and salt piles

The 4x4 leaves Uyuni and crosses into the salt at Colchani, passing hand-raked mounds of harvested salt drying in the sun and a small monument to the Dakar Rally, which has crossed the Salar several times, before the convoy pushes out into featureless white in every direction.

🚙 ~20 min from Uyuni to the salt flat's edge 💵 Included in the tour 🕐 Depart by 10-10:30 AM
Afternoon

Incahuasi Island and the Ojos de Sal

Rising improbably out of the flat, Incahuasi Island is the fossilized remains of an ancient coral reef, now covered in cacti that grow just a centimeter a year, some over a thousand years old; en route, the 4x4 usually stops at the Ojos de Sal, small bubbling springs where underground water pushes up through cracks in the crust.

🌵 A short, worthwhile climb to the summit viewpoint 💵 ~$5 island entry fee (pay locally) 🕐 Late morning into early afternoon
Evening

Forced-perspective photos and the sunset mirror

With nothing on the horizon to give a sense of scale, the flat is famous for playful forced-perspective photos using toy figures and props; if there's even a thin layer of water on the surface, the last hour before sunset is when the Salar performs its best trick, turning the whole horizon into a seamless mirror of the sky, before an almost unbelievable density of stars appears once true dark sets in.

📸 Bring small props for perspective shots 💵 Included in the tour 🕐 The hour before and after sunset
Morning

Sol de Mañana geysers at sunrise

This extension day starts early, a pre-dawn departure reaches the Sol de Mañana geyser field around sunrise, when the cold air makes the bubbling mud pots and hissing fumaroles at nearly 5,000 meters even more dramatic.

🚙 ~3-3.5 hr drive from Uyuni 💵 Included in the extension tour 🕐 Arrive by sunrise, around 6 AM
Afternoon

Laguna Colorada's flamingos

A short drive further brings you to Laguna Colorada, a rust-red lagoon colored by algae and sediment, home to three species of flamingo, including the rare James's flamingo, wading through the shallows against a backdrop of distant volcanoes.

🦩 Best light and bird activity in the morning 💵 Reserve entry fee ~$15 (often included) 🕐 Mid-to-late morning
Evening

Termas de Polques hot springs

Before heading back toward Uyuni, most tours stop at the Termas de Polques, a natural thermal pool right at the edge of the Salar de Chalviri, a genuinely welcome soak after a long, cold, dusty day in the 4x4 at high altitude.

♨️ A short drive from Laguna Colorada 💵 Small entry fee, ~$3-4 🕐 Late afternoon, before the return drive
Morning

Sunrise on the mirror

The salt flat is at its most magical in the first hour of daylight, still, cool, and often near-empty of other vehicles, the pastel sunrise colors doubling perfectly on the wet surface for what's usually the best photography of the whole trip.

🌅 Depart the salt hotel before dawn 💵 Included in the tour 🕐 Roughly 30-45 min before sunrise
Midday

One last look across the flat

The drive back toward Uyuni gives one final, slower pass across the salt before the pace of the trip changes, a good stretch to simply take in the scale of the place before returning to paved roads and phone signal.

🚙 ~1.5-2 hr drive back to Uyuni 💵 Included in the tour 🕐 Mid-morning into midday
Afternoon

Transfer out of Uyuni

Most 4-day tours end back in Uyuni by early afternoon, leaving time to grab a late lunch in town before catching a return flight to La Paz or Sucre, or boarding an overnight bus if you're continuing on through Bolivia.

✈️ Confirm your return flight time before booking 💵 ~$100-150 round-trip flight (if not already booked) 🕐 Leave at least 2 hrs before a domestic flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Tipping isn't obligatory but is genuinely appreciated; around $10-15 per person for the driver-guide at the end of a 3-4 day tour is standard practice.
  • Round up at restaurants; a service charge is rarely added automatically outside La Paz.
  • Cooks and support staff at salt hotels and desert refugios also welcome a small tip if the service was good.

👗 Dress Code

  • Layering matters more than any single item, mornings and salt-hotel nights can hit near freezing while midday sun feels intense at this altitude.
  • Locally, dress is modest and practical; swimwear is fine only at the hot springs, not elsewhere on the trip.
  • Sturdy, closed shoes handle salt crust, gravel, and volcanic terrain far better than sandals.

🚶 Getting Around

  • Uyuni town itself is small and walkable, most guesthouses and tour offices sit within a few blocks of the plaza.
  • The salt flat, colored lagoons, and geysers are only accessible by licensed 4x4, there's no way to see them independently.
  • Distances are deceptively large; the colored-lagoon extension alone covers several hundred kilometers of remote dirt road.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Uyuni sits at roughly 3,656m and the geyser field near 4,850m, give yourself a day or two at altitude in La Paz or Sucre before pushing higher rather than flying straight in and heading out the next morning.
  • Watch for symptoms of altitude sickness (headache, nausea, dizziness) and tell your guide immediately if they appear.
  • The Aymara and Quechua communities around the Salar are not photo props, ask before photographing people, homes, or ceremonies, and expect some areas to be off-limits.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Spanish

  • Buenos días / Buenas tardes: good morning / good afternoon
  • Muchas gracias: thank you very much
  • ¿Cuánto cuesta?: how much does it cost?

💳 Money & Payments

  • Bolivia uses the boliviano (Bs); cash is essential once you leave Uyuni town, there are no ATMs or card machines on the salt flat or in the lagoon region.
  • Withdraw enough bolivianos in La Paz or Uyuni before departing, tours are typically pre-paid but meals, tips, and entry fees are usually cash.
  • Uyuni has a handful of ATMs, but they run out of cash or go offline often, don't count on finding one working on a tight schedule.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🚙

Join a shared 3-4 day tour instead of a private one

Shared group tours cost a fraction of a private vehicle and cover the exact same route, private only makes sense for a group of four or more splitting the cost.

🎒

Bring your own snacks and water for the extension day

The remote colored-lagoon circuit has almost no shops, and what little exists is priced for captive travelers, stock up in Uyuni before departing.

🏨

Skip the fancier "luxury" salt hotels

The classic salt-brick refugios cost far less, and the experience of sleeping inside walls made of salt is the real draw either way.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌵

Ask about a shifted Incahuasi visit time

Most tours arrive at the island around the same midday window, requesting a slightly earlier or later stop means far fewer people around the cacti for photos.

🚂

Wander deeper into the train cemetery

Most visitors stay near the entrance tracks, walking further back finds quieter, more overgrown wrecks with nobody else around.

⭐

Step outside after dinner at the salt hotel

With almost zero light pollution this far from any city, a five-minute walk from the dining room delivers one of the most intense night skies most travelers will ever see, no separate tour required.

✈️ Flights

  • Uyuni's small airport connects mainly to La Paz and Sucre on domestic carriers, there's no direct international service.
  • Flight schedules can shift with weather, especially in the wet season, build at least one buffer day around a major international connection.
  • Book flights and your 3-4 day salt flat tour separately, most tour operators don't handle flights.

🚕 Transfers & Shuttles

  • Uyuni's airport is about 5km from the town center, taxis and hotel pickups cover it in roughly 10 minutes.
  • Overnight buses also connect Uyuni to La Paz, Oruro, and Potosí, a slower but far cheaper alternative to flying.
  • Rideshare apps have no real presence in Uyuni, arrange taxis through your guesthouse instead.

🚙 4x4 Tour Booking

  • Book a licensed operator running the classic 3-day/2-night salt flat and colored-lagoon circuit, then add the extra day in Uyuni around the train cemetery, nearly every operator in town runs some version of this route.
  • Independent driving isn't realistic or permitted across most of the reserve, a guide and vehicle are effectively mandatory.
  • Compare a few operators before booking, vehicle quality, driver experience, and group size vary a lot even at similar prices.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Uyuni town: the practical base for Day 1 and Day 4, with the widest range of guesthouses near the plaza and train station.
  • Salt hotels on the flat: the classic overnight for Day 2, built almost entirely from salt bricks, usually included in your 4x4 tour package.
  • Desert refugios near the colored lagoons: basic, cold, and unforgettable, again typically included on Day 3 of the extension tour.

🎟️ Reserve Access & Timing

  • The colored-lagoon extension passes through the Eduardo Avaroa Andean Fauna National Reserve, which charges a separate entry fee, confirm whether it's included in your tour price before booking.
  • Altitude climbs fast on Day 3, from Uyuni's 3,656m to nearly 4,850m at the geysers, don't rush straight into this leg without at least a day or two already spent at altitude.
  • Wet-season road conditions (Dec-Apr) can force route changes on short notice, build in flexibility if traveling then.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Most nationalities need only a valid passport for short tourist stays in Bolivia, but some require a visa in advance, check current requirements well before booking flights.
  • Cell signal is minimal to nonexistent for most of Days 2 and 3, let people know you'll be hard to reach.
  • Travel insurance covering high-altitude remote evacuation is strongly worth it given how far this route sits from proper medical care.
