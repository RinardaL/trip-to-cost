4 Days in Sarandë: Butrint, Ksamil & the Blue Eye

Ruins at Butrint, beach time at Ksamil, the cold turquoise Blue Eye and a sunset from Lëkurësi Castle: four relaxed days on the Albanian Riviera, mostly by cheap local bus.

Ideal SeasonMay – Sep
Est. Budget$300-500
Duration4 Days
PaceRelaxed
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival: Tirana or Corfu to Sarandë

No flights land in Sarandë. From Tirana airport (TIA), take the airport bus to the south bus terminal (about €3), then a coach to Sarandë: about €15 and 4.5 to 5 hours. From Corfu, a ferry or hydrofoil takes 30 to 60 minutes and costs about €20 to 30. Winter has one sailing a day, so check.

🚌 Tirana: ~20 min airport bus, then ~4.5 to 5 h by coach. Corfu: 30 to 60 min by ferry 💵 ~€3 + ~€15 by coach, or ~€20-30 by ferry (check current) 🕐 Take a morning coach and arrive by mid-afternoon
Afternoon

Check-in & the Ancient Synagogue

The coach stops in the centre, near Friendship Park, and most hotels are within a 10-minute walk or a short taxi ride. Drop your bags, then see the mosaic floors of the 5th-century synagogue of ancient Onchesmos, close to the drop-off. Allow 20 minutes, and check the opening hours and any small fee on site.

🚶 ~10 min on foot from the bus drop-off 💵 Small fee or free (check on site) 🕐 Mid-afternoon, after check-in
Evening

Sunset on the Promenade & a First Dinner

Sarandë faces west across the strait to Corfu, so the seafront promenade is the place for sunset and the evening stroll that fills it. Walk the curve of the bay, then eat a street or two back from the water. Start with a byrek or a salad, and ask the price per kilo before ordering grilled fish.

🚶 On foot along the flat seafront 💵 Walk free, ~€10-20 for dinner (check current) 🕐 Sunset is around 8:30 PM in June and 7 PM in mid-September
Morning

Butrint National Park

Butrint is a UNESCO site of Greek, Roman and Byzantine ruins south of Sarandë. Take the local bus from the stop near the ferry terminal (about €2, check current) and buy your ticket at the gate: around 1,000 lek (€10). Allow 2 to 3 hours for the theatre and the Lion Gate, and bring water and mosquito repellent.

🚌 ~30 to 45 min by local bus from Sarandë 💵 ~€2 bus + ~€10 (1,000 lek) entry (check current) 🕐 Opens around 8:30 AM in summer, so go early for shade
Afternoon

Ksamil Beaches & the Islets

Ksamil is a short bus hop from Butrint, around €1. Its beaches face small wooded islets, but most of the sand is covered by paid sunbeds: two loungers and an umbrella cost roughly €10 to 30 in summer, less in shoulder months and away from the main strip. Pay in lek cash and settle the price before you sit down.

🚌 ~10 min by local bus from Butrint to Ksamil 💵 ~€1 bus, sunbeds ~€10-30 per pair (check current) 🕐 Plan 3 to 4 hours of sea and sand
Evening

Back to Sarandë: Harbour Dinner

Return to Sarandë by bus or taxi. Last buses leave early in the evening in summer and earlier out of season, so check before you swim. A taxi costs about €20 to 40, so agree the fare first. Eat at the harbour: Ksamil mussels, grilled fish or a village salad with local cheese. Ask for an itemised bill.

🚌 ~15 to 20 min by bus from Ksamil to Sarandë 💵 ~€1-2 by bus, ~€12-20 for dinner (check current) 🕐 Last buses leave in the early evening in summer (check current)
Morning

The Blue Eye (Syri i Kaltër)

The Blue Eye is a deep spring about 20 km inland, with cold turquoise water. Entry is about 50 lek (€0.50), parking roughly €3, and the entrance is a 20 to 30 minute walk or a short train ride. Swimming is banned. Go before 10 AM. A taxi return with waiting costs about €25 to 50.

🚕 ~25 to 30 min by taxi, or a Gjirokastër bus to the turn-off 💵 ~50 lek entry, ~€3 parking, taxi ~€25-50 return (check current) 🕐 Before 10 AM, ahead of the day-trip crowds
Afternoon

Gjirokastër, the Stone City (Optional)

Gjirokastër, another UNESCO town, is about 1.5 hours from Sarandë by bus (400 to 500 lek). Walk the steep cobbled bazaar lanes up to the castle (roughly 200 to 400 lek, sources differ) for views over the slate roofs. It adds three hours and a late return, so skip it for a slow afternoon in Sarandë if you prefer.

🚌 ~1.5 to 2 h by bus from Sarandë, or by taxi after the Blue Eye 💵 ~€4-5 by bus each way, castle ~200-400 lek (check current) 🕐 Check the last bus back before you commit
Evening

Lëkurësi Castle at Sunset

Lëkurësi Castle, a 16th-century fort on the hill above Sarandë, gives the best view of the bay and Corfu. Entry to the grounds is free. Take a taxi up and agree the fare back down, or walk down in daylight. A coffee on the terrace costs less than dinner, but reserve a table in July and August.

🚕 ~10 min by taxi, or a steep uphill walk from the centre 💵 Free entry, coffee ~€2-4, taxi fare agreed first 🕐 Arrive about 45 min before sunset
Morning

A Quiet Morning at Gjiri i Hartës

Spend the morning at Gjiri i Hartës (Map Bay) or Mirror Beach (Pasqyra), near Ksamil, reached by bus or taxi. Sunbeds and parking are paid, so ask the price first. Arrive before about 9 AM, when much of the sand is not yet being sold and the water is calm. Carry cash in lek and swim near the shore only.

🚌 ~15 to 20 min by bus or taxi from Sarandë 💵 ~€1 bus, sunbeds ~€10-30, small parking fee (check current) 🕐 Before about 9 AM for free sand and calm water
Afternoon

Harbour Lunch & Last Souvenirs

Back in Sarandë, check out and ask the hotel to hold your bags. For a cheap lunch, buy a byrek (about €1 to 2) from a bakery and eat it on the quay. For a sit-down meal, choose a harbour restaurant and agree the price of fish by the kilo first. Buy olive oil, honey or raki to take home.

🚶 Everything is within walking distance in the centre 💵 ~€1-2 for a byrek, ~€12-20 for a fish lunch (check current) 🕐 Finish lunch by about 1:30 PM
Evening

Transfer to Tirana Airport or the Corfu Ferry

Coaches to Tirana take 4.5 to 5 hours and cost about €15, so leave Sarandë by early afternoon for an evening flight. Times vary by season, so confirm at the bus station near the harbour, then take the airport bus from the south terminal (about €3). For Corfu, take the ferry. Allow three hours before your flight.

🚌 ~4.5 to 5 h by coach, then ~20 min on the airport bus 💵 ~€15 coach + ~€3 airport bus (check current) 🕐 Leave by early afternoon for an evening flight

💶 Tipping & Payments at the Table

  • Tipping is appreciated but not compulsory. Rounding up the bill or leaving about 10 percent in a restaurant is normal, and a few euros for a good guide or driver is welcome.
  • Check whether a service charge is already on the bill. Many small places take cash only, so keep lek or euro notes for lunch stops and for tips.
  • Taxis are not metered in most small towns. Agree the fare before you get in, and round up a little if the driver was fair.

🥧 Eating the Albanian Way

  • Byrek is flaky filo pastry filled with cheese, spinach or meat, sold in bakeries for a euro or two. It is the standard cheap breakfast or lunch.
  • Try tavë kosi, baked lamb with a yoghurt and egg topping, and fërgesë, a baked pepper and cheese dish. On the coast, look for grilled sea bream, calamari and Ksamil mussels.
  • Fresh fish is sold by weight. Ask the price per kilo and have the fish weighed at the table, then check the bill. Raki is the local spirit, and tap water is generally not recommended for drinking, so buy bottled.

🏖️ Seasons, Beaches & Local Manners

  • The swimming season is roughly June to September. May and late September are quieter and cheaper but the sea is cooler. From November to March many beach clubs, restaurants and boats close, and ferries run less often.
  • Albanians are hospitable and curious, and may offer a coffee or a rakia. Accept a small one politely. Some people nod and shake their heads the opposite way to what visitors expect, so confirm answers in words.
  • Dress modestly at churches and mosques. Beach clubs often take the best sand, so be polite but firm if you want a free spot.

⚠️ Safety, Scams & Etiquette Warnings

  • Driving is the biggest risk: overtaking is aggressive, roads can be poorly lit and in poor repair, and pedestrians and animals appear on highways. Rental cars from Corfu cannot be taken to Albania, and you need proper insurance. Many visitors do better by bus.
  • Coastal waters can be dangerous. Lifeguards are not on every beach, so heed flags and warning signs, and be careful of jellyfish and sea urchins. Jet skis and hired boats may lack proper safety equipment, so check before you rent.
  • Beach-club touts, parking attendants and some drivers add fees. Ask the price first, ignore anyone charging a "tip" to drive closer at the Blue Eye, and use only registered taxis.
  • For current advice read your government pages (such as the UK FCDO, US State Department or Australia Smartraveller) and call 112 in an emergency.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Albanian

  • "Përshëndetje" (per-shun-DET-yeh, hello), "Mirëmëngjes" (meer-mun-JES, good morning), "Mirëmbrëma" (meer-MUM-bruh-mah, good evening)
  • "Ju lutem" (yoo LOO-tem, please), "Faleminderit" (fah-leh-meen-DEH-reet, thank you), "Më falni" (muh FAHL-nee, excuse me)
  • "Sa kushton?" (sah koosh-TOHN, how much is it?), "Llogarinë, ju lutem" (loh-gah-REE-nuh, the bill, please)
  • "A flisni anglisht?" (ah FLEES-nee ahn-GLEESHT, do you speak English?), "Po" (yes), "Jo" (no), "Gëzuar" (guh-ZOO-ar, cheers)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Albania uses the lek (ALL). The rate is roughly 100 lek to the euro (check current). Euros are often accepted in tourist places, but change usually comes back in lek at a poorer rate.
  • Cards work in hotels and larger restaurants, but buses, the Blue Eye gate, small cafés and most sunbeds are cash only. Carry lek in small notes.
  • Use bank ATMs and decline conversion into your home currency when asked. Avoid street exchangers and compare exchange offices before changing a large sum.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🚌

Use the local bus, not a taxi

Buses between Sarandë, Ksamil and Butrint cost roughly 100 to 200 lek each way, against €20 to 40 for a taxi. Pay the driver in lek cash, and ask about the last bus back.

🥐

Eat byrek and shop at the bakery

A byrek from a bakery costs about €1 to 2 and fills you up. Buy water and fruit in shops away from the beach strip, where prices are lower than at the clubs.

🏖️

Time the beach, or go in the shoulder season

In peak summer, sunbeds can cost €10 to 30 a pair. Arrive before about 9 AM, or come in May or late September, when prices and crowds both drop.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

Have a coffee at Lëkurësi, not dinner

The terrace at Lëkurësi Castle gives the same sunset view as a dinner table, for the price of a coffee. Arrive early for a table, and walk or ride down before it is fully dark.

🌊

Go early at the Blue Eye

The spring is calm and the light is soft in the first hour, before the day-trip groups arrive. The shoulder months of April, May, September and October have far fewer visitors.

🏛️

Add Gjirokastër if you have a fifth day

The stone town deserves more than three hours, with its castle, bazaar and old houses. A night there gives you a quiet evening once the day-trippers have gone.

✈️ Flights

  • Tirana (TIA) is the main airport for Albania, about 4.5 to 5 hours from Sarandë by coach. There is no direct airport bus, so you change at the Tirana south bus terminal.
  • Corfu (CFU) in Greece is the other common gateway. Take the airport bus to the port, then a ferry or hydrofoil to Sarandë (about €20 to 30, check current).
  • Ioannina in Greece and the newer airport at Vlorë are possible alternatives, but onward bus links are thinner, so check before booking.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • A taxi from Tirana airport to Sarandë is about €200 at the official fixed rate. It is quicker but costs far more than the coach.
  • In Sarandë, taxis are cheapest when you agree a fixed price first: Ksamil is about €20 to 40, and a Blue Eye return with waiting time is about €25 to 50 (check current).
  • Ride apps such as Bolt operate in Tirana, but coverage in Sarandë is limited, so rely on agreed prices.

🚌 Public Transport & Tickets

  • Local buses and minibuses (furgons) run along the coast from near the ferry terminal. Sarandë to Ksamil is about 100 to 150 lek, and Butrint about 200 lek, with services every 20 to 30 minutes in summer.
  • Intercity coaches go to Gjirokastër (about 400 to 500 lek) and to Tirana (about 1,500 lek, or €15). Pay the driver in lek cash.
  • There is no central timetable, and services thin out in winter. Check times the day before, and ask if your bus stops at the Blue Eye turn-off.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Sarandë centre: walkable to the promenade, restaurants and bus stops, but busy and noisy in July and August.
  • Ksamil: close to the beaches and Butrint, with many apartments and quieter evenings, but you rely on the bus or a taxi to reach Sarandë.
  • The hillside above Sarandë: sea views and cooler nights, but steep walks and fewer shops.
  • Book early for July and August, and choose free cancellation. Many places close or cut their prices outside summer.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Albania is not in the EU, so EU roaming rules may not apply. Check your operator, or buy an eSIM or a local prepaid SIM.
  • Wi-Fi is common in hotels and cafés. Offline maps help on the inland roads.
  • Withdraw lek from bank ATMs, and carry small notes for buses, gates and sunbeds.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Most visitors, including those from the EU, UK, US, Canada and Australia, can enter visa-free for a stay of up to 90 days. Check your own nationality with the Albanian foreign ministry or your government advice before booking.
  • If you arrive by ferry from Corfu, expect passport control at Sarandë port and carry your passport.
  • Book summer ferries and rooms in advance. Winter has a reduced ferry timetable, and many places are closed.
  • Rental cars from Greece cannot be taken to Albania, and you need proper insurance for any car in Albania. This itinerary works without one.

About $300-500 per person for 4 days, roughly $75-125 a day, excluding international flights. The estimate covers lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and real costs vary with the season and your spending habits.
