5 Days in Marrakech: Medina Souks, Palaces & the High Atlas

Spice-stacked souks, a square that flips from snake charmers to smoke-and-drum food stalls after dark, and a cobalt-blue garden hidden behind a Medina wall. Five days deep in Marrakech's Red City, capped with one full day out in the High Atlas foothills.

Ideal SeasonMar – May, Oct – Nov
Est. Budget$600-850
Duration5 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Marrakech Menara Airport transfer & riad check-in

Petit taxis line up outside arrivals for the short ride into the Medina; agree the fare before getting in, since meters are rarely used for the airport run. Riads sit behind unmarked wooden doors, so most hosts meet you at a fixed nearby landmark and walk you in.

🚕 ~20 min by petit taxi 💵 ~$8-10 🕐 Confirm check-in time with your riad
Afternoon

First wander into Souk Semmarine

The covered main artery of the souks runs north from Jemaa el-Fnaa, its slatted roof throwing stripes of light over stalls of lanterns, leather goods, and stacked babouches. Get comfortably lost for an hour before doubling back for dinner.

🚶 steps from Jemaa el-Fnaa 💵 Free to wander 🕐 Mid-afternoon, before the evening crowds
Evening

Jemaa el-Fnaa by night

As the sun drops, the square transforms: food stalls fire up in rows, drummers and storytellers gather a crowd, and the smoke and noise turn the UNESCO-listed plaza into Marrakech's biggest open-air kitchen.

🚶 in the heart of the Medina 💵 ~$6-9 dinner at a stall 🕐 After sunset, busiest by 8 PM
Morning

Bahia Palace

Built in the 1860s-80s for a grand vizier, the palace's painted cedar ceilings and tiled courtyards were designed, in his own words, to be the greatest palace of its time. Arrive at opening to see the courtyards before the tour groups.

🚶 ~10 min from Jemaa el-Fnaa 💵 ~$7 🕐 Right at 9 AM opening
Afternoon

Saadian Tombs

Sealed up for centuries and rediscovered only in 1917, this small royal necropolis holds some of the finest zellige tilework and marble in the city. The entrance passage is narrow, so expect to queue in high season.

🚶 ~12 min walk from Bahia Palace 💵 ~$7 🕐 Early afternoon
Evening

Rooftop dinner with Koutoubia Mosque views

The Koutoubia's 12th-century minaret, the model for Seville's Giralda, is lit gold after dark. A handful of rooftop terraces near Jemaa el-Fnaa line up a direct view of it over a tagine and mint tea.

🚶 rooftop terraces near the square 💵 ~$18-25 dinner 🕐 Sunset onward
Morning

Spice, leather & dye souks

Beyond Souk Semmarine, the trail splits into specialist alleys: cones of paprika and cumin in the spice souk, tanned leather bags and babouches, and the open dye pits of Souk des Teinturiers where wool is still hand-dyed in vivid vats.

🚶 walkable Medina alleys 💵 Free to browse, bargain hard on purchases 🕐 Morning, before the midday heat
Afternoon

Ben Youssef Madrasa

Once North Africa's largest Quranic school, this 16th-century Saadian-era madrasa is a masterclass in carved stucco, cedar, and zellige tile wrapped around a reflecting-pool courtyard.

🚶 ~5 min from the souks 💵 ~$7 🕐 Mid-afternoon
Evening

Traditional hammam & massage

A black-soap scrub and steam-room soak at a neighborhood hammam is as central to Marrakech life as the souks themselves. Book a traditional public-style hammam for the real experience, or a private spa version for more comfort.

🚶 hammams throughout the Medina 💵 ~$15-40 depending on style 🕐 Late afternoon, before dinner
Morning

Jardin Majorelle & the YSL Museum

French painter Jacques Majorelle spent decades building this garden of cactus and bamboo around his signature cobalt-blue villa; Yves Saint Laurent and Pierre Bergé later restored it, and the adjacent museum now holds Saint Laurent's archive.

🚕 ~15 min taxi from the Medina 💵 ~$16 combined garden & museum 🕐 Right at opening, it fills up fast
Afternoon

Wander Gueliz, the Ville Nouvelle

Marrakech's French-built new town is a change of pace entirely: tree-lined boulevards, concept stores, and cafe terraces instead of souk alleys, good for a slower afternoon and any last souvenir shopping.

🚶 walkable from Jardin Majorelle 💵 ~$10-15 cafe lunch 🕐 Afternoon
Evening

Le Jardin Secret at golden hour

Tucked behind an unmarked door in the Medina, this restored 19th-century riad garden splits into an Islamic and an exotic garden, with a small tower for one more skyline view before dinner.

🚶 back in the Medina 💵 ~$8 entry 🕐 Late afternoon, before closing
Morning

Drive into the High Atlas foothills

The road south from Marrakech climbs quickly into the Ourika Valley, swapping palm groves for terraced fields, mudbrick Berber villages, and the Atlas peaks rising ahead.

🚗 ~1h drive 💵 ~$35-50 shared day tour 🕐 Depart by 9 AM
Afternoon

Berber villages & the Setti Fatma waterfalls

From the village of Setti Fatma, a marked trail climbs past a series of waterfalls tumbling down the valley wall; the first two pools are an easy walk, with mules and guides on hand for anyone going further.

🚶 ~45 min easy hike to the first falls 💵 ~$5 optional guide 🕐 Midday
Evening

Return to Marrakech & a farewell dinner

Back in the Medina by early evening, close out the trip with a last dinner near Jemaa el-Fnaa as the square settles into its nightly rhythm one more time.

🚗 ~1h drive back 💵 ~$15-20 dinner 🕐 Evening

💵 Tipping Etiquette

  • Not obligatory, but round up or add roughly 10% at sit-down restaurants.
  • A few dirhams for porters, parking attendants ("gardiens"), and hammam attendants is customary.
  • Riad staff who go out of their way often receive a modest tip at checkout.

👗 Dress Code

  • Cover shoulders and knees, especially outside the tourist-heavy stretches of the Medina and Gueliz.
  • Lightweight, loose clothing works better than shorts and tank tops, both for respect and for comfort in the sun.
  • Swimwear is fine at riad pools, not out in the streets or souks.

🛍️ Bargaining in the Souks

  • Haggling is expected at souk stalls, not in fixed-price shops or supermarkets.
  • Start around a third to half of the first quoted price and meet somewhere in the middle.
  • Walking away is a normal negotiating tactic and often brings the price down further.

🕌 Mosque Etiquette & Ramadan

  • Non-Muslims cannot enter most working mosques in Morocco, including the Koutoubia, so admire the exteriors and minarets instead.
  • During Ramadan, avoid eating, drinking, or smoking in public during daylight hours out of respect.
  • Restaurant hours shift around the sunset iftar meal during Ramadan, so confirm opening times if traveling then.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Jemaa el-Fnaa and the souks are generally safe but crowded; keep bags zipped and in front, especially at night.
  • Sun and heat are serious outside the shoulder season; carry water and pace outdoor sightseeing for the cooler hours.
  • Snake charmers and henna artists in the square can approach assertively for photos or henna; a firm decline works, and agree a price upfront if you do want either.

🧭 Getting Around the Medina

  • The Medina's alleys are a genuine maze; GPS often struggles with narrow, unnamed passages, so screenshot directions or note nearby landmarks.
  • Self-appointed "guides" may offer to walk you somewhere for a tip; a polite "la, shukran" (no, thank you) works if you'd rather navigate alone.
  • Petit taxis and Careem cover longer distances between the Medina and Gueliz cheaply.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Darija/French

  • Salam: hello
  • Shukran: thank you
  • Bezaf: too much (useful when bargaining)

💳 Money & Payments

  • The Moroccan Dirham (MAD) is a closed currency and is difficult or impossible to buy before you arrive.
  • Cash is still king in the souks and small cafes; cards are common in riads, restaurants, and larger shops.
  • ATMs are widespread in the Medina and Gueliz; check your card's foreign transaction fees before you go.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🍵

Eat where the vendors eat

Stalls without English menus around the edges of Jemaa el-Fnaa are the cheapest and often the best food in the square.

🛍️

Bargain in dirham, not dollars

Negotiating in the local currency keeps prices grounded and avoids inflated "tourist math."

🚕

Fix taxi fares before boarding

A few dirhams' negotiation upfront beats an inflated meter-less fare after the fact.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌇

A rooftop cafe over the souks

A handful of unmarked rooftop cafes tucked above the souk alleys give a free, crowd-free view over the Medina's rooftops.

🏺

Smaller museums beyond the palace circuit

The Marrakech Museum and Dar Si Said hold ceramics and crafts with a fraction of Bahia Palace's crowds.

🌵

Agafay Desert over a full Sahara trip

For travelers short on time, the rocky Agafay desert is about 45 minutes from the city and gives a taste of desert camp life without the two-day Sahara drive.

✈️ Flights

  • Marrakech Menara Airport (RAK) sits about 15-20 minutes from the Medina, close enough that a day-one nap isn't necessary.
  • Direct flights run from major European hubs, with many other routes connecting through Casablanca.
  • Most Western passport holders don't need a visa for short tourist stays; confirm the current rule for your nationality before booking.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Petit taxis (small, beige or khaki cars) handle short in-city hops; agree the fare before getting in, since meters are rarely used with tourists.
  • Careem and inDrive both operate in Marrakech and tend to be more transparent on price than flagging a taxi on the street.
  • Grand taxis (shared, older sedans) run fixed routes out of the city, useful for day trips if you're not booking a tour.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Not necessary for the Medina days: everything from Jemaa el-Fnaa to the souks and palaces is walkable.
  • Driving inside the Medina itself isn't possible or advisable, its alleys are pedestrian and often too narrow for cars.
  • For the Ourika Valley day trip, a shared or private tour driver is simpler than self-driving mountain roads you don't know.

🏨 Riads & Where to Stay

  • A riad, a traditional house built around a central courtyard, is the classic Marrakech stay; most sit behind unmarked doors deep in the Medina.
  • Most riad hosts meet you at a fixed nearby landmark and walk you in, since exact addresses are nearly useless in the alleys.
  • Gueliz hotels trade the Medina's atmosphere for easier street access and a shorter taxi ride to the airport.

🧭 Medina Navigation

  • GPS apps frequently lose the plot in the Medina's narrow, unnamed alleys; screenshot directions in advance and note a landmark near your riad.
  • Save your riad's phone number: staff can often talk you in by phone if you get turned around.
  • The Koutoubia minaret is visible above the rooftops from much of the Medina and makes a reliable way to reorient yourself.

💱 Currency Exchange

  • The Moroccan Dirham (MAD) is a closed currency and can be difficult or impossible to buy outside Morocco.
  • Exchange bureaus at the airport and around Jemaa el-Fnaa offer competitive rates, generally better than hotels.
  • Keep a few dirhams aside to reconvert before departure, since they're hard to offload once you've left the country.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Travel insurance is worth carrying for the Ourika Valley excursion and any hammam or spa treatments booked independently.
  • Pack for a genuine temperature swing: the Medina runs hot even in shoulder season, while the Atlas foothills can be notably cooler.
  • Summer (roughly June-September) brings brutal heat; the Mar-May and Oct-Nov shoulder seasons are the honest sweet spot for this itinerary.
