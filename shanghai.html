5 Days in Shanghai: The Bund, Yu Garden & Zhujiajiao

From neon towers rising over the Huangpu River to a lantern-lit Ming garden and a canal town frozen in time, five days is enough to feel both Shanghai's speed and its quieter past.

Ideal SeasonMar – May, Sep – Nov
Est. Budget$800-1,100
Duration5 Days
PaceActive
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Pudong International Airport & transfer into the city

Land at PVG and ride the Maglev into Longyang Road station in about 8 minutes, one of the fastest airport transfers anywhere, then connect to the metro for the last leg downtown.

🚄 ~8 min Maglev + ~20 min metro 💵 ~$10 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time
Afternoon

Check-in & a first walk along the Bund promenade

Drop your bags and walk the riverside promenade in daylight first, past the colonial-era buildings on the Bund's western bank, so the skyline transformation at dusk lands even harder.

🚶 Walkable from most central hotels 💵 Free 🕐 Mid-afternoon, before the evening crowds
Evening

The Bund at dusk, facing the Pudong skyline

Stake out a spot along the railing as the Oriental Pearl Tower, Jin Mao Tower, and Shanghai Tower light up across the Huangpu River, Shanghai's single most iconic view.

🚶 Along the Bund's riverside walkway 💵 Free 🕐 Just after sunset, when the skyline lights switch on
Morning

Yu Garden, early

This Ming-dynasty garden of rockeries, ponds, and pavilions is at its calmest right at opening, before the tour groups arrive.

🚇 Metro to Yuyuan Garden station 💵 ~$5 🕐 Right at the 8:30 AM opening, before tour groups
Midday

Yuyuan Bazaar's old streets

The bazaar surrounding the garden is packed with snack stalls and souvenir shops under curved tile roofs; queue for xiaolongbao at Nanxiang Mantou Dian if the line looks manageable.

🚶 Surrounds Yu Garden's entrance 💵 ~$8 (snacks) 🕐 Late morning, before the lunch rush
Evening

Nanjing Road Pedestrian Street

Shanghai's mile-long shopping street turns into a wall of neon after dark; ride the small sightseeing tram if your feet need a break.

🚇 Metro to East Nanjing Road station 💵 ~$2 (tram, optional) 🕐 After 7 PM, once the neon signs are lit
Morning

Tree-lined streets of the former French Concession

Wander Wukang Road and the surrounding lanes, where plane trees arch over Art Deco villas and quiet cafes, a completely different pace from downtown.

🚇 Metro to Jiaotong University station 💵 Free to wander 🕐 Mid-morning, when the streets are quiet
Afternoon

Tianzifang's lanes

A maze of narrow alleys packed with independent boutiques, art studios, and craft stalls, built into a former residential longtang neighborhood.

🚶 ~15 min walk or short metro ride 💵 Free to browse 🕐 Early afternoon, before the evening crowds pack the alleys
Evening

Cafe hopping & dinner in the Concession

The French Concession has Shanghai's best concentration of independent cafes and small restaurants; pick one with outdoor seating under the plane trees.

🚶 Along Anfu Road or Julu Road 💵 ~$25 (dinner) 🕐 7 to 9 PM
Morning

Transfer to Zhujiajiao & the old street

A direct bus or a private car gets you to this canal town about an hour outside the city; the old street along the water fills up fast, so arrive as early as you can.

🚌 ~1 hr bus, or ~50 min by car 💵 ~$3 bus, ~$25 car 🕐 Depart by 8 AM to beat the day-trip buses
Midday

Canal boat ride & Fangsheng Bridge

A short hand-poled boat ride along the canals passes under Fangsheng Bridge, the town's largest stone bridge, with whitewashed Ming and Qing-era houses lining the banks.

🚣 Boats depart from the main canal dock 💵 ~$12 shared boat 🕐 Late morning, before the midday tour groups peak
Afternoon

Old street snacks & the return trip

Try the local specialty braised pork (zhu titou) from a street stall before heading back into the city in time for a quieter evening.

🚌 ~1 hr bus back to Shanghai 💵 ~$5 (snacks) 🕐 Head back by mid-afternoon to avoid rush hour
Morning

Shanghai Tower or Oriental Pearl Tower observation deck

Go up whichever tower fits your budget: the Shanghai Tower's deck is the higher and more dramatic of the two, while the Oriental Pearl Tower's glass-floor level looks straight down over the Bund.

🚇 Metro to Lujiazui station 💵 ~$40 Shanghai Tower, ~$25 Pearl Tower 🕐 Right at opening, for the clearest visibility
Midday

Lujiazui & a last riverside lunch

The malls and riverside walkways beneath the towers make an easy final few hours, with the Bund itself visible just across the water.

🚶 Around Lujiazui's riverside park 💵 ~$15 (lunch) 🕐 Early afternoon
Evening

Transfer to Pudong Airport for departure

Loop back to the Maglev at Longyang Road for the fastest possible ride to the airport, and budget extra buffer time for an international flight.

🚄 ~8 min Maglev + metro connection 💵 ~$10 🕐 Leave at least 3 hrs before an international flight

💴 Tipping Etiquette

  • Tipping is not customary anywhere in mainland China: restaurants, taxis, hotels, and tour guides are all fine without one.
  • Trying to leave a tip can sometimes confuse or embarrass service staff, who may chase you down to hand the money back.
  • High-end hotels catering to international guests are the rare exception where a small gratuity is quietly accepted.

👗 Dress Code

  • Smart-casual works for the vast majority of restaurants and rooftop bars along the Bund and in the French Concession.
  • Yu Garden and other temple courtyards don't enforce a strict dress code, but modest clothing is still appreciated.
  • Comfortable, broken-in walking shoes matter more than style, given the cobblestones of Tianzifang and the long Bund promenade.

🚇 Getting Around Shanghai

  • The metro network is vast, clean, and cheap, and covers every stop on this itinerary except the Zhujiajiao day trip.
  • Google Maps doesn't work reliably in mainland China; download Amap (Gaode Maps) or Baidu Maps before you land.
  • Add transit to Alipay or buy a Shanghai Public Transportation Card on arrival to skip single-ticket machines entirely.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Shanghai is a very low-crime city; the main risk is pickpocketing in crowded tourist spots like Yu Garden's bazaar and Nanjing Road.
  • Jaywalking is common among locals but technically illegal, and scooters and e-bikes often ignore crosswalks, so stay alert.
  • Many Western social media and messaging apps are blocked entirely, which is why a VPN is worth arranging before arrival, not after.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Mandarin

  • Ni hao: hello
  • Xie xie: thank you
  • Zhe ge duo shao qian?: how much is this?

💳 Money & Payments

  • China is overwhelmingly a mobile-payment society: Alipay and WeChat Pay cover nearly everything, from taxis to street snacks.
  • Foreign credit cards work at international hotels and some larger stores, but surprisingly little else, and cash isn't as widely used as visitors expect.
  • Set up Alipay's tourist mode with a linked foreign card before you land; it's the most reliable way to pay like a local for five days.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🥟

Eat where the queue is local

Nanxiang Mantou Dian's side counter sells the same xiaolongbao as the sit-down restaurant for a fraction of the price.

🚇

Load transit onto Alipay

Skip single-ticket machines entirely; it works out cheaper over five days of metro rides than buying tickets one at a time.

🎫

Book observation deck tickets online ahead

Shanghai Tower and Oriental Pearl Tower same-day walk-up prices run noticeably higher than advance online booking.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🚲

Wukang Road's side lanes

Quieter than Tianzifang's main alley, with the same tree-lined French Concession charm and far fewer tour groups.

🌅

Zhujiajiao before 9 AM

The canal town's day-trip buses arrive around 10; the early morning is nearly yours alone.

🌆

The Bund Sightseeing Tunnel over the tourist ferry

A cheesy but functional way to cross under the river to Pudong when the ferry queue is too long.

✈️ Flights

  • Shanghai Pudong International Airport (PVG) is the main international gateway, with Hongqiao (SHA) handling mostly domestic and regional routes.
  • The Shanghai Maglev train covers the roughly 30 km from Pudong Airport to Longyang Road station in about 8 minutes, reaching speeds over 300 km/h, one of the fastest airport transfers in the world.
  • From Longyang Road, metro Line 2 connects directly into the city center, including the Bund and People's Square.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Uber doesn't operate in mainland China; Didi is the local rideshare app and works much the same way, with an English-language option in its settings.
  • Metered taxis are plentiful and cheap, though few drivers speak English, so having your destination written in Chinese characters helps.
  • Didi and taxis both get stuck in Lujiazui and Bund-area traffic at rush hour, when the metro is usually faster.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Not practical for this itinerary or for most foreign visitors: a Chinese driving permit and vehicle registration process make short-term self-driving impractical.
  • Every stop on this itinerary sits on the metro network or is a short taxi or Didi ride away.
  • Skip it entirely and put the budget toward the Maglev, Didi rides, and the Zhujiajiao day trip instead.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • The Bund or People's Square: puts Day 1's skyline views and Day 2's Nanjing Road and Yu Garden within easy walking or a short metro ride.
  • French Concession (near Xintiandi or Wukang Road): a leafy, cafe-lined base for Day 3, with easy metro access back to the center.
  • Lujiazui, on the Pudong side: convenient for Day 5's Shanghai Tower or Oriental Pearl Tower visit and for departure via the Maglev.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • This itinerary has no real boat leg as transport: the closest thing is the short canal boat ride through Zhujiajiao on Day 4, more of a local sightseeing activity than a way to get anywhere.
  • A Huangpu River night cruise past the Bund and Pudong skyline is a popular add-on if you want actual boat time, but it's optional, not essential.
  • Beyond that, the metro, Maglev, and Didi cover everything else in this plan.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Most Western travelers need a visa for China arranged in advance, though some nationalities qualify for a 144-hour transit exemption through Shanghai; confirm your specific case before booking flights.
  • A VPN app, installed and tested before departure, is close to essential for reaching Google, WhatsApp, and most Western social media and news apps.
  • Plan around China's mobile-payment reality: set up Alipay or WeChat Pay with a linked foreign card before arrival, since cash and foreign cards cover far less than in most other countries.
