5 Days in Los Angeles: A Local-Style Itinerary and Budget

Hollywood and Griffith Observatory, a Downtown day by Metro, the Getty Center and the beaches by rideshare, plus an optional Universal day: five days in Los Angeles with real costs.

Ideal SeasonMar to May or Sep to Nov

Duration5 Days

5-day Los Angeles itinerary

How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at LAX & the way into town

The FlyAway bus runs from LAX to Union Station for $12.75, about 30 to 45 minutes; tap a contactless card to board. By Metro, take the free shuttle from the terminal's lower level to LAX/Metro Transit Center, then the K, E and B Lines to Hollywood: 70 to 90 minutes for $1.75. Rideshare pick-up is at LAX-it.

🚌 FlyAway ~30-45 min to Union Station, or Metro ~70-90 min 💵 FlyAway $12.75, Metro $1.75, rideshare ~$40-70 (check current) 🕐 Allow extra time between 4 and 7 PM, when the freeways are slowest
Afternoon

Hollywood Boulevard, the Walk of Fame & the Chinese Theatre

The Metro B Line stops at Hollywood/Highland, steps from the TCL Chinese Theatre, where celebrity handprints fill the forecourt. The Walk of Fame stars line Hollywood Boulevard, free all day. The upper decks at Ovation Hollywood frame the Hollywood Sign. Skip the costumed characters, who ask for tips, and watch your bag: the boulevard feels rougher after dark.

🚇 B Line to Hollywood/Highland, then on foot 💵 Free (Metro $1.75) 🕐 Early afternoon, before the evening crowds
Evening

Sunset at Griffith Observatory

Griffith Observatory is free: open Tuesday to Friday 12 to 10 PM, weekends 10 AM to 10 PM, closed Mondays. Take the B Line to Vermont/Sunset, then the DASH Observatory bus (50 cents, 35 with a TAP card, every 20 to 25 minutes). Parking is paid and limited. Arrive an hour before sunset for the best light.

🚇 B Line to Vermont/Sunset, then ~20 min on the DASH bus 💵 Free entry, DASH 50 cents, dinner ~$15-25 🕐 About 1 hour before sunset, closed Mondays
Morning

Union Station & Olvera Street

Union Station opened in 1939, and its waiting hall is free to walk through. Olvera Street, in El Pueblo where the city began in 1781, is across the road, with market stalls and Mexican snacks. The B Line from Hollywood/Highland to Union Station takes roughly 25 minutes for $1.75. Go before 10 AM to avoid the tour groups.

🚇 B Line to Union Station, ~25 min 💵 Free, snacks ~$5-10 🕐 Before 10 AM, when it is quietest
Afternoon

Grand Central Market, Angels Flight & The Broad

Grand Central Market is open daily 8 AM to 9 PM, and a stall lunch costs roughly $10 to $18. Across Hill Street, Angels Flight climbs Bunker Hill for $1.50 ($0.75 with TAP). The Broad is about 5 minutes from the top: free, closed Mondays, and best reserved as a timed ticket, though a standby line runs daily.

🚇 B Line two stops to Pershing Square, then on foot 💵 Broad free, lunch ~$10-18, Angels Flight $1.50 🕐 Lunch at 12, The Broad from 11 AM (Tue-Fri) or 10 AM (weekends)
Evening

Walt Disney Concert Hall & dinner in the Arts District

The Walt Disney Concert Hall is next door to The Broad. Frank Gehry's steel exterior and the public garden are free to see, while concerts need tickets. Then ride the A or E Line to Little Tokyo/Arts District for murals, breweries and dinner, with mains at roughly $20 to $35. Check the last train back before a late meal.

🚶 ~2 min on foot, then A or E Line two stops 💵 Exterior free, dinner ~$20-35 🕐 Golden hour for the steel, dinner from 6 PM
Morning

The Getty Center

The Getty Center is free but needs a timed reservation booked online, and it is closed Mondays. Parking is $25, or $15 after 3 PM, and a tram climbs the hill. Metro bus 761 or a rideshare drop-off avoids the fee. It closes to the public from 15 March 2027 for about a year, so check dates.

🚕 Rideshare ~25-40 min from Hollywood, or bus 761 💵 Free entry, parking $25 or $15 after 3 PM (check current) 🕐 Opens at 10 AM, Tue-Fri and Sun to 6:30 PM, Sat to 9 PM (check current)
Afternoon

Santa Monica Pier

A rideshare from the Getty to Santa Monica Pier takes about 20 minutes. The pier is free to walk on and open daily 6 AM to 10 PM; you pay only for Pacific Park rides (check current hours). The Metro E Line ends at Downtown Santa Monica, a few blocks away. Eat a block inland for lower prices.

🚕 ~20 min by rideshare from the Getty 💵 Pier and beach free, rides extra, lunch ~$12-20 🕐 Mid-afternoon, with the beach light building
Evening

Venice Beach Boardwalk & Abbot Kinney

Walk or cycle the beach path south from the pier, about 2.5 miles, to the Venice Boardwalk. Expect street performers, a skate park and open-air gyms. Watch the sunset from the sand, then eat inland on Abbot Kinney Boulevard. Keep bags with you, and take a rideshare to Hollywood after dark ($30 to $55, check current).

🚶 ~2.5 miles on the beach path, or a short rideshare 💵 Free, dinner ~$20-35, rideshare back ~$30-55 (check current) 🕐 Arrive an hour before sunset
Morning

Metro to Universal & the Studio Tour

Take the B Line to Universal City/Studio City, then cross the bridge to the free hill shuttle. One-day tickets start around $110 online and rise on busy dates (check current). Ride the Studio Tour tram early, when waits are short; it passes film sets such as the Psycho house. Skipping it saves about $110 or more.

🚇 B Line to Universal City (one stop from Hollywood/Highland), then the free shuttle 💵 Ticket from ~$110, more on busy dates (check current) 🕐 Arrive at opening, hours vary by date (check current)
Afternoon

The Wizarding World of Harry Potter & Super Nintendo World

Head for the Wizarding World of Harry Potter and Super Nintendo World after the tour. Queues usually build through midday, so check wait times in the app and use single-rider lines where offered. Food inside the park is pricey (check menus), so eat a good breakfast and carry a refillable bottle for the heat.

🚶 On foot inside the park 💵 Included in the ticket, meals ~$15-25 (check current) 🕐 Early afternoon, then check wait times in the app
Evening

Universal CityWalk & back by Metro

CityWalk, the outdoor street of restaurants and neon at the park entrance, is free to walk and stays open into the evening. Parking is paid (check current), so take the shuttle down to the Metro: the B Line to Hollywood/Highland is one stop, about 5 minutes. For a free alternative to the park, ride the A Line to Pasadena.

🚇 Shuttle to the station, then B Line ~5 min 💵 CityWalk free, dinner ~$20-35, Metro $1.75 🕐 After the park closes, trains run until late
Morning

Lake Hollywood Park & Runyon Canyon

Take a rideshare, about 20 minutes, to Lake Hollywood Park, a free park with a long view of the Hollywood Sign across the reservoir; street parking is very limited. The sign itself is not open to visitors. For a longer walk, Runyon Canyon Park is free from sunrise to sunset and has no toilets (check trail closures).

🚕 ~20 min by rideshare from Hollywood 💵 Free, rideshare ~$15-25 each way (check current) 🕐 Early morning, for cooler air and clearer views
Afternoon

Rodeo Drive & Beverly Gardens Park

Rodeo Drive and Beverly Gardens Park, with its Beverly Hills sign and long lawns, are free to walk. A rideshare from Hollywood takes 20 to 30 minutes and costs roughly $20 to $35 (check current). Window-shop on Rodeo, then have lunch a few blocks away: a casual counter costs far less than a sit-down meal on the drive.

🚕 ~20-30 min by rideshare, then on foot 💵 Free, lunch ~$12-20 🕐 Late morning to mid-afternoon, shops open from about 10 AM
Evening

Transfer to LAX

Reach LAX at least 3 hours before an international flight, 2 for domestic. A rideshare or taxi costs about $40 to $70 depending on traffic (check current); the FlyAway from Union Station is $12.75. By Metro, the B, E and K Lines plus the free shuttle take 70 to 90 minutes. Rideshare drops you at the departures level.

🚕 ~30-60 min by rideshare, traffic permitting 💵 Rideshare ~$40-70, FlyAway $12.75, Metro $1.75 (check current) 🕐 Leave at least 3 hours before an international flight

Los Angeles: what to know before you go

💵 Tipping, Sales Tax & Hotel Fees

  • Tipping is expected in the US: 15 to 20% in sit-down restaurants, $1 to $2 per drink at a bar, and a tip for rideshare drivers is customary but optional. Service is rarely included, so check the bill.
  • Menu and shelf prices exclude sales tax, which is added at the till (roughly 9.5 to 10%, check current). Self-service counters often show a tip screen, where a small tip or none is fine.
  • Hotels add occupancy tax (around 14% in the City of Los Angeles, check current), and many charge a daily parking or resort fee, so compare the total price before booking.

🌮 Eating & Drinking Like an Angeleno

  • Tacos from a truck or stall, Korean barbecue in Koreatown, and burgers at In-N-Out are local staples. Food halls such as Grand Central Market give a cheap lunch in one stop.
  • Tap water is safe to drink and restaurants bring it free. Bring photo ID, as you must be 21 to buy alcohol, and drinking in public is banned, including on beaches and in parks.
  • Dinner service is busiest from about 6:30 to 9 PM, and popular places need a reservation on Friday and Saturday. A portion is often large enough to share.

🚗 Traffic, Parking & Getting Around

  • Los Angeles is built around the car. Freeways clog from about 7 to 10 AM and 4 to 7 PM, so a 10-mile trip can take 15 minutes or over an hour.
  • Metro covers Hollywood, Downtown, Santa Monica and Universal. Griffith Observatory, the Getty Center and Beverly Hills need a bus, the DASH shuttle or a rideshare.
  • Read every parking sign: street cleaning, permit zones and short time limits all lead to tickets. Never leave anything visible in a parked car.

⚠️ Safety, Scams & Etiquette Warnings

  • Hollywood Boulevard has costumed characters who ask for tips after a photo, and people who press a free CD or tour flyer into your hand and then ask for money. Say no and keep walking.
  • Car break-ins are common. Take valuables with you, and keep phones and wallets in a front pocket or zipped bag on Metro and on crowded streets.
  • Parts of Downtown, including streets east of Main Street, have large homeless encampments. Use Metro or rideshare after dark and avoid walking alone through quiet blocks.
  • Check your own government's travel advice for the USA and heat or wildfire alerts before you go. The emergency number is 911.

🗣️ Useful Phrases in American English & Spanish

  • "For here or to go?" (eating in or taking away), "Could we get the check, please?" (the bill)
  • "I have a reservation under..." (hotel or restaurant), "Do you take cards?" (can I pay by card)
  • "Hola" (OH-lah, hello), "Gracias" (GRAH-see-ahs, thank you), "Por favor" (por fah-VOR, please)
  • "¿Cuánto cuesta?" (KWAN-toh KWES-tah, how much is it?), "La cuenta, por favor" (lah KWEN-tah, the bill, please)

💳 Money & Payments

  • The US dollar is the only currency. Cards and contactless work almost everywhere, but carry a little cash for tips and small stalls.
  • Use bank-branded ATMs, and if a terminal offers to charge you in your home currency, choose dollars. Hotels usually put a deposit hold on your card at check-in.
  • For Metro, buy a TAP card at a station machine or add one to your phone wallet through the TAP app, then load stored value. Tapping gets you the daily and weekly fare caps.

Insider tips for Los Angeles

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🚇

Let Metro cap your fares

Metro charges $1.75 a ride with free transfers within 2 hours, and stops charging at $5 in a day and $18 in a week when you tap with a TAP card or phone. Cash on buses does not count towards the cap.

🆓

Build days around free entry

Griffith Observatory, The Broad, the Getty Center, Olvera Street, the Walk of Fame and the public beaches cost nothing to enter. The extras are parking and reserved slots, so these days are far cheaper than a theme-park day.

🍽️

Eat at food halls and counters

Grand Central Market stalls cost roughly $10 to $18 a meal, and food trucks and taco counters are cheaper still. Restaurant portions are large, so sharing a main saves money, and tap water is free.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🚆

Swap Universal for Pasadena

The Metro A Line runs from Union Station to Pasadena in about 30 minutes for the standard fare. Old Pasadena has historic streets and cafes, and the Rose Bowl Flea Market runs on the second Sunday of each month (check current).

🏛️

Visit the Getty Villa

The Getty Center closes to the public from 15 March 2027 for about a year, but the Getty Villa in Pacific Palisades stays open with Greek and Roman art and gardens. Admission is free with a timed reservation (check current).

🌅

Beat the traffic and the crowds

Freeways clog from 7 to 10 AM and 4 to 7 PM. Start at opening, plan longer drives between 10 AM and 3 PM, and save sunset for the coast or Griffith Observatory.

Travel services for Los Angeles

✈️ Flights

  • Los Angeles International (LAX) is the main airport, roughly 15 to 20 miles from Hollywood and Downtown, with free shuttles to the LAX/Metro Transit Center and a FlyAway bus to Union Station.
  • Burbank (BUR) is much closer to Hollywood and Universal. Long Beach (LGB), Ontario (ONT) and John Wayne (SNA) can be cheaper for some routes, but add ground time.
  • The LAX Automated People Mover was still not open to the public at last check (early 2027 was reported), so the shuttle to the Metro Transit Center remains the rail link.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Rideshare pick-up at LAX is at LAX-it, just east of Terminal 1 (about a 3-minute walk), with a free shuttle from the other terminals. Drop-offs for rideshare and taxis are at the departures level.
  • Taxis from LAX use flat fares to set areas, for example roughly $50 to $60 to Downtown (check current). Uber and Lyft prices vary with demand, so compare in the app.
  • Sharing a rideshare between two to four people usually costs less than Metro plus buses on the Getty and Beverly Hills days.

🚇 Public Transport & Tickets

  • LA Metro runs the B, D, A, E, K and C Lines and the buses. A ride costs $1.75, transfers are free within 2 hours, and fares cap at $5 a day and $18 a week when you tap (check current).
  • The B Line serves Hollywood, Union Station, Pershing Square and Universal City. The E Line goes to Santa Monica, and the A Line goes to Pasadena.
  • Griffith Observatory is reached by DASH bus from Vermont/Sunset (50 cents). The Getty Center is served by Metro bus 761. Beverly Hills and Rodeo Drive are easiest by rideshare.
  • Keep bags zipped on trains and sit near other riders late at night.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Hollywood: close to the B Line, Walk of Fame and Griffith bus, so it works for four of the five days, but the boulevard is busy and some blocks are rough.
  • Downtown Los Angeles: central on Metro and walkable to the Broad and Grand Central Market, though quiet and less lively at night outside a few areas.
  • Santa Monica: pleasant and walkable near the beach and the E Line, but it is the priciest base and far from Hollywood.
  • Koreatown and Mid-Wilshire: good-value rooms with Metro access, and some of the city's best late-night food.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • US networks work well in the city, though signal can drop in canyons. Visitors can use an eSIM, a prepaid SIM or international roaming, and many cafes and hotels have Wi-Fi.
  • Cards and contactless work nearly everywhere, but keep a few dollars in cash for tips and small stalls.
  • Use bank ATMs and decline dynamic currency conversion.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Check entry rules before you book: many visitors need an ESTA (about $40, check current) or a visa to enter the USA. Do this before you fly.
  • You can do Days 1, 2 and 4 by Metro. A rental car runs roughly $45 to $90 a day, plus fuel and $10 to $40 for parking (check current), so compare it with rideshare for Days 3 and 5.
  • Griffith Observatory, the Getty Center and The Broad are closed on Mondays, so check your arrival day and swap the order if needed.
  • The Getty Center is scheduled to close to the public from 15 March 2027 for about a year. The emergency number is 911.

How much does a 5-day trip to Los Angeles cost?

About $800-1,050 per person for 5 days, roughly $160-210 a day, excluding international flights. The estimate covers lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and real costs vary with the season and your spending habits.
