4 Days in Bordeaux: Old Town, La Cité du Vin & Saint-Émilion

A UNESCO-listed riverfront of honey-colored stone, a wine museum shaped like wine swirling in a glass, and a full day among the vines and monolithic church of Saint-Émilion, four unhurried days through France's wine capital.

Ideal SeasonMay – Jun, Sep
Est. Budget$750-1,000
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrive & check in near the Old Town

The TGV from Paris Montparnasse takes just over two hours into Gare Saint-Jean; flying in, the Jet'Bus shuttle covers the 45 minutes from Bordeaux-Mérignac Airport into the center (the tram doesn't reach the airport). Either way, settle into the Port of the Moon, the crescent-shaped old town along the Garonne.

🚄 TGV ~2h from Paris, or 🚌 Jet'Bus ~45 min from BOD 💵 ~€45-75 TGV, ~€8 Jet'Bus 🕐 After 3 PM check-in
Afternoon

Place de la Bourse & the Miroir d'Eau

The 18th-century Place de la Bourse faces the world's largest reflecting pool, a thin sheet of water that cycles into a rolling mist every fifteen minutes or so. Go barefoot if it's warm; locals and kids do the same.

🚶 Central, on the riverfront quay 💵 Free 🕐 Late afternoon, for the mist cycle in good light
Evening

Rue Sainte-Catherine & a first Bordelaise dinner

Europe's longest pedestrian shopping street runs straight through the old town; duck off it into the side streets near Place du Parlement for a first dinner of duck, oysters, or a simple steak-frites.

🚶 Runs through the Old Town 💵 ~€25-35 dinner 🕐 8-9 PM dinner
Morning

Cathédrale Saint-André & the Pey-Berland tower

The cathedral itself is free to enter; next door, the separate 15th-century Pey-Berland bell tower is a 233-step climb to the best rooftop panorama in the city, well worth the legwork before the day heats up.

🚋 Tram A or C to Hôtel de Ville 💵 Free cathedral, ~€6 tower climb 🕐 Right at the 10 AM opening
Afternoon

La Cité du Vin

This swirl of glass and aluminum, designed to evoke wine turning in a glass, houses an immersive wine-culture museum covering the whole world, not just Bordeaux. Every ticket ends with a tasting on the 8th-floor Belvedere, glass in hand, city spread out below.

🚋 Tram B to Cité du Vin 💵 ~€22, tasting included 🕐 Book a timed-entry slot ahead
Evening

Wine bars in the Chartrons quarter

The old wine-merchants' district, a short walk or one tram stop from La Cité du Vin, is now lined with natural-wine bars and antique shops inside the same 18th-century warehouses that once stored barrels for export.

🚶 15 min walk from Cité du Vin 💵 ~€6-9 per glass 🕐 Early evening, before dinner
Morning

The monolithic church & village streets

A direct TER regional train from Gare Saint-Jean reaches Saint-Émilion in about 35-40 minutes. Spend the morning on the cobbled village lanes and inside Europe's largest monolithic church, an entire 12th-century church carved from one block of limestone.

🚆 TER train, ~35-40 min from Gare Saint-Jean 💵 ~€10 round-trip train, ~€10 guided church tour 🕐 Morning train, before day-trip buses arrive
Afternoon

Château tour & tasting

Saint-Émilion's nickname, the "hill of a thousand châteaux," is barely an exaggeration; Merlot and Cabernet Franc vines cover the limestone slopes in every direction. Book a château visit and tasting ahead of time, most require a reservation and don't take walk-ins.

🚕 Short taxi or a guided minivan tour from the village 💵 ~€20-35 per tasting 🕐 Early afternoon booking
Evening

Return to Bordeaux for dinner

Catch the early-evening TER back into Gare Saint-Jean and unwind with a low-key dinner near your hotel, a good night to trade wine for a Bordeaux beer or a simple carafe of the house red.

🚆 TER train, ~35-40 min back to Bordeaux 💵 Included in round-trip fare 🕐 Trains run until mid-evening
Morning

Marché des Capucins

Bordeaux calls this covered market "the belly of Bordeaux." Arrive hungry for a plate of Arcachon Bay oysters with a glass of white at one of the counter stalls, and pick up a warm canelé, the city's caramel-crusted, rum-and-vanilla pastry, for the road. Closed Mondays.

🚋 Tram C to Sainte-Croix, then a short walk 💵 ~€12-18 oysters & wine 🕐 Morning, Tue-Sun (closed Mon)
Afternoon

Jardin Public & last-minute wine shopping

This English-style 18th-century park, with a small botanical garden and natural history museum inside, is an easy, unhurried close to the trip. Swing down Cours de l'Intendance afterward for a last bottle or two to bring home.

🚋 Tram B/C to Jardin Public 💵 Free park entry 🕐 Early afternoon, ahead of departure
Evening

Transfer to Gare Saint-Jean or the airport

The same TGV or Jet'Bus that brought you in runs the reverse route; budget extra time if you're carrying wine, since checked bags with bottles need padding and airline limits on liquids apply.

🚄 TGV to Paris, or 🚌 Jet'Bus to BOD 💵 Varies by route 🕐 Leave at least 2 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is included by law (service compris), so tipping is never required.
  • Rounding up a euro or two for a wine bar or restaurant you loved is a nice, optional gesture.
  • No tip is expected at market stalls, cafes, or for the château tasting-room staff.

👗 Dress Code

  • Smart-casual covers nearly everything here, including most château tasting rooms.
  • Sturdy, comfortable shoes matter more than style: cobblestones in the old town, gravel paths in the vineyards.
  • Shoulders should be covered to enter Cathédrale Saint-André or the monolithic church in Saint-Émilion.

🍷 Wine-Tasting Etiquette

  • Spitting into the provided bucket (cracher) after a taste is completely normal, especially if you're tasting at more than one château.
  • Let the pourer lead the tasting order; it's usually structured from lightest to most full-bodied.
  • Most Saint-Émilion châteaux require a booking in advance and don't accept walk-in visitors.

🚋 Getting Around Bordeaux

  • Three tram lines (A, B, C) cover the whole old town and reach La Cité du Vin and the Chartrons quarter directly.
  • A single ticket costs about €1.80 and covers trams and buses for an hour of travel with transfers.
  • The Old Town itself is compact and very walkable; save the tram for longer hops like Cité du Vin.

⚠️ Safety & Practical Warnings

  • Bordeaux is generally very safe, but keep an eye on bags around Gare Saint-Jean and the Saturday market crowds.
  • Many shops, and the Marché des Capucins, close on Mondays: plan market visits for Tuesday through Sunday.
  • Book Saint-Émilion château tastings and La Cité du Vin tickets ahead in peak season (May-Sep); popular slots sell out.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases

  • Bonjour: hello (always lead with this before asking anything)
  • Un canelé, s'il vous plaît: one canelé, please
  • L'addition, s'il vous plaît: the bill, please

💳 Money & Payments

  • Cards and contactless payment are accepted almost everywhere, including market stalls and château tasting rooms.
  • Small producers at the Saturday quai markets sometimes prefer cash, so carry some small euro notes.
  • ATMs are widely available; avoid currency-exchange kiosks near the main tourist sites.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎫

Bordeaux City Pass

Bundles free public transit with entry or discounts to La Cité du Vin, the Pey-Berland tower, and other sights, and pays for itself after two or three stops.

🦪

Lunch at Marché des Capucins

A half-dozen oysters and a glass of white at a market counter costs a fraction of the same plate at a riverside restaurant.

🚆

TER over a guided minivan

The regional train to Saint-Émilion costs a lot less than a day tour and drops you right in the village, walkable to several châteaux without a booked tour.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🎨

Darwin Ecosystem

A graffiti-covered former army barracks turned skate park, urban farm, and organic cafe across the river in the Bastide district, a world away from the Old Town's stone facades.

🏖️

Dune du Pilat, if you can stretch to a 5th day

Europe's tallest sand dune and the oysters of Arcachon Bay are about an hour from Bordeaux by train, worth an add-on day if your schedule allows it.

🌉

Pont de Pierre at night

Napoleon's 19th-century stone bridge, lit up after dark with far fewer visitors than the Miroir d'Eau crowd just upstream.

✈️ Flights

  • Bordeaux-Mérignac Airport (BOD) is the main gateway, about 12 km west of the center.
  • The Jet'Bus shuttle runs to Gare Saint-Jean and the city center in around 45 minutes; there's no direct tram link to the airport.
  • Travelers coming from within France often skip flying altogether and take the TGV, just over two hours from Paris.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Uber and Bolt both operate reliably throughout Bordeaux.
  • A taxi is the easiest way to reach a château outside Saint-Émilion village if a tour isn't booked.
  • Fares are metered, and service is included, so no tip is expected beyond rounding up.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Not needed for this itinerary: the tram covers the city, and the TER train handles the Saint-Émilion day trip.
  • A car becomes worthwhile only if you plan to chase multiple Médoc châteaux beyond what's in this plan.
  • Parking in the Old Town is limited and the pedestrianized core restricts driving anyway.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Port of the Moon (Old Town): this itinerary's base, walking distance to Place de la Bourse, the cathedral, and Rue Sainte-Catherine.
  • Near Gare Saint-Jean: convenient if you're catching the early Saint-Émilion train on Day 3.
  • Chartrons: a quieter, wine-trade-history base close to Day 2's evening wine bars.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • Not required for getting around: trams and the TER train handle every leg of this itinerary.
  • A Garonne river cruise past the Port of the Moon's UNESCO waterfront is a pleasant optional add-on, not built into this plan.
  • Longer river cruises also depart Bordeaux for multi-day trips into the Médoc and Dordogne, worth considering for a return visit.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Standard Schengen entry rules apply, so non-EU visitors should confirm visa requirements before booking flights.
  • Most wine shops and larger châteaux can arrange shipping for bottles, often easier than fitting them into checked luggage.
  • Travel insurance is worth it given the pre-booked château tastings and Cité du Vin slots, which are usually non-refundable.
