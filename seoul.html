5 Days in Seoul: Palaces, Markets & Neon Nights

From a hanbok-clad morning at a 600-year-old palace to neon-lit market alleys and a Zaha Hadid-designed plaza, five days is enough to feel Seoul's whiplash between centuries-old tradition and round-the-clock modern energy. Go in spring or fall if you can: Seoul's summers run brutally humid and its winters bite hard, while cherry blossoms and autumn foliage make this the city at its best.

Ideal SeasonApr – Jun, Sep – Oct
Est. Budget$800-1,100
Duration5 Days
PaceActive
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Incheon International Airport & AREX transfer

Land at ICN and ride the AREX Express Train nonstop to Seoul Station in about 43 minutes, or take the slower All-Stop line for a cheaper fare if you're not racing the clock.

🚄 ~43 min Express, ~66 min All-Stop 💵 ~$7 Express, ~$3.50 All-Stop 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time
Afternoon

Check-in & up Namsan by cable car

Drop your bags, then ride the Namsan Cable Car up to N Seoul Tower for sweeping views over the city's rooftops and surrounding mountains while the light is still good.

🚡 ~3 min cable car from the Myeongdong side 💵 ~$11 round-trip 🕐 Mid-afternoon, before the sunset queue forms
Evening

Myeongdong's street food stalls & neon shopping

Myeongdong turns into a wall of steam and neon after dark; work through skewers, hotteok, and tornado potatoes between stops at the Korean beauty and fashion shops that stay open late.

🚇 Metro to Myeongdong station 💵 ~$15 (street food) 🕐 After 7 PM, once the stalls are lit up
Morning

Gyeongbokgung Palace in hanbok

Rent a hanbok from one of the shops near Gwanghwamun Gate, wearing one gets you free palace entry, then time your visit around the changing-of-the-guard ceremony at the main gate.

🚇 Metro to Gyeongbokgung station 💵 ~$15 hanbok rental, palace free in hanbok 🕐 Right at the 9 AM opening, before tour groups
Afternoon

Bukchon Hanok Village's hillside alleys

Climb the narrow lanes between Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung, where roughly 600 traditional hanok houses still line the hillside, many now cafes, guesthouses, and craft studios. Keep voices down; people actually live here.

🚶 ~15 min walk from Gyeongbokgung 💵 Free to wander 🕐 Early afternoon, before tour buses arrive
Evening

Insadong's tea houses & craft streets

Insadong-gil's winding alleys are Seoul's best concentration of traditional tea houses, calligraphy shops, and antique dealers; duck into a hanok tea house for a citron or jujube tea before dinner.

🚶 ~10 min walk or short metro ride 💵 ~$20 (tea + dinner) 🕐 Early evening, 6 to 8 PM
Morning

Gwangjang Market's food stalls

Korea's oldest surviving market has run since 1905; squeeze onto a bench for bindaetteok (mung bean pancakes) and mayak gimbap, then browse the fabric and hanbok stalls in the older wings.

🚇 Metro to Jongno 5(o)-ga station 💵 ~$12 (food crawl) 🕐 Late morning, before the lunchtime crush
Afternoon

Dongdaemun Design Plaza

Zaha Hadid's neofuturistic curves house rotating design exhibitions and a small museum built around the old Dongdaemun Stadium's excavated foundations, worth an hour even if you just wander the building itself.

🚇 Metro to Dongdaemun History & Culture Park station 💵 Free to ~$8 (exhibitions vary) 🕐 Mid-afternoon
Evening

Hongdae's street art & nightlife

The streets around Hongik University fill with buskers, indie bands, and mural-covered alleys after dark; grab chimaek (fried chicken and beer) before the neighborhood's clubs and bars pick up.

🚇 Metro to Hongik University station 💵 ~$20 (dinner + drinks) 🕐 After 8 PM
Morning

Biking & picnicking at Yeouido Hangang Park

Rent a bike from a riverside kiosk and ride the paved paths along the Han River, or just spread a mat on the grass with convenience-store snacks like everyone else does on a clear day.

🚇 Metro to Yeouinaru station 💵 ~$5 (bike rental) 🕐 Mid-morning, before the afternoon heat
Afternoon

A jjimjilbang sweat & sauna session

Spend a couple of relaxed hours moving between hot and cold pools, kiln saunas, and the shared heated-floor rooms of a Korean-style bathhouse, a genuinely local way to recover from three days of walking.

🚇 Widely available near most neighborhoods 💵 ~$13 entry 🕐 Early afternoon, 2 to 3 hours
Evening

Korean BBQ & the Banpo Bridge fountain

Grill samgyeopsal or galbi tableside over charcoal, then walk to Banpo Bridge if the timing lines up: its Moonlight Rainbow Fountain sends colored jets off both sides of the bridge into the Han River after dark.

🚶 Near most neighborhoods 💵 ~$25 (BBQ dinner) 🕐 Dinner around 7 PM; fountain runs April–October
Morning

Starfield Library & a Gangnam stroll

The Starfield Library's two-story wall of bookshelves inside COEX Mall is one of Seoul's most photographed interiors, free to browse and a good excuse to see Gangnam's glossier side of the city.

🚇 Metro to Samseong station 💵 Free 🕐 Late morning
Midday

Last-minute shopping at Namdaemun Market

Korea's oldest and largest traditional market is a fast, chaotic final stop for souvenirs, ginseng, and cheap Korean snacks to take home; haggling is normal here in a way it isn't at most of Seoul's other markets.

🚇 Metro to Hoehyeon station 💵 Budget for souvenirs 🕐 Early afternoon
Evening

AREX transfer to Incheon Airport for departure

Loop back to Seoul Station for the AREX Express Train, and check whether your airline offers city check-in at the AREX terminal so you can drop bags before you even board the train.

🚄 ~43 min Express Train 💵 ~$7 🕐 Leave at least 3 hrs before an international flight

💴 Tipping Etiquette

  • Tipping is not customary anywhere in South Korea: restaurants, taxis, hotels, and tour guides are all fine without one.
  • Trying to leave cash on the table can genuinely confuse servers, who may chase you down to return it.
  • A service charge is sometimes already built into hotel bills or upscale restaurant checks; check before adding anything extra.

🥢 Dining & Drinking Etiquette

  • When someone older pours you a drink, hold your glass with both hands (or support your pouring arm with your free hand) as a sign of respect, and return the favor.
  • Never stick chopsticks upright in a bowl of rice; it resembles a funeral ritual and unsettles Korean diners.
  • A slight bow when greeting or thanking someone, especially anyone older, goes a long way and is genuinely appreciated by locals.

👟 Shoe-Removal Customs

  • Shoes come off at the door in most hanok guesthouses, traditional tea houses, some restaurants with floor seating, and always at a jjimjilbang.
  • Look for a raised floor or a shoe rack by the entrance; it's the universal signal to change into the slippers provided.
  • Wear socks without holes and shoes you can slip on and off quickly, you'll be doing it more often than expected.

🚇 Getting Around Seoul

  • The subway network is vast, spotless, and easy to navigate, with English signage and announcements at every station on this itinerary.
  • Google Maps' walking and transit directions are unreliable in South Korea due to local mapping restrictions; download Naver Map or KakaoMap before you land instead.
  • Load a T-money card at any convenience store to tap in and out of subways, buses, and most taxis without buying single tickets.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Seoul is an exceptionally low-crime, walkable city; petty theft is rare even in crowded markets like Gwangjang, though normal city awareness still applies at night in Hongdae.
  • Jaywalking carries real fines and is far less common than in some other Asian megacities; use the marked crossings and signals.
  • Smoking is banned on most streets outside designated zones, with fines for violations, look for a small ashtray sign before lighting up.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Korean

  • Annyeonghaseyo: hello
  • Gamsahamnida: thank you
  • Igeo eolmayeyo?: how much is this?

🪖 A Note on the DMZ

  • The Korean Demilitarized Zone is a popular half-day trip from Seoul, but it doesn't comfortably fit alongside this itinerary's five core city days.
  • If it's a priority, most travelers add it as a sixth day or swap it in for Day 4's slower Han River and jjimjilbang day.
  • DMZ tours require booking a licensed operator in advance and bringing your passport; you cannot visit independently.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🚇

Load a T-money card immediately

It saves a small percentage on every subway and bus fare compared to single paper tickets, and it works for taxis and even some convenience stores.

🥟

Eat where the locals queue

Gwangjang Market's food stalls cost a fraction of a sit-down restaurant for the same, often better, versions of Korean street food classics.

👘

Compare hanbok rental shops

Prices near Gyeongbokgung vary more than you'd expect; shops a block or two further from the main gate are often noticeably cheaper for the same quality.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌄

Bukchon before 9 AM

The village's steepest, most photographed lanes are nearly empty right at sunrise, before the tour groups arrive around mid-morning.

🎨

Mullae-dong over Hongdae for street art

This former industrial neighborhood a few stops south has mural-covered metal workshops with a fraction of Hongdae's crowds, if you have extra time.

🌉

Time Banpo Bridge with the fountain schedule

The Moonlight Rainbow Fountain only runs seasonally (roughly April to October) on set showtimes; check the schedule before making the trip out.

✈️ Flights

  • Incheon International Airport (ICN) is the main international gateway; Gimpo (GMP), closer to the city center, handles mostly domestic and a handful of regional routes.
  • The AREX Express Train covers Incheon Airport to Seoul Station in about 43 minutes with no stops, one of the more comfortable airport transfers in Asia.
  • From Seoul Station, subway Line 1 and Line 4 connect directly into the rest of the city, including Myeongdong and Gyeongbokgung.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Uber operates in Seoul in partnership with local taxi networks, and Kakao T is the dominant local ride-hailing app with an English-language option.
  • Metered taxis are plentiful and reasonably priced; deluxe (black) taxis cost more but come with English-speaking, more experienced drivers.
  • Rides between Gangnam and downtown can crawl at rush hour, when the subway is almost always faster.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Not worth it for this itinerary: every stop sits on the subway network or a short taxi ride away, and Seoul's traffic and parking are genuinely stressful for visitors.
  • An International Driving Permit is required for foreign visitors who do want to self-drive, typically for trips beyond the city like the countryside or coast.
  • Skip it entirely and put the budget toward T-money fares, Kakao T rides, and the hanbok rental instead.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Myeongdong: puts Day 1's arrival, the Namsan cable car, and Day 2's Gyeongbokgung walk within easy reach, plus round-the-clock food right outside your door.
  • Insadong or Jongno: closer to Bukchon Hanok Village and Insadong's tea houses, a quieter base with easy subway access to everything else.
  • Hongdae: convenient if Day 3's nightlife is a priority, with direct subway and AREX-adjacent access for Day 5's departure.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • This itinerary has no boat leg as transport; the closest thing is watching the Banpo Bridge fountain from the riverbank on Day 4.
  • A Han River cruise from Yeouido or Banpo, with skyline views at sunset, is a popular optional add-on if you have extra time, but it's not essential to this plan.
  • Beyond that, the subway, AREX, and Kakao T cover everything else here.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Most Western nationalities can enter South Korea visa-free for short tourist stays, but confirm current K-ETA registration requirements for your passport before booking flights.
  • A T-money card, loaded on arrival, is close to essential; it works across nearly every form of public transport in this itinerary.
  • If the DMZ interests you, book a licensed tour operator well in advance and bring your passport; see the Need to Know tab for how it fits (or doesn't) around these five days.
