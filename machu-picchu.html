5 Days at Machu Picchu: The Sacred Valley & the Lost City of the Incas

From Cusco, the old Inca capital, the road drops into the Sacred Valley: the concentric farming terraces of Moray, the cascading salt pans of Maras, Pisac's ruins and market, and the still-inhabited streets of Ollantaytambo, before boarding the train into cloud forest. Five days built around one culminating morning, entering Machu Picchu at first light to walk the Temple of the Sun and the carved Intihuatana stone as the mist lifts off Huayna Picchu.

Ideal SeasonMay – Sep
Est. Budget$700-1,000
Duration5 Days
PaceActive
How many days do you have?

Morning

Fly into Cusco, then keep moving

Most trips land on a short domestic hop from Lima into Alejandro Velasco Astete International Airport at 3,400 meters; rather than lingering at altitude, this itinerary heads straight down into the Sacred Valley, which sits several hundred meters lower and is noticeably easier on the lungs.

✈️ ~1 hr 15 min flight from Lima 💵 ~$80-150 round-trip domestic flight 🕐 Aim for an early flight to leave the day open
Afternoon

Moray's concentric terraces

Sunken rings of terraces descend nearly 30 meters into a natural bowl, each level a few degrees warmer than the one above it; historians believe the Incas used Moray as an agricultural laboratory, testing seed varieties across a manufactured range of microclimates.

🚙 ~1 hr drive from Cusco via Chinchero 💵 ~$20 Sacred Valley circuit ticket 🕐 Early-to-mid afternoon
Evening

Salineras de Maras at golden hour

Thousands of small pools cut into the hillside have been evaporating mineral-rich spring water into salt since pre-Inca times, still worked by hand by local families; the terraced pans glow gold, white, and pink as the sun drops, before an overnight stay in the Sacred Valley itself, already lower and warmer than Cusco.

🚙 ~30 min from Moray 💵 ~S/10 (~$3) entry 🕐 Golden hour, then overnight in the Sacred Valley
Morning

Pisac's terraces and hilltop ceremonial center

Massive agricultural terraces climb the mountainside toward a hilltop complex of temples, plazas, and a former garrison, one of the best-preserved examples of Inca urban planning in the whole valley, with sweeping views down over the Urubamba River and the farming town below.

🚙 ~15 min from the Sacred Valley base 💵 Included on the Boleto Turístico, or ~$20 standalone 🕐 Depart early to beat the tour buses
Afternoon

Pisac's market, down in the town

Stalls spread across the main square selling handwoven textiles, silverwork, ceramics, and produce brought in from villages throughout the valley; it's one of the widest selections of Andean handicrafts on this whole route, and friendly bargaining is part of the transaction.

🛍️ Bargaining is normal and good-natured 💵 Textiles and alpaca goods from ~$5 🕐 Early-to-mid afternoon
Evening

Ollantaytambo's fortress and living Inca streets

A steep stack of terraces and temple ruins rises directly above a town where the original Inca street grid and stone irrigation channels are still in daily use; staying the night here, rather than driving back to Cusco, shaves a full hour off tomorrow's pre-dawn departure to the train station.

🚙 ~1 hr from Pisac 💵 Included on the Boleto Turístico, or ~$20 standalone 🕐 Overnight in Ollantaytambo
Morning

Board the train at Ollantaytambo station

PeruRail and IncaRail both run the only rail line into Aguas Calientes, panoramic Vistadome windows overhead as the train follows the Urubamba River through a gorge that narrows steadily, the dry highland scenery of the valley giving way to increasingly dense cloud forest.

🚂 ~1.5-2 hr scenic ride from Ollantaytambo 💵 ~$70-150 one-way, PeruRail or IncaRail 🕐 Book well ahead, seats sell out in peak season
Afternoon

Settle into Aguas Calientes

Also called Machu Picchu Pueblo, this small, car-free town wedged into the river gorge exists almost entirely for the ruins above it; it's easily walkable in an hour, so use the afternoon to confirm tomorrow's Machu Picchu entry time, and buy a shuttle bus ticket up if you haven't already.

🚶 Compact, car-free, and entirely walkable 💵 Free to wander, bus tickets up ~$24 round-trip 🕐 Confirm tomorrow's entry window today
Evening

Hot springs, dinner, and an early night

The thermal baths that give the town its name make a genuinely good soak for legs about to face a lot of stone stairs; dinner around the main square is easy to find, then turn in early, the first shuttle buses up to Machu Picchu start running well before dawn.

♨️ The thermal baths give the town its name 💵 ~$10-15 baths, ~$15-20 dinner 🕐 Early night, buses start around 5:30 AM
Morning

The switchback bus and a sunrise entry

Buses climb a dirt switchback road for about 25 minutes; queuing for the first departures means reaching the classic overlook terrace while the citadel is still wrapped in mist, watching it lift off the ruins and Huayna Picchu's peak behind them is the single most photographed moment in Peru for good reason.

🚌 ~25 min shuttle bus up the switchbacks 💵 Machu Picchu entry ~$45-55, guide required 🕐 Join the queue by 4:30-5 AM in peak season
Afternoon

The Temple of the Sun & the Intihuatana stone

A licensed guide, mandatory on every entry ticket since 2019, leads the walk through the citadel's core: the curved, precisely fitted stonework of the Temple of the Sun, and the Intihuatana, a carved outcrop believed to have functioned as a solar marker and one of the few such stones the Spanish never found and destroyed.

🚶 Guided circuit, roughly 2-3 hrs 💵 Guide included in most entry bookings 🕐 Mid-morning into early afternoon
Evening

The terraces, and an optional climb higher

Stone terraces step down the mountainside on every side, still holding the slope after five centuries; travelers wanting more can add Huayna Picchu or Machu Picchu Mountain, both steep hikes on separate, tightly capped tickets that must be booked weeks to months ahead. (Ambitious travelers sometimes hike in instead, via the classic multi-day Inca Trail, which this itinerary bypasses in favor of the train, and which closes every February for maintenance.) Buses back to Aguas Calientes run all afternoon.

🥾 Huayna Picchu & Machu Picchu Mtn need separate tickets 💵 Huayna Picchu ~$20-75 extra, Mtn ~$20-30 extra 🕐 Book these months ahead, they sell out
Morning

Train back to Ollantaytambo or Cusco

The same gorge unspools in reverse, cloud forest thinning back into open highland valley; book the train that runs direct to Cusco's Poroy station if the schedule allows it, saving an extra road transfer on a travel day.

🚂 ~1.5-2 hr return ride 💵 Included if round-trip was booked, or ~$70-150 🕐 Mid-morning departure from Aguas Calientes
Afternoon

A last Peruvian lunch: lomo saltado

Back in Cusco or a Sacred Valley town along the way, lomo saltado, strips of beef stir-fried with onion, tomato, and soy sauce and served over fries and rice, is the dish to close the trip on, a Peruvian-Chinese classic found on nearly every menu.

🚙 ~1.5-2 hr drive back to Cusco 💵 ~$15-20 lunch 🕐 Early afternoon
Evening

Airport transfer & departure

Cusco's compact airport sits close to the center, a short taxi ride from most hotels; build in extra buffer time, since flights here are occasionally delayed by weather, especially outside the dry season.

🚕 ~15-20 min to the airport 💵 Flights vary widely by route and season 🕐 Arrive at least 2 hrs before departure

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Not obligatory, but genuinely appreciated; round up 10% or so at restaurants that haven't already added a service charge.
  • A Machu Picchu guide is typically tipped ~$10-15 per person for a half-day guided circuit of the citadel.
  • Sacred Valley drivers and train staff welcome a small tip at the end of the day if the service was good.

👗 Dress Code

  • Layer heavily: Sacred Valley mornings run cold, Aguas Calientes' cloud forest is humid and prone to sudden rain, and Machu Picchu itself sits somewhere between the two.
  • Sturdy, broken-in hiking shoes matter more here than almost anywhere else in Peru; stone steps and uneven terraces are constant across every single site.
  • Locally, dress is modest and practical away from the ruins themselves; save trekking gear for the trail and the citadel.

🚶 Getting Around

  • The Sacred Valley and Ollantaytambo are covered by private driver or organized tour; there's no useful public transit link for this route.
  • Aguas Calientes is entirely car-free and walkable in about an hour end to end; no road reaches the town at all, only the train.
  • Shuttle buses are the only way up to Machu Picchu itself; private cars and taxis aren't permitted on the switchback road.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Machu Picchu operates a strict timed-entry system with a mandatory licensed guide; arrive within your booked entry window, arriving late can mean being turned away.
  • The citadel is an actively protected sacred site: no climbing on the walls, no stepping over roped-off areas, and no drones without a separate permit.
  • Cusco's 3,400m altitude still applies on arrival even though this itinerary moves quickly into the lower Sacred Valley; take the first afternoon easy and stay well hydrated.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Spanish

  • Buenos días / Buenas tardes: good morning / good afternoon
  • Muchas gracias: thank you very much
  • ¿A qué hora sale el tren?: what time does the train leave?

💳 Money & Payments

  • Peru uses the sol (S/); cards work at hotels and most Aguas Calientes restaurants, but cash is still king in Pisac's market and for tips.
  • ATMs exist in Aguas Calientes and Ollantaytambo but can run low on cash during peak season; withdraw soles in Cusco before setting out.
  • Small bills matter for the market, tips, and the shuttle bus tickets up to Machu Picchu.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🚂

Book the train's cheaper Expedition class

Expedition runs the same route and schedule as the pricier Vistadome cars for noticeably less, the panoramic windows are the main thing you're paying extra for.

🎟️

Buy the Boleto Turístico for the Sacred Valley days

The Cusco tourist ticket bundles entry to Pisac and Ollantaytambo (among other sites) into one pass, cheaper than paying each site's solo entrance fee.

🥾

Skip Huayna Picchu if the budget's tight

The classic overlook view and the citadel's core sites don't require the extra climb; it's a genuinely optional add-on, not a must-do.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌉

Walk the Inca Bridge trail inside the citadel

A quiet, mostly flat side path leads to a dramatic cliffside stone bridge, included in standard entry but skipped by most groups short on time.

🏘️

Overnight in Ollantaytambo, not Cusco, before the train

Shaves a full hour off the pre-dawn departure and puts you in a genuine living Inca town rather than a transit stop.

🥾

Consider the classic Inca Trail as a slower alternative

A multi-day hike over high mountain passes reaches Machu Picchu on foot through the Sun Gate instead of by train; it closes every February for annual maintenance, so it isn't an option that month.

✈️ Flights

  • Cusco's Alejandro Velasco Astete Airport connects mainly to Lima on domestic carriers, with a handful of other regional routes.
  • Flights can be delayed by weather, especially outside the May-Sep dry season; build in a buffer day around a major international connection.
  • Book flights separately from your train tickets and Machu Picchu entry, most operators don't bundle all three.

🚄 PeruRail & IncaRail Trains

  • These two companies run the only rail line into Aguas Calientes, departing from Ollantaytambo (closest, this itinerary's route) or from Cusco's Poroy station (longer, roughly 3.5 hrs).
  • Book weeks to months ahead in peak season (Jun-Aug), seats on the earliest and latest trains sell out first.
  • Expedition, Vistadome, and the luxury Hiram Bingham service are the three main classes, priced roughly in that order.

🎟️ Machu Picchu Tickets & Timed Entry

  • Entry is timed and capped at a set number of visitors per slot; book weeks ahead in shoulder season and months ahead for June-August.
  • A licensed guide has been mandatory on every general entry ticket since 2019, book one alongside your ticket or through your hotel.
  • Huayna Picchu and Machu Picchu Mountain both require separate, additionally ticketed time slots with their own daily caps, they sell out well before the general entry does.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Sacred Valley towns like Urubamba or Ollantaytambo make a scenic, lower-altitude base for the first two nights, with easier access to the train station than Cusco.
  • Aguas Calientes is necessary for the third night, its hotels exist purely to serve travelers heading up to the ruins the next morning.
  • Cusco itself is only needed for the arrival and departure days on this itinerary, not as a multi-night base.

🚕 Transfers & Shuttles

  • Private drivers or shared vans cover the Sacred Valley legs between Cusco, Moray, Pisac, and Ollantaytambo, no useful public transit link exists for this specific route.
  • The shuttle buses up to Machu Picchu from Aguas Calientes run continuously from before dawn until mid-afternoon, tickets are sold separately from the entry fee.
  • Registered taxis are the safest way around Cusco itself on arrival and departure days.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Most nationalities need only a valid passport for short tourist stays in Peru, but check current requirements well before booking flights.
  • The classic multi-day Inca Trail trek closes every February for maintenance; the train route this itinerary uses runs year-round.
  • Travel insurance covering high-altitude travel and trip interruption is genuinely worth it given how tightly this itinerary's train and entry times are booked in advance.
