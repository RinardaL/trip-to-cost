4 Days in Dublin: Trinity College, Guinness & the Wicklow Mountains

Dublin packs a walkable, story-filled old town into a bend of the River Liffey, and four days is enough to see the Book of Kells and the Long Room at Trinity College, tour Dublin Castle and St Patrick's Cathedral, ride the Gravity Bar's glass elevator at the Guinness Storehouse, take in Kilmainham Gaol's role in Irish independence, and still get out to Glendalough's monastic ruins in the Wicklow Mountains before you fly home. Bring a rain jacket regardless of the forecast, it's genuinely part of the experience.

Ideal SeasonMay – Sep
Est. Budget$850-1,150
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival & transfer into the city center

Dublin Airport (DUB) sits about 7 miles (11 km) north of the city center, and there's no rail link yet, so the Airlink 787/757 bus or Dublin Bus routes run straight into the center in well under an hour.

🚌 ~35-45 min on the Airlink bus 💵 ~€8 one-way 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time
Afternoon

Trinity College: the Book of Kells & the Long Room

The 9th-century illuminated Book of Kells opens into the Old Library's Long Room, a 65-meter barrel-vaulted hall lined with 200,000 of the college's oldest books and marble busts of scholars, one of the most photographed rooms in Ireland.

🚶 Right in the city center, Trinity's front gate faces College Green 💵 ~€22 entry, book a timed slot online ahead 🕐 Early afternoon, right after check-in
Evening

Grafton Street & Temple Bar's first pints

Walk Grafton Street's buskers and shopfronts down toward the river, then cross into Temple Bar's cobbled lanes for dinner and a pint, live trad music spills out of doorways most nights, even if this particular pub district leans touristy.

🚶 ~15 min walk, Grafton Street to Temple Bar 💵 ~€6.50-7.50 per pint 🕐 Early evening onward
Morning

Dublin Castle

Built on the site of a Viking settlement and rebuilt over 800 years, the castle's State Apartments and the medieval Record Tower, its oldest surviving structure, sit a short walk from Trinity, with the Chester Beatty Library's manuscripts free to visit in the same complex.

🚶 ~10 min walk from Trinity College 💵 ~€10 State Apartments entry, Chester Beatty free 🕐 Right at 9:45 AM opening
Afternoon

St Patrick's Cathedral

Ireland's largest cathedral, founded beside the well where St Patrick is said to have baptized converts, holds Jonathan Swift's grave (he was dean here for over 30 years) and choral evensong most weekday afternoons in term time.

🚶 ~15 min walk from Dublin Castle 💵 ~€10 entry, evensong is free 🕐 Mid-afternoon
Evening

Guinness Storehouse & the Gravity Bar

The old fermentation plant is now a seven-floor self-guided tour ending at the circular, glass-walled Gravity Bar, where a pint you pulled yourself comes with a 360-degree view over the city at dusk.

🚶 ~20 min walk from St Patrick's, or a short bus ride 💵 ~€30 entry, includes one pint 🕐 Late afternoon into evening, book ahead for a sunset slot
Morning

Kilmainham Gaol

The leaders of the 1916 Easter Rising were executed in this prison's stone-breakers' yard, and the guided tour through its cross-shaped Victorian wing is one of the most sobering, essential stops for understanding modern Irish history; tickets sell out days ahead in summer.

🚌 ~20 min bus or tram from the city center 💵 ~€8 entry, guided tour only, book online ahead 🕐 First tour slot of the morning
Afternoon

National Museum of Ireland – Archaeology

Free to enter and back in the city center, the museum's Treasury holds the Ardagh Chalice and the Tara Brooch, and the preserved Iron Age bog bodies upstairs are unforgettable, budget at least 90 minutes.

🚌 ~20 min back into the center from Kilmainham 💵 Free entry 🕐 Early-to-mid afternoon
Evening

A real trad session in Smithfield

Skip back to Temple Bar and instead head to Smithfield's Cobblestone, a working-class local where musicians gather around a table with fiddles and bodhráns for an unamplified session, no cover charge, just buy a round and find a seat.

🚶 ~15 min walk from the city center, or a Luas tram stop 💵 Free to watch, ~€6 per pint 🕐 Sessions usually start around 9:30 PM
Morning

Drive to Glendalough via the Wicklow Mountains

A day tour bus (or rental car) climbs out of Dublin through the Wicklow Mountains to Glendalough, a monastic settlement founded by St Kevin in the 6th century, its round tower and ruined chapels sit in a glacial valley between two lakes.

🚌 ~1 hr drive from Dublin, tour or rental car 💵 ~€35-45 for a guided day tour 🕐 Morning departure, right after breakfast
Midday

Upper Lake hike & Lough Tay viewpoint

Walk the lakeshore trail to Glendalough's Upper Lake, then most day tours swing past the Sally Gap viewpoint over Lough Tay, nicknamed the Guinness Lake for its dark water and pale sandy "head" at one end.

🚶 Easy 40-min loop around the Upper Lake 💵 Free, part of the day tour 🕐 Midday
Afternoon

Return to Dublin & departure

Tour buses drop back in the city center by mid-to-late afternoon, leaving time to cross the Liffey one last time and grab an early dinner before heading to Dublin Airport for an evening flight.

🚌 ~1 hr drive back into the center 💵 Included in the day tour 🕐 Leave for the airport at least 2.5 hrs before an international flight

💷 Tipping Etiquette

  • Around 10-12% is standard at sit-down restaurants, check the bill first since some already add a service charge.
  • Tipping isn't expected at the bar for a pint, but rounding up or leaving a euro for table service is appreciated.
  • Taxi drivers appreciate rounding up to the nearest euro, it's not obligatory.

🍺 Pub Etiquette & Buying Rounds

  • If you're drinking with a group, buying rounds is the norm, everyone takes a turn covering the table.
  • Order and pay at the bar rather than waiting for table service in most traditional pubs.
  • A well-poured pint of Guinness takes about two minutes to settle, don't rush the bartender.

🗣️ Conversational Warmth & Small Talk

  • Dubliners are famously chatty, a "how's it going" from a stranger is a real conversation opener, not just a greeting.
  • Self-deprecating humor and gentle slagging (friendly teasing) are common and generally a sign of warmth, not hostility.
  • Asking a local for a pub or restaurant recommendation almost always gets an enthusiastic, detailed answer.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Temple Bar gets rowdy with stag and hen parties on weekend nights, keep an eye on belongings in the crowds.
  • O'Connell Street and parts of the north inner city can feel edgier late at night, stick to well-lit main streets.
  • Rain gear isn't optional, a sudden downpour can arrive with almost no warning even on a sunny forecast day.

☘️ Crucial Phrases & Hiberno-English

  • Grand: fine, okay, good ("how are you?" "grand, thanks")
  • Craic: fun, good times, as in "what's the craic?"
  • Your man/your one: a way to refer to a person whose name you don't know or aren't using

💳 Money & Payments

  • The Republic of Ireland uses the euro (EUR), unlike Northern Ireland which uses pounds sterling.
  • Contactless cards are accepted almost everywhere, including pubs, buses, and the Luas tram.
  • Carry a little cash for small independent shops and buskers around Grafton Street and Temple Bar.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Book Trinity's Book of Kells online

Advance online tickets are cheaper than walk-up and guarantee a timed slot, skipping the line that forms outside Front Gate by mid-morning.

🏛️

Stack the free museums

The National Museum of Ireland (Archaeology), the National Gallery, and the Chester Beatty Library are all free, worth slotting in around the paid sights.

🚌

Compare Glendalough tour operators

Several companies run near-identical day tours from Dublin, prices and included stops vary more than you'd expect, worth a quick comparison before booking.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🎻

Skip Temple Bar for a real session

The Cobblestone in Smithfield, or O'Donoghue's near St Stephen's Green, draw working musicians rather than tourist-facing cover bands.

🌳

Walk St Stephen's Green at opening

The Victorian park in the city center is nearly empty right at its early opening time, a quiet contrast to Grafton Street a block away.

🍺

Ask about the "split the G"

A local challenge to drink a pint of Guinness so the foam line splits the Guinness logo exactly, harmless pub fun if you want to fit in.

✈️ Flights

  • Dublin Airport (DUB) is Ireland's main hub, about 7 miles (11 km) north of the city center.
  • The Airlink express bus and regular Dublin Bus routes both reach the center in well under an hour, no rail link exists yet.
  • Ryanair and Aer Lingus both hub here, so short-notice fares to other European cities are often cheap if you extend the trip.

💳 Leap Card & Public Transit

  • A Leap Card (or a contactless bank card, now accepted directly) covers Dublin Bus, the Luas tram, and DART rail at a discount over cash fares.
  • Pick one up at the airport, Spar/Centra shops, or Luas ticket machines, top up as needed.
  • The Luas Red and Green lines are handy for Smithfield and St Stephen's Green, most Day 1-3 sights are still walkable.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • FreeNow is the dominant ride-hailing app in Dublin, Uber operates but mostly dispatches licensed taxis rather than private cars.
  • Metered black taxis can be flagged on the street or found at ranks near Temple Bar and O'Connell Street.
  • Avoid unlicensed drivers touting for fares outside the airport arrivals hall.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Temple Bar/Trinity area: puts Day 1 and Day 2's sights within easy walking distance, but expect weekend noise.
  • St Stephen's Green/Grafton Street: quieter, still central, close to the National Museum and good transit links.
  • Smithfield: closer to Day 3's Kilmainham Gaol and the Cobblestone, a more local, less touristy base.

🚌 Day-Trip Tours & Rental Cars

  • Guided day tours to Glendalough and the Wicklow Mountains run daily from the city center, no car needed.
  • A rental car gives more flexibility for Day 4 but isn't necessary, and city-center parking and one-way streets add hassle.
  • Longer trips to the Cliffs of Moher run as very long single-day tours from Dublin (10-12 hrs round trip); Wicklow fits the 4-day pace better.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Ireland is not in the Schengen Area, so separate entry rules apply for non-Irish/non-UK visitors.
  • The currency is the euro (EUR); Northern Ireland, if you cross the border, uses pounds sterling instead.
  • Travel insurance is worth it given the pre-booked Trinity, Kilmainham, and Guinness Storehouse tickets, which are usually non-refundable.
