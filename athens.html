7 Days in Athens: Ancient Ruins & Island Escapes

Marble ruins at sunrise, backstreet tavernas at night, and two easy day trips to the temples and islands just outside the city.

Ideal SeasonApr – Jun, Sep – Oct
Est. Budget$880-1,300
Duration7 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Athens airport transfer & check-in

The blue metro line takes about 40 minutes to Syntagma, with trains roughly every 30 minutes until late evening. Ordinary city tickets are not valid on the airport section, so buy the airport ticket (check current fare). The X95 bus is slower but runs all night, and a taxi has a fixed fare of about €40 by day.

🚇 ~40 min by metro 💵 ~€9 (check current) 🕐 Confirm check-in time
Afternoon

Wander Plaka's lanes

Plaka is the old quarter below the Acropolis, and Adrianou Street is its busiest lane, so turn into the stepped side streets towards Anafiotika, a whitewashed corner that feels like a Cycladic village. Shops stay open until about 9 PM, and prices drop away from the main strips. Wear shoes with grip, as the paving is polished smooth.

🚶 walkable old town 💵 Free to wander 🕐 Mid-afternoon, before dinner crowds
Evening

First Acropolis view at sunset

Areopagus Hill rises just below the Acropolis entrance, and a short flight of worn marble steps leads to the top. The rock is slippery, especially at dusk, so wear shoes with grip and carry a small torch for the descent. Arrive about 45 minutes before sunset, because the best spots fill quickly.

🚶 ~10 min from Plaka 💵 Free 🕐 Sunset
Morning

Acropolis & the Parthenon

The full adult ticket has been €30 since April 2025, so budget more than the badge shows (check current), and entry is by timed slot, so book online at the official ticketing site. Summer hours run roughly 8 AM to 8 PM, shorter in winter. Allow two hours, carry water and wear flat shoes on the polished marble.

🚶 ~15 min uphill from Plaka 💵 ~€30 🕐 Right at 8 AM opening
Afternoon

Acropolis Museum

The ground floor looks down through glass onto an excavated ancient neighbourhood, and the top-floor Parthenon Gallery follows the temple's real orientation, with the Acropolis visible through the windows. The adult ticket is about €20 (check current), separate from the Acropolis ticket. Allow around two hours, and use the café terrace as a cool break.

🚶 ~5 min downhill 💵 ~€20 🕐 Early afternoon, air-conditioned break
Evening

Ancient Agora at golden hour

The Temple of Hephaestus dates from the fifth century BC and survives so well partly because it served as a church for centuries. The site was the civic heart of ancient Athens. Entry is about €20 (check current). There is little shade, so bring water, and spend fifteen minutes in the small museum inside the reconstructed Stoa of Attalos.

🚶 ~10 min from the Acropolis 💵 ~€20 🕐 Last two hours before closing
Morning

National Archaeological Museum

Entry is about €20 according to recent listings (check current), and on Tuesdays the museum opens only at 1 PM, so choose another day for a morning visit. The Mask of Agamemnon and the Antikythera Mechanism both have their own displays. Allow two to three hours, and take metro line 1 to Victoria, a short walk away.

🚇 ~15 min by metro 💵 ~€20 (check current) 🕐 Before 10 AM, before tour buses
Afternoon

Monastiraki flea market

Monastiraki station is served by metro lines 1 and 3, and the stalls along Ifestou Street fill with antiques, vinyl and souvenirs. Sunday is the main market day, from about 9 AM to mid afternoon, though many shops in the surrounding streets open on other days too. Haggle politely at street stalls, and keep bags zipped in the crowds.

🚇 ~10 min by metro 💵 Free to browse 🕐 Sunday morning is the biggest market day
Evening

Dinner in Psiri

The tavernas and mezze bars around Iroon Square serve dinner from about 8 PM, although most Athenians eat later, so the lanes are liveliest after 9 PM. Order several small plates to share, ask which venues have live rebetiko, and reserve a table on Friday and Saturday. About €22 per person covers mezze and wine.

🚶 ~8 min from Monastiraki 💵 ~€22 🕐 9 PM onward, when it gets lively
Morning

Sleep in, then head for the coast road

KTEL Attiki buses to Sounion leave from the Mavromateon terminal near Pedion Areos (check current) and follow the coast road past Glyfada and Vouliagmeni; allow 1h30 to 2 hours each way. The fare is about €7 one-way. Check the last return bus before you go, because evening services are limited and a taxi back costs far more.

🚌 ~1h 30min by KTEL bus 💵 ~€7.50 one-way 🕐 Depart late morning
Afternoon

Beach time near Sounion

Small sandy and pebbly coves lie just below the cape, and the bay is sheltered enough for an easy swim. Facilities are basic, so bring water, a towel and shoes for the rocks, and keep to shade during the hottest hours. Plan to be back at the temple gate by about 4 PM, ahead of the sunset crowds.

🚶 walkable from the bus stop 💵 Free 🕐 Early afternoon, avoid peak sun
Evening

Temple of Poseidon at sunset

The Temple of Poseidon was built around 440 BC and stands roughly 60 metres above the sea. The site closes at sunset, and the ticket costs about €10 to €20 depending on season (check current). Sunset ranges from about 5 PM in winter to 8:45 PM in summer, so arrive an hour early and confirm the last bus back.

🚶 5 min from the parking area 💵 ~€10-20 (check current) 🕐 Sunset
Morning

Drive into the mountains

Most guided day tours leave Athens around 7:30 AM and reach Delphi in two and a half to three hours, often with a coffee stop in Arachova, a hillside village on Mount Parnassus. Check whether entry is included in the roughly €45 fee, because many budget tours exclude it. The combined site and museum ticket has recently been about €20.

🚗 ~2h 30min drive 💵 ~€45 guided tour 🕐 Depart by 7:30 AM
Afternoon

Sanctuary of Apollo & the Oracle's temple

The Sacred Way zigzags up past the treasuries and the Temple of Apollo, where the Pythia gave her prophecies, to the theatre and the stadium at the top. Allow about 90 minutes. The paths are steep and uneven with little shade, so carry water. The combined site and museum ticket has recently been about €20 (check current).

🚶 site is uphill, wear good shoes 💵 ~€20 (site + museum) 🕐 Midday, once the tour buses thin out
Evening

Delphi Archaeological Museum & return drive

The Charioteer is a rare bronze statue from about 470 BC with inlaid glass eyes, and it sits beside the Sphinx of the Naxians in the museum next to the site. Plan 45 to 60 minutes, and check the closing time, which is earlier in winter. Entry is included in the combined ticket, and the drive back takes about 2.5 hours.

🚗 ~2h 30min drive back 💵 included in site ticket 🕐 Before 6 PM closing
Morning

Ferry from Piraeus to Hydra

High-speed boats from Piraeus take roughly 1h15 to 1h40 depending on the operator and stops, so book online in advance, especially at weekends in summer. One-way fares are typically €40 to €55, which makes the round trip dearer than the badge suggests (check current). Reach the Piraeus gate 30 minutes before departure.

⛴️ ~1h 30min by high-speed ferry 💵 ~€75-100 round trip (check current) 🕐 Earliest morning departure
Afternoon

Harbor town & a swim off the rocks

Hydra's harbour town has no cars or motorbikes, and donkeys and mules still carry luggage up the stepped lanes. There is little sand, so most people swim from rocks with ladders along the coastal paths on either side of the harbour. Wear water shoes, expect a short walk of up to 15 minutes, and avoid windy days.

🚶 walkable harbor town 💵 ~€15 sunbed & drink 🕐 Midday swim
Evening

Seafood dinner before the last ferry

Waterfront tavernas around the harbour serve grilled fish and other seafood, often priced by weight, so ask the price before ordering. Check the last high-speed sailing back to Piraeus, which is usually early evening, and be at the quay 20 minutes before departure. Missing it means finding a bed on the island overnight.

⛴️ ~1h 30min back to Piraeus 💵 ~€28 dinner 🕐 Confirm last ferry time
Morning

Changing of the Guard, Syntagma Square

The Evzones guard the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier below Parliament, with a short change every hour on the hour and a larger ceremony at 11 AM on Sundays, when the full band and parade appear. Arrive about ten minutes early for a front-row spot. Syntagma station joins metro lines 2 and 3, which is handy for the airport.

🚇 ~5 min by metro from Plaka 💵 Free 🕐 On the hour, Sunday's version is grandest
Afternoon

Last-minute shopping & a light lunch

Ermou is a pedestrianised stretch of about one kilometre, mostly international chain shops, with the small Byzantine church of Kapnikarea standing in the middle of the street. For Greek souvenirs such as olive oil soap or honey, try the smaller shops in the side streets around Monastiraki. Many shops close on Sundays, so check the day.

🚶 pedestrian shopping street 💵 ~€15 lunch 🕐 Early afternoon
Evening

Rooftop bar view, then airport transfer

Several rooftop bars around Monastiraki face the floodlit Acropolis, and a drink there usually costs about €12 to €16. Leave time for the transfer: the airport metro takes about 40 minutes from Monastiraki, and ordinary tickets do not cover that section (check current airport fare and last train). Reach the airport three hours before an international flight.

🚇 ~40 min metro to the airport 💵 ~€14 drink, ~€9 metro (check current) 🕐 3 hrs before flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Not obligatory: round up or leave 5-10% at sit-down restaurants.
  • No tipping expected at cafes, bakeries, or takeaway souvlaki spots.
  • Rounding up for taxis and tour guides is appreciated but not required.

👗 Dress Code

  • Cover shoulders and knees when visiting churches and monasteries.
  • The Acropolis path is uneven, ancient marble, worn smooth, so wear real walking shoes, not sandals.
  • Casual dress is fine everywhere else, including most tavernas.

🚇 Getting Around

  • A single metro/bus ticket covers 90 minutes across all transfers, cheaper by the day-pass if sightseeing hard.
  • Validate paper tickets at the machine before boarding: inspectors do check.
  • The X95 airport bus is a reliable backup if the metro line is disrupted.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Pickpocketing occurs on crowded metro lines and around Monastiraki, so keep bags zipped and in front.
  • Summer heat is intense: visit the Acropolis at opening or in the last two hours before closing.
  • Taxi meters should always be running; agree on a fare upfront for any fixed-rate airport trip.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Greek

  • Yassas (Γεια σας): hello, formal
  • Efharisto (Ευχαριστώ): thank you
  • Ton logariasmo, parakalo (Τον λογαριασμό, παρακαλώ): the bill, please

💳 Money & Payments

  • Euro is the currency; cards are widely accepted in Athens, including most tavernas.
  • Small island tavernas and market stalls on Hydra may be cash-only, so carry some euros.
  • ATMs are common throughout the city center and at the ferry ports.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎫

Buy the combined archaeological ticket

One pass covers the Acropolis, Ancient Agora, and several other sites for less than paying separately.

🚌

KTEL buses over taxis for day trips

Cape Sounion and other coastal towns are a fraction of a private driver's price by regional bus.

🍽️

Eat where the lunch crowd is Greek

Neighborhood tavernas away from Plaka's main strip serve the same food for noticeably less.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌆

Lycabettus Hill over the Acropolis viewpoints

The city's highest point gives a full panorama, including the Acropolis itself, with far fewer crowds.

🏝️

Aegina over Hydra for a quieter island

A shorter ferry ride with its own Doric temple, ideal if Hydra's crowds sound like too much.

🏘️

Anafiotika's whitewashed lanes

A tiny Cycladic-style pocket tucked into Plaka, built by island workers in the 1800s.

✈️ Flights

  • Athens International Airport "Eleftherios Venizelos" (ATH) sits about 40 minutes from the center by metro.
  • The metro's blue line runs direct into downtown, with the X95 bus as a reliable backup if the line is disrupted.
  • Book the metro or bus over a taxi for the airport run: highway traffic into the city can be unpredictable at peak hours.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Beat, the local rideshare app, and metered taxis both work well and are easy to book from central Athens.
  • Insist the meter is running for any in-city ride, and agree a fare upfront only for a fixed-rate airport transfer.
  • For Cape Sounion or other coastal spots, the KTEL regional bus is cheaper than a taxi and simpler than arranging a driver.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Not needed for the Athens city days: the metro and walking cover Plaka, the Acropolis, and Monastiraki easily.
  • Cape Sounion is reached by KTEL bus and Delphi by guided tour in this itinerary, so a self-drive rental isn't necessary for either.
  • If you'd rather drive yourself to Delphi, rent for that single day only rather than for the whole week.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Plaka: closest base to the Acropolis and the first-night sunset viewpoint, walkable to almost everything early in the trip.
  • Monastiraki or Psiri: near the flea market and the taverna-and-nightlife scene for the busier evenings out.
  • Syntagma: convenient for the Changing of the Guard and the direct metro line out to the airport on departure day.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • Essential for this itinerary: the Hydra day trip runs by high-speed ferry from Piraeus.
  • Catch the earliest departure: it's the calmest crossing and leaves the most time on the car-free island.
  • Confirm the last return ferry time before sitting down to dinner on Hydra: missing it means an unplanned night away.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • EU/Schengen entry rules apply; non-EU visitors should confirm visa requirements before booking flights.
  • Travel insurance is worth it for the ferry crossing and the long Delphi drive day alone.
  • Carry some euros for small island tavernas on Hydra and market stalls: not everywhere takes cards.
