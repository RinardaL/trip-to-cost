4 Days in Tallinn: Old Town, Toompea & Kalamaja

One of Europe's best-preserved medieval towns, a hilltop old town with a Russian Orthodox cathedral and panoramic red-roof views, an intact ring of city walls and towers, the colorful wooden streets of Kalamaja, and a maritime museum inside a century-old seaplane hangar, four days across Estonia's compact and endlessly walkable capital.

Ideal SeasonMay – Sep
Est. Budget$550-750
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival & check-in near the Old Town

Lennart Meri Tallinn Airport sits just 4 km from the center, and tram line 4 or a short taxi ride gets you to the Old Town in about 15 minutes, one of the shortest, simplest airport transfers of any European capital.

🚊 ~15 min tram from the airport to Old Town 💵 ~€2 tram ticket (~$2.20) 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time
Midday

Raekoja plats & the Town Hall

Tallinn's Town Hall Square has been the city's civic heart since the 13th century, framed by the only surviving Gothic town hall in Northern Europe and a ring of pastel merchant buildings now filled with cafes. Climb the Town Hall tower for one of the best low-angle views of the square, then wander the surrounding lanes for amber and hand-knit wool.

🚶 Central, walkable from most Old Town hotels 💵 Free to wander; ~€5 Town Hall tower 🕐 Any time, though early afternoon is liveliest
Evening

A medieval-style dinner & first taste of Estonia

Old Town's candlelit medieval-themed restaurants lean touristy but are genuinely fun on a first night, serving dense black rye bread, elk stew, and other dishes by candlelight and live folk music. Order a glass of Vana Tallinn, Estonia's dark spiced rum-based liqueur, to close out the meal.

🚶 On or just off Raekoja plats 💵 ~€20-30 dinner per person 🕐 From 6 PM, book ahead in summer
Morning

Toompea Hill & Alexander Nevsky Cathedral

Toompea, the fortified upper town, has been Estonia's seat of power since the 13th century. Its onion-domed Russian Orthodox cathedral, built in 1900, stands in deliberate contrast to the Lutheran spires of Lower Town below, a visible layer of the country's Danish, German, and Russian rule.

🚶 ~10 min uphill walk from Raekoja plats 💵 Free entry to the cathedral 🕐 Right at morning opening, before tour groups
Afternoon

Toompea Castle & the Kohtuotsa viewing platform

The pink baroque wing of Toompea Castle now houses the Riigikogu, Estonia's parliament, tucked behind the medieval fortress walls of Pikk Hermann tower. A few minutes away, the Kohtuotsa and Patkuli viewing platforms deliver the postcard shot of the whole Old Town: a sea of red-tiled roofs, church spires, and the Baltic Sea beyond.

🚶 5 min between the castle and viewpoints 💵 Free viewpoints; castle exterior only 🕐 Late afternoon for the best light on the rooftops
Evening

St. Olaf's Church tower & a Vana Tallinn nightcap

St. Olaf's Church was once among the tallest buildings in the world and its spire still dominates the Lower Town skyline; the narrow spiral climb to its viewing gallery rewards with a different angle over the walls. Follow it with a proper Vana Tallinn tasting, straight, in coffee, or over ice cream, at one of Old Town's wine bars.

🚶 Back down into Lower Town 💵 ~€5 tower climb, drinks vary 🕐 Early evening, before dinner
Morning

The city walls & Kiek in de Kök

Roughly two kilometers of Tallinn's original medieval wall still stand, one of the most complete surviving town fortifications in Europe, punctuated by nearly 20 towers. Kiek in de Kök, a squat 15th-century cannon tower whose name roughly translates to "peek into the kitchen," now holds a museum on the city's defenses, with tunnels connecting to the Bastion Passages below.

🚶 Along the western edge of Old Town 💵 ~€8 combined tower and tunnel ticket 🕐 Morning, before the tunnel tours fill up
Afternoon

Kalamaja's colorful wooden houses

A 10-minute walk from the medieval walls, Kalamaja feels like an entirely different city: a former fishing and industrial quarter of 19th-century wooden houses painted mustard, teal, and rust, now one of Tallinn's most photogenic and lived-in neighborhoods. Streets like Vana-Kalamaja and Kotzebue are worth wandering slowly, camera out.

🚶 ~10 min walk or tram 1/2 from Old Town 💵 Free to wander 🕐 Early-to-mid afternoon light
Evening

Telliskivi Creative City after dark

Next door to Kalamaja, Telliskivi Creative City repurposed a Soviet-era railway workshop complex into Tallinn's liveliest hub of bars, design studios, street art, and street-food stalls at the F-hoone food hall. It's the clearest sign of Tallinn's fast-moving, design-forward, tech-savvy younger generation.

🚶 Adjacent to Kalamaja, near Balti Jaam station 💵 Street food from ~€6-10 🕐 After 6 PM, once the food stalls open
Morning

Seaplane Harbour & Patarei Sea Fortress

Lennusadam, the Seaplane Harbour museum, fills a striking 1917 concrete seaplane hangar with a full-size submarine, a seaplane, and the icebreaker Suur Tõll moored alongside. A short walk along the water, Patarei Sea Fortress, a 19th-century tsarist coastal fortress that later served as a Soviet-era prison, is a stark, atmospheric counterpoint open for self-guided wandering.

🚶 ~20 min walk from Old Town, or tram 1 💵 ~€18 Seaplane Harbour entry 🕐 Morning, allowing 2-3 hours
Afternoon

e-Estonia & last-minute shopping

Estonia runs on some of the most advanced digital-government infrastructure anywhere, from e-Residency to online voting to a health system built on blockchain, a legacy of the same small, agile country that gave the world Skype. Spend a last afternoon back in Old Town's lanes picking up a bottle of Vana Tallinn, a loaf of vacuum-packed black bread, or hand-knit wool for the trip home.

🚶 Back in the Old Town 💵 Souvenirs from ~€10-30 🕐 Early afternoon, ahead of departure
Evening

Transfer to Lennart Meri Airport for departure

The same tram or a short taxi ride brings you back to the airport, so budget about 15 to 20 minutes depending on where you're staying, one of the easiest departures of any European capital.

🚊 ~15 min tram to the airport 💵 ~€2 tram ticket 🕐 Leave at least 2.5 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Tipping isn't obligatory, but 10% at restaurants is common when service is good and often simply rounded up.
  • Card payments are the norm; just say "koos" or ask to add a tip before the terminal is handed over.
  • At cafes, food halls, and takeaway counters, tipping isn't expected at all.

🤫 Estonian Reserve

  • Estonians are famously reserved and value personal space; small talk with strangers, including shopkeepers, is minimal by default and not a sign of unfriendliness.
  • A brief, direct greeting is normal and appreciated over prolonged chatter; warmth tends to show through helpfulness rather than effusiveness.
  • Quiet is the default register on trams and in queues, so lowering your voice in public spaces will help you blend in.

👟 Dress Code & Footwear

  • Estonia is casual almost everywhere; smart-casual is enough even for a nicer dinner.
  • Old Town's cobblestones are uneven and can be slick after rain, so comfortable, sturdy shoes matter more here than in most cities.
  • Baltic weather shifts fast even in summer, so layers you can add or shed through the day are more useful than a single heavy jacket.

💻 A Digitally Native Culture

  • Estonia pioneered e-Residency, online voting, and a fully digital public-services system; 99% of government services are available online.
  • Free public WiFi is genuinely widespread, a holdover from Estonia declaring internet access a legal right in 2000.
  • It's a point of quiet national pride, and locals are often happy to talk about it if you ask.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Estonian

  • Tere: hello
  • Aitäh: thank you
  • Palun arvet: the bill, please

💳 Money & Payments

  • Estonia uses the euro (€), and it's one of the most card-friendly and cashless countries in Europe.
  • Contactless payment is accepted almost everywhere, including trams, market stalls, and public toilets.
  • Carrying cash is rarely necessary, though a small amount is handy at the odd craft stall or food-hall vendor.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Consider the Tallinn Card only if you're museum-heavy

It bundles transit and dozens of museum entries, but Old Town's biggest draws (the walls, squares, and viewpoints) are free to walk, so it only pays off with a packed sightseeing schedule.

🍽️

Eat at Telliskivi's F-hoone food hall

A full meal at Telliskivi's street-food stalls can cost half of the same dish at an Old Town restaurant, and it's a genuine slice of how younger Tallinners actually eat.

🚊

Walk instead of transit within Old Town

The entire medieval core is under a square kilometer; save your tram tickets for trips to Kalamaja, Telliskivi, and Seaplane Harbour.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌳

Patkuli viewpoint over the busier Kohtuotsa

Just a few minutes from the crowded Kohtuotsa platform, Patkuli offers a nearly identical panorama over the rooftops and harbor with a fraction of the visitors.

🏚️

Kalamaja's back streets over the main routes

Wander off Vana-Kalamaja onto smaller lanes like Kotzebue and Kalju for the neighborhood's most colorful, least-photographed wooden houses.

🕯️

Old Town after the day-trippers leave

Tallinn is a popular Baltic Sea cruise stop, and the Old Town empties out noticeably by early evening once the ships depart, leaving the squares to locals and overnight guests.

✈️ Flights

  • Lennart Meri Tallinn Airport (TLL) is just 4 km from the center, one of the closest capital-city airports in Europe.
  • Tram line 4 connects the airport to the Old Town in about 15 minutes; taxis and rideshare take roughly the same time.
  • Regularly voted one of the most pleasant small airports in the world, with a genuinely calm, uncrowded feel.

🚊 Getting Around Tallinn

  • The Old Town, Toompea, and Kalamaja are close enough to walk between at a relaxed pace; trams cover the longer hops to Telliskivi and Seaplane Harbour.
  • A single tram ticket (~€2) or a day pass (~€5) covers the whole network, and Bolt operates reliably for rideshare.
  • Public transit within Tallinn is free for registered residents, but visitors still pay standard fares.

⛴️ The Helsinki Ferry Day Trip

  • Helsinki is a roughly 2-hour ferry ride across the Gulf of Finland, and it's a genuinely popular add-on for travelers with an extra day.
  • Several operators run frequent daily crossings from the Old City Harbour, a short walk from Tallinn's Old Town.
  • It's easily done as a long day trip without an overnight stay if you want to extend a Tallinn base into two countries.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Old Town: puts Raekoja plats, Toompea, and Day 1's evening within a five-minute walk, though it's the busiest and priciest base.
  • Kalamaja: a quieter, design-forward, more affordable base a short tram ride from Old Town, popular with repeat visitors.
  • Rotermann Quarter: a modern district just outside the walls, convenient for an easy Day 4 departure to the airport.

🎫 Guided Tours

  • Free walking tours of the Old Town run daily and are a good low-cost orientation on Day 1.
  • The Bastion Passages beneath Kiek in de Kök can only be visited on a guided tour, booked at the tower itself.
  • A guide adds useful context on Toompea's layered Danish, German, and Russian history that's easy to miss on a self-guided walk.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Standard Schengen entry rules apply, so non-EU visitors should confirm visa requirements before booking flights.
  • Estonia uses the euro, and its card-first culture means ATM withdrawals are rarely necessary.
  • Tallinn is genuinely lovely at Christmas market season in Nov-Dec too, with Raekoja plats hosting one of Europe's oldest town-square markets, though pack for cold.
