What kinds of USA trip are here?

These six trips cover a different USA from the usual city break. Three are long drives (Utah's national parks, the Pacific Coast Highway and the western half of Route 66), two are compact red-rock bases in Arizona and one is a short stop in New York. Daily rates run from about $155 to $375 per person.

Utah is the long loop: 7 days, $950-1,500 per person, a rental car and four national parks. New York City is 2 days at $650-850 per person, so it is brief and costly per day at $375 a day. It fits a stopover or an add-on to a longer trip better than a holiday of its own.

Pacific Coast Highway and Route 66 are the one-way drives, 7 days each. The Pacific Coast Highway runs from San Francisco to Los Angeles at $1,325-1,810 per person and Route 66 from Albuquerque to Santa Monica at $840-1,345. Both totals include each person's half of the car for two travellers, and each page lists car hire, fuel and the one-way fee in a separate table.

Sedona suits travellers who want hiking, sunsets and galleries over 4 days at $800-1,100 per person. Page is 3 days in Page, Arizona, at $700-1,070 per person, with slot canyons, Horseshoe Bend and Lake Powell.

Which month is right for each?

Utah is suggested for April to May and September to October, which avoids the worst of the summer heat in canyon country. Sedona runs March to May and September to November, and New York City April to June and September to November. The two new drives name the same shoulder seasons: Pacific Coast Highway late September to mid-October, with late April to May second best, and Route 66 mid-April to May or late September to October, because summer desert heat is extreme. Late April, May, late September and early October therefore suit five of the six trips.

Page is the exception, with June to August listed because of the light beams in Upper Antelope Canyon. The beams need direct sun, so they appear around midday on clear days, and the trip page says to reserve months ahead for summer.

How do you combine these trips and keep costs down?

Page and Sedona are both in Arizona and Utah in Utah, and Route 66 crosses Arizona on its way from Albuquerque to Santa Monica, so several of these can be strung together on one south-west road trip if you have the days. Page is about 2 hours 15 minutes from Flagstaff and Sedona is about two hours from Phoenix, according to their pages. Flying in and out of different airports can add a one-way drop fee for the rental car, so price the loop first.

New York City works best as a bookend, either before or after the desert trips. On cost, the car rental is the line you can control: the Utah and Sedona pages both say to book weeks ahead. Sharing a car between two or more travellers cuts the per-person cost, and the two new drive pages show the car as its own line so you can see what changes with the number of travellers. Book the timed items early too, such as the Angels Landing permit lottery in Zion and the Upper Antelope Canyon midday tour.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a trip to USA cost?

Our 8 USA itineraries cost from $650 to $1,810 per person for the whole trip, which is about $155 to $375 a day. Prices cover lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and exclude international flights.

Which USA itinerary is the cheapest?

New York City ($650-850), Page ($700-1,070), Sedona ($800-1,100) are the three lowest, per person and excluding flights.

How many days do I need in USA?

Our USA itineraries run from 2 to 7 days, and the most common length is 7 days. Each page has a day picker with a price for every length from one day up to the full plan.

When is the best time to visit USA?

It depends on the trip, and each itinerary lists its ideal season. For example: Utah, Apr – May, Sep – Oct; New York City, Apr – Jun, Sep – Nov; Page, Jun – Aug (Light Beams). The table above shows the ideal season for every USA trip.

Do I need a car for these USA trips?

Yes for the Utah, Sedona and Page trips and for the Pacific Coast Highway and Route 66 drives: the pages all plan around a rental car, and the Utah page notes that services thin out near the parks. The two one-way drives add a one-way drop fee, which the pages mark as something to check with the hire company. New York City is the exception. Its page mentions the subway and the free Staten Island Ferry as ways to get around.

Is two days enough for New York?

It is enough for a first look, and the 48-hour plan covers Midtown, Central Park, downtown and a free view of the Statue of Liberty from the Staten Island Ferry. It is a brisk pace at $375 a day.
