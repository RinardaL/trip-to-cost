3 Days in Barcelona

Gaudí's rooftops, Gothic Quarter alleys, and a beach afternoon in between, a short trip that doesn't feel rushed.

Ideal SeasonMay – Jun, Sep
Est. Budget$500-700
Duration3 Days
PaceRelaxed
How many days do you have?

Morning

Sagrada Família, timed entry

Book the basic ticket on the official site, since slots are timed, named and often sold out ten days or more ahead in busy months. It was about €26 at last check, with the tower around €10 extra (check current, as a centenary surcharge has been reported). Allow two hours, cover shoulders and knees, and look for the blue glass.

🚇 Sagrada Família metro stop 💵 ~€26 🕐 Morning light on Nativity façade
Afternoon

Casa Batlló & Passeig de Gràcia

Tickets are timed and tiered, roughly €29 to over €40 (check current), and the cheapest tier may exclude the roof terrace. The smart guide shows the rooms as Gaudí imagined them. Allow 90 minutes, then walk Passeig de Gràcia past Casa Amatller and Casa Lleó Morera, and continue uphill to Gaudí's Casa Milà if you still have energy.

🚇 ~15 min by metro 💵 ~€29 🕐 Golden hour for the façade
Evening

Tapas dinner in Eixample

Locals sit down from about 9 PM, though many tapas bars open their kitchens earlier. Order a few plates to share, such as patatas bravas, croquetas, pa amb tomàquet and grilled padrón peppers. Try side streets such as Carrer d'Enric Granados rather than La Rambla, and expect about €25 a head. Service is included, so a tip is optional.

🚶 walkable neighborhood 💵 ~€25 🕐 8-9 PM dinner
Morning

Gothic Quarter & the Cathedral

Start at the Cathedral, where a tourist visit costs roughly €9 to €11 (check current) and Sunday visits are limited to the afternoon, when Mass fills the morning. Then wander Plaça Sant Jaume, Plaça del Rei and the neo-Gothic bridge on Carrer del Bisbe. Keep bags zipped, since pickpockets work the crowded lanes.

🚶 old town, walkable 💵 ~€9 cathedral entry 🕐 Early morning, fewer crowds
Afternoon

Barceloneta Beach

Barceloneta is about a 15-minute walk from the old town, or one stop on metro line 4. The sand is free, and beach bars rent loungers. Swimming is comfortable from roughly June to September, while other months suit the promenade. Keep valuables close, as thieves target towels and bags, and walk a block or two inland for better value paella.

🚶 ~15 min from the Gothic Quarter 💵 Free 🕐 Late afternoon, avoid peak sun
Evening

Sunset at Bunkers del Carmel

The bunkers have been closed to visitors after dusk since 2023, so aim for late afternoon light rather than the sunset itself (check current hours, roughly 9 AM to 7:30 PM in summer and 5:30 PM in winter). Take bus 119, or the L4 metro to Alfons X and a steep walk. There is no shade, so bring water.

🚌 ~25 min by bus 💵 Free 🕐 Late afternoon (closed after dusk)
Morning

Park Güell, timed entry

Entry to the Monumental Zone is timed in 30-minute windows and recent price lists show about €18 (check current), well above the older €10 listing, so buy on the official site before you travel. The salamander fountain and the mosaic bench are the stars. Take bus 24 from Plaça Catalunya or walk uphill from Lesseps metro, and allow 90 minutes.

🚌 ~25 min by bus from the center 💵 ~€18 (check current) 🕐 Right at opening
Afternoon

La Rambla & La Boqueria Market

La Rambla runs about 1.2 km from Plaça Catalunya to the Columbus monument, so walk it for the atmosphere, not the prices. La Boqueria, beside Liceu metro, opens 8 AM to 8:30 PM Monday to Saturday and is closed on Sundays. Stalls at the front charge tourist prices, so buy juice and ham from the stands further inside.

🚇 ~20 min by metro 💵 Free to browse 🕐 Before 1 PM, less crowded
Evening

Transfer to the airport

Aerobús runs from Plaça Catalunya to Terminal 1 (line A1) and Terminal 2 (line A2) in about 35 minutes, roughly every 5 to 10 minutes, for around €7 one way (check current). Check your airline's terminal before boarding. Metro line 9 Sud also serves both terminals if you prefer to avoid the road traffic.

🚌 ~35 min to El Prat 💵 ~€7 Aerobús 🕐 3 hrs before flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Not obligatory: round up or leave 5–10% at sit-down restaurants.
  • No tipping expected at tapas bars or cafes.
  • Rounding up for taxis is common but not required.

👗 Dress Code

  • Casual everywhere, but cover shoulders and knees for the Cathedral and Sagrada Família.
  • Beachwear stays at the beach, so cover up when heading back into town.
  • Comfortable shoes for the Gothic Quarter's uneven, centuries-old paving.

🚇 Transit Nuances

  • A T-Casual card (10 rides) is far cheaper than single metro tickets.
  • Validate your ticket at the turnstile every time.
  • The Aerobús is the easiest airport connection: faster than city buses.

⚠️ Safety & Scam Warnings

  • Pickpocketing is a real risk on La Rambla and the metro, so keep bags zipped and in front.
  • Be wary of "spilled" ketchup or bird-poop scams used as a distraction to pick pockets.
  • Use only official taxis (black and yellow) or a licensed rideshare app.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases

  • Hola: hello
  • Gracias: thank you (Spanish) / Gràcies (Catalan)
  • La cuenta, por favor: the bill, please

💳 Money & Payments

  • Cards widely accepted, including most tapas bars.
  • Some small, traditional bars are cash-only, so carry some euros.
  • ATMs are common throughout the city center.

✈️ Flights

  • Barcelona-El Prat Airport (BCN) is the main gateway, about 15 km southwest of the city center.
  • The Aerobús and R2 Nord train both run directly into town, no need to book an airport transfer in advance.
  • Terminal 1 handles most international carriers; confirm your terminal before booking a pickup.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Free Now and Cabify both operate reliably in Barcelona; Uber is more limited than in other major cities.
  • Official black-and-yellow taxis are metered and easy to hail on the street, especially near Plaça Catalunya.
  • Agree on a fixed fare only for the flat-rate airport run; everything else should run on the meter.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Not needed for this itinerary: the metro, buses, and walking cover every stop comfortably.
  • Parking in the Gothic Quarter and Eixample is scarce and expensive, so a car would be a liability, not a convenience.
  • If extending the trip to Costa Brava or Montserrat afterward, rent on your way out of the city rather than for the Barcelona days.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Eixample: central, well-connected by metro, and close to Casa Batlló and Sagrada Família.
  • Gothic Quarter: walkable to almost everything on this itinerary, though rooms can be smaller and noisier at night.
  • Barceloneta: best if a beach morning matters most to you, a short walk or bus ride from the old town.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • Not required for this 3-day plan, but a short Golondrinas harbor cruise from Port Vell is an easy add-on if you have a spare hour.
  • Day trips to Costa Brava fishing towns typically go by rental car or organized tour rather than ferry.
  • No inter-island or coastal ferry legs are part of this itinerary.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • EU/Schengen entry rules apply; non-EU visitors should confirm visa requirements before booking flights.
  • Travel insurance is worth it mainly for the Gaudí site tickets and any flight delays, since most days involve minimal risk.
  • A T-Casual transit card (10 rides) covers metro and bus for the whole trip more cheaply than single tickets.

How much does a 3-day trip to Barcelona cost?

About $500-700 per person for 3 days, roughly $165-235 a day, excluding international flights. The estimate covers lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and real costs vary with the season and your spending habits.
