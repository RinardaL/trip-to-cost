4 Days in Edinburgh: Castle, Old Town & the Scottish Highlands Edge

Edinburgh Castle stands watch over the city from its volcanic crag, and four days is enough time to walk the length of the Royal Mile, hike Arthur's Seat for a view over the Firth of Forth, wander the Georgian streets of the New Town, and still get out to Stirling Castle on the edge of the Highlands before you fly home.

Ideal SeasonMay – Sep
Est. Budget$700-900
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival & transfer into the Old Town

Edinburgh Airport (EDI) sits about 8 miles (13 km) west of the city center, and the Airlink 100 bus or the tram both run straight into the heart of the Old Town.

🚌 ~30 min on the Airlink 100 bus, or ~35 min by tram 💵 ~£4.50 one-way 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time
Afternoon

Edinburgh Castle

Perched on Castle Rock, the extinct volcanic plug at the top of the Old Town, the castle holds the Honours of Scotland, the country's Crown Jewels, the Stone of Destiny, and fires the One O'Clock Gun every day but Sunday.

🚶 ~10 min walk up from the Grassmarket or Royal Mile 💵 ~£19.50 entry, book ahead online 🕐 Right at 9:30 AM opening, before the tour groups
Evening

Royal Mile amble & a historic Old Town pub

Walk down from the Castle esplanade past Gladstone's Land, a restored 17th-century tenement, and the crown-spired St Giles' Cathedral, then settle into one of the Royal Mile's centuries-old pubs for a whisky flight or a pint of local ale.

🚶 The full length of the Royal Mile, Castle to Holyrood 💵 ~£6 per dram, ~£5 per pint 🕐 Early evening, once the shops start closing
Morning

Palace of Holyroodhouse & the Scottish Parliament

The monarch's official residence in Scotland sits at the foot of the Royal Mile beside its ruined abbey, directly across from Enric Miralles' striking modern Scottish Parliament building.

🚶 ~20 min walk from the Old Town, or a short bus ride 💵 ~£19.50 palace entry, Parliament free 🕐 Mid-morning, right after opening
Midday

Hike up Arthur's Seat

This extinct volcano rises 251 meters above Holyrood Park, and the main path up via Salisbury Crags takes about an hour round trip for a panoramic view over the city, the Firth of Forth, and the hills beyond.

🚶 Trailhead right behind the Palace 💵 Free 🕐 Midday, when the ground is driest and the wind is lowest
Afternoon

National Museum of Scotland

Free to enter and a short walk back into town, the museum's galleries run from the Lewis Chessmen to Dolly the Sheep, and the Grand Gallery's glass roof is worth a look even if you skip everything else.

🚶 ~15 min walk from Holyrood Park 💵 Free entry 🕐 Mid-to-late afternoon, a good rest after the climb
Morning

New Town, Princes Street Gardens & the Scott Monument

Cross the valley into the Georgian New Town, a UNESCO World Heritage streetscape of grand terraces, then climb the 287 spiral steps of the Scott Monument for a close-up view over Princes Street Gardens.

🚶 Across the Mound from the Old Town 💵 ~£10 to climb the Monument, gardens free 🕐 Mid-morning, before the steps get busy
Afternoon

Dean Village

A ten-minute walk from the West End, this former grain-milling village on the Water of Leith feels centuries removed from the New Town, with the arched Dean Bridge overhead and a footpath along the river.

🚶 ~10 min walk from Charlotte Square 💵 Free to walk 🕐 Early afternoon, good light for photos
Evening

Sunset from Calton Hill

The city's own acropolis, with the unfinished National Monument, the Nelson Monument, and the Dugald Stewart Monument, gives arguably the best skyline view in Edinburgh, castle on one side and Arthur's Seat on the other.

🚶 ~15 min walk from the east end of Princes Street 💵 Free, small fee to climb the Nelson Monument 🕐 About 45 minutes before sunset
Morning

Train to Stirling & Stirling Castle

ScotRail trains reach Stirling from Edinburgh Waverley in under an hour, and the castle above the town, once home to the Stewart kings, looks out over the Forth Valley toward the first hills of the Highlands.

🚆 ~50 min direct train from Waverley 💵 ~£15 return train, ~£17 castle entry 🕐 Morning departure, right after breakfast
Midday

National Wallace Monument

The 67-meter tower atop Abbey Craig commemorates William Wallace and the 1297 Battle of Stirling Bridge, and the 246 steps to the top reward with a view stretching toward Ben Lomond.

🚌 Local bus or a 25-min walk from Stirling Castle 💵 ~£11.50 entry 🕐 Early afternoon
Afternoon

Return to Edinburgh & departure

Trains run back to Waverley roughly every half hour, leaving enough time to collect luggage and reach Edinburgh Airport for an evening flight, or catch a train onward.

🚆 ~50 min train back to Waverley 💵 Included in the return ticket 🕐 Leave Stirling with at least 3 hrs before an international flight

💷 Tipping Etiquette

  • Around 10% is a common guide at sit-down restaurants, though many already add a service charge, so check the bill first.
  • Card machines rarely have a tip line, round up or tell the server the total to add at the till instead.
  • At pubs and takeaway counters, tipping isn't expected, though rounding up is always appreciated.

👗 Dress Code

  • Scotland's weather can turn in an hour, layers matter more than any particular dress code here.
  • St Giles' Cathedral expects modest, respectful dress, but there's no strict shoulders-and-knees rule to plan around.
  • Solid, waterproof shoes matter most on Day 2's Arthur's Seat climb and Day 1's cobbled Royal Mile.

🚌 Getting Around Edinburgh

  • Lothian Buses covers the whole city on a flat fare, exact change or contactless both work on board.
  • The single tram line runs from the airport through the West End to York Place, handy for Day 1's arrival.
  • The Old Town's steep closes and wynds are best done on foot, and most Day 1 and Day 2 sights sit within walking distance of each other.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Unofficial "guides" approaching near the Castle esplanade offering tours aren't licensed, book through Historic Environment Scotland or a registered operator instead.
  • Arthur's Seat's paths get slippery after rain, wear proper shoes and check the weather before the climb.
  • Pickpockets occasionally work the Royal Mile's tourist crowds in the summer festival season, keep bags zipped and to the front.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases & Scots

  • Aye: yes
  • Braw: great, fine
  • Ken: know, as in "d'ye ken"

💳 Money & Payments

  • The UK uses pounds sterling (GBP), and Scottish banknotes issued by Bank of Scotland, RBS, and Clydesdale are legal tender, though sometimes refused outside Scotland, so spend them before you leave.
  • Contactless cards are accepted almost everywhere, including buses and the tram.
  • Carry a little cash for small independent shops on the Royal Mile and in Dean Village.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Combine Edinburgh & Stirling Castle tickets

If you're doing the Day 4 trip, a joint Historic Environment Scotland ticket covering both castles can beat paying for each separately.

🍽️

Order the lunchtime set menu

Old Town restaurants often post a fixed lunch menu, a starter and main for a fraction of the same dishes' evening à la carte price.

🚆

Book ScotRail tickets in advance

Advance single fares to Stirling are noticeably cheaper than paying on the day at Waverley.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌄

Take the Whinny Hill route up Arthur's Seat

A quieter path to the summit with the same panoramic payoff as the main trail and a fraction of the foot traffic.

⛪

Swap in Rosslyn Chapel for Day 4

If you'd rather skip Stirling, this 15th-century chapel with its carved Apprentice Pillar sits a short bus ride south of the city.

🥃

Ask for the upstairs whisky bar

Some of the Old Town's quietest whisky bars sit one floor above the crowded street-level pubs, worth asking staff for a recommendation.

✈️ Flights

  • Edinburgh Airport (EDI) is Scotland's busiest, about 8 miles (13 km) west of the city center.
  • The Airlink 100 bus and the tram both run directly into the city center in well under 40 minutes.
  • Glasgow Airport is a viable backup for cheaper fares, about an hour away by bus or train.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Uber operates in Edinburgh alongside local firms like City Cabs and Central Taxis.
  • Black cabs can be flagged on the street and are metered, a safe standard option.
  • Avoid unlicensed drivers touting for business outside the airport or Waverley Station.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Not needed for the Old Town or New Town days, everything sits within walking distance or a short bus ride.
  • Handy only if you extend the trip further into the Highlands beyond Stirling.
  • Parking in the city center is limited and metered, factor that in if you do rent.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Old Town, near the Royal Mile: puts Day 1's castle and pub crawl and Day 2's Holyrood walk within easy reach.
  • New Town, near Princes Street: closest to Day 3's Georgian streets and Calton Hill, with easy tram and bus links.
  • West End: a quieter base near Dean Village with good access to the airport tram.

🚆 Trains & Buses

  • Edinburgh Waverley is the hub for ScotRail trains, including the Day 4 trip to Stirling.
  • Lothian Buses runs the city's bus network on a flat fare, day tickets are worth it if you're moving around a lot.
  • The Edinburgh Trams line connects the airport to the city center through the West End.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • The UK is not in the Schengen Area, so separate entry rules apply for non-UK visitors.
  • Remember the currency is pounds sterling, and Scottish banknotes look different from Bank of England notes but spend the same.
  • Travel insurance is worth it given the pre-booked castle and Wallace Monument tickets, which are usually non-refundable.
