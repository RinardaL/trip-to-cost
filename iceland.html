5 Days in Iceland: Golden Circle, South Coast & the Northern Lights

Half-frozen waterfalls, a black sand beach, icebergs drifting past a glacier lagoon, and the aurora as a nightly possibility, a winter road trip built around Iceland's short, dramatic daylight.

Ideal SeasonSep – Mar
Est. Budget$1,600-2,200
Duration5 Days
PaceActive
How many days do you have?

Morning

Land at Keflavik & pick up a 4x4 with winter tires

Keflavik International sits on the Reykjanes peninsula, about 45 minutes from the city. Book a rental with winter tires already fitted, that's standard practice here, not an upgrade.

🚗 ~45 min to Reykjavik 💵 ~$70-90/day 4x4 rental 🕐 Winter daylight often starts around 10-11 AM
Afternoon

Blue Lagoon geothermal spa

Sitting between the airport and Reykjavik, it's the natural first stop before you've even checked in anywhere. Book a timed entry slot weeks ahead, walk-ups get turned away routinely.

🚗 ~20 min from Keflavik Airport 💵 ~$70-110 entry, book ahead 🕐 Reserve a 2-3 hr slot
Evening

Downtown Reykjavik & a first Northern Lights watch

Settle in near Hallgrimskirkja, then walk down to the Sun Voyager sculpture on the waterfront, one of the easiest dark-sky spots in the city on a clear, aurora-forecast night.

🚶 walkable downtown 💵 Free 🕐 From ~9 PM, weather and solar activity dependent, never guaranteed
Morning

Thingvellir National Park

Walk the rift valley where the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates visibly pull apart, and where Iceland's original parliament met in 930 AD. Paths ice over, so grip on your boots matters here.

🚗 ~45 min from Reykjavik 💵 Free entry, small parking fee 🕐 Late morning, once there's enough light
Afternoon

Geysir geothermal field & Strokkur

The original "geyser" that gave English the word is dormant now, but its neighbor Strokkur erupts a reliable 15-20 meters roughly every 5-10 minutes, steam rising hard against the cold air.

🚗 ~45 min from Thingvellir 💵 Free 🕐 Early afternoon
Evening

Gullfoss waterfall

A two-tiered waterfall dropping into a canyon, hung with ice by midwinter, arguably the Golden Circle's most dramatic stop precisely because of the cold.

🚗 ~10 min from Geysir 💵 Free 🕐 Before the light fades, as early as 4-5 PM
Morning

Seljalandsfoss & Gljufrabui

Walk the path that circles behind Seljalandsfoss's curtain of water, genuinely slippery in winter, so traction spikes earn their weight, then find Gljufrabui tucked into a narrow canyon a short walk further on.

🚗 ~2 hr from Reykjavik 💵 Free, small parking fee 🕐 Traction spikes strongly recommended
Afternoon

Skogafoss waterfall

A wide, thundering 60-meter drop you can walk right up to the base of, or climb the steep staircase beside it for a view back over the coastline.

🚗 ~25 min from Seljalandsfoss 💵 Free 🕐 Early afternoon
Evening

Reynisfjara black sand beach & Vik

Basalt columns, roaring surf, and the Reynisdrangar sea stacks offshore, but stay well back from the waterline: the sneaker waves here are genuinely dangerous and have caused fatalities.

🚗 ~10 min from Vik 💵 Free 🕐 Respect the marked safety line, always
Morning

Drive to Vatnajokull National Park & a guided blue ice cave

An early start from Vik, roughly 2.5 hours east. Natural ice caves are only safe to enter when the glacier is frozen solid, typically November through March, so this is a genuinely winter-only bucket-list stop. Always go with a certified guide, never alone.

🚗 ~2.5 hr drive from Vik 💵 ~$150-220/person guided tour 🕐 Book months ahead, winter-only
Afternoon

Jokulsarlon Glacier Lagoon & Diamond Beach

Icebergs calved from Breidamerkurjokull drift across the lagoon before washing onto the black sand across the road as glinting "diamonds." Give yourself real time here; it's the trip's signature stop.

🚶 lagoon-side walk 💵 Free to view; boat tours ~$50-80, seasonal 🕐 Short winter daylight, plan tightly
Evening

Long drive back toward the South Coast

Be honest with yourself here: this is the itinerary's longest day behind the wheel. Don't push through fatigue on icy roads after dark, check road.is before you leave, and consider overnighting closer to Jokulsarlon if the forecast looks rough.

🚗 ~3.5-4 hr drive back 💵 Free 🕐 Roads can close with storms, check road.is
Morning

Scenic drive back to Reykjavik

Retrace the South Coast at a relaxed pace, with room for one more stop if a waterfall got rushed on day 3, or just an easy drive under the low winter sun.

🚗 ~2.5-3 hr drive 💵 Free 🕐 Depart by mid-morning
Afternoon

Harpa Concert Hall & downtown Reykjavik

The glass, honeycomb-paneled Harpa is worth a walk-through even without a ticketed event, and Laugavegur's shops and cafes are an easy way to spend a last afternoon.

🚶 walkable downtown 💵 Free to browse Harpa 🕐 Early-mid afternoon
Evening

Return rental car & depart from Keflavik

Return the car at the airport and leave a genuine buffer: winter weather can slow the roads, and security lines get long in peak season.

🚗 ~45 min to the airport 💵 rental already paid 🕐 Arrive 3 hrs early for international flights

💵 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is already built into prices at restaurants and tour operators, so tipping is not expected.
  • Rounding up a bar tab or leaving a little extra for outstanding service is welcome, never required.
  • Guides on ice cave, glacier, and Northern Lights tours don't expect tips, though it's appreciated for a great trip.

🧥 Dress Code

  • Practical winter layering over style: a moisture-wicking base layer, insulating mid-layer, and waterproof windproof shell.
  • Waterproof boots with real tread matter more here than anywhere: ice, wet lava rock, and black sand all demand grip.
  • Pack a swimsuit no matter the forecast, it's needed for the Blue Lagoon and any geothermal pool, even in a snowstorm.

🚗 Getting Around

  • A rental car is close to essential for this route: the Golden Circle and South Coast aren't realistically covered by public transit.
  • Route 1, the Ring Road, is kept plowed in winter, but the F-road mountain routes are fully closed until summer.
  • Check road.is and the Icelandic Met Office (vedur.is) every morning before driving, conditions change fast.

⚠️ Safety Warnings

  • Reynisfjara's sneaker waves are genuinely dangerous and unpredictable: always stay behind the marked line, no exceptions.
  • Never enter a natural ice cave without a certified guide, structural collapse is a real risk without local expertise.
  • Winter daylight can run as short as 4-5 hours in December, so plan driving legs around it, not around a fixed schedule.

🗣️ Crucial Icelandic Phrases

  • Takk fyrir: thank you
  • Þetta reddast: "it'll work out", the national attitude toward weather, delays, and everything else
  • Gjörðu svo vel: you're welcome / here you go

💳 Money & Payments

  • Iceland runs on cards: contactless is the default even for a coffee or a public restroom fee.
  • Cash is rarely needed and some smaller places don't accept it at all, don't bother exchanging much in advance.
  • Icelanders are famously direct and informal, first names and casual conversation are the norm, not a breach of etiquette.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🛒

Grocery-shop at Bonus or Kronan

Iceland's restaurant prices are famously steep, stocking up on lunches at a discount grocery chain saves real money across five days.

🌊

Book the Blue Lagoon's earliest or latest slot

Early morning and late evening entries tend to run a little cheaper and noticeably less crowded than midday.

🚙

Skip the SUV upgrade for this route

A compact 4x4 with winter tires handles the Ring Road and Golden Circle just fine, since this itinerary never touches the highland F-roads.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌋

Kerid volcanic crater

A quick detour off the main Golden Circle loop, a red volcanic rim ringing a teal-colored lake, with a fraction of Gullfoss's crowds.

🏞️

Fjadrargljufur canyon

A short detour off Route 1 near Kirkjubaejarklaustur, a winding, moss-lined canyon worth the walk in low winter light, check ahead for seasonal path closures.

✈️

The Solheimasandur plane wreck

A rusted DC-3 fuselage sitting alone on black volcanic sand near Skogafoss, reached by a flat, roughly 45-minute walk or a shuttle from the parking area.

✈️ Flights

  • Keflavik International Airport (KEF) is the main gateway, with direct routes from most major European and North American hubs.
  • Icelandair and Play both run frequent seasonal winter schedules, book early since Northern Lights season pushes fares up.
  • Build in a buffer on the return flight, winter weather occasionally delays departures out of Keflavik.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Reykjavik has metered taxis and a local ride-hailing app (Hopp), useful for short in-town trips.
  • Uber and Lyft don't operate in Iceland, don't count on the apps you'd use at home.
  • Outside Reykjavik, taxis are scarce to nonexistent, this itinerary depends on the rental car once you leave the city.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Effectively required for this route: winter tires come standard, confirm full insurance coverage including gravel and wind protection.
  • A 4x4 is recommended for South Coast and Golden Circle driving in winter, even though this itinerary avoids the closed F-roads.
  • Check road.is and vedur.is (the Icelandic Met Office) every morning, road closures and high-wind warnings are common in winter.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Reykjavik: home base for days 1 and 2, walkable to Hallgrimskirkja, Harpa, and the Sun Voyager.
  • Vik i Myrdal: the natural base for day 3's South Coast stops and the day 4 departure toward Jokulsarlon.
  • Hofn or a Jokulsarlon-area guesthouse: worth considering for one night if you'd rather split day 4's long drive in two.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • No ferry is needed for this itinerary, it's entirely a South Coast and Golden Circle road trip.
  • Amphibian boat tours run on Jokulsarlon lagoon itself in some winter months, but schedules are weather-dependent and some operators pause in the coldest weeks, check availability before counting on one.
  • Skip it without worry if it's not running: the lagoon is just as striking viewed from the shore.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Travel insurance that covers winter driving and adventure activities matters more here than on a typical city break.
  • Book the Blue Lagoon and the blue ice cave tour well ahead, both are genuinely capacity-limited in winter.
  • Download an aurora forecasting app before you land, and remember sightings are never guaranteed on any single night.
