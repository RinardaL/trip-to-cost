4 Days in Sedona: Red Rocks, Vortexes & Art Galleries

Red rock hikes, an easy pathway, a scenic canyon drive with a swimming hole, and Sedona's famous vortex sites and gallery-lined streets, mapped into four unhurried days out of Phoenix.

Ideal SeasonMar – May, Sep – Nov
Est. Budget$800-1,100
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate-Active
How many days do you have?

Morning

Land in Phoenix & pick up rental car

A rental car is essential for this trip: no rideshare or transit line reaches the trailheads. Book the SUV weeks ahead in peak season.

🚗 ~2 hr drive to Sedona 💵 ~$60/day rental 🕐 Book weeks ahead
Afternoon

Airport Mesa vortex & overlook walk

An easy loop to one of Sedona's most-visited energy vortex sites, with a sweeping panorama over the red rock formations below.

🚶 easy 0.8 km loop 💵 Free (Red Rock Pass req.) 🕐 Late afternoon
Evening

Uptown Sedona galleries & sunset dinner

Browse the art galleries and boutiques lining Highway 89A, then grab dinner on a patio facing the glowing rocks.

🚶 walkable in town 💵 ~$30 dinner 🕐 Sunset
Morning

Cathedral Rock Trail

A short but steep scramble up sandstone slickrock to the saddle between the spires, Sedona's most-photographed hike. Start early to beat the heat and the crowds.

🥾 ~1.5 km, steep scramble 💵 Free (Red Rock Pass) 🕐 Start by 8 AM
Afternoon

Bell Rock & the Bell Rock Pathway

A gentle, mostly flat pathway circling one of Sedona's other vortex sites, an easy counterpart to the morning's climb.

🚶 ~6.5 km pathway 💵 Free (Red Rock Pass) 🕐 Early afternoon
Evening

Tlaquepaque Arts & Crafts Village

Cobblestone courtyards, fountains, and galleries built to resemble a Mexican village, now home to some of Sedona's best browsing and dinner spots.

🚗 5 min from uptown 💵 ~$35 dinner 🕐 Early evening
Morning

Oak Creek Canyon scenic drive (SR-89A)

A winding highway climbing switchbacks through red-rock canyon walls and ponderosa pine, one of Arizona's most scenic drives.

🚗 ~30 min one-way 💵 Free 🕐 Mid-morning
Afternoon

Slide Rock State Park

Natural sandstone chutes and swimming holes on Oak Creek, plus a historic homestead apple orchard. Arrive early: parking fills fast on summer weekends.

🏊 natural water slides 💵 $20-30/vehicle 🕐 Arrive before 11 AM
Evening

West Fork Trail & dinner back in town

An easy, shaded walk that crosses Oak Creek a dozen times beneath towering canyon walls, then back to Sedona for dinner.

🚶 ~5 km round trip 💵 $12/vehicle day-use 🕐 Late afternoon
Morning

Boynton Canyon vortex hike

A quieter, forested trail to Sedona's other well-known vortex site, described by believers as a balanced blend of masculine and feminine energy and by skeptics as simply a beautiful canyon. Either way, worth the walk.

🥾 ~10 km round trip 💵 Free (Red Rock Pass) 🕐 Morning
Afternoon

Pink Jeep or off-road tour

A guided off-road tour bounces over slickrock routes like Broken Arrow, reaching viewpoints a rental sedan never could. Book ahead in peak season.

🚙 ~2-3 hr tour 💵 ~$100-130/person 🕐 Book ahead
Evening

Return rental car & fly out of Phoenix

Budget the full 2 hours back to Phoenix Sky Harbor, plus a buffer for weekend I-17 traffic.

🚗 ~2 hr drive to PHX 💵 rental already paid 🕐 Arrive 2 hrs early

💵 Tipping Etiquette

  • Standard US tipping applies at sit-down restaurants: 15–20% of the bill.
  • No tipping expected at vortex sites, trailheads, or park kiosks.
  • Jeep tour guides: 15–20% is customary for a good tour.

🥾 Dress Code

  • Casual, practical hiking wear is the norm everywhere, even at nicer restaurants.
  • Big day-to-night temperature swings in the high desert: pack layers.
  • Closed-toe shoes required on most maintained trails; sandals are fine in town.

🔮 Vortex Culture, Explained

  • The "energy vortex" idea traces to 1980s New Age tourism, not a park service or geological designation.
  • Some visitors treat the sites as scenic overlooks; others book a guided vortex tour to feel the difference for themselves.
  • Either approach works: the trails to Airport Mesa, Bell Rock, Cathedral Rock, and Boynton Canyon reward the walk regardless of your take.

⚠️ Safety Warnings

  • Summer monsoon season (Jul–Sep) brings sudden afternoon thunderstorms and flash flood risk in canyons.
  • Slickrock is deceptively slippery, especially near edges and when wet.
  • Carry more water than feels necessary: dry desert air hides how fast you're dehydrating.

🗣️ Trail Talk

  • Slickrock: the smooth sandstone surface much of Sedona's trails are cut into
  • Vortex: a site some believe concentrates spiritual or energy fields
  • Red Rock Pass: the parking permit required at most Coconino National Forest trailheads

💳 Money & Payments

  • Cards accepted almost everywhere in town, including some trailhead self-pay kiosks.
  • Keep a few dollars cash for the automated Red Rock Pass machines that don't take cards.
  • Sales tax runs higher than nearby Phoenix: factor it into gallery purchases.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Buy the $5 Red Rock Pass

Covers most trailhead parking for the day, cheaper than paying per-lot fees.

🥪

Pack a picnic

Skip the Jeep-tour box lunch upsell and bring your own for the trailheads instead.

🏨

Stay in the Village of Oak Creek

Noticeably cheaper nightly rates than uptown, still a short drive to every trailhead in this itinerary.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌄

Doe Mountain or Fay Canyon

Similar red rock views to Cathedral Rock, with a fraction of the crowd.

🌅

Sunrise at Bell Rock, not sunset

Get there at first light and the pathway is often nearly empty.

💧

Grasshopper Point

A quieter Oak Creek swimming hole than Slide Rock, with far less of a parking wait.

✈️ Flights

  • Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport (PHX) is the nearest major airport, about 2 hours south by car.
  • Flagstaff Pulliam (FLG) is closer (~45 min) but has far fewer direct flights and higher fares.
  • Pick up your rental car at PHX and drive straight up I-17 into Sedona.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Uber and Lyft operate in Sedona but coverage thins out fast near the trailheads.
  • Fine for short hops between uptown and Tlaquepaque, not a substitute for a rental car.
  • No rideshare on the PHX-to-Sedona drive: you'll need your own vehicle.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Essential for this itinerary: no public transit connects the trailheads, Oak Creek Canyon, and uptown.
  • A Sedona Red Rock Pass ($5/day or $15/week) covers trailhead parking at nearly every stop on this route.
  • A standard sedan covers this whole itinerary; higher clearance only matters for optional unpaved forest roads.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Uptown Sedona: walkable to galleries and restaurants, closest to Airport Mesa.
  • West Sedona: more budget-friendly, central to the Cathedral Rock and Bell Rock trailheads.
  • Village of Oak Creek: quieter and often cheaper, still a short drive to everything on this itinerary.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • No ferry or boat leg is needed, this itinerary is entirely inland high desert.
  • The closest thing on the water is wading and swimming at Slide Rock State Park on day 3.
  • Nothing to book ahead here beyond the park's day-use entrance fee.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Buy a Red Rock Pass on arrival, or the $80 America the Beautiful pass if stacking with other parks.
  • Book the Jeep or off-road tour ahead in peak season, popular routes sell out.
  • Download offline maps before you lose signal in Oak Creek Canyon.
