What does East Asia and the Pacific cover here?

This hub spans Japan, China, South Korea and New Zealand, so the trips share a region rather than a climate or a style. Each is a short, focused stay of three to seven days with a clear price per person. Japan has the most choice: Tokyo & Kyoto for a seven-day route through both cities, Hakone and Fuji Five Lakes for lake and mountain views, Yoshino for cherry blossom and Niseko for skiing.

China and Seoul are the cheaper end. Zhangye runs at $85 a day, Chongqing and Zhangjiajie at $95, Beijing at $105, and Seoul and Shanghai at $120. At the other end, the Tokyo and Kyoto route costs $235 a day and Waitomo, New Zealand's glowworm caves, $260 a day.

For a city break, look at Seoul, Shanghai or Beijing. For scenery on a budget, look at Zhangjiajie or Zhangye. For a short nature stay, Waitomo, Hakone and Yoshino each take only three days.

How do the seasons differ across these trips?

Spring is the most common listing. Tokyo and Kyoto run from March to May, Beijing from April to May, and Shanghai and Chongqing from March to May. Yoshino is the tightest of all, mid to late April, and because the bloom shifts every year it is worth booking flexible tickets.

Autumn suits Seoul from September to October and the Chinese cities again from September, with Beijing and Zhangjiajie listed to October and Shanghai and Chongqing to November. The Japanese mountain trips lean towards the cold season: Hakone from October to February, Fuji Five Lakes from November to February, and Niseko from December to March. Zhangye is a summer trip, June to September, and Waitomo runs from November to March because New Zealand's summer falls in the northern winter.

How do you combine them and save money?

In Japan, Yoshino can be reached from Kyoto by Kintetsu limited express with one change at Kashiharajingu-mae, in roughly 1 hour 40 minutes to 2 hours, so it can follow the Tokyo and Kyoto trip if you travel in April. Hakone and Fuji Five Lakes both start from Tokyo and share the November to February window, which makes them an easy pair for winter. In China, Beijing and Shanghai are linked by high-speed rail, roughly four and a half hours on the fastest trains, and both are ideal in April to May and September to October.

China is where daily costs are lowest, and Zhangye West station has high-speed trains to and from Lanzhou. In Japan, the Niseko trip is priced at $620-820 per person and uses one All Mountain Pass across four resorts, with ramen and izakaya dinners in Kutchan. The daily figure is the quickest way to compare these trips, so look at it before the total.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a trip to East Asia and the Pacific cost?

Our 12 East Asia & Pacific itineraries cost from $260 to $1,900 per person for the whole trip, which is about $85 to $260 a day. Prices cover lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and exclude international flights.

Which East Asia & Pacific itinerary is the cheapest?

Zhangye ($260-420), Chongqing ($300-460), Beijing ($300-520) are the three lowest, per person and excluding flights.

How many days do I need in East Asia and the Pacific?

Our East Asia & Pacific itineraries run from 3 to 7 days, and the most common length is 4 days. Each page has a day picker with a price for every length from one day up to the full plan.

When is the best time to visit East Asia and the Pacific?

It depends on the trip, and each itinerary lists its ideal season. For example: Seoul, Apr – Jun, Sep – Oct; Waitomo, Nov – Mar; Tokyo & Kyoto, Mar – May. The table above shows the ideal season for every East Asia & Pacific trip.

Which trip suits cherry blossom season?

Yoshino, which is planned for mid to late April and has around 30,000 cherry trees on the mountain slopes. Tokyo and Kyoto also fits spring, from March to May. Bloom dates move each year, so book flexible tickets and accommodation rather than fixed dates.

Can I see Mount Fuji on a short trip from Tokyo?

Yes. Hakone takes three days and adds Lake Ashi, an onsen and the Open-Air Museum, while Fuji Five Lakes takes four and focuses on Chureito Pagoda and Lake Kawaguchiko. Both are listed for the colder months, and views depend on the weather, so keep your days flexible.
