What does the Balkans hub cover, and which trip fits you?

These twelve trips spread across Montenegro, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia, Bulgaria and Albania. What they share is mixed terrain: Adriatic and Ionian shoreline, Ottoman and medieval town centres, and mountain ranges with ski resorts and national parks. The styles split into old towns, beaches, ski resorts and a few nature trips. Pick the style first, then the country.

For a first visit built around a walled or Ottoman town, Kotor and Mostar are the compact choices, and Sofia is the city option. Beach holidays are Budva, Sarandë and Dhërmi & Palasë. Skiers can choose between Sarajevo & Jahorina, Kopaonik and Borovets, and Bansko is a mountain base listed for summer instead. Plitvice Lakes is the shortest at three days, built around boardwalk trails.

Daily spend varies widely across the hub. Mostar is $80 per day and Dhërmi & Palasë $95, while Borovets is $150 and Kotor and Plitvice Lakes are $145.

When should I go?

Beach trips fall in summer: Dhërmi & Palasë is listed for June to September, and Budva and Sarandë for May to September. The old towns lean towards spring and early autumn. Mostar lists April to June and September to October, while Kotor and Sofia list May to June and September, so high summer is not their listed slot. Check the listed months against your dates before booking flights.

Winter is a separate trip. Sarajevo & Jahorina, Kopaonik and Borovets all list December to March, with a ski day at the centre of each. Plitvice Lakes suits May to June and September to October, while the six-day Krujë road trip and Bansko both list June to September.

Can I combine trips, and where does the money go?

Several itineraries overlap, so read them before you stack them. Kotor already ends in Budva's old town, Sarandë and Krujë both visit the Blue Eye and Gjirokastër, and Dhërmi & Palasë and Krujë share Palasë and the Llogara Pass. Rila Monastery appears in Sofia, Bansko and Borovets, so you only need one of them for that visit.

Natural pairings do exist. Mostar and Sarajevo & Jahorina both start from Sarajevo, though in different seasons, and Kotor and Budva sit on the same Montenegrin coast. The Mostar page lands at Sarajevo and takes the train south rather than starting in Mostar itself.

To save money, start with the lowest totals. Mostar at $220-420 per person and Dhërmi & Palasë at $280-480 are the cheapest options on the hub. For a ski trip, Sarajevo & Jahorina at $115 per day costs less than Kopaonik at $125 or Borovets at $150. Whichever you choose, budget separately for getting from the airport to your base, since the Kopaonik page begins with the road from Belgrade.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a trip to the Balkans cost?

Our 12 Balkans itineraries cost from $220 to $750 per person for the whole trip, which is about $80 to $150 a day. Prices cover lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and exclude international flights.

Which Balkans itinerary is the cheapest?

Mostar ($220-420), Dhërmi & Palasë ($280-480), Budva ($300-520) are the three lowest, per person and excluding flights.

How many days do I need in the Balkans?

Our Balkans itineraries run from 3 to 6 days, and the most common length is 4 days. Each page has a day picker with a price for every length from one day up to the full plan.

When is the best time to visit the Balkans?

It depends on the trip, and each itinerary lists its ideal season. For example: Kotor, May – Jun, Sep; Budva, May – Sep; Plitvice Lakes, May – Jun, Sep – Oct. The table above shows the ideal season for every Balkans trip.

Which Balkan trips suit a ski holiday?

Three trips are built around skiing and list December to March: Sarajevo with Jahorina, Kopaonik in Serbia, and Borovets in Bulgaria. Borovets adds a Rila Monastery day and a final stop in Sofia, while Kopaonik ends with a monastery visit at Studenica or Sopoćani, so compare which extra sightseeing you prefer alongside the daily cost.

Why do some Balkan itineraries repeat the same sights?

Neighbouring bases share attractions. The Blue Eye appears in both the Sarandë and the six-day Albania trips, and Rila Monastery appears in the Sofia, Bansko and Borovets itineraries. If you plan to do several, pick one trip for each shared sight so you do not spend a day on the same place twice.
