4 Days Skiing in Val Gardena: Sella Ronda, Seceda & Alpe di Siusi

Four winter days in the Dolomites: the Sella Ronda circuit, the Seceda slopes and the La Longia run, a gentle day on Alpe di Siusi, plus pass prices, rentals and transfers from Bolzano.

4-day Val Gardena itinerary

How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Bolzano Airport & bus 350 to the valley

Bolzano airport (BZO) is about 40 km from the valley, with SkyAlps flights from several European cities. Otherwise land at Innsbruck, Verona, Venice or Munich and take a shared transfer or a train to Bolzano. From Bolzano station, bus 350 reaches Ortisei, Santa Cristina and Selva in about an hour (check current times).

🚌 ~1 hr by bus 350 from Bolzano station 💵 Bus ~€5-10, free with a Guest Pass (check current) 🕐 Plan around your landing time, then drop your bags
Afternoon

Ski rental & the Resciesa plateau above Ortisei

Collect skis and boots today, typically about €30-45 a day for the full set (check current), so tomorrow starts on the slopes. Then walk Ortisei's centre and take the Resciesa funicular up to the plateau for a flat walk to a small chapel with valley views. Check the funicular's winter hours first.

🚠 Funicular from the edge of Ortisei, then ~30 min on foot 💵 Rental ~€30-45 a day, funicular extra (check current) 🕐 Mid-afternoon, before the light fades
Evening

Dusk in Ortisei & a South Tyrolean dinner

Ortisei is the largest of the three villages, and the church tower is lit after dark. Eat in a South Tyrolean Stube: canederli (bread dumplings), Schlutzkrapfen (spinach ravioli) or venison goulash suit a cold evening. Main courses usually cost about €14-22 (check current). Book at weekends, and sleep early for tomorrow's long day.

🚶 On foot or by the free valley bus 💵 ~€25-40 for dinner with a drink 🕐 Dinner from 19:00, early night
Morning

Selva to Passo Gardena, Colfosco & Corvara

The Sella Ronda is a 40 to 44 km circuit round the Sella massif over four passes: Gardena, Campolongo, Pordoi and Sella. Orange signs mean clockwise. Take the lifts from Selva towards Passo Gardena, then ski down to Colfosco and Corvara. Start before 10:00. It suits solid intermediates and needs a Dolomiti Superski pass.

⛷️ Orange route: Selva, Passo Gardena, Colfosco, Corvara 💵 Dolomiti Superski pass ~€329 for 4 days in high season (check current) 🕐 First lift at Selva, and start before 10:00
Afternoon

Passo Campolongo, Arabba & Passo Pordoi

From Corvara the lifts lead over Passo Campolongo to Arabba, then up to Passo Pordoi, above 2,100 m. Lunch at a hut on the route: a plate of pasta or canederli is typically €12-18 (check current). Cross the last pass before about 15:30, because lifts close in the late afternoon. Check lift status on the day.

⛷️ Corvara, Campolongo, Arabba, Pordoi by lift and piste 💵 Lunch ~€12-18 at a hut (check current) 🕐 Cross the last pass before about 15:30
Evening

Canazei, Passo Sella & back to Selva

From Pordoi, ski down towards Canazei and take the Lupo Bianco lift to Passo Sella, with Sassolungo ahead, then the last run returns you to Selva. With lifts the whole circuit takes 5 to 6 hours. Green signs run the same loop anticlockwise, starting with Passo Sella, so use them for a repeat. Pizza in Selva is about €10-14.

⛷️ Canazei, Lupo Bianco lift, Passo Sella, Selva 💵 Included in the pass, ~€20-30 for dinner 🕐 Back in Selva by about 16:30 if you start early
Morning

The Ortisei to Seceda cable car

The Seceda lift runs in two stages from Ortisei: a gondola to Furnes, then a cable car to the top. Seceda faces south and is one of the sunniest areas in the valley. Skiers use their pass. Walkers can buy a return ticket at the valley station (check current) and walk about 10 minutes to the viewpoint with microspikes.

🚠 Gondola to Furnes, then cable car to Seceda 💵 Pass, or a walkers' return ticket (check current) 🕐 First lifts, to have the slopes quiet
Afternoon

La Longia, 10 km back to Ortisei

La Longia is a 10.5 km red run from Seceda to Ortisei with a drop of about 1,273 m, one of the longest in the Dolomites. It suits confident intermediates, so allow 1 to 1.5 hours with stops. Pack a supermarket roll, or pay hut prices at Seceda. Check slope and lift status on the day.

⛷️ Seceda to Ortisei by piste, about 1 to 1.5 hours 💵 Included in the pass, picnic ~€5 🕐 Start the run by mid-afternoon
Evening

Après-ski, ice skating or a swim in Ortisei

Ortisei has an ice rink, about €8 for adults with skates (check current), and the Mar Dolomit pool and sauna for tired legs (check hours and prices). Guest Pass holders may get discounts at partner venues, so ask at the hotel. Dinner of pizza, polenta or a Tyrolean platter usually costs about €12-20 (check current).

🚌 Free valley bus, or walk within Ortisei 💵 Skating ~€8, dinner ~€12-20 (check current) 🕐 Ice and pool in the evening, check opening hours
Morning

The Ortisei to Alpe di Siusi gondola

The gondola from Ortisei climbs to Alpe di Siusi, Europe's largest high-alpine plateau, which has gentle pistes for beginners and families. Both the Val Gardena pass and the Dolomiti Superski pass cover it. Walkers pay a separate fare (check current). The cabins start around 8:30, so go early for firm snow and fewer people.

🚠 Gondola from the edge of Ortisei 💵 Included in the pass, walkers pay extra (check current) 🕐 First cabins around 8:30 (check current)
Afternoon

Skiing, sledging or snowshoeing on the plateau

Choose your pace: ski the wide plateau, hire a sledge or follow the groomed winter paths. Do not rely on a car: the Compatsch road is restricted by day (check current), so use the gondola. Huts serve lunch, and Kaiserschmarrn (shredded pancake) costs about €10-14 (check current). Leave the plateau by about 15:00.

🚶 Winter paths and pistes from the gondola station 💵 Sledge hire and a hut lunch, about €20-30 (check current) 🕐 Leave the plateau by about 15:00
Evening

Bus 350 to Bolzano & the journey home

Collect your bags and take bus 350 to Bolzano, about an hour. Bolzano airport is a short local bus or taxi from the station. For other airports, book a shared transfer in advance or take a train from Bolzano. Allow at least 3 hours before an international flight, and avoid Saturday midday traffic on the valley road.

🚌 ~1 hr by bus 350, then a short hop to the airport 💵 Bus ~€5-10 (check current) 🕐 Leave Ortisei at least 4 hours before your flight

Val Gardena: what to know before you go

💶 Tipping, Huts & Cover Charges

  • Tipping is not expected in Italy. If service was good, round up the bill or leave a euro or two per person, and say the total when you pay.
  • Restaurants in the valley may add a small cover charge (coperto) or a service charge. Both should be shown on the menu, so check it.
  • Mountain huts are often busy at lunchtime. Order at the counter or the table, and pay at the end before you put your skis back on.

🍲 Eating & Drinking in South Tyrol

  • Local dishes are canederli (bread dumplings), Schlutzkrapfen (spinach ravioli), speck, goulash and Kaiserschmarrn (shredded pancake). Pizza and pasta are everywhere too, since the valley is Italian.
  • Hot drinks on the slopes are Glühwein (mulled wine), hot chocolate and Tee mit Rum. Drink sensibly before skiing down, since slope accidents are common at the end of the day.
  • Lunch usually runs from 12:00 to 14:00 and dinner from 19:00. Many kitchens close between meals, so a late lunch can be hard to find in the villages.

⛷️ Piste Rules, Insurance & Helmets

  • Italy requires skiers to have third-party liability insurance, and helmets are compulsory for under-18s (check current rules). Many adults wear one anyway.
  • Follow the signs: blue is easy, red is intermediate and black is expert. Slow down at junctions and stop only at the edge of the piste, never below a crest.
  • Lift passes are read by electronic gates, so keep yours in a jacket pocket rather than a bag.

⚠️ Safety, Avalanches & Driving Warnings

  • Stay on marked and open pistes. Off-piste skiing outside the patrolled area carries avalanche risk, so check the South Tyrol avalanche bulletin at lawinen.report and go only with a guide and the right gear.
  • In an emergency call 112 (or 118 for mountain rescue). Mountain rescue and helicopter costs can be high, so check your travel insurance covers skiing.
  • Weather can change fast above 2,000 m. Check lift status and webcams on the valley website, and turn back if visibility drops.
  • Check official advice before you go: the UK FCDO (gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/italy) or the US State Department (travel.state.gov) travel pages for Italy.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in German, Italian & Ladin

  • "Grüß Gott" (hello, German, say "GROOS got"), "Buongiorno" (good day, Italian), "Bun dé" (good day, Ladin)
  • "Danke" (thank you, German), "Grazie" (thank you, Italian), "Bitte" or "Prego" (please or you are welcome)
  • "Einen Tisch für zwei, bitte" (a table for two, please), "Die Rechnung, bitte" (the bill, please)
  • "Sprechen Sie Englisch?" (do you speak English?), "Wo ist die Gondel?" (where is the gondola?), "Wie viel kostet das?" (how much is it?)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Italy uses the euro. Cards and contactless work at lift stations, hotels and most restaurants, but keep some cash for small huts and shops.
  • Use bank-branded ATMs in Ortisei or Selva and avoid standalone exchange booths. If a terminal offers to charge you in your home currency, choose euros.
  • A small local tourist tax per person per night is added at hotels and apartments, often paid in cash. Check the amount when you book.

Insider tips for Val Gardena

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Buy online and pick the right pass

For 2026/27 high season the Dolomiti Superski adult pass is about €257 for 3 days and €329 for 4 days, and the Val Gardena and Alpe di Siusi pass is about €239 and €306 (check current). Booking two days ahead saves 5%. Only the Superski pass covers the Sella Ronda.

📅

Travel in the shoulder weeks

Shoulder-season passes cost less: about €231 for 3 days and €296 for 4 on the Dolomiti Superski pass, in late November, mid-January and after 21 March (check current). Hotels are also cheaper outside Christmas, New Year and the school-holiday weeks.

🥪

Picnic once, hut lunch once

A hut meal can cost €12-18 a head (check current). Take a supermarket roll and fruit on one of your four days, and book huts only on the Sella Ronda day. Staying in Selva or Santa Cristina instead of Ortisei, with a kitchenette, can also cut costs.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

Seceda early and Passo Sella late

Seceda faces south and is sunniest at midday, but the first lift gives the quietest slopes. Passo Sella, towards the end of the Sella Ronda, shows Sassolungo in warm light in the late afternoon.

🛷

Night sledging with a hut dinner

Evening sledging with a three-course dinner starts at the Seceda valley station several nights a week in winter. Listings show about €98-102 including the lift, sledge hire and headlamp (check current). Book ahead and wrap up warmly.

🚌

Use the Guest Pass on the ski bus

Most partner hotels give a free Guest Pass that covers valley buses and most regional public transport. The ski bus runs about every 15 minutes between Ortisei, Santa Cristina and Selva, so skip the taxi (check current timetable).

Travel services for Val Gardena

✈️ Flights

  • Bolzano (BZO) is the closest airport, about 40 km away, with SkyAlps flights from several European cities. Innsbruck (INN) is about 120 km, Verona (VRN) about 190 km, Venice (VCE) about 270 km and Munich (MUC) about 300 km.
  • Driving takes roughly 1.5 hours from Innsbruck, 2 to 2.5 hours from Verona, 3 hours from Venice and 3.5 hours from Munich in normal conditions (check current).
  • Roadworks on the Lueg Bridge on the Brenner route may slow the drive from Innsbruck and Munich, so the valley tourist office suggests considering Italian airports (check current).

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Shared door-to-door transfers, such as Südtirol Transfer, take you from Bolzano or other airports to your hotel. Book ahead, especially on Saturdays.
  • A private transfer is the easiest option for a group. Third-party estimates for a family of four are about €200-300 door to door (check current).
  • Do not count on rideshare apps in the mountains. Pre-book a transfer or call a local taxi, and ask the hotel about luggage and ski transport (Insam Express moves skis and bags between locations).

🚌 Public Transport & Tickets

  • Val Gardena has no railway station. The nearest main stations are Bolzano and Bressanone, and bus 350 (operated by Alto Adige Bus, formerly SAD) runs from Bolzano to Ortisei, Santa Cristina and Selva.
  • A winter ski bus runs about every 15 minutes between Ortisei, Santa Cristina and Selva. Validate every ticket each time you board, including the Guest Pass.
  • The Guest Pass is free with partner hotels and covers valley buses and nearly all South Tyrol public transport, from the arrival day to the departure day. It does not cover the Nightbus (about €3) or the Monte Pana and Saltria bus (about €5 single).
  • Check whether your Guest Pass covers the Bolzano leg on bus 350 before you travel.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Selva: the nearest base for the Sella Ronda lifts, with the liveliest evenings.
  • Santa Cristina: quieter, between the other two villages, with its own lifts and the valley bus.
  • Ortisei: the biggest village, with shops, the old centre and the lifts to Seceda and Alpe di Siusi, but usually pricier.
  • Apartments with a kitchen save on meals. Compare weekly and shoulder-season rates, and check whether the property gives a Guest Pass.
  • Book early for Christmas, New Year, February half-term and Saturday changeovers.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Italy is in the EU, so EU SIM holders roam at home rates. Others can use an eSIM or a local prepaid SIM, and hotels offer Wi-Fi.
  • Signal is good in the villages but can drop on remote slopes, so download offline maps and the lift map before you go.
  • Cards work at most lifts, hotels and restaurants, but keep cash for small huts, shops and the hotel tourist tax.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • If you drive, winter tyres or chains on board are required on signposted roads from 15 November to 15 April (check current), with fines that vary by region. Ask your rental firm to confirm the car is equipped.
  • Ortisei's centre is a restricted traffic zone, so ask your hotel about a permit. Avoid driving on Saturdays in peak season, between about 10:00 and 16:00.
  • Check your passport's Schengen rules before booking, including whether ETIAS applies on your travel dates. Take out travel insurance that covers skiing.
  • Check the official sources before you go: valgardena.it, dolomitisuperski.com and the Alto Adige Bus (SAD) timetable.
  • If you want a slower trip, see our summer guide to the same area at /dolomites for Alpe di Siusi and the passes.
