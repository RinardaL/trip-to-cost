48 Hours in New York City

💵 Tipping Etiquette

  • 18–20% is standard at sit-down restaurants, so check if it's already added for large groups.
  • $1–2 per drink at bars, $2–5 for hotel bellhops/housekeeping.
  • No tipping expected at fast-casual counters.

👟 Dress Code

  • Casual is the norm everywhere, including most restaurants.
  • Comfortable shoes matter, since you'll likely walk 8-10 miles a day.
  • Smart-casual for a Broadway show or a nicer dinner reservation.

🚇 Transit Nuances

  • Tap a contactless card or phone directly at the subway turnstile (OMNY), with no need for a MetroCard anymore.
  • The subway runs 24/7, though service changes on weekends are common.
  • Yellow cabs and rideshare apps both work well; traffic makes the subway faster at rush hour.

⚠️ Safety Notes

  • NYC is generally safe for tourists in central areas, though standard city awareness applies.
  • Avoid empty subway cars late at night; ride in cars with other passengers.
  • Only use official yellow cabs or licensed rideshare apps, never unmarked "gypsy cabs" at the airport.

🗣️ Good to Know

  • English-speaking: no language barrier to plan around.
  • OMNY: the tap-to-pay subway/bus fare system.
  • TKTS booth: same-day discount Broadway tickets in Times Square.

💳 Money & Payments

  • Cards and tap-to-pay accepted virtually everywhere, minimal cash needed.
  • Sales tax (around 8.875%) is added at checkout, not included in listed prices.
  • ATMs are everywhere, though bank ones avoid extra withdrawal fees.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌳

The High Line

An elevated park built on an old rail line: a quieter, greener way to cross the West Side.

🌉

DUMBO over the Brooklyn Bridge itself

The classic skyline photo spot, with far less foot traffic than the bridge walkway at peak hours.

🚡

Roosevelt Island Tram

A cheap aerial cable car with skyline views most visitors never think to ride.

✈️ Flights

  • Three airports serve the city: JFK, LaGuardia (LGA), and Newark (EWR), so pick whichever has the best flight.
  • JFK and LGA connect more directly into Manhattan; EWR needs an extra transfer by train or bus.
  • Skip booking a separate airport transfer car, yellow cabs and rideshare both queue right outside arrivals.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Yellow cabs and rideshare apps (Uber, Lyft) both work well here, unlike in road-trip destinations.
  • Traffic makes cabs slower than the subway at rush hour, worth checking before hailing one.
  • Only use official yellow cabs or licensed rideshare pickups at the airport, never unmarked "gypsy cabs."

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Skip it entirely: a car in Manhattan is a liability, not a convenience.
  • Parking is scarce and expensive, and traffic makes driving slower than walking or the subway here.
  • Everything on this two-day plan is reachable on foot, by subway, or by the free Staten Island Ferry.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Midtown: closest to Times Square, Top of the Rock, and the Theater District for the Broadway show.
  • Near Central Park: convenient for the museum walk and a quieter base than Times Square itself.
  • Lower Manhattan: puts you closer to the Staten Island Ferry and Brooklyn Bridge for day two.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • The Staten Island Ferry is already built into day two: free, and it sails right past the Statue of Liberty.
  • No other boat legs are needed, everything else on this itinerary is walkable or a subway ride.
  • Ride before 9 AM as planned to skip both the ferry line and the crowds.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Set up OMNY (tap-to-pay) on your card or phone before you land, no need to buy a MetroCard anymore.
  • Sales tax (about 8.875%) is added at checkout, so budget slightly above listed prices.
  • Book Broadway tickets ahead or line up at the TKTS booth for same-day discounts.
