4 Days in Sarajevo & Jahorina: Old Town, Olympic Sites & Skiing

Sarajevo's old bazaar and 1984 Olympic sites, then 30 km up to Jahorina for a budget ski break: four winter days with ćevapi, burek and every price in convertible marks (KM).

4-day Sarajevo & Jahorina itinerary

How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Sarajevo Airport & the bus to Baščaršija

Sarajevo airport (SJJ) is about 12 km from the old town. The Centrotrans airport bus to Baščaršija costs about 5 KM (about €2.50, check current), takes around 30 minutes, and one bag up to 23 kg is free. Taxis wait outside Terminal B and charge roughly 30 KM (about €15) by meter. Use licensed taxis only, with a roof sign.

🚌 ~30 min by airport bus or taxi to Baščaršija 💵 ~5 KM bus, ~30 KM taxi (check current) 🕐 Plan around your landing time, then drop your bags
Afternoon

Baščaršija & the Sebilj

Baščaršija is the Ottoman-era bazaar at the heart of the old town. Start at the Sebilj, the wooden fountain on Pigeon Square, then walk Kazandžiluk, the lane of the coppersmiths, and the courtyard of Morića Han. A small Bosnian coffee costs about 3 to 4 KM (check current). Cover shoulders and knees before you enter a mosque.

🚶 On foot, everything is within ~10 min 💵 Free to wander, coffee ~3-4 KM (check current) 🕐 Afternoon, when the shops and coffee houses are open
Evening

The Vijećnica, the Latin Bridge & a first ćevapi

The Vijećnica, the 1896 city hall, glows beside the Miljacka river. It reopened in 2014 after wartime fire damage, and interior visits are ticketed (check current). The Latin Bridge is about 5 minutes on foot. For dinner, order a desetka, ten ćevapi in somun bread with onions, at Željo or Petica: about 10 KM (about €5), often cash only.

🚶 ~10 min on foot from Baščaršija to the Vijećnica 💵 Dinner ~10-14 KM (about €5-7, check current) 🕐 After dark, when the Vijećnica is lit
Morning

Trebević cable car & the Olympic bobsled track

The Trebević cable car starts near Baščaršija and the ride takes about 7 minutes. A return ticket for foreign visitors was 20 KM (about €10, check current), and it can close in strong wind, so check winter hours. At the top, walk the signed path to the 1984 bobsled track. Wear boots with grip and stay on marked paths.

🚡 ~7 min by cable car, then a signed walk 💵 ~20 KM return for visitors (check current) 🕐 Mid-morning, check winter hours and wind closures
Afternoon

The Zetra Olympic Hall

Zetra is the hall built for the 1984 Winter Games, rebuilt after heavy war damage. It lies about 3 km from Baščaršija, so take a short taxi (roughly 5 to 8 KM, check current). The exterior is free to see, and the rink may offer public skating in winter (check current). Buy a burek (about 5 KM) on the way.

🚕 ~10 min by taxi from Baščaršija 💵 Exterior free, taxi ~5-8 KM (check current) 🕐 Early afternoon, then collect your bags
Evening

Transfer to Jahorina

Jahorina is about 30 km from Sarajevo, 45 to 60 minutes by road. A taxi costs about 60 KM (about €31, check current) for the car, so split it. Direct buses are seasonal and sometimes weekends only: ask Centrotrans or the Jahorina Olympic Centre, or take a bus to Pale and a taxi. Agree the fare before you set off.

🚕 ~45-60 min by taxi, ~30 km 💵 ~60 KM per taxi (check current) 🕐 Leave before dark, as mountain roads can be icy
Morning

Lift pass, rental & the Poljice slopes

The 2026/27 adult day pass is 98 KM (about €50) and a 2-day pass is 166 KM (about €85), with 15 to 30 percent off in some periods (oc-jahorina.com). Rent skis near the lifts (about 25 to 40 KM a day, check current). Take the Poljice gondola and start on the gentle slopes.

🚠 Poljice gondola up from the village 💵 2-day pass 166 KM, rental ~25-40 KM a day (check current) 🕐 Be at the lifts by 9:00 to beat the queues
Afternoon

Ogorjelica & the medium runs

After lunch, try the Ogorjelica area for medium runs. Complete beginners should stay on the gentler Poljice slopes. A one-hour private lesson was 70 KM (about €36) on the 2025/26 ski school list (check current). A pita or grilled sausages at a mountain hut costs about 8 to 12 KM (check current).

⛷️ Chairlifts between the slopes, no extra fare 💵 Lesson ~70 KM per hour, lunch ~8-12 KM (check current) 🕐 Ski until about 15:30, when the snow turns heavy
Evening

Dinner in the village

Night skiing costs about 80 KM (about €41), so a budget evening is better spent in the village. Grilled meat, pita and a mug of kuhano vino (mulled wine) cost roughly 15 to 25 KM (check current). A guesthouse or apartment costs far less than a slopeside hotel, and some pensions in Pale are cheaper still.

🚶 On foot around the village 💵 Dinner ~15-25 KM (check current) 🕐 Dinner from 18:00 after the lifts close
Morning

Last runs on Jahorina

Your 2-day pass covers this morning, since multi-day passes are used on consecutive days. Ski until about midday, then return your rental gear. Keep the key card, as a deposit applies and is refunded when you hand it back (check current). Book your taxi down for early afternoon the evening before.

⛷️ Chairlifts to the same slopes 💵 Included in the 2-day pass 🕐 Ski until ~12:00, taxi booked for early afternoon
Afternoon

The road down through Pale

Take the taxi back to Sarajevo (about 60 KM, 45 to 60 minutes) or a bus via Pale if one runs. Pale, about 12 km from Jahorina, is the largest town on the way, with shops and cafés. Have a burek in Baščaršija (about 5 KM, check current) and collect your bags before heading out.

🚕 ~45-60 min by taxi via Pale 💵 ~60 KM per taxi (check current) 🕐 Leave Jahorina by 14:00 for an evening flight
Evening

Transfer to Sarajevo Airport

Take the Centrotrans bus from Baščaršija to the airport for about 5 KM (check current), or a taxi at about 30 KM (about €15). Buses run until late evening, so check the timetable on centrotrans.com. Arrive at least 2 hours before a European flight, and spend leftover marks before you leave, since KM is hard to exchange abroad.

🚌 ~30 min by bus or taxi 💵 ~5 KM bus, ~30 KM taxi (check current) 🕐 Arrive at the airport at least 2 hours before your flight

Sarajevo & Jahorina: what to know before you go

💶 Tipping & Hospitality

  • Tipping is not compulsory, but rounding up the bill or leaving about 10 percent in a restaurant is normal for good service. For taxis, round up to the next mark.
  • Service is rarely added to the bill, so check it before you tip. In small ćevabdžinice and bakeries, nobody expects a tip.
  • In a Bosnian home or a mosque, take off your shoes at the door. A small gift such as sweets is welcome if you are invited to a home.

🥙 Eating & Drinking Like a Local

  • Ćevapi are small grilled minced-meat sausages served in somun bread with chopped onion, and kajmak (a soft dairy cream) is a popular side. A portion of ten is called a desetka.
  • Burek is the spiral pastry filled with minced meat. The cheese version is sirnica, spinach is zeljanica and potato is krompiruša, so say which one you want. Eat it with a cup of yoghurt.
  • Bosnian coffee is served in a small copper pot with a cup, a sugar cube and often a piece of Turkish delight. Sip it slowly and leave the grounds in the cup, as it is meant to be a long, unhurried pause.

🕌 Mosques, Dress & Winter Hours

  • Cover shoulders and knees when entering a mosque, and women should bring a headscarf. Take off your shoes and keep your voice down. Do not enter during prayer times.
  • Sarajevo mixes mosques, Orthodox and Catholic churches and a synagogue within a few streets, and alcohol is widely sold, so dress and behaviour are relaxed outside religious sites.
  • Many cafés and restaurants close early in winter, and some museums keep shorter hours or close on Mondays. Check hours before you go.

⚠️ Safety, Scams & Etiquette Warnings

  • Unexploded landmines from the 1990s war remain in parts of the countryside and mountains. Stay on marked paths, tracks and groomed ski runs, never go off-piste in unfamiliar forest, and never touch war debris. Read the latest advice on GOV.UK or travel.state.gov before you travel.
  • Pickpocketing is the main crime visitors meet in Baščaršija and on crowded trams. Keep your phone and wallet in a front pocket.
  • Use licensed taxis only, with a roof sign, and agree a price for long trips such as Jahorina. Unlicensed drivers at the airport and station may overcharge.
  • On still, cold days, Sarajevo can have heavy air pollution. Check a live air-quality app, and make sure your travel insurance covers skiing and mountain rescue.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Bosnian

  • "Dobar dan" (DOH-bar dahn, good day), "Dobro veče" (DOH-bro VEH-cheh, good evening), "Zdravo" (ZDRAH-vo, hello)
  • "Molim" (MOH-leem, please), "Hvala" (HVAH-lah, thank you), "Izvinite" (eez-VEE-nee-teh, excuse me)
  • "Desetka sa lukom, molim" (DEH-set-ka sa LOO-kom MOH-leem, ten ćevapi with onion, please), "Račun, molim" (RAH-choon, the bill, please)
  • "Govorite li engleski?" (GO-vo-ree-teh lee EN-gle-skee, do you speak English?), "Koliko košta?" (KOH-lee-ko KOSH-ta, how much?)

💳 Money & Payments

  • The currency is the convertible mark (KM or BAM), pegged to the euro at about 1.96 KM per €1. Some places accept euros but at a poor rate, so pay in marks.
  • Cards work in hotels, supermarkets and larger restaurants, but small ćevabdžinice, bakeries and taxis are often cash only (check current). Carry some marks.
  • Withdraw cash at bank-branded ATMs and avoid exchange booths with poor rates. If a terminal offers to charge you in your home currency, choose marks.

Insider tips for Sarajevo & Jahorina

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Buy a multi-day pass, not two day tickets

The 2026/27 official list prices an adult day pass at 98 KM and a 2-day pass at 166 KM, so two days cost about 30 KM less than two single days. Periods outside Christmas and mid-February have 15 to 30 percent off, so check the dates on oc-jahorina.com.

🥙

Eat ćevapi and burek for most meals

A desetka of ćevapi is about 10 KM (about €5) and a burek is about 5 KM (check current). Local ćevabdžinice and bakeries cost far less than resort restaurants, so eat the big meals in Baščaršija and keep mountain lunches simple.

🚕

Split the taxi or sleep in Pale

A Sarajevo to Jahorina taxi of about 60 KM (check current) costs a fraction per person when split by three or four. Guesthouses and pensions in Pale are usually cheaper than slopeside hotels, but you then need a bus or taxi to the lifts.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🕳️

Visit the Tunnel of Hope

The wartime tunnel museum in Butmir is close to the airport, so it fits an arrival or departure afternoon. It is a short, powerful stop, but check opening hours and prices before you go.

🌅

Watch the sun go down from Trebević

On a clear day, the viewpoint at the top of the Trebević cable car looks over the whole city. Check the last cable car down before the sun sets, as it can close earlier in winter.

☕

Take a Bosnian coffee break

Sit down in a Baščaršija coffee house for a coffee and a piece of Turkish delight, and take your time. It is a social ritual, and it costs only a few marks (check current).

Travel services for Sarajevo & Jahorina

✈️ Flights

  • Sarajevo International Airport (SJJ) is the only airport for this trip, about 12 km from the old town, with several European budget airlines flying in (check your route).
  • The Centrotrans airport bus to Baščaršija costs about 5 KM and takes about 30 minutes (check current). A taxi costs roughly 30 KM.
  • Winter weather can delay flights, so allow some slack on the day you travel home.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Licensed taxis start at about 1.5 KM plus about 1 KM per km (check current). Within Sarajevo, most rides cost 5 to 10 KM.
  • For Jahorina, expect about 60 KM for the car, or a private transfer from about €50 for a small car booked in advance (check current).
  • Ask your hotel for a trusted local taxi company or app, and always confirm the fare before a long ride.

🚇 Public Transport & Tickets

  • Sarajevo has trams, trolleybuses and buses run by GRAS. A single ticket costs about 1.60 KM at a kiosk and 1.80 KM from the driver on the visitsarajevo.ba list, though newer guides quote higher fares (check current).
  • Validate your ticket on board, and keep it, since inspectors do check.
  • The Jahorina bus lines are seasonal, so ask Centrotrans or the Jahorina Olympic Centre, or combine a bus to Pale with a taxi.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Baščaršija: the most atmospheric base for arrival, walkable to the sights but busy at night.
  • Marijin Dvor and the city centre: more local, with trams and easier access to Zetra and the bus links.
  • Jahorina village: the best for early lifts and night skiing, but the priciest, and many hotels have minimum stays in peak weeks.
  • Pale: cheaper pensions and apartments, about 12 km from the ski area, so you need a bus or taxi.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Bosnia and Herzegovina is not in the EU, so EU roaming may not apply. Check your plan, or use an eSIM or a local prepaid SIM. Most hotels and cafés offer Wi-Fi.
  • Cards work in hotels, supermarkets and larger restaurants. Keep marks for small ćevabdžinice, bakeries and taxis.
  • Use bank-branded ATMs and withdraw in marks.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Many nationalities, including EU, UK and US citizens, can enter without a visa for up to 90 days, but check the rules for your passport before you book (check current).
  • Take out travel insurance that covers skiing, and check what it says about mountain rescue.
  • Winter tyres and chains are expected on mountain roads in winter, so check the rules if you rent a car (check current). You do not need a car for this itinerary.
  • The Jahorina ski season usually runs from early December to April, depending on snow, so check conditions on oc-jahorina.com before you book.
