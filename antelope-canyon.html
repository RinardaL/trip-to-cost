3 Days in Page, Arizona: Antelope Canyon, Horseshoe Bend & Lake Powell

Two entirely different slot canyons carved from the same sandstone, a river bend so dramatic it stops traffic, and a turquoise reservoir threading through thousand-foot canyon walls, all of it Navajo Nation land, all of it reachable in three focused days out of Page. Three days is enough to cover Lower and Upper Antelope Canyon on separate guided tours, catch Horseshoe Bend at sunset, and spend a morning on Lake Powell toward Rainbow Bridge before a look inside Glen Canyon Dam.

Ideal SeasonJun – Aug (Light Beams)
Est. Budget$550-750
Duration3 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival & orientation on Navajo Nation land

Page sits entirely within the Navajo Nation, and nearly everything on this trip, both Antelope Canyons, Horseshoe Bend's tribal-run entrance, and Lake Powell's Navajo-side shoreline, sits on or borders Navajo land. Check into a hotel in town and pick up a rental car ahead of the afternoon's canyon tour.

🚗 ~2 hr 15 min drive from Flagstaff, or a short hop via Page Municipal Airport 💵 Rental car from ~$55/day 🕐 Land by early afternoon to make a same-day Lower Antelope slot
Afternoon

Lower Antelope Canyon's ladders & corkscrew walls

Lower Antelope Canyon, Hazdistazí or "spiral rock arches" in Navajo, is a separate slot canyon from the famous Upper Canyon, entered by descending a series of steel ladders into a narrower, more athletic corridor of corkscrewing sandstone. It draws noticeably fewer tour groups than Upper Antelope and gives a closer, more physical feel for how these canyons were carved.

🪜 Guided walking tour, ~5 ladders, moderate fitness needed 💵 ~$40-65/person including the Navajo permit fee 🕐 Book a 1.5 to 2 hr slot in the early or late afternoon
Evening

Horseshoe Bend at sunset

A short, paved-then-sandy walk from the parking area leads to a cliff-edge overlook where the Colorado River bends nearly 270 degrees around a sandstone plateau a thousand feet below, one of the most photographed viewpoints in the Southwest and dramatically lit as the sun drops.

🚶 ~1.5 mi round-trip walk from the parking lot 💵 $10 per vehicle parking fee 🕐 Arrive an hour before sunset to claim a spot at the rim
Morning

Navajo Village Heritage Center

A short stop at this Navajo-run cultural center just outside Page walks through traditional hogans, weaving, and history well before the midday canyon tour, useful context for a place where every canyon visit is also, by tribal law, a Navajo-guided one.

🚶 ~10 min drive from most Page hotels 💵 ~$25/person guided walk-through 🕐 Late morning, timed to finish before your canyon tour slot
Midday

Upper Antelope Canyon's famous light beams

Upper Antelope Canyon, Tsé bighánílíní or "the place where water runs through rocks," is the wider, more level canyon where sunlight drops almost straight down through narrow openings roughly between 11 AM and 1:30 PM on clear days from late spring through summer, the light beams that made this canyon famous. Photography is guided and fast-paced, and tripods, monopods, and selfie sticks are restricted or banned on standard tours to keep the line moving.

🚙 Navajo-guided open-air truck tour into the canyon 💵 ~$80-120/person for a midday beam-focused tour 🕐 Book the 11 AM to 1:30 PM window, reserve months ahead for summer
Evening

Dinner in Page & sunset over Lake Powell

Wind down back in town with dinner, then drive out to the Wahweap area for a quieter look at Lake Powell's shoreline as the light fades over the water, a preview of tomorrow morning's boat tour.

🚗 ~10 min drive to Wahweap from downtown Page 💵 ~$25-35/person for dinner 🕐 After dinner, the last hour before sunset
Morning

Lake Powell boat tour to Rainbow Bridge

A half-day boat tour crosses the turquoise water of Lake Powell between towering sandstone walls to Rainbow Bridge National Monument, the world's largest known natural bridge and a site sacred to several Native nations, reached by a short trail from the dock.

⛴️ ~4.5 to 6.5 hr round-trip boat tour from Wahweap Marina 💵 ~$150-180/person 🕐 Morning departure, book the boat tour well ahead in summer
Midday

Glen Canyon Dam & Visitor Center

Back in town, the Glen Canyon Dam Visitor Center overlooks the 710-foot concrete arch dam that impounds the Colorado River to form Lake Powell, with a bridge-view overlook and a guided walk down into the dam itself for those who book ahead.

🚶 Visitor center overlook is a short walk from parking 💵 Visitor center free, guided dam tour ~$5 🕐 Early afternoon, allow 45 min to 1.5 hrs
Afternoon

Departure from Page

Page Municipal Airport has limited direct commercial routes, so most visitors make the roughly 2 hr 15 min drive to Flagstaff or continue further to Phoenix Sky Harbor for onward flights.

🚗 ~2 hr 15 min to Flagstaff, ~4.5 hrs to Phoenix 💵 ~$60-90 one-way rental drop fee if not returning to Page 🕐 Leave by early-to-mid afternoon for an evening flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Navajo canyon guides work hard in the heat and depend partly on tips, 15 to 20 percent of the tour price in cash is standard and genuinely appreciated.
  • Boat tour crews and dam-tour guides are tipped on a similar scale if the service was good.
  • Restaurant tipping in Page follows normal US convention, 15 to 20 percent at sit-down places.

👗 Dress Code

  • Closed-toe shoes with real grip matter for Lower Antelope Canyon's ladders, sandals are genuinely a hazard there.
  • Sun protection is non-negotiable, a wide-brim hat, sunglasses, and lightweight long sleeves handle the exposed overlooks and boat deck better than sunscreen alone.
  • Layers help for chilly desert mornings and evenings even when the midday heat is intense, especially outside summer.

🚶 Getting Around Page

  • A rental car is close to essential, distances between the canyons, Horseshoe Bend, and Lake Powell's marinas are short but not walkable.
  • Rideshare coverage in Page is thin and unreliable, don't count on Uber or Lyft for canyon tour pickups.
  • Most Antelope Canyon and boat tour operators include pickup from the meeting point or your hotel, check your booking confirmation.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Flash flood risk inside the slot canyons is real and taken seriously, tours can be canceled or cut short with no notice if storms are forecast anywhere upstream, even under a clear sky at the entrance.
  • Neither canyon can be entered independently under any circumstance, licensed Navajo guides are legally required for both Upper and Lower Antelope Canyon.
  • Summer heat is extreme and the overlooks and dam area offer little shade, carry more water than feels necessary and watch for signs of heat exhaustion.

🗣️ A Few Words in Navajo (Diné bizaad)

  • Yá'át'ééh: hello/it is good
  • Ahéhee': thank you
  • Diné bikéyah: the Navajo homeland, the term guides often use for this land

💳 Money & Payments

  • US dollars only, no currency exchange needed for most international visitors already carrying cash from elsewhere in the country.
  • Navajo permit fees and some smaller tour operators are cash-preferred or cash-only, bring some with you rather than assuming a card reader will be on hand.
  • ATMs are available in Page but not at the canyon entrances or Horseshoe Bend parking lot, withdraw before heading out for the day.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🪜

Choose Lower Antelope Canyon if budget-conscious

Lower Antelope tours run roughly half the price of an Upper Antelope beam tour and still deliver a genuine, dramatic slot canyon experience, just without the midday light shafts.

🚗

Return your rental car in Page

A one-way drop fee to Flagstaff or Phoenix can add $60-90, returning to Page and taking a shuttle or shared ride onward often works out cheaper.

🧊

Bring a cooler and stock up before you arrive

Page is a small town with limited grocery options and tourist-priced dining, a cooler with drinks and snacks picked up in Flagstaff or on the drive in saves real money over three days.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🎫

Ask about smaller "X" canyon tours

Smaller Navajo-run operators run tours into lesser-known slot canyons in the area with similar light-beam potential and a fraction of the crowds of Upper Antelope's main entrance.

🌅

Horseshoe Bend at sunrise instead

The overlook faces roughly north, so sunrise light is softer and far less crowded than the popular sunset slot, worth considering if you're an early riser.

🕳️

Waterholes Canyon

A smaller, quieter slot canyon south of town gets a fraction of the visitors of the two Antelope Canyons and is worth asking local operators about if you have extra time.

✈️ Flights

  • Page Municipal Airport (PGA) has very limited direct commercial routes, useful mainly for regional or charter connections.
  • Flagstaff Pulliam Airport (FLG), about 2 hr 15 min away, offers a short regional hop with a wider range of connections.
  • Phoenix Sky Harbor (PHX), roughly 4.5 hrs south, has by far the most international and domestic flight options and is the most common gateway for this trip.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Uber and Lyft coverage in Page is thin and inconsistent, don't rely on rideshare to make a tour departure time.
  • Most canyon and boat tour operators include hotel or meeting-point pickup, confirm this when booking.
  • A handful of local taxi and shuttle services can be arranged in advance through your hotel.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • A rental car covers the airport transfer and every stop outside the canyons themselves, which are guided-truck-tour only regardless of how you arrive.
  • Page's rental fleet is small, book ahead for a summer visit alongside your canyon and boat tour slots.
  • Free parking is available at Horseshoe Bend, Glen Canyon Dam, and most hotels, though the Horseshoe Bend lot fills up fast around sunset.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Downtown Page: puts you within a short drive of both Antelope Canyons, Horseshoe Bend, and the marinas.
  • Wahweap, on the Lake Powell shoreline: a handful of properties here have direct lake views for sunset, a short drive from town.
  • Book 2 to 3 months ahead for a summer visit, hotel rooms and canyon tour slots both sell out together in peak season.

🎟️ Canyon Tours & Permits

  • Both Antelope Canyons are only accessible with a licensed Navajo guide, independent or self-guided entry is not permitted under any circumstance.
  • Book Upper Antelope's midday beam-window tours as far ahead as possible, especially June through August, when popular slots sell out weeks or months in advance.
  • Lower Antelope Canyon tours generally have more same-week availability but should still be booked a few days ahead in peak season.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Arizona does not observe Daylight Saving Time, but the Navajo Nation does, so Page effectively shifts an hour relative to the rest of the state for part of the year, double-check tour departure times rather than trusting your phone's auto-updated clock.
  • Tripods, monopods, and selfie sticks are restricted or banned on standard canyon tours to keep the guided groups moving, plan your shots accordingly.
  • Travel insurance covering flash flood-related tour cancellations is a sensible add-on given how weather-dependent the canyon tours are.
