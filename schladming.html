4 Days in Schladming: Skiing, the Dachstein Glacier & Ramsau

Four winter days in the Enns valley on a budget: skiing Planai and Hochwurzen, the Dachstein glacier and Ice Palace, Ramsau's cross-country trails and a January Nightrace option.

Ideal SeasonDec – Mar
Est. Budget$620-850

4-day Schladming itinerary

How many days do you have?

Morning

Salzburg Airport, the bus & the train to Schladming

Salzburg Airport (SZG) is about 100 km from Schladming. Bus 2 reaches Salzburg main station in about 23 minutes, every 15 to 20 minutes. Trains to Schladming then take roughly 1.5 to 1.75 hours, direct or via Bischofshofen, with advance fares from about €10 (check ÖBB). A taxi is about €180 a car, cheaper for four.

🚌 ~23 min on bus 2, then ~1.5 to 1.75 hrs by train 💵 Bus a few euros, train from ~€10 in advance, taxi ~€180 per car (check current) 🕐 Check the ÖBB timetable for the train that fits your landing time
Afternoon

The Planai base, hire & your lift pass

The Planai valley station is the heart of the ski area, and the ski bus stops at Schladming station. Collect hire kit now to skip tomorrow's queue: skis and boots usually cost about €30 to €45 a day (check current). Buy passes online early: for 2026/27 a main-season day pass is €69.50 to €81.50 online, €81.50 at the desk.

🚌 Free ski bus from Schladming station once you hold a pass 💵 Hire ~€30-45 a day, day pass €69.50-81.50 (check current) 🕐 Buy the pass online before you leave, collect hire on arrival
Evening

Schladming centre & a Gasthaus dinner

Schladming is a former mining town with a flat, walkable centre, and the red-brick Rathaus (town hall) is worth a look. Eat where locals do: a Gasthaus serving Kaspressknödel soup or Käsespätzle costs roughly €12 to €18 a main (check current). Skip slope-side bars, which charge more for the same Glühwein. Sleep early: the gondola queue forms early.

🚶 Everything is within a short walk of the centre 💵 ~€12-18 for a main course (check current) 🕐 Eat early, many kitchens close by about 21:00 (check)
Morning

The Planai gondola & the first runs

Take the Planai gondola from the valley station and join the queue before the lifts open. Planai is one of four linked mountains in the Schladming region, with about 120 km of pistes in total (check current). Ski the blue runs first while the snow is freshly groomed, and check the lift status on planai.at the evening before.

🚡 Planai gondola from the valley station in Schladming 💵 Day pass €69.50-81.50 online, €81.50 at the desk (2026/27 main season) 🕐 Be at the gondola before it opens, lift times change by date
Afternoon

Hochwurzen & a pedestrian option

Hochwurzen (1,852 m) belongs to the same lift company and is reached by the Gipfelbahn from Rohrmoos, or by free ski bus from town. It is a good quieter choice for the afternoon. Friends who do not ski can ride Planai's gondola as pedestrians for €32.50 return (check current), then meet you for lunch on a terrace.

🚌 Free ski bus to the Hochwurzen valley station with your pass 💵 Included in the day pass, pedestrian gondola €32.50 (check current) 🕐 Afternoon, when Planai is busiest
Evening

The Nightrace (late January) or night skiing & tobogganing

The Nightrace is the men's World Cup slalom and giant slalom on the Planai, held in late January: 26 and 27 January 2027 (check the schedule), with runs at about 17:45 and 20:45 in 2026. Book tickets online ahead. On other evenings, Hochwurzen runs night skiing and tobogganing on selected days: €36 for skiing and about €25 for sledging.

🚌 Event shuttle buses on race days, night bus on other evenings 💵 Night ski ticket €36, toboggan ~€25, race tickets not yet published (check) 🕐 Races at about 17:45 and 20:45, no evening lifts on race nights
Morning

The glacier cable car, Sky Walk & Ice Palace

Take the ski bus or a car to the glacier cable car in Ramsau and ride up to about 2,700 m. At the top: the Sky Walk, the Stairway to Nothingness, the suspension bridge and the Ice Palace. The summer 2026 list price was €73 with attractions (€61 for the ride only); the operator had not published winter prices at the time of writing, so check before you go. Dress warmly, as it is far colder.

🚡 ~10 min by gondola, ski bus from Schladming (free only with a pass and ski gear) 💵 Gondola + attractions €73 on the summer 2026 list, winter price: check 🕐 Attractions 9:00-16:15 on the summer list, check the glacier report
Afternoon

Cross-country skiing on the Ramsau plateau

Back in the valley, try Ramsau's trails: over 100 km of classic and 70 km of skating track, from November. A 2026/27 main-season day ticket is €21.50, or €18.50 with the WinterCard, and children up to 15 ride free. The WinterCard is free with a night in a participating Ramsau lodging. Choose classic kit for first-timers.

🚌 Local bus on the plateau, ~€3 with the WinterCard (check current) 💵 Trail ticket €21.50 (€18.50 with WinterCard), half-day from 12:00 €15.50 🕐 Afternoon, after the glacier
Evening

Dachsteinbad, a floodlit loop & dinner in Ramsau

Try the floodlit trail at the Ramsau stadium, open daily from 16:00 to 19:00, then warm up in the Dachsteinbad pool and sauna, where the WinterCard takes €2.50 off pool entry. Eat in the village for a quieter evening. Check the last bus back to Schladming before you settle in: the fare is about €5.70 with the WinterCard.

🚌 Bus Ramsau to Schladming, ~€5.70 with WinterCard (check the last departure) 💵 Pool entry less €2.50 with WinterCard, dinner ~€15-25 (check current) 🕐 Floodlit trail 16:00-19:00
Morning

Hauser Kaibling from Haus im Ennstal

Take the free ski bus from Schladming station to the Hauser Kaibling valley station at Haus im Ennstal, then ride up. The summit is at 2,015 m, and it is the eastern end of the four-mountain region. A 2-day pass for 2026/27 costs €137.50 to €161.50 online, which is cheaper per day than two single passes (check current).

🚌 Free ski bus from Schladming station, then the cable car 💵 2-day pass €137.50-161.50, day pass €69.50-81.50 (check current) 🕐 Ride up early, the slopes are quietest before 11:00
Afternoon

A mountain-hut lunch & the last runs

Eat at a mountain hut near the top, where soup or Kaspressknödel costs less than a full meal (check current). Ski until about 14:30 if you fly in the evening, then take the free ski bus back to Schladming and collect your bags. Non-skiers can ride the cable car up for the ridge views and return the same way.

🚌 Free ski bus back to Schladming station 💵 Hut soup or dumplings ~€8-14 (check current) 🕐 Last lift is usually mid-afternoon, check before you go up
Evening

The train to Salzburg & the airport

Take the train to Salzburg Hauptbahnhof, about 1.5 to 1.75 hours, then bus 2 to the airport in about 23 minutes. Leave Schladming roughly 5 hours before an international flight, because winter delays happen. Book ÖBB Sparschiene fares in the app, and keep a spare buffer if you have a tight connection.

🚆 ~1.5 to 1.75 hrs by train, then ~23 min on bus 2 💵 Train from ~€10 in advance, bus a few euros (check current) 🕐 Leave about 5 hours before your flight

Schladming: what to know before you go

💶 Tipping & Service

  • Service is included in Austrian menu prices, but rounding up is normal. Add about 5 to 10 per cent in restaurants for good service.
  • Tell the server the total you want to pay when the bill comes, rather than leaving cash on the table. "Stimmt so" means keep the change.
  • At mountain huts, for porters and ski instructors, a euro or two is appreciated but never expected.

🥘 Mountain Food & Drink

  • Look for Kaspressknödel (cheese dumplings, often in broth), Käsespätzle (cheese noodles) and Kaiserschmarrn (a shredded sweet pancake), the standard hut desserts.
  • Hot drinks include Glühwein (mulled wine) and Jagertee (tea with rum). Tap water is safe to drink, and you can ask for Leitungswasser, which is often free or cheap.
  • Lunch on the mountain costs more than in the valley. A picnic of bread, cheese and a flask saves money, but buy a drink if you sit at a hut's tables.

⛷️ Piste Rules & Ski-Day Etiquette

  • Follow the FIS piste rules: the skier ahead has right of way, look uphill before you start or merge, and stop only at the sides or where others can see you.
  • Stay on marked, open runs. Closed runs and rope barriers mean avalanche or grooming risk, so never duck under them.
  • Wear a helmet. Hire shops usually offer one for a few euros. Lock your skis at the rack, and keep your lift pass in an outside pocket away from your phone.
  • Ask the time of the last lift, and ride down in good time, because some huts and lifts shut by about 16:00.

⚠️ Mountain Safety, Insurance & Official Advice

  • Stay on marked pistes and check the regional avalanche bulletin (lawinen.report) before touring. Mountain rescue in Austria is often charged to the casualty, so buy travel insurance that covers piste and mountain rescue.
  • Emergency numbers: 112 from any phone, and 140 for the Austrian mountain rescue (Bergrettung).
  • The Dachstein glacier is above 2,500 m, so it is colder and windier than the valley, and sun glare is strong. Wear sunglasses and a hat, and keep to the marked paths around the Sky Walk and the bridge.
  • Crime is low, but skis and hire gear do get stolen from racks and car parks. Check your government's travel advice for Austria, such as the UK FCDO or US State Department pages, before you go.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in German

  • "Grüß Gott" (grooss got, hello, common in Austria), "Servus" (SAIR-vooss, hi or bye, informal), "Auf Wiedersehen" (owf VEE-der-zay-en, goodbye)
  • "Bitte" (BIT-uh, please or you are welcome), "Danke" (DAHN-kuh, thank you), "Entschuldigung" (ent-SHUL-di-goong, excuse me)
  • "Eine Tageskarte, bitte" (EYE-nuh TAH-guss-kar-tuh BIT-uh, one day pass, please), "Die Rechnung, bitte" (dee RECH-noong BIT-uh, the bill, please)
  • "Sprechen Sie Englisch?" (SHPREKH-en zee ENG-lish, do you speak English?), "Wo ist die Gondelbahn?" (voh ist dee GON-del-bahn, where is the gondola?), "Wie viel kostet das?" (vee feel KOS-tet dahs, how much is it?)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Austria uses the euro. Cards and contactless work at most hotels, lift desks and restaurants, but some huts and small shops prefer cash, so carry coins and notes.
  • Use a bank-branded ATM (Bankomat) in the centre of Schladming, and choose euros if the machine offers to charge you in your home currency.
  • Most lodgings add a small local tax per person per night, paid at the hotel or apartment, so ask when you book.
  • Budget about €25 to €35 a day for food if you eat at inns and shop at a supermarket (check current).

Insider tips for Schladming

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Book your lift pass online, early

For 2026/27, the main-season one-day pass is €69.50 to €81.50 online, depending on date and demand, and €81.50 at the desk. Early and late season days cost less (€64.50 to €76). A 3-hour or from-noon ticket is €59 to €69.50 online if you only want half a day.

🏨

Pick a lodging that gives the Ramsau WinterCard

The WinterCard is free from one night in a participating Ramsau lodging. It cuts a cross-country day ticket from €21.50 to €18.50, takes €2.50 off Dachsteinbad entry and gives 10% off ski hire at Sport Pitzer. I found no free winter guest card for Schladming itself (check current).

🚌

Use the free ski bus

The Planai ski bus links the four valley stations, Ramsau, the Dachstein glacier lift and Galsterberg, and it is free with a valid ski pass if you carry ski gear. It stops at Schladming station. Day tickets are not valid on the night ski bus.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🛷

Go night tobogganing on Hochwurzen

On selected evenings the Hochwurzen summit lift opens for night tobogganers and skiers, with a separate evening ticket (about €25 for sledging, check current). Ask a local shop about sled hire, and ride down floodlit slopes as a cheap alternative to après-ski.

🥾

Ride up without skis

You do not need to ski to enjoy the mountains. A pedestrian ticket on the Planai gondola costs €32.50 return (check current), and non-skiers can walk near the upper station and meet friends for lunch.

☀️

Summer note: the Sommercard

The region works in summer too. The Dachstein glacier cable car runs from 9 May to 8 November in 2026. The Schladming-Dachstein Sommercard, for overnight guests in participating lodgings, covers lifts, the Schladming city bus and attractions (check what is included).

Travel services for Schladming

✈️ Flights

  • Salzburg (SZG) is the closest airport, about 100 km from Schladming. Bus 2 runs to Salzburg main station every 15 to 20 minutes in about 23 minutes, and trains to Schladming take roughly 1.5 to 1.75 hours.
  • Graz (about 179 km) has direct ÖBB EC or IC trains to Schladming in roughly 2.5 hours (check the timetable). Linz (155 km) needs a change at Selzthal, and Vienna (309 km) takes about 4.5 hours by train.
  • Munich (about 233 km) is a long transfer: allow roughly 3.5 to 4 hours by train via Salzburg. Compare the total cost, including the transfer, before choosing an airport.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • The regional tourism site gives standard taxi rates for four people of about €180 from Salzburg airport, €380 from Graz, €500 from Munich and €550 from Vienna. Agree the fare before you start.
  • Local taxi and bus firms, such as Habersatter in Schladming, serve the region. Pre-book for airport runs and ski bags.
  • I could not confirm ride-hailing coverage in the Enns valley, so book taxis by phone or through your lodging.

🚆 Public Transport & Tickets

  • ÖBB EC and IC trains stop at Schladming about every two hours, with direct links from Graz, Innsbruck and Salzburg. Regional trains also serve Haus, Gröbming and other valley stations. Use the ÖBB app for Sparschiene fares.
  • Ski buses are free with a valid ski pass if you carry ski gear. They serve Hauser Kaibling, Planai, Hochwurzen, Reiteralm, Ramsau, the Dachstein glacier lift and Galsterberg.
  • With the WinterCard, Ramsau plateau buses cost about €3.00, the bus to or from Schladming about €5.70 and the Dachsteinstrasse to Türlwand about €7.00 including the toll (check current).
  • Cable car guests can use the Dachsteinstrasse toll free if they validate their ticket.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Schladming centre: walkable, with the station and the Planai gondola close by, and the most choice. It is also the priciest in Christmas and Nightrace weeks.
  • Rohrmoos: on the Hochwurzen side, quieter and well served by ski buses.
  • Haus im Ennstal: the base for Hauser Kaibling, and often cheaper than the centre (check current).
  • Ramsau am Dachstein: the best base for cross-country and the glacier lift, and it gives the free WinterCard, but it is farther from Planai.
  • For savings, look at self-catering apartments and guesthouses, and book early for school holidays and the Nightrace.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Austria is in the EU, so EU SIM holders roam at home rates. Other visitors can use an eSIM or a local prepaid SIM, and most hotels offer Wi-Fi. Reception can be poor high on the mountain.
  • Download offline maps and the Schladming-Dachstein app, and check lift status and the glacier report each morning.
  • Cards work at most places, but keep some cash for small huts, and use bank-branded ATMs.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Check your passport and Schengen rules before booking, including whether ETIAS applies on your travel dates.
  • Buy travel insurance that covers skiing and mountain rescue, since rescue is often charged to the casualty.
  • Book your lift pass online, plus Nightrace tickets and lodging, as early as you can: the cheaper online prices rise with demand.
  • You do not need a car, since trains and free ski buses cover this itinerary. If you drive, winter tyres are required in wintry conditions.
