4 Days in Rome: The Colosseum, the Vatican & Trastevere

The Colosseum and the Forum, the Vatican and the Pantheon, then pasta in Trastevere: four days in Rome with timed tickets booked ahead and one calm day trip to Ostia Antica.

Ideal SeasonApr – Jun, Sep – Oct
Est. Budget$650-900
Duration4 Days
PaceActive
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Fiumicino & the Leonardo Express to Termini

Fiumicino (FCO) is about 30 km from the centre. The non-stop Leonardo Express to Roma Termini takes roughly 32 minutes, costs about €14 and leaves every 15 to 30 minutes. Regional FL1 trains cost about €8 and stop at Trastevere, Ostiense and Tiburtina. Validate a paper regional ticket at the platform machine.

🚆 ~32 min by Leonardo Express to Termini 💵 ~€14 Leonardo Express, ~€8 regional FL1 (check current) 🕐 Plan around your landing time, then drop your bags
Afternoon

The Pantheon & Piazza Navona

The Pantheon's concrete dome, open to the sky through an oculus about 9 m wide, is still the largest unreinforced concrete dome in the world. Entry is about €5 (check the current price), and sightseeing pauses during Mass. Piazza Navona is about 5 minutes on foot; skip its sit-down cafés, where coffee costs far more.

🚶 ~5 min on foot between the two 💵 Pantheon ~€5 (check current), Piazza Navona free 🕐 Mid-afternoon, or first thing in the morning to avoid the queue
Evening

Trevi Fountain, the Spanish Steps & a first carbonara

The Trevi Fountain is one of the most crowded spots in Rome, so check the city's current notice on access and go early or after dark. The Spanish Steps are about 10 minutes away on foot, and sitting on them can be fined. For dinner, walk a few streets away from both piazzas.

🚶 ~10 min on foot from the Pantheon to Trevi, then ~10 to the Spanish Steps 💵 Sights free, ~€25-35 for dinner 🕐 After dark, away from peak tour hours
Morning

The Colosseum

A combined ticket covers the Colosseum, the Roman Forum and the Palatine Hill, is valid for 24 hours, and costs about €18 online (check the current price). Tickets are named, so bring ID, and they sell out days ahead in busy months. Book the first slot on the official site, not a reseller.

🚇 Metro B to Colosseo, two stops from Termini 💵 ~€18 combined ticket, booked online (check current) 🕐 First slot of the day, usually around 8:30 to 9:00 depending on season
Afternoon

The Roman Forum & Palatine Hill

Your combined ticket also covers the Forum and the Palatine, so walk up past the Arch of Titus and along the Via Sacra. Look for the Curia, where the Senate met, then climb the Palatine to the emperors' palace ruins above the Circus Maximus. There is little shade, so bring water. Allow about 3 hours.

🚶 On foot from the Colosseum via the Arch of Titus 💵 Included in the combined ticket 🕐 Midday, but bring water and a hat: there is little shade
Evening

Capitoline Hill & dinner in Monti

The Capitoline Museums around Piazza del Campidoglio are an optional paid visit. For a free view, take the passage on the right side of the Palazzo Senatorio to the terrace above the Forum, best at golden hour. Then walk about 15 minutes to Monti, reserve a trattoria table and ask for the daily specials.

🚶 ~10 min up from the Forum, then ~15 min on foot to Monti 💵 Piazza and terrace free, ~€25-35 for dinner 🕐 Late afternoon for the Forum view, dinner from 8 PM
Morning

The Vatican Museums & the Sistine Chapel

Entry to the Vatican Museums is about €20, plus an online reservation fee of about €5 (check the current price), and visits are timed, so book the earliest slot on the official site. They are closed on Sundays except the last of the month, when entry is free but queues are very long. Cover shoulders and knees.

🚇 Metro A to Ottaviano or Cipro, then a short walk 💵 ~€25 including the online booking fee (check current) 🕐 Earliest timed slot, closed Sundays except the last of the month
Afternoon

St. Peter's Basilica & the dome

St. Peter's Basilica is free to enter, but a security queue builds up in the morning, so early afternoon is often quicker. Cover shoulders and knees. The dome climb is about 551 steps on foot (about €8) or about €10 with a lift (check current). The Wednesday morning general audience can limit access.

🚶 ~15 min on foot around the walls from the museums 💵 Basilica free, dome ~€8 on foot or ~€10 with the lift (check current) 🕐 Early afternoon, when the queue is usually shorter
Evening

Trastevere: Santa Maria & dinner

Start at the church of Santa Maria in Trastevere, with a golden apse mosaic from the 12th century. It is free to enter. For dinner, wander the side lanes instead of the main piazza and avoid places with a tout outside. Book ahead on Friday and Saturday, and expect a cover charge (coperto) of usually €1 to €3.

🚶 ~35 min on foot from the Vatican, or take a bus or taxi 💵 Church free, ~€25-35 for dinner plus cover charge 🕐 Dinner from 8 PM, book ahead at weekends
Morning

Ostia Antica, ancient Rome's port

Ostia Antica was ancient Rome's port and is quieter than the Forum. Take Metro B to Piramide, then the Roma-Lido line, about 30 minutes on a regular city ticket. Entry is about €18 at last check (confirm the current price), and the site is closed on Mondays. Bring water and allow 2.5 to 3 hours.

🚆 ~30 min on the Roma-Lido line from Porta San Paolo 💵 ~€1.50 city ticket + ~€18 entry (check current) 🕐 Arrive soon after opening, closed on Mondays
Afternoon

The Aventine Keyhole & the Orange Garden

Take the train back to Piramide and walk up the Aventine Hill. The Orange Garden is a free terrace with a wide view over Rome, open until dusk. A few minutes along, the bronze door of the Priory of the Knights of Malta has a keyhole that frames the dome of St. Peter's. Queues grow at sunset.

🚶 ~15 min uphill on foot from Piramide, then ~5 min to the keyhole 💵 Free 🕐 Late afternoon light, the garden closes at dusk
Evening

Transfer to Fiumicino

Return to Termini for the Leonardo Express to Fiumicino, about 32 minutes and about €14. Plan to leave Termini at least 3 hours before your flight, and check the last train of the evening. Regional FL1 trains from Trastevere or Ostiense are cheaper, and a taxi has a fixed fare.

🚆 ~32 min by Leonardo Express 💵 ~€14 (check current) 🕐 Leave Termini at least 3 hours before your flight

💶 Tipping, Cover Charge & Coffee

  • Tipping is not expected in Italy, and a service charge (servizio) is sometimes added to the bill. If service is not included, rounding up or leaving a few euros for good service is plenty.
  • Most restaurants add a small cover charge (coperto) per person, usually €1 to €3. It is legal if it is shown on the menu, so check it.
  • An espresso at the counter of a bar costs much less than one at a table, and Romans drink their coffee standing up. Cappuccino is a breakfast drink, so ordering one after a meal marks you as a visitor.

🍝 Eating Like a Roman

  • The classics are carbonara, cacio e pepe, amatriciana and gricia. Supplì (fried rice balls) and pizza al taglio (pizza by weight) are the quick snacks. Roman-style artichokes (carciofi) are a spring speciality.
  • Lunch is usually 12:30 to 14:30 and dinner starts around 19:30 to 20:00. Kitchens in the tourist centre stay open all afternoon, but locals eat later than most visitors expect.
  • Be wary of restaurants with a tout outside or a photo menu, especially around the Colosseum, Piazza Navona and Termini. Real gelato is stored in covered tubs and its colours are natural, so pistachio should not be bright green.

⛪ Churches, Dress & Opening Hours

  • Shoulders and knees must be covered in churches, including St. Peter's and the Sistine Chapel. A light scarf in your bag solves it on a hot day.
  • The Vatican Museums are closed on Sundays except the last Sunday of the month. Many state museums are closed on Mondays, and Ostia Antica is closed on Mondays too, so check before you plan.
  • Churches are working places of worship. Keep your voice down, do not take photos during services, and expect some to close for a midday break.
  • In mid-August (around Ferragosto) many locals leave town and some small restaurants and shops close for a few weeks.

⚠️ Safety, Scams & Etiquette Warnings

  • Pickpockets work crowded places, especially Termini station, the metro and buses on busy routes, the Colosseum and the Trevi Fountain. Keep your phone and wallet in a front pocket or a zipped bag.
  • People dressed as gladiators ask for money after a photo, and others tie bracelets on your wrist or hand you a rose. Decline politely, and agree on a price in advance if you do want a photo.
  • Use only official white taxis with a Roma Capitale sign and a meter, or a taxi app. Ignore drivers who approach you inside the station or the terminal.
  • Sitting on the Spanish Steps, eating on monument steps and bathing in fountains are banned, and fines are real. Respect the dress code at churches.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Italian

  • "Buongiorno" (good morning or good day), "Buonasera" (good evening), "Ciao" (hi, bye, informal)
  • "Per favore" (please), "Grazie" (thank you), "Prego" (you're welcome), "Scusi" (excuse me)
  • "Un tavolo per due, per favore" (a table for two, please), "Il conto, per favore" (the bill, please)
  • "Parla inglese?" (do you speak English?), "Dov'è...?" (where is...?), "Quanto costa?" (how much is it?)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Italy uses the euro. Cards and contactless work in most hotels, museums and restaurants, but carry some cash for small bars, markets and tips.
  • Use bank-branded ATMs and avoid standalone exchange booths. If a terminal offers to charge you in your home currency, choose euros.
  • Rome charges a city accommodation tax (tassa di soggiorno) per person per night, paid at the hotel, usually in cash. The rate depends on the star rating, so check it when you book.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🚰

Fill your bottle at a nasone

Rome has thousands of free public drinking fountains, called nasoni (big noses), with cold, safe tap water. Carry a refillable bottle, which saves a few euros a day on bottled water.

☕

Drink your coffee at the bar

An espresso or cappuccino at the counter costs a fraction of the same drink at a table, and tables in the busiest piazzas add a surcharge. Pay at the till first, then show the receipt at the bar.

🎟️

Count your rides before buying a pass

A single metro, bus or tram ticket costs about €1.50 and is valid for 100 minutes, and a 24-hour ticket is about €7 (check current fares). Most of this itinerary is walkable, so a few single tickets often cost less than a pass.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

See the big sights early or late

The Pantheon, the Trevi Fountain and the Spanish Steps are far calmer before 8 AM or after 9 PM. Lighting at night also changes the whole feeling of the fountains.

🌇

Watch the sunset from the Pincio or the Janiculum

The Pincio terrace above Piazza del Popolo and the Janiculum Hill both give free sweeping views of the city at sunset. The Janiculum also fires a ceremonial cannon every day at noon.

🚲

Add the Appian Way on a Sunday

If you have a spare day, the Appian Way is closed to traffic on Sundays and holidays, which makes it pleasant to walk or cycle past ancient tombs and the catacombs.

✈️ Flights

  • Rome Fiumicino (FCO) is the main international airport, about 30 km from the centre, with a railway station beside the terminals. Ciampino (CIA) is a smaller airport used by some budget airlines, about 15 km from the centre, with shuttle buses to Termini.
  • The Leonardo Express to Termini takes about 32 minutes and costs about €14 (check current). Regional FL1 trains cost about €8 and stop at Trastevere, Ostiense and Tiburtina.
  • Check which airlines serve both airports from your home city, since fares can differ a lot.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Official taxis are white, show a Roma Capitale sign and use a meter. A fixed fare applies from the airports to the centre inside the Aurelian Walls, so ask for it before you set off.
  • Taxi apps such as FreeNow and IT Taxi book official taxis, and Uber also operates in Rome. Check the apps for current coverage and prices.
  • Taxis are not allowed to pick you up from the street at some pedestrian-only spots, so walk to a taxi rank.

🚇 Public Transport & Tickets

  • ATAC runs the metro (lines A, B and C), buses and trams. A single ticket costs about €1.50 and is valid for 100 minutes, and 24, 48 and 72-hour tickets are also sold (check current fares).
  • Validate paper tickets at the machine on board a bus or tram or at the metro gate, and keep them until the end of the trip, as inspectors check.
  • Metro A serves the Spanish Steps (Spagna) and the Vatican (Ottaviano and Cipro). Metro B serves the Colosseum (Colosseo) and Piramide for the train to Ostia Antica. Termini is where both lines meet.
  • Buses on busy routes are a pickpocket hotspot, so keep your bag zipped and in front of you.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Centro Storico (Pantheon and Piazza Navona): the most walkable base for the sights, but the priciest and busiest.
  • Monti: a short walk from the Colosseum and the Forum, with local restaurants and a quieter evening feel.
  • Trastevere: lively evenings and good food, but noisy at night and a longer walk or bus ride from the Colosseum.
  • Prati: calm and well connected, close to the Vatican, with good restaurants and less tourist traffic.
  • Near Termini: practical for airport trains and the metro, but the streets are rougher at night, so check recent reviews.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Italy is in the EU, so EU SIM holders roam at home rates. Other visitors can use an eSIM or a local prepaid SIM, and many cafés and hotels offer Wi-Fi.
  • Cards work at most hotels, museums and larger restaurants, but keep cash for small bars, markets and the hotel tourist tax.
  • Use bank-branded ATMs and avoid standalone exchange booths.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Check your passport's Schengen rules before booking, including whether ETIAS applies on your travel dates (it was planned to start in the last quarter of 2026).
  • Book the Colosseum and the Vatican Museums online ahead, especially from April to October and on Fridays and Saturdays. Both sell out.
  • The Roma Pass bundles transport with a few sights, but prices and inclusions change, so compare it with your plan before buying.
  • You do not need a car. Much of the historic centre is a limited traffic zone (ZTL) with camera fines, and trains, the metro and walking cover this itinerary.
