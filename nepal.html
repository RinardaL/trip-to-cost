6 Days in Nepal: Kathmandu, Pokhara & the Poon Hill Trek

Six days is enough for a genuinely well-rounded first trip into the Himalaya, without the multi-week commitment of an Everest Base Camp expedition. Two days move through Kathmandu's UNESCO-listed heart, the pagoda temples of Durbar Square, the great Buddhist stupa at Boudhanath, and the riverside shrines of Pashupatinath, before a short flight drops you into Pokhara's lakeside calm beneath the Annapurna range. From there, a well-trodden, genuinely achievable trek climbs through rhododendron forest and Gurung villages to Poon Hill, where sunrise breaks over Annapurna and Dhaulagiri without requiring expedition-level fitness or weeks of acclimatization.

How many days do you have?

Morning

Land in Kathmandu and settle into Thamel

Most international flights land at Tribhuvan International Airport by mid-morning or afternoon; the tourist hub of Thamel, a tangle of narrow lanes packed with gear shops, bakeries, and guesthouses, makes the easiest base for the whole trip, with an easy walk to Durbar Square.

✈️ International arrivals into Tribhuvan International Airport 💵 Airport transfer ~$8-12 by taxi 🕐 Check in and rest a couple of hours before heading out
Afternoon

Kathmandu Durbar Square's temples and palaces

The former royal square is a dense cluster of carved wooden temples, courtyards, and the old Hanuman Dhoka palace complex, still a working center of Newar religious life; a licensed local guide at the entrance is genuinely worth hiring for an hour to explain what's in front of you.

🚶 A short, flat walk from most Thamel guesthouses 💵 Foreign entry fee ~$10-15 🕐 Mid-to-late afternoon, before the light softens for photos
Evening

First dal bhat in Thamel

Dal bhat, a refillable plate of lentil soup, rice, vegetable curry, and pickle, is the backbone of Nepali eating and the fuel that gets trekkers up mountains; a Thamel restaurant terrace is a relaxed place to try it before the trip gets busier.

🍽️ Thamel has restaurants at every price point 💵 ~$4-8 for a full dal bhat set 🕐 Early night, there's a full day ahead tomorrow
Morning

Boudhanath, one of the world's largest stupas

Boudhanath is a genuinely significant site for Tibetan Buddhist culture, its huge whitewashed dome ringed by monasteries, incense shops, and pilgrims; walk the kora clockwise with the crowd, as tradition requires, and take time on a rooftop café terrace to watch the stupa from above.

🚕 ~20-30 min taxi from Thamel 💵 Entry ~$4-5 🕐 Morning, when the kora is at its most active
Afternoon

Swayambhunath, the "Monkey Temple"

A steep staircase (or an easier side road) climbs to Swayambhunath's hilltop stupa, genuinely one of the oldest religious sites in the valley, with resident monkeys and a sweeping view over all of Kathmandu; keep food and loose bags zipped, the monkeys are opportunistic.

🚕 ~20 min taxi from Boudhanath 💵 Foreign entry fee ~$3-4 🕐 Late afternoon for the best valley views
Evening

Momos back in Thamel

Nepal's other signature dish, steamed or fried momo dumplings filled with buffalo, chicken, or vegetables and served with a spicy tomato achar, make an easy, cheap dinner; Thamel has dozens of solid momo spots within a five-minute walk of most guesthouses.

🥟 Momos are sold everywhere, from street stalls to sit-down restaurants 💵 ~$2-5 for a plate 🕐 Pack for the trek tonight, tomorrow moves fast
Morning

Pashupatinath, on the Bagmati River

Pashupatinath is one of the most sacred Hindu sites in the world and a working temple, not a museum; non-Hindus can't enter the main golden-roofed shrine but can view the complex and the riverside ghats from across the water. The site includes active cremation ghats, observe quietly and respectfully from the designated viewing areas, and never photograph funeral rites up close.

🚕 ~15-20 min taxi from Thamel 💵 Foreign entry fee ~$10 🕐 Late morning, allow at least an hour
Afternoon

The short flight (or tourist bus) to Pokhara

A 25-minute domestic flight is the fastest way to Pokhara and often clears mountain views over the foothills; a scenic but much longer tourist bus (6-7 hours along the Prithvi Highway) is the budget alternative if flights are full or the schedule allows for it.

✈️ ~25 min flight, or ~6-7 hr tourist bus 💵 Flight ~$90-130 one-way, bus ~$8-15 🕐 Book an early-afternoon flight to leave the evening free in Pokhara
Evening

First sight of the Annapurnas over Phewa Lake

Pokhara's lakeside district faces Phewa Lake with the sharp fishtail summit of Machapuchare and the Annapurna range rising directly behind it, on a clear evening the peaks catch the last light while reflecting in the water; a relaxed lakeside dinner is the right way to end the city portion of the trip.

🚶 Lakeside is compact and walkable 💵 Dinner ~$6-12 🕐 Sunset, then an early night before the trek begins
Morning

Phewa Lake and the World Peace Pagoda

A short boat ride across Phewa Lake reaches the trail up to the white-domed World Peace Pagoda, a genuinely worthwhile couple of hours with the Annapurna range as a backdrop; keep it low-key, the guide and driver will collect you from your hotel by early afternoon for the drive to the trailhead.

🚣 Rowboat rental ~$8-10/hr 💵 Pagoda access is free once across the lake 🕐 Keep it to the morning, the trailhead drive leaves after lunch
Afternoon

Drive to Nayapul and the first climb

A roughly hour-long drive from Pokhara reaches the trailhead at Nayapul, where the ACAP and TIMS permits are checked at the entry gate; the trail follows the Modi Khola river before climbing a long, genuinely steep run of stone steps through rhododendron and oak forest toward the overnight stop.

🥾 ~5-6 hrs walking, climbing roughly 1,000m over the day 💵 Included in a guided trek package 🕐 Depart Pokhara by early afternoon
Evening

First teahouse night in Ghorepani

Ghorepani's teahouses are basic but warm, a simple room, a wood-stove common room, and a menu built around dal bhat and noodle soup; turn in early, tomorrow's summit push to Poon Hill starts well before sunrise.

🏠 Teahouse rooms ~$3-8/night, meals expected in return 💵 Dinner ~$5-8 🕐 Lights out by 8-9 PM
Morning

The pre-dawn push to Poon Hill

A 4-4:30 AM wake-up and a headlamp-lit, roughly 45-minute climb reach the 3,210m summit of Poon Hill in time for sunrise over an unbroken wall of peaks, Annapurna South, Dhaulagiri, Nilgiri, and Machapuchare among them; it's genuinely one of the most rewarding short treks anywhere, and far more accessible than a high-altitude expedition.

🥾 ~45 min climb, ~300m gain, in the dark 💵 Included in the trek package 🕐 Departing camp around 4-4:30 AM for sunrise near 6 AM
Afternoon

Descending through forest to Ghandruk

After breakfast back in Ghorepani, the trail drops through more rhododendron forest and past small Magar and Gurung farming hamlets on the long walk down to Ghandruk, a well-preserved Gurung village with stone-paved lanes and its own Annapurna views.

🥾 ~6-7 hrs walking, mostly descending 💵 Included in the trek package 🕐 Full day, arriving by late afternoon
Evening

Prayer flags and a Gurung dinner in Ghandruk

Ghandruk's lanes pass mani walls and strings of prayer flags, always walked and passed on the left, clockwise, a genuine local custom worth observing rather than cutting through; a Gurung heritage museum in the village is worth a quick stop before a last teahouse dal bhat.

🏠 Ghandruk has some of the trek's most comfortable teahouses 💵 Dinner ~$5-8 🕐 A well-earned early night after the day's climax
Morning

The last descent to the trailhead

A final few hours of walking, mostly downhill through forest and farmland, retraces part of the route back to Nayapul, where the guide and driver are waiting; it's a quieter, more reflective walk after the previous day's sunrise high point.

🥾 ~3-4 hrs walking, mostly downhill 💵 Included in the trek package 🕐 Depart Ghandruk after breakfast
Afternoon

Back to Pokhara, then on to Kathmandu

A short drive returns you to Pokhara for a hot shower and a real bed, tip the guide and porter here if they haven't been already; from there it's the same choice as Day 3 in reverse, a quick flight or a longer scenic ride back along the Prithvi Highway to Kathmandu.

✈️ ~25 min flight, or ~6-7 hr tourist bus 💵 Flight ~$90-130 one-way, bus ~$8-15 🕐 Aim to be back in Kathmandu by early evening
Evening

Last-night shopping and a farewell dinner in Thamel

A final evening in Thamel is the moment for pashmina scarves, singing bowls, and trekking-gear souvenirs, with plenty of room to bargain; close out the trip with one more round of dal bhat or momos before an early flight out the next morning.

🛍️ Bargaining is expected in Thamel's shops 💵 Farewell dinner ~$6-12 🕐 Confirm your departure transfer time before turning in

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Tipping the trekking guide and porter is genuinely expected, not optional; a rough guide is $8-12/day for a guide and $5-8/day for a porter, pooled and given at the end of the trek.
  • Round up at Kathmandu and Pokhara restaurants; a service charge is rarely included outside larger hotels.
  • Drivers on the Kathmandu-Pokhara transfer also welcome a small tip if the service was good.

👗 Dress Code

  • Cover shoulders and knees when visiting Durbar Square, Boudhanath, Swayambhunath, and especially Pashupatinath; save shorts and tank tops for the trek itself.
  • Layering matters more than any single item, Kathmandu and Pokhara are mild but mornings on the trail and at Poon Hill's summit before sunrise are genuinely cold.
  • Broken-in hiking boots handle the trek's stone-step trails far better than sneakers or sandals.

🚶 Getting Around

  • Kathmandu and Pokhara are best covered by taxi for anything beyond a short walk; agree on a fare before getting in, meters are inconsistently used.
  • The Poon Hill trek itself is walked point to point with a guide, there's no vehicle access once past Nayapul.
  • Domestic flights between Kathmandu and Pokhara are frequently delayed or cancelled by weather, especially in the morning; build in a buffer day if a tight international connection follows.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Solo trekking without a licensed guide is not permitted on Annapurna-area routes, book through a registered trekking agency rather than attempting it independently.
  • Pashupatinath includes active cremation ghats along the river; observe respectfully from the designated far bank, never photograph funeral rites or grieving families up close.
  • Prayer flags and mani walls along the trek route are genuinely sacred, walk and pass them on the left (clockwise) and don't touch or climb on mani stones.
  • Poon Hill's 3,210m summit is far more moderate than Everest-region altitudes, but mild altitude symptoms (headache, light dizziness) are still possible; drink water and tell your guide if you feel unwell.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Nepali

  • Namaste: hello, a respectful greeting used everywhere, including on the trail
  • Dhanyabad: thank you
  • Kati parcha?: how much does it cost?

💳 Money & Payments

  • Nepal uses the Nepali rupee (NPR); cash is essential in Thamel's smaller shops, at temple entries, and everywhere on the trek, cards are accepted mainly at larger Kathmandu and Pokhara hotels.
  • Withdraw rupees in Kathmandu or Pokhara before heading out, there are no reliable ATMs on the trek route past Nayapul.
  • Small bills are far more useful than large notes for teahouses, tips, and trailside purchases.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🚌

Take the tourist bus instead of flying at least one way

The Kathmandu-Pokhara tourist bus costs a fraction of the flight and, on a clear day, offers genuinely scenic views along the Trishuli River valley; save the flight for whichever leg you're more time-pressed on.

🧣

Buy pashmina and gear in Thamel, not at the airport

The same scarves, singing bowls, and trekking gear sell for noticeably more at the airport departure shops than in Thamel, where bargaining is expected.

🥘

Eat dal bhat over imported-menu items

Dal bhat is usually refillable at no extra cost and is a fraction of the price of pizza or Western food on trekking-route menus, where every imported ingredient has to be carried in.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌄

Push a little past the main Poon Hill viewing platform

The main platform gets crowded fast once the bulk of trekking groups arrive; walking a short distance along the ridge finds a quieter spot with the same unobstructed Annapurna and Dhaulagiri panorama.

🏯

Visit Boudhanath at dusk, not midday

The evening kora, when butter lamps are lit and the stupa is floodlit against the darkening sky, is quieter and more atmospheric than the busier midday hours.

🏘️

Spend an extra hour in Ghandruk itself

Most itineraries treat Ghandruk as just an overnight stop; its small Gurung heritage museum and stone-paved upper lanes reward a slower look most trekkers skip in favor of pushing on.

✈️ Flights

  • Tribhuvan International Airport in Kathmandu is Nepal's main international gateway, connecting through hubs like Delhi, Doha, Bangkok, and Kuala Lumpur.
  • The Kathmandu-Pokhara domestic hop is short but weather-sensitive, especially in the morning; build a buffer day around a tight onward connection.
  • Book domestic flights separately from your trekking package, most trekking agencies handle permits and guides but not flights.

🪪 TIMS & ACAP Permits

  • The Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP) is required for every foreign trekker on the Poon Hill route and is checked at trailhead checkpoints; a registered trekking agency arranges it as part of a guided package.
  • A TIMS (Trekkers' Information Management System) card is also standard paperwork for the region, again typically handled by the trekking agency alongside the ACAP permit.
  • Independent solo trekking without a licensed guide is not permitted on Annapurna-area trails, book a registered agency in Kathmandu or Pokhara rather than planning to go it alone.

🏠 Teahouse Trekking

  • The Poon Hill route is a classic teahouse trek, simple family-run lodges provide a bed and meals at each overnight stop, no camping gear required.
  • Rooms are cheap or sometimes free, the real cost is in the meals, which you're expected to buy from the same teahouse.
  • Rooms can be basic (shared bathrooms, thin walls, no heating beyond a communal stove), pack accordingly rather than expecting hotel-level comfort.

🚌 Kathmandu-Pokhara Transfers

  • Domestic flights take about 25 minutes and offer mountain views on a clear day; tourist buses take 6-7 hours along the scenic but winding Prithvi Highway.
  • Private car transfers are also available for a higher price and more flexible timing than the shared tourist bus.
  • Book the return leg in advance during the October-November peak season, seats and flights fill up quickly.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Thamel is the practical base in Kathmandu, dense with guesthouses, gear shops, and restaurants within easy walking distance of Durbar Square.
  • Pokhara's Lakeside (Baidam) area, right on Phewa Lake, is the equivalent base there, with mountain views from many rooftop terraces.
  • On the trek itself, teahouses are booked on arrival or arranged by the guide, advance reservations aren't the norm outside peak season.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Most nationalities can get a visa on arrival at Tribhuvan International Airport with a passport photo and payment in cash or card, check current requirements before flying.
  • Cell signal is patchy on the trek past Nayapul, let people know you'll be briefly hard to reach on trek days.
  • Travel insurance covering trekking up to at least 4,000m and medical evacuation is genuinely worth having, even though Poon Hill's altitude is moderate by Himalayan standards.
