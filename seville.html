4 Days in Seville: Alcázar, Cathedral & Triana

From the horseshoe arches of Plaza de España to the Moorish courtyards of the Real Alcázar, four days is enough time to see Seville's grandest monuments, wander the Santa Cruz quarter, and catch a flamenco show without wilting in the Andalusian sun.

Ideal SeasonMar – May, Oct – Nov
Est. Budget$600-800
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival & check-in near the historic center

Seville Airport (SVQ) sits about 10 km from downtown, and the EA airport bus drops you near Puerta de Jerez in about 35 minutes, or a taxi covers the same route in roughly 20.

🚌 ~35 min EA airport bus, or ~20 min taxi 💵 ~€4 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time
Late Afternoon

Plaza de España at golden hour

The plaza's vast curved facade, tiled alcoves for each Spanish province, and the canal you can row across were built for the 1929 Ibero-American Exposition, and low sun turns the brickwork amber.

🚶 ~15 min walk from Santa Cruz, or bus C1/C2 💵 Free to visit 🕐 Golden hour, about an hour before sunset, once the heat breaks
Evening

First tapas dinner in the historic center

Ease into Seville's rhythm with a tapas crawl through the lanes near the cathedral, small plates of salmorejo, jamón, and fried fish shared standing at the bar.

🚶 A few minutes' walk from Santa Cruz 💵 ~€20 for a tapas crawl 🕐 9 to 11 PM, the local dinner hour
Morning

Real Alcázar at opening

Book timed tickets ahead for the Real Alcázar, still a working royal residence, whose Mudéjar courtyards, carved arches, and the Patio de las Doncellas' reflecting pool are best seen before tour groups fill the halls.

🚶 Steps from the Cathedral, in the historic center 💵 ~€14 entry (book ahead online) 🕐 Right at the 9:30 AM opening, before the heat builds
Late Morning

The Alcázar's gardens

Shaded orchards, sunken pavilions, and the odd wandering peacock make the palace gardens the coolest corner of the whole complex once the sun climbs.

🚶 Within the Alcázar grounds 💵 Included with Alcázar entry 🕐 Late morning, before the midday heat peaks
Evening

Wandering the Santa Cruz quarter

Once the afternoon heat eases, the Barrio Santa Cruz's whitewashed lanes, orange-tree courtyards, and hidden plazas are Seville's most atmospheric place for an aimless wander and dinner.

🚶 Adjoins the Alcázar and Cathedral 💵 Free to wander, ~€18 dinner 🕐 After 6 PM, once the streets cool down
Morning

Seville Cathedral

The world's largest Gothic cathedral holds Christopher Columbus's tomb and rises on the site of a former mosque; arrive early to beat both the heat and the tour buses.

🚶 Central, in the historic core 💵 ~€12 entry (combined with the Giralda) 🕐 Right at the 10 AM opening
Late Morning

Climbing the Giralda tower

The Giralda's ramps, built wide enough for guards on horseback rather than stairs, climb to a viewing platform with rooftop views over the cathedral, the Alcázar, and the river.

🚶 Inside the cathedral complex 💵 Included with cathedral entry 🕐 Late morning, before the midday sun makes the climb brutal
Evening

Flamenco show

Seville is flamenco's birthplace, and a tablao in Triana or near Santa Cruz delivers the real thing: raw guitar, percussive footwork, and singing that carries more emotion than melody.

🚶 Or a short taxi to a tablao venue 💵 ~€25 per ticket 🕐 Evening shows, typically 7 to 10:30 PM
Morning

Triana's ceramics workshops & market

Cross the Puente de Triana into the neighborhood that supplied Seville's decorative tilework for centuries; ceramics workshops still line Calle Alfarería, and the Mercado de Triana sits on the site of a Moorish fortress.

🚶 Across the Puente de Triana (Puente Isabel II) 💵 Free to browse, ceramics from ~€10 🕐 Mid-morning, before shops close for the siesta break
Early Afternoon

Riverside tapas in Triana

Triana's bars along the Guadalquivir serve some of the city's least touristy tapas, worth prioritizing over the more crowded Santa Cruz options if time is short.

🚶 Along Calle Betis, facing the river 💵 ~€15 tapas lunch 🕐 Early afternoon, before the siesta-hour lull
Evening

Transfer to Seville Airport for departure

If a Córdoba day trip appeals more than a slower Triana morning, the high-speed AVE train gets you there in under 45 minutes each way, easily doable before an evening flight. Otherwise, the airport bus retraces Day 1's route back to SVQ.

🚌 ~35 min EA airport bus 💵 ~€4 🕐 Leave at least 3 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is typically included in the bill (servicio incluido), so tipping isn't expected the way it is in the US.
  • Rounding up or leaving 5 to 10% at sit-down restaurants for good service is appreciated, not required.
  • Bar tabs for drinks and tapas rarely get more than loose change, if anything at all.

👗 Dress Code

  • The Cathedral and Real Alcázar expect shoulders and knees covered, so carry a light scarf even in summer heat.
  • Light, breathable fabrics and a wide-brim hat matter more than style here, given how intense the Andalusian sun gets by midday.
  • Smart-casual works for evening tapas and the flamenco show, though comfortable shoes matter most for the cobblestones of Santa Cruz and Triana.

🚲 Getting Around Seville

  • The historic center, covering the Cathedral, Alcázar, and Santa Cruz, is compact enough to see entirely on foot, with the Giralda visible as a landmark from most streets.
  • SEVICI, the city's bike-share system, has stations throughout downtown and is a fast, flat way to reach Triana or Plaza de España.
  • A single TUSSAM bus ticket costs about €1.40, useful mainly for the airport connection and longer hops across the river.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Summer heat is a genuine safety concern: July and August regularly hit 40°C (104°F), so schedule outdoor sightseeing for morning or evening and carry water at all times.
  • Many small shops and some sights close for a siesta break roughly 2 to 5 PM, so plan errands and museum visits around that window.
  • Pickpocketing is the main petty-crime risk around the Cathedral, the Triana bridge, and crowded tapas bars, so keep bags zipped and in front of you.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Spanish

  • Buenos días: good morning/hello
  • Muchas gracias: thank you very much
  • La cuenta, por favor: the bill, please

💳 Money & Payments

  • The euro is the currency, and contactless cards are accepted at nearly every restaurant, shop, and ticket counter.
  • Carry some small cash for the Triana ceramics workshops and smaller tapas bars that still prefer it.
  • Withdraw from bank-branded ATMs rather than standalone tourist kiosks to avoid inflated withdrawal fees.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Book Alcázar and Cathedral tickets online, well ahead

Timed entry regularly sells out in high season, and skipping the ticket line saves real time you'd otherwise lose standing in the sun.

🕌

Check for free or discounted entry windows

The Real Alcázar and other city-run sites occasionally offer reduced entry during off-peak hours, worth checking the official site before booking full-price tickets.

🥘

Treat the tapas crawl as dinner

Three or four €3 to €4 tapas plates spread across a couple of bars costs less than a sit-down restaurant meal and is more true to how Sevillanos actually eat.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🏺

Triana over Santa Cruz for tapas

The neighborhood across the river has the same ceramics tradition and river-view bars, with a fraction of the tourist markup found near the Cathedral.

🌇

Metropol Parasol's rooftop walkway

A modern wooden structure a short walk from the center offers sweeping rooftop views with none of the queues of the Giralda.

🌳

Plaza de España before 9 AM

Arrive early and the same plaza that's crowded with tour groups by midday is nearly empty, with soft morning light instead of a golden-hour crowd.

✈️ Flights

  • Seville Airport (SVQ) is the city's gateway, about 10 km northeast of downtown, with mostly European and domestic connections plus a handful of seasonal long-haul routes.
  • The EA airport bus runs to Puerta de Jerez and other central stops in about 35 minutes for around €4.
  • A taxi covers the same route in roughly 20 minutes at a fixed fare, a reasonable option if you're arriving late or with heavy luggage.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Cabify and Free Now both operate well in Seville and tend to be more transparently priced than hailing a taxi on the street.
  • Uber has a limited presence in the city, so Cabify or Free Now are the more reliable app options.
  • Official white taxis are still easy to hail and use fixed fares for the airport run, worth confirming with the driver before setting off.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • A car isn't needed for the city days; the Cathedral, Alcázar, Santa Cruz, and Triana all sit within walking distance or a short SEVICI bike ride of each other.
  • A rental could make sense for a Córdoba day trip if you'd rather drive than take the train, though the high-speed AVE is faster and avoids Seville's tight, one-way historic streets.
  • Parking near the center is scarce and often restricted, so skip the rental unless the Córdoba leg specifically calls for it.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Santa Cruz: puts the Cathedral, the Alcázar, and Days 2 and 3 within a five-minute walk, though it's the busiest and priciest base.
  • El Arenal: a quieter option near the Cathedral and the bullring, an easy walk into the historic core without the Santa Cruz crowds.
  • Triana: a livelier, more local base across the river for Day 4, with riverside tapas bars right outside the door and a short bridge crossing to everything else.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • This itinerary has no real boat leg; Seville sits on the Guadalquivir river but isn't a coastal city, so there's no practical boat excursion to add.
  • Short sightseeing river cruises do exist along the Guadalquivir near the Torre del Oro, but they're a novelty rather than a necessity here.
  • If you want real water time, that's better saved for a coastal add-on before or after Seville, such as Cádiz or the Costa del Sol.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Standard Schengen entry rules apply, so non-EU visitors should confirm visa requirements before booking flights.
  • Travel insurance is a smart add-on given the pre-booked Alcázar, Cathedral, and flamenco tickets, which are usually non-refundable.
  • Build the summer heat into your schedule from the start: front-load sightseeing into the morning and evening, and treat the 2 to 5 PM siesta window as built-in downtime rather than lost time.
