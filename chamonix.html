5 Days in Chamonix: Mont Blanc, Aiguille du Midi & Alpine Villages

Five days beneath western Europe's highest peak are enough to ride the highest cable car on the continent, walk out onto a receding glacier, hike a classic balcony trail, and still linger over fondue in a valley town that has been welcoming mountaineers for two centuries.

Ideal SeasonJun – Sep
Est. Budget$1,850-2,550/person
Duration5 Days
PaceActive
How many days do you have?

Morning

Transfer in from Geneva

Chamonix has no airport of its own, so most visitors land in Geneva and ride a shuttle or train and bus combination up through the valley, with Mont Blanc's summit appearing well before the town itself does.

🚐 ~1 hr shuttle from Geneva Airport, or ~2 hr by train and bus 💵 ~€40 shared shuttle 🕐 Book the midday transfer to arrive with daylight left
Afternoon

Town center and the church of Saint-Michel

The pedestrian streets around Place Balmat and Place de l'Église are anchored by the 18th-century church of Saint-Michel, its onion-domed bell tower a fixture of every Chamonix postcard since mountaineering began here.

🚶 Flat walk through the pedestrian center 💵 Free to explore 🕐 Mid-afternoon, once you've checked in and dropped bags
Evening

Riverside walk and a Savoyard dinner

The path along the Arve river gives a quiet, glacier-fed counterpoint to the busy center, and it leads naturally back toward a Savoyard restaurant for a first round of fondue or raclette.

🚶 ~20 min riverside walk along the Arve 💵 ~€35 for a fondue dinner 🕐 Early evening, before restaurants fill up
Morning

Cable car to the Aiguille du Midi

The Téléphérique de l'Aiguille du Midi climbs in two stages to 3,842 meters, the highest cable car station in Europe, gaining nearly 2,800 vertical meters from the valley floor in under half an hour.

🚡 ~30 min in two stages from Chamonix 💵 ~€75 round trip 🕐 First departure, to beat both the queues and the midday clouds
Midday

Step into the Void and the summit terraces

A glass-floored skywalk cantilevers out over a 1,000-meter drop, while the connecting galleries and outdoor terraces open onto the full Mont Blanc massif, including the summit itself just a few kilometers away.

🚶 On-site at the summit station 💵 Included with the lift ticket 🕐 Late morning, while visibility is typically best
Afternoon

Panoramic Mont-Blanc gondola to Pointe Helbronner

Weather and wind permitting, the Panoramic Mont-Blanc gondola swings out over the Glacier du Géant to Pointe Helbronner at 3,466 meters, crossing the border into Italy with views back over the whole massif.

🚠 ~35 min gondola crossing, weather-dependent 💵 ~€30 extra on top of the Aiguille du Midi ticket 🕐 Early afternoon, check the day's operating status first
Morning

Montenvers rack railway from town

The little red cog train has been climbing from central Chamonix to Montenvers since 1908, reaching 1,913 meters in about 20 minutes with the Mer de Glace unfolding below the final stretch.

🚞 ~20 min cog railway from Chamonix station 💵 ~€38 round trip including the ice cave 🕐 Morning departure, before the platform crowds build
Midday

The ice cave and the glacier's retreat

A gondola and stairway lead down to a cave carved fresh into the ice each year, and the marked panels along the way note how far the Mer de Glace has retreated since the 1990s, a sobering measure now built into the visit itself.

🚶 Gondola plus stairs down to the ice 💵 Included in the Montenvers ticket 🕐 Midday, allowing time for the queue at the ice cave
Afternoon

Belvedere trail and the ride back down

A short loop around the Montenvers terminus takes in the glacier viewpoints from several angles before the train carries you back to town in time for an easy evening.

🥾 ~45 min easy loop around Montenvers 💵 No extra cost on the same ticket 🕐 Mid-afternoon, before the last trains fill up
Morning

Planpraz lift up to the Grand Balcon Nord

The Planpraz cable car climbs the opposite side of the valley from Mont Blanc, dropping you onto the Grand Balcon Nord, a walking path that traces the Aiguilles Rouges with the entire massif laid out across the valley.

🚡 ~10 min cable car from town, then a walking trail 💵 ~€22 round trip lift 🕐 Morning start, for calm air and clear peaks
Midday

Onward to Lac Blanc for a bigger hike

Guests wanting more can continue from La Flégère to Lac Blanc, a turquoise alpine lake that mirrors the Mont Blanc massif on a calm day, adding several hours of steady climbing to the balcony route.

🥾 ~3.5 hr round trip extension to Lac Blanc 💵 Free once you're on the trail 🕐 Midday, packing a lunch for the lake
Afternoon

Afternoon in Argentière or Les Houches

Argentière sits quieter and closer to its own glacier at the head of the valley, while Les Houches anchors the softer, greener southern end, either making an easy, low-key close to an active day.

🚆 ~10 min on the Mont-Blanc Express valley train 💵 ~€3 one-way local train fare 🕐 Late afternoon, for coffee before the shops close
Morning

Spa morning or a walk to Lac des Gaillands

A hotel spa session eases legs tired from two days of hiking and altitude, or trade it for a flat walk to Lac des Gaillands, a small lake just outside town that mirrors Mont Blanc on a still morning.

🚶 ~20 min walk to Lac des Gaillands 💵 Free lake access 🕐 Early morning, before the transfer window
Midday

Last coffee and souvenir shopping in town

The mountaineering shops and bakeries around Rue du Docteur Paccard are an easy way to spend a final hour before packing, with tarte aux myrtilles a fitting last taste of the valley.

🚶 Flat walk through the pedestrian center 💵 ~€10 for coffee and a pastry 🕐 Late morning, once shops open
Afternoon

Shuttle back to Geneva

The same shuttle and train and bus options that brought you in run in reverse, with the descent giving one last long look back at the massif before the road drops toward the Swiss border.

🚐 ~1 hr shuttle to Geneva Airport 💵 ~€40 shared shuttle 🕐 Leave at least 3.5 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is included by law in French restaurant and hotel bills, so tipping isn't expected the way it is in the US.
  • Rounding up the bill or leaving a couple of euros for good service is a nice gesture, common among locals but never required.
  • Mountain restaurants at Montenvers or the Aiguille du Midi follow the same rule, the menu price is the price you pay.

👗 Dress Code

  • Alpine weather shifts fast, so layer up: a moisture-wicking base layer, an insulating mid-layer, and a windproof or waterproof shell that packs down small.
  • The Aiguille du Midi summit can sit 20 to 25 degrees Celsius colder than the valley floor, even in summer, so bring gloves and a warm hat regardless of season.
  • Town restaurants are smart-casual at most; hiking boots at dinner are completely normal here.

🚌 Getting Around the Valley

  • The Mont-Blanc Express valley train links Chamonix with Argentière, Les Houches, and the other valley villages in a few minutes each way.
  • A free local bus network called Chamonix Bus connects the town center to the main lift stations, included with most hotel guest cards.
  • Chamonix's own center is compact and largely pedestrian, most of Days 1 and 5 can be done entirely on foot.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Altitude at the Aiguille du Midi can bring on light-headedness, shortness of breath, or headaches, move slowly and drink water once you're off the cable car.
  • Sun exposure is far stronger above 3,000 meters, wear high-SPF sunscreen and sunglasses even on overcast days, especially near snow and glaciers.
  • The Aiguille du Midi cable car and the Panoramic Mont-Blanc gondola both close for high wind or poor visibility, build a buffer day into your plans if the summit views matter most.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in French

  • Bonjour: hello
  • Merci beaucoup: thank you very much
  • L'addition, s'il vous plaît: the bill, please
  • Basic French goes a long way here, though most staff at the lifts and hotels also speak good English.

💳 Money & Payments

  • France uses the euro (€), and Chamonix, sitting a short drive from both Switzerland and Italy, sees Swiss francs occasionally accepted near the border but euros are standard.
  • Cards and contactless payments are accepted almost everywhere, including lift ticket counters and mountain restaurants.
  • Chamonix runs pricier than an average French town given the lift tickets and alpine dining, budget accordingly for a mountain-resort trip.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Get a multi-day Mont Blanc MultiPass

A 2 or 3-day MultiPass covering the Aiguille du Midi, Montenvers, and the valley's smaller lifts costs meaningfully less per day than buying each ticket separately.

🥪

Picnic instead of the summit restaurant

Buying sandwiches and snacks in town before heading up saves considerably over the restaurant prices at the Aiguille du Midi or Montenvers.

🏨

Stay in Argentière or Les Houches for lower rates

Hotel rates a short train ride from central Chamonix run noticeably cheaper during peak summer weeks, with the valley train covering the gap in minutes.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌄

Le Brévent for the classic Mont Blanc panorama

The Brévent cable car on the same ridge as Planpraz gives one of the widest Mont Blanc panoramas in the valley, with a fraction of the Aiguille du Midi's crowds.

🏔️

Lac Blanc for reflection photos

Reaching the lake on foot rather than as a quick lift stop rewards early arrivals with a still, mirror-calm surface before midday wind picks up.

🌲

Cascade du Dard at first light

This waterfall walk just outside town gets moving early and offers a quiet, near-empty stop before the bigger lift stations fill up for the day.

✈️ Flights

  • Chamonix has no airport of its own, Geneva (GVA) is the main international gateway, about an hour's drive up the valley.
  • Lyon (LYS) works as a backup option, running around 2 hours by road, useful if Geneva fares spike during peak weeks.
  • Book the shuttle or train connection in advance during summer, seats on the Chamonix leg can sell out on peak-season weekends.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Taxis are easily found at the train station or booked through your hotel for late arrivals or early lift departures.
  • Ride-hailing apps have limited presence in the valley; hotels and the taxi stand outside the station are the more reliable option.
  • Most everything on this itinerary is within walking distance or a short bus ride, so taxis are mainly useful for luggage transfers.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • A car is genuinely optional for this trip, the free Chamonix Bus and the Mont-Blanc Express valley train cover every village on this itinerary.
  • Parking near the Aiguille du Midi and Montenvers stations fills early in summer, so a car adds hassle without much time saved.
  • Renting still makes sense for side trips further afield, like Annecy or the Italian side of the massif via the Mont Blanc road tunnel.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Central Chamonix, near Place Balmat: closest to the train station, shops, and the Aiguille du Midi lift, convenient for the Day 1 arrival and Day 5 departure.
  • Argentière: quieter and closer to the Grands Montets side of the valley, good for a slower mood with easy train access back to town.
  • Les Houches: a softer, greener base at the valley's southern end, well placed for the Day 4 hike and villages.

🚞 Cable Cars & Lift Passes

  • The Aiguille du Midi, Montenvers, and Panoramic Mont-Blanc gondola all run on separate tickets unless bundled into a MultiPass.
  • Buy tickets online ahead of time in summer, walk-up lines for the Aiguille du Midi can run over an hour at midday.
  • Most lifts run seasonally with reduced schedules or full closures in the shoulder months, check operating dates before finalizing a summer trip near the edges of June or September.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • France is part of both the EU and the Schengen area, so non-EU travelers should still double-check passport and visa rules before booking.
  • Remember the local currency is the euro, when budgeting for lift tickets, meals, and lodging.
  • Travel insurance that covers altitude activities and cable car disruptions is worth adding, given the Day 2 and Day 3 mountain excursions.
