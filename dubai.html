4 Days in Dubai: Burj Khalifa, Old Dubai & the Desert

The skyline and the fountain show, the creek souks by abra, a desert sunset and the Marina: four winter days in Dubai by Metro, with prices, dress rules and Ramadan 2027 dates.

Ideal SeasonNov – Mar
Est. Budget$450-700
Duration4 Days
PaceActive
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Dubai International & the Metro Downtown

Dubai International (DXB) has Metro stations at Terminals 1 and 3 only, so check your terminal. From Terminal 1 the Red Line reaches Burj Khalifa/Dubai Mall in about 33 minutes. Buy a Silver Nol card for AED 25 (it includes AED 19 credit); the fare is about AED 7.50. A taxi starts at AED 25 (check current).

🚇 ~33 min on the Red Line to Burj Khalifa/Dubai Mall 💵 Nol card AED 25 (AED 19 credit), fare ~AED 7.50 (check current) 🕐 Metro runs about 5 AM to midnight, Fridays from 10 AM to 1 AM
Afternoon

The Dubai Mall & the Burj Khalifa At the Top

A covered, air-conditioned walkway of about 10 to 15 minutes links the Metro station to the Dubai Mall. At the Top (levels 124 and 125) costs roughly AED 180 to 250, with sunset slots dearest (check current). Winter sunset falls about 5:30 to 6:15 PM, so book a slot 30 minutes before it.

🚶 ~10-15 min along the covered walkway from the station 💵 ~AED 180-250 (about $50-70), sunset slots dearest (check current) 🕐 Book a slot ~30 min before sunset, about 5 to 5:45 PM
Evening

The Dubai Fountain & dinner at the mall

The Dubai Fountain is free to watch from the Dubai Mall waterfront or the Souk Al Bahar bridge. Evening shows run about every 30 minutes from 6 PM to 11 PM and last around five minutes. Emaar sets the schedule, which changes for works and holidays, so check it. The mall food court is far cheaper than waterfront restaurants.

🚶 ~5 min from the mall to the waterfront 💵 Show free, ~AED 40-60 for a food-court meal 🕐 Every 30 min, 6 to 11 PM (check current)
Morning

Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood

From Burj Khalifa/Dubai Mall take the Red Line to BurJuman, then the Green Line to Al Fahidi, about 25 to 30 minutes in total. The Bastakiya lanes, with wind towers and courtyard houses, are free to walk, and small galleries and cafés open around 9 AM. The Dubai Museum in Al Fahidi Fort is closed for restoration (check current).

🚇 ~25-30 min by Metro via BurJuman (Red to Green Line) 💵 Lanes free, Metro ~AED 5-7.50 (check current) 🕐 9 to 11 AM, when the galleries open and the lanes are quiet
Afternoon

An abra across the creek, then the Gold & Spice Souks

Walk 10 minutes to Bur Dubai Abra Station and cross to Deira Old Souk for about AED 1, in cash. The Gold and Spice Souks are close by. Shops usually open about 10 AM to 10 PM, but many only from 4 PM on Fridays (check current). Bargain on spices, and compare gold with the daily rate.

⛴️ ~10 min on foot to the abra, then ~5-10 min across the creek 💵 Abra ~AED 1 (cash), souks free to walk 🕐 Mid-afternoon, but not before 4 PM on a Friday
Evening

Al Seef waterfront & dinner by the creek

Take the abra back to Bur Dubai, then walk about 10 minutes to Al Seef, a creekside promenade of heritage-style lanes with cafés and restaurants. It is free to stroll and liveliest after dark. Waterfront menus are usually pricier, so for a cheaper dinner try the small Indian and Lebanese restaurants on the side streets of Bur Dubai.

⛴️ ~10 min by abra, then ~10 min on foot 💵 Free to stroll, ~AED 40-100 for dinner depending on where you sit 🕐 After sunset, when the lanes are lit and cooler
Morning

Jumeirah Mosque & Kite Beach

Non-Muslims can enter the Jumeirah Mosque only on a guided visit run by the Sheikh Mohammed Centre for Cultural Understanding: about 75 minutes, roughly AED 45, usually at 10 AM Saturday to Thursday (check current). Cover shoulders and knees, and women also cover their hair. Then take a short taxi to free Kite Beach for Burj Al Arab views.

🚕 ~15 min by taxi from Downtown, then a short hop to Kite Beach 💵 Tour ~AED 45 (check current), beach free, taxis ~AED 20-35 a ride 🕐 Tour at 10 AM, Saturday to Thursday (check current)
Afternoon

Desert safari: dune bashing & sunset

Evening safaris collect you from your hotel around 3 to 3:30 PM and include dune bashing, a sunset stop and a camp dinner. Basic shared packages cost roughly AED 150 to 250, with upgrades up to AED 500 (check current). Book a licensed operator. Skip it if you have back problems, are pregnant or get travel sick.

🚙 ~45-60 min each way by 4x4 from your hotel 💵 ~AED 150-250 shared basic, up to ~AED 500 with upgrades (check current) 🕐 Pick-up about 3 to 3:30 PM
Evening

The desert camp: barbecue dinner & show

Most camps serve a barbecue buffet with a show, and camel rides, henna or quad bikes often cost extra, so check what is included. Alcohol is usually not part of the package. The desert feels cooler than the city after dark in winter, so bring a jumper. Expect to be back around 9:30 PM.

🚙 4x4 return to your hotel is included 💵 Dinner included, extras like quad bikes cost more 🕐 Dinner and show about 7 to 9 PM, back by ~9:30 PM
Morning

Dubai Marina Walk & JBR beach

Take the Red Line to DMCC, then the tram into Dubai Marina and JBR (check the journey planner). The marina promenade is flat and free, and The Walk at JBR has cafés on one side and a free beach on the other. The winter sea is about 21 to 23°C, cool but swimmable. Wear swimwear only on the beach.

🚇 ~35-45 min by Metro and tram from Downtown 💵 Metro and tram ~AED 5-7.50 (check current), walk and beach free 🕐 Mid-morning, then lunch at JBR
Afternoon

Palm Jumeirah by monorail: Atlantis & The Pointe

The tram stops at Palm Jumeirah, with a footbridge of about 10 minutes to Palm Gateway monorail station. The monorail takes about nine minutes to Atlantis Aquaventure and reopened in 2026 after maintenance. Fares were reported at about AED 10 one way, though older sources say AED 20. The Pointe promenade is free, but Atlantis attractions cost far more.

🚝 Tram, a ~10 min footbridge walk, then ~9 min by monorail 💵 Monorail ~AED 10-20 one way (check current), promenade free 🕐 Monorail runs about 9 AM to 10 PM, every 15 min
Evening

The Metro to Dubai International

Return to the Marina area for the Red Line to Terminal 1 or 3, roughly 40 to 50 minutes for about AED 7.50 (check current). Leave at least three hours before an international flight. The Metro closes around midnight, so a late flight may need a taxi, perhaps AED 80 to 120 from the Marina (check current).

🚇 ~40-50 min by Metro from the Marina area 💵 Metro ~AED 7.50, taxi ~AED 80-120 (check current) 🕐 Leave 3 hours before your flight, last Metro about midnight

👗 Dress & Conduct

  • In malls, souks, old Dubai and on public transport, cover shoulders and knees for both men and women, and avoid see-through clothes or slogans that could offend. Swimwear is for beaches and pools only.
  • Mosques need more: no shorts or sleeveless tops, a headscarf for women, and shoes off. Some restaurants and clubs have their own dress codes, so check before you go.
  • Public displays of affection are frowned on, and kissing in public can lead to arrest. Keep to handholding at most, and keep your voice down.
  • Respect the weekly rhythm. Friday is the main prayer day, and many souks and shops open later or close around midday prayer.

🍽️ Food, Alcohol & Ramadan

  • Food is halal, and pork is sold only in some licensed venues. Eat and hand things over with your right hand. Try shawarma, falafel, hummus, grilled meats and a biryani plate in Bur Dubai or Deira.
  • Alcohol is served only in licensed venues such as hotel bars and restaurants, at higher prices. Drinking in public or being drunk in public is illegal, and the drink-driving limit is zero. The legal drinking age is 21.
  • Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin around 8 February, depending on the moon sighting, and Eid al-Fitr around 9 or 10 March. In daylight, do not eat, drink, smoke or chew gum in public, and expect screened dining areas, shorter hours and no lunchtime fountain shows.
  • Offered Arabic coffee (gahwa) is poured in small cups: accept with your right hand and gently shake the cup to say you have had enough.

💶 Tipping, Service Charge & Bargaining

  • Many restaurants add a service charge, and VAT of 5% applies to most bills. If no service charge is listed, 10% for good service is usual, and it is not expected in cheap cafés.
  • Round taxi fares up to the next AED 5 or 10, and leave AED 5 to 10 for hotel porters. Tipping in cash reaches staff more reliably than adding it to a card.
  • Bargaining is normal in the Gold, Spice and Textile Souks, not in malls or supermarkets. Start at about half the first price, stay friendly, and walk away if it is too high.

⚠️ Laws Visitors Break by Accident

  • Drugs have zero tolerance, and even traces in the blood count as possession. Some CBD products, vape refills and prescription medicines are controlled, so check the UAE Ministry of Health lists before you travel.
  • Do not photograph people, especially women, without permission, or government and military buildings. Flying a drone needs prior permission, so leave it at home. Swearing and rude gestures are offences, including online.
  • Cross roads only at marked crossings, because jaywalking is illegal and fined, and drivers often do not stop at zebra crossings. Do not drink in public, and do not eat or drink on the Metro.
  • UK FCDO advice was updated on 27 October 2026 with warnings about regional tensions and possible airspace closures and flight disruption. Check your own government advice, sign up for alerts and take insurance that covers disruption. Emergency numbers: 999 police, 998 ambulance.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Arabic

  • "As-salamu alaykum" (peace be upon you, the standard greeting), reply "Wa alaykum as-salam"
  • "Shukran" (thank you), "Afwan" (you're welcome), "Min fadlak" (please, to a man) or "min fadlik" (to a woman)
  • "La, shukran" (no, thank you), "Bikam hatha?" (how much is this?), "Mumkin al-hisab?" (can I have the bill?)
  • "Hal tatakallam al-inglizia?" (do you speak English?), "Ayna...?" (where is...?), "Yalla" (let's go). English is widely used in hotels, shops and taxis.

💳 Money & Payments

  • The currency is the UAE dirham (AED), pegged at about 3.67 to the US dollar, so AED 100 is roughly $27. Cards and contactless work almost everywhere, but carry coins and small notes for abras, small cafés and tips.
  • Use bank-branded ATMs in malls and avoid airport exchange counters. If a terminal offers to charge you in your home currency, choose dirhams.
  • Hotels add a tourism dirham fee per room per night, about AED 7 to 20 depending on the star rating (check current), plus VAT and service charges. Tourists can claim back VAT on some shop purchases at the airport, so ask before you buy.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Let the Nol daily cap work for you

A Silver Nol card (AED 25, with AED 19 credit) caps your daily spending at about AED 14 on the Metro, tram and buses. Tap in and out, and extra rides that day are usually free (check current).

🍛

Eat where the locals eat

Small Indian, Pakistani and Lebanese cafés in Bur Dubai, Deira and Karama serve filling plates for a fraction of mall prices. Mall food courts are the next cheapest choice, and waterfront terraces the dearest.

🌆

Pay for one view, enjoy the rest free

The Burj Khalifa deck is the one big ticket worth buying. The fountain show, Al Seef, the Marina promenade and Kite Beach are all free, and each one gives its own view of a different part of the city.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

⛴️

Cross the creek at dusk

The AED 1 abra is the cheapest boat ride in Dubai. Cross between Bur Dubai and Deira late in the day for lit-up views of both banks, and carry a few coins because the boatman takes cash only.

🌅

Watch the Burj Al Arab from Kite Beach

Kite Beach is a free public beach with a long view of the Burj Al Arab, kitesurfers and a running track. Late afternoon gives the best light, and the cafés along the sand are cheaper than the hotels nearby.

🗓️

Avoid the peak weeks

Hotel prices are highest from mid-December to early January and rise again in February, often 20 to 40% above shoulder weeks. Late November or early March usually cost less, though Ramadan 2027 changes opening hours.

✈️ Flights

  • Dubai International (DXB) is the main airport, about 12 km from Downtown, and has Metro stations at Terminals 1 and 3. Terminal 2 has no Metro station, so use a taxi or a bus link.
  • Winter is peak season, with the highest fares around Christmas and New Year, so book early. Your passport should be valid for at least six months on arrival.
  • Check the exact terminal of your airline before you set off, because Metro, taxi and arrival times differ between terminals.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Official RTA taxis are cream with coloured roofs and use a meter. Airport pick-ups have a flag fall of AED 25 plus roughly AED 2 per km (check current), and Salik toll charges are added to the fare.
  • Careem and Uber also operate in Dubai, and app fares are often lower than street taxis. Pink-roofed taxis driven by women are available for women and families.
  • Taxis are plentiful, but rush hour from about 5 to 8 PM and Friday evenings are slow and costly, so use the Metro for Downtown and Marina trips.

🚇 Public Transport & Tickets

  • The Metro (Red and Green Lines), tram, buses and water taxis all use the Nol card. A Silver Nol costs AED 25 with AED 19 credit, and fares are about AED 3, 5 or 7.50 by zone (check current).
  • Gold Class cars cost double and the first car is reserved for women and children. Do not eat or drink on the Metro, as fines apply.
  • The Palm Jumeirah monorail is a separate system with its own fares, while an abra costs about AED 1 in cash.
  • The Metro runs roughly 5 AM to midnight and from 10 AM on Fridays, so check the last train if you plan a late night.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Bur Dubai and Al Fahidi: close to the old town and abra, with the best budget choice and Metro links.
  • Deira: cheap rooms near the Gold and Spice Souks and the Green Line, though a bit dated.
  • Downtown Dubai: the most convenient base for the Burj Khalifa and fountain, but the priciest.
  • Dubai Marina and JBR: beach, restaurants and tram, but a long way from the old town.
  • Al Barsha: practical mid-range hotels near Mall of the Emirates and the Red Line, with fewer sights nearby.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • WhatsApp and FaceTime voice and video calls are blocked in the UAE, while messaging works. A travel eSIM or a local tourist SIM from du or e& covers data, and malls and cafés offer Wi-Fi.
  • Cards and Apple Pay work almost everywhere, but keep cash for abras, small cafés and tips.
  • Use bank-branded ATMs and avoid standalone exchange counters.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Visa rules depend on your passport. UK citizens get a free visa on arrival for up to 90 days, many others are visa-free or get one on arrival, and some need an e-visa. Check the UAE ICP website for your nationality.
  • Weather: December to February brings highs of about 23 to 26°C, sunshine most days, light rain on a few days and a sea of about 21 to 23°C. Summers are extremely hot, with highs above 40°C.
  • Book Burj Khalifa and safari slots online in advance, especially around Christmas and New Year, and check the Dubai Fountain schedule closer to the day.
  • Your travel insurance should cover your itinerary and flight disruption, because FCDO advice warns of regional tensions. Check current advice before you travel.

How much does a 4-day trip to Dubai cost?

About $450-700 per person for 4 days, roughly $115-175 a day, excluding international flights. The estimate covers lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and real costs vary with the season and your spending habits.
