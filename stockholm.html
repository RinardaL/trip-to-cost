5 Days in Stockholm: Gamla Stan, the Vasa Museum & the Archipelago

Built across fourteen islands where the Baltic Sea meets Lake Mälaren, Stockholm trades in water and long summer light — medieval lanes in Gamla Stan, a 17th-century warship raised whole from the harbor, and an archipelago of some 30,000 islands just a ferry ride from downtown.

Ideal SeasonMay – Sep
Est. Budget$1,000-1,400
Duration5 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival & transfer into central Stockholm

Stockholm Arlanda Airport (ARN) sits about 40 km north of downtown, and the dedicated Arlanda Express train reaches Stockholm Central Station in around 20 minutes, one of the fastest airport rail links in Europe.

🚆 ~20 min on the Arlanda Express 💵 ~SEK 340 one-way (from ~SEK 149 booked ahead) 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time
Midday

The Royal Palace & changing of the guard

One of Europe's largest palaces still in active royal use hosts a full changing-of-the-guard ceremony, marching band included, most days around midday through summer, right on the edge of Gamla Stan.

🚶 ~15 min walk from Stockholm Central 💵 Free to watch, ~SEK 180 for palace entry 🕐 Guard change around 12:15 on weekdays
Evening

Gamla Stan's golden-hour lanes

Sweden's best-preserved old town sits on its own small island, its narrow lanes like Västerlånggatan lined with ochre and mustard-colored merchant houses dating back to the 13th century, at their best once the day-trip crowds thin out.

🚶 Runs the length of the old town island 💵 Free to wander 🕐 Early evening, before dinner
Morning

The Vasa Museum

The Vasa is a fully intact royal warship that sank on its maiden voyage in 1628 and spent 333 years at the bottom of Stockholm's harbor before being raised nearly whole in 1961, now the best-preserved 17th-century ship anywhere in the world.

🚋 ~15 min tram or ferry from Gamla Stan 💵 ~SEK 190 entry, free under 19 🕐 Right at opening, before tour groups arrive
Afternoon

Skansen open-air museum

The world's oldest open-air museum gathers historic farmhouses, workshops, and a small zoo of Nordic wildlife moved here from across Sweden, spread across a hillside a short walk from the Vasa Museum.

🚶 ~10 min walk from the Vasa Museum 💵 ~SEK 230 entry (summer rate) 🕐 Early afternoon, allow at least two hours
Evening

The ABBA Museum

An interactive, unapologetically fun museum built around the band's original costumes, gold records, and a re-created recording studio, a five-minute walk from Skansen on the same island.

🚶 ~5 min walk from Skansen 💵 ~SEK 295 entry 🕐 Late afternoon into evening, before dinner
Morning

Ferry departure into the archipelago

Sweden's Stockholm archipelago spreads across roughly 30,000 islands and skerries east of the city, and a Waxholmsbolaget ferry from Strömkajen, near the Grand Hôtel, is the easiest way to see a slice of it in a single day.

⛴️ Ferries depart from Strömkajen, near the Grand Hôtel 💵 ~SEK 195 round-trip to Vaxholm 🕐 Morning departure, boats run roughly hourly in summer
Afternoon

Vaxholm's town & fortress

The archipelago's unofficial capital is a pastel-painted wooden town built around a 16th-century fortress that once guarded the sea approach to Stockholm, now home to a small museum and an easy, walkable harbor.

🚶 Fortress reachable by a short passenger boat from the harbor 💵 ~SEK 100 fortress entry 🕐 Midday, allow a couple of hours before the return boat
Evening

Return cruise & a waterfront dinner

The return crossing catches the low evening light over the islands, and Stockholm's harborfront restaurants near Strömkajen and Skeppsbron are made for a relaxed dinner after a day out on the water.

⛴️ ~1 hr return crossing to central Stockholm 💵 Dinner from ~SEK 250 per person 🕐 Early evening, before the light fades
Morning

Fotografiska photography museum

Housed in a converted Art Nouveau customs building on Stadsgårdshamnen, Fotografiska runs major touring photography exhibitions across several floors, with a rooftop café looking straight across the water to Gamla Stan.

🚶 ~10 min walk from Slussen 💵 ~SEK 225 entry 🕐 Late morning, allow around ninety minutes
Afternoon

Södermalm cafes, design shops & a proper fika

Söder's grid of streets around Nytorget and SoFo (South of Folkungagatan) is Stockholm's design and coffee neighborhood, independent shops and bakeries built for exactly the kind of unhurried coffee-and-pastry break Swedes call fika.

🚶 Centered around Nytorget and Folkungagatan 💵 ~SEK 70 for coffee and a cinnamon bun 🕐 Mid-afternoon, the classic fika hour
Evening

Stadshuset (City Hall) at sunset

The redbrick City Hall on Lake Mälaren hosts the Nobel Prize banquet every December, and its Blue Hall and gold-mosaic Golden Hall are open on guided tours, with a lakefront terrace made for watching the sun go down.

🚶 ~15 min walk from Södermalm via Riddarholmen 💵 ~SEK 140 guided tour 🕐 Early evening, tours run into the late afternoon
Morning

The Nobel Prize Museum

Tucked into a historic former Stock Exchange building right on Stortorget, this compact museum traces the Nobel Prize's history through short films and personal objects from more than a century of laureates.

🚶 On Stortorget, in the heart of Gamla Stan 💵 ~SEK 140 entry 🕐 Mid-morning, allow about an hour
Midday

Kungsträdgården & last souvenirs

Central Stockholm's public living room hosts open-air cafes and the occasional summer festival under its rows of trimmed chestnut trees, a relaxed spot for a last coffee before browsing design shops on nearby Biblioteksgatan.

🚶 ~10 min walk from Gamla Stan 💵 Budget to taste 🕐 Late morning into early afternoon
Afternoon

Transfer to the airport

The Arlanda Express runs directly back to Arlanda Airport from Stockholm Central in about 20 minutes, making even a fairly late departure from downtown realistic.

🚆 ~20 min on the Arlanda Express 💵 ~SEK 340 🕐 Leave at least 3 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is already included in prices at Swedish restaurants and bars, so tipping is not expected the way it is in North America.
  • Rounding up the bill or leaving loose change is plenty for cafes, taxis, and casual meals.
  • At a nicer sit-down dinner, an extra 5 to 10% for excellent service is a kind gesture, never an obligation.

👗 Dress Code

  • Stockholm dresses in clean, understated layers rather than anything flashy, locals dress for the weather more than the occasion.
  • Even in July, evenings on the water or an archipelago boat can turn cool fast, so bring a real layer.
  • Flat, comfortable shoes matter most on Gamla Stan's cobblestones and Day 3's ferry docks.

☕ Fika & the Idea of "Lagom"

  • Fika, a proper coffee-and-pastry break taken seriously once or twice a day, is closer to a social ritual than a quick snack, sit down and put the phone away.
  • "Lagom," roughly "just the right amount," shapes everything from portion sizes to small talk, showiness is generally not admired.
  • Queueing is taken seriously, look for the numbered ticket machine (kölapp) in bakeries, pharmacies, and shops before walking straight to the counter.

🚇 Getting Around Stockholm

  • The SL system (tunnelbana metro, buses, and commuter trains) runs on a single ticket, with an app or a rechargeable SL Access card the easiest way to pay.
  • A 72-hour SL travelcard comfortably covers the Djurgården ferry used on Day 2 and most of this itinerary's transit.
  • The Day 3 archipelago boats are run by a separate operator, Waxholmsbolaget, though SL travelcards are honored on many of its routes within the city zone.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Stockholm is genuinely low-crime, but Gamla Stan's narrow lanes and Central Station draw pickpockets during peak summer.
  • Jaywalking against a red pedestrian signal, even on an empty street, draws disapproving looks here, Swedes wait for the green.
  • Photographing the Royal Guard is fine, just keep a respectful distance and don't block the changing-of-the-guard route.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Swedish

  • Hej: hi
  • Tack så mycket: thank you very much
  • Notan, tack: the check, please

💳 Money & Payments

  • Sweden uses the Swedish krona (SEK), and remains one of the most cashless countries in the world.
  • Contactless card and phone payments are accepted almost everywhere, including archipelago ferries and Skansen's food stalls.
  • Cash is rarely useful, some shops and cafes decline it outright, so there's little reason to carry much SEK at all.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Get a Stockholm Pass

Covers free or discounted entry to the Vasa Museum, Skansen, the ABBA Museum, and dozens more attractions plus transit, and it pays for itself after just two or three stops.

🥐

Ask about the late fika discount

Some Södermalm cafes and bakeries mark down unsold pastries in the late afternoon, the same cinnamon buns for a fraction of the morning price.

🚶

Join a tip-based walking tour

Several companies run free, guide-led walks through Gamla Stan departing daily from Stortorget, pay what the tour was worth to you at the end.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🏞️

Tantolunden

A riverside park on Södermalm where locals picnic, tend tiny allotment gardens, and swim off the rocks in summer, a genuine slice of local life.

🎨

Nytorget's design studios

The streets around this Södermalm square hide small, independent ceramics and design shops far from the souvenir stalls of Gamla Stan.

🏊

Public bathing jetties

Stockholm's water is clean enough to swim in right in the city, and a public bathing spot like the one at Hellasgården is where locals cool off in summer.

✈️ Flights

  • Stockholm Arlanda Airport (ARN) is the main hub, about 40 km north of downtown.
  • The Arlanda Express reaches Stockholm Central in around 20 minutes, the fastest link by far.
  • Slower but cheaper options include the Flygbussarna airport coach or regional trains via Arlanda Central.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Metered taxis can be flagged on the street or booked through apps like Bolt or Uber, both active in Stockholm.
  • Always confirm a fixed price for airport transfers, some taxi firms post inflated rates for arriving tourists.
  • Ride-hailing is usually cheaper and more predictable than a street-hailed taxi for cross-town trips.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Not needed for this itinerary, Gamla Stan, Djurgården, Södermalm, and the archipelago boats all run on transit, ferry, or foot.
  • Parking in central Stockholm is expensive and tightly restricted.
  • Skip the rental and put the budget toward museum tickets and the Vaxholm boat instead.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Gamla Stan or Norrmalm (city center): closest to the Royal Palace, Stortorget, and Central Station.
  • Södermalm: Stockholm's trendiest base, with Day 4's cafes and Fotografiska right on your doorstep.
  • Östermalm: quieter and upscale, a short walk or ferry ride from Djurgården's museums.

⛴️ Ferries & the Archipelago

  • Waxholmsbolaget runs the public ferries out to Vaxholm and the wider archipelago from Strömkajen, near the Grand Hôtel.
  • Book Day 3's return trip in advance during peak July weeks, boats can sell out on the busiest summer days.
  • The short Djurgården ferry from Slussen or Nybroplan is covered by a standard SL ticket, no separate fare needed.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Sweden is in the Schengen Area, so standard Schengen entry rules apply for non-EU visitors.
  • Remember the currency is the Swedish krona (SEK), not the euro, even though Sweden is an EU member.
  • Travel insurance is worth it given the pre-booked Vasa Museum and archipelago ferry tickets, which are often non-refundable.
