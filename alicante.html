4 Days in Alicante: The Castle, Tabarca Island & the Elche Palm Grove

Winter sun on the Costa Blanca: a free hilltop castle, tapas in Santa Cruz, a ferry to the walled island of Tabarca and a train to the Elche palm grove. Honest about the cool sea.

Ideal SeasonYear-round (mild winters, Apr – Jun and Sep – Oct best)
Est. Budget$270-480
Duration4 Days
PaceRelaxed
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Alicante-Elche Airport & the C-6 bus

Alicante-Elche airport (ALC) is about 10 km from the centre. The C-6 bus leaves from outside arrivals roughly every 20 to 30 minutes and takes 20 to 30 minutes to Puerta del Mar and Luceros. A single costs about €4.70, paid to the driver (check current). A taxi to the centre is roughly €20 to €25 on the meter.

🚌 ~25 min on the C-6 bus to the centre 💵 ~€4.70 bus, ~€20-25 taxi (check current) 🕐 Buses run from early morning to around midnight (check current)
Afternoon

Postiguet Beach under the castle

Postiguet is the city beach, a 10 minute walk from the old town and free to use. Be realistic about winter: January highs are about 16 to 17°C and the sea is only 14 to 15°C. It suits sunbathing in a sheltered spot, a paddle and a long walk. Obey the beach flags, as winter swell can be strong.

🚶 ~10 min on foot from the old town 💵 Free (a drink at a beach bar ~€3) 🕐 Early afternoon, when the sun is warmest
Evening

The Explanada de España & the marina

The Explanada is the palm-lined promenade along the marina, paved with about 6.6 million marble tiles in a wave pattern. Walk it at dusk, when the castle is floodlit above the boats. It is free. Many front-row restaurants charge a lot for average paella, so eat a few streets inland instead.

🚶 ~5 min on foot from the port to the old town 💵 Promenade free, ~€20-30 for dinner inland 🕐 Sunset is about 17:45 in December and 18:30 in February
Morning

The Central Market

The Mercado Central is open Monday to Friday 7:00 to 14:30 and Saturday 7:00 to 15:00, and closed on Sundays, so swap days if needed. Buy olives, cheese, fruit and mojama (salt-cured tuna) for a cheap picnic lunch. Go before noon, when the best stalls still have stock and the aisles are quiet.

🚶 ~10 min on foot from the Explanada 💵 ~€5-10 for picnic food 🕐 Before noon, closed Sundays
Afternoon

Santa Bárbara Castle

Santa Bárbara Castle crowns Mount Benacantil, about 166 m above the sea. Entry is free. The lift opposite Postiguet beach costs about €2.70 (check current), or walk up in about 30 minutes. Winter hours (15 November to 27 February) are 10:00 to 18:00, and access stops 30 minutes before closing. It is windy on top, so bring a layer.

🛗 Lift opposite Postiguet beach, or ~30 min on foot 💵 Entry free, lift ~€2.70 (check current) 🕐 Winter 10:00-18:00, last entry 30 min before closing
Evening

Santa Cruz & tapas in the old town

Walk down from the castle through Santa Cruz, a steep quarter of narrow lanes, small stairways and potted flowers. It is free and best in the low afternoon light. Then eat tapas in the old town around Calle Villavieja, about €2.50 to €4 a plate (check current). Try mojama, local anchovies and a coca topped with tuna.

🚶 ~15 min downhill on foot from the castle 💵 ~€15-25 for tapas and a drink 🕐 Tapas bars fill after 20:00, dinner from 21:00
Morning

The ferry from Alicante to Tabarca

Cruceros Kontiki sails from the port beside the Explanada. An adult return is €24 and the timetable lists departures at 10:45 and 12:15, returning at 16:00 and 17:30 (confirm at the ticket office). The crossing takes about an hour. Arrive 40 minutes early, and note that winter sailings can be cancelled in rough seas.

⛴️ ~1 hour by ferry from the port 💵 ~€24 return (check current) 🕐 10:45 or 12:15 departure, confirm the winter timetable
Afternoon

The walled village & a caldero lunch

Tabarca is a small walled village built in the 1760s for families resettled from an island off Tunisia. Walk through the Puerta de Tierra gate, past the church and along the lanes in under an hour. Lunch on caldero, a fish and rice dish, costs about €25 to €35 a head (check current). Book ahead in winter.

🚶 The whole village is ~10 min across on foot 💵 ~€25-35 for a caldero lunch 🕐 Lunch from 14:00, book ahead
Evening

Coves, the marine reserve & the boat home

A marine reserve, the first in Spain, surrounds the island. With a winter sea of about 14 to 15°C, snorkelling needs a wetsuit and is best saved for summer. Instead walk the coastal path to the pebble coves and the lighthouse. Take the 16:00 or 17:30 boat back, then order arroz a banda, rice cooked in fish stock, with alioli.

⛴️ ~1 hour back to the port 💵 ~€20-30 for arroz a banda for one 🕐 Return at 16:00 or 17:30, the last boat is firm
Morning

Train to Elche & the Palmeral

Take the Renfe Cercanías C-1 from Alicante Terminal to Elche Parque, about 25 to 30 minutes for a couple of euros (check the timetable and fare). The Palmeral is a UNESCO-listed palm grove of roughly 200,000 palms, with shaded paths and old irrigation channels. Walking through it is free, and the palms are best in clear morning light.

🚆 ~25-30 min by Cercanías C-1 to Elche Parque 💵 ~€2 each way (check current) 🕐 Catch a train around 9:00 and check the return times
Afternoon

Huerto del Cura & central Elche

The Huerto del Cura is a garden inside the palm grove, planted with cacti, old date palms and ponds. Entry is about €7 (check current) and an hour is plenty. Then see the Basilica of Santa María and the Calahorra tower in the centre, and try arroz con costra, Elche's baked rice topped with a crust of egg.

🚶 ~10-15 min on foot from the garden to the basilica 💵 Garden ~€7 (check current), lunch ~€12-16 🕐 Lunch at 14:00 or later, Spanish time
Evening

Transfer to Alicante-Elche Airport

From Elche, a Vectalia bus or a taxi reaches the airport in 15 to 20 minutes. Check the current bus line and fare, and expect a taxi to cost about €15 to €20. If you return to Alicante first, take the C-6. Allow 2 to 3 hours before your flight, as security and check-in queues can be long.

🚌 ~15-20 min by bus or taxi from Elche 💵 ~€2 bus or ~€15-20 taxi (check current) 🕐 Be at the airport 2 to 3 hours before departure

💶 Tipping, Payments & Coffee

  • Service is included in Spanish prices, so tipping is not expected. For good service, leave small change or round up, and about 5 to 10 percent is generous for a sit-down meal. Round a taxi fare up to the next euro.
  • Cards and contactless work almost everywhere in Alicante, from restaurants to the bus. Carry a little cash for market stalls, small bars and the odd kiosk.
  • Coffee is cheaper at the bar than at a terrace table. Try a café bombón (espresso with condensed milk), a local favourite.

🍚 Eating & Drinking, Spanish Style

  • Spanish mealtimes run late. Lunch is 14:00 to 16:00 and dinner starts at 21:00 or later. Many kitchens close between lunch and dinner, so a snack at 17:00 is easier than a full meal.
  • The menú del día, a fixed weekday lunch of two courses, a drink and dessert, is the best value, usually about €12 to €16 (check current). Paella and other rice dishes are lunch dishes, and are made for two or more people.
  • Try arroz a banda (rice in fish stock with alioli), caldero on Tabarca, coca (a flatbread, often with tuna), turrón (hard Alicante and soft Jijona), and horchata, a cold drink made from tiger nuts.

🎆 Opening Hours, Siesta & Fiestas

  • Many small shops close from about 14:00 to 17:00 and on Sundays. The Central Market is closed on Sundays, and some museums close on Mondays, so check before you plan.
  • The Hogueras de San Juan, from 20 to 24 June, is the city's big fiesta, with giant papier-mâché monuments, fireworks and the burning of the monuments on the last night. Book rooms well ahead and expect noise and crowds.
  • In winter, daylight is short (sunset about 17:45 in December), so visit outdoor sights such as the castle early.

⚠️ Safety, Sun & Tourist-Trap Warnings

  • Pickpockets target the airport bus, crowded squares and the Explanada. Keep your phone and wallet in a front pocket or zipped bag, and never leave bags on the sand while you walk to the water. Do not leave valuables in a parked car.
  • Restaurants on the Explanada and the port often use photo menus and sell expensive, average paella. Walk a few streets into the old town, and check prices before sitting down.
  • Winter sun is milder but still strong, and the wind makes the sea feel colder. Use sunscreen on the castle and Tabarca, and respect the beach flags: red means do not swim.
  • In an emergency call 112. Check the official travel advice for your own country, such as the UK FCDO or US State Department pages for Spain, before you travel.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Spanish

  • "Buenos días" (good morning), "Buenas tardes" (good afternoon or evening), "Hola" (hi). In Valencian, say "Bon dia" (bon DEE-ah) and "Gràcies" (GRAH-see-es).
  • "Por favor" (please), "Gracias" (thank you), "De nada" (you're welcome), "Perdone" (excuse me)
  • "Una mesa para dos, por favor" (a table for two, please), "La cuenta, por favor" (the bill, please)
  • "¿Habla inglés?" (do you speak English?), "¿Dónde está...?" (where is...?), "¿Cuánto cuesta?" (how much is it?)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Spain uses the euro. Cards and contactless work in most hotels, restaurants, shops and buses, but keep some cash for small bars, markets and taxis that may not take cards.
  • Use bank-branded ATMs and decline the "convert to your home currency" option, choosing euros instead. Avoid standalone exchange booths, which give poor rates.
  • Prices on menus include VAT. Some hotels charge a local tourist tax, so check your booking (check current).

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🍽️

Make lunch the main meal

The menú del día, available at lunchtime on weekdays, gives two courses, a drink and dessert for about €12 to €16 (check current). It is far cheaper than ordering the same dishes at dinner.

🥖

Picnic from the Central Market

Buy olives, cheese, bread and fruit from the Mercado Central for a few euros, and eat on the castle terraces or on Tabarca. It saves a lot on a day with ferry and lift costs.

🏰

Use free sights and the lift only if needed

The castle, the Explanada, Postiguet, Santa Cruz and the Elche Palmeral paths are all free. The castle lift is only about €2.70 (check current), so walk up and take the lift down, or the other way round.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🎆

Time a trip to the Hogueras

From 20 to 24 June the city holds the Hogueras de San Juan, with fireworks and the burning of giant monuments. It is a great week to visit, but book rooms months ahead and expect higher prices.

🎄

Look for the Christmas lights

In December the old town and the Explanada are decorated for Christmas, and markets and stalls usually appear (check current dates). It is a lively time, and a good one for buying turrón.

🌅

See the castle at golden hour

The terraces face the sea and the marina, and the light is best an hour before closing. In winter that is around 16:30, so go up in the afternoon and stay until the castle closes.

✈️ Flights

  • Alicante-Elche Airport (ALC) is the main airport for the Costa Blanca, about 10 km from the city centre, with many direct budget flights from across Europe.
  • The C-6 bus runs to the centre every 20 to 30 minutes and costs about €4.70 (check current). The journey takes 20 to 30 minutes.
  • Check flights from several nearby airports and compare fares, since winter prices are usually lower than summer.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Official taxis wait at the rank outside arrivals, use a meter and cost roughly €20 to €25 to the centre (check current).
  • Apps such as Uber and Cabify operate in Alicante (check current coverage and prices).
  • Use taxi ranks or an app rather than a driver who approaches you in the terminal.

🚇 Public Transport & Tickets

  • Vectalia runs the city buses, with a single ticket around €1.45 to €1.50 (check current). Most of this itinerary is walkable.
  • The TRAM d'Alacant light rail runs from Luceros to Playa de San Juan, El Campello and Benidorm. Check the line map and zone fares before you ride.
  • Renfe Cercanías C-1 runs from Alicante Terminal to Elche Parque in about 25 to 30 minutes for a couple of euros (check current).
  • Cruceros Kontiki runs the Tabarca ferry from the port, for about €24 return (check current).

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Old town and Santa Cruz: the most atmospheric base, close to the castle, tapas bars and the market, but with steep streets and some noise at weekends.
  • Near the Explanada and the port: central, flat and well placed for the ferry and Postiguet, but a bit more expensive.
  • Around Luceros and the Central Market: good-value hotels, a short walk from everything, and next to the tram and bus stops.
  • Playa de San Juan: a modern beach resort 15 to 20 minutes by tram, with a long sandy beach and apartment hotels, but less atmosphere in winter.
  • Winter prices are usually low, but weekends and the Hogueras week are busy, so book ahead.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Spain is in the EU, so EU SIM holders roam at home rates. Other visitors can use an eSIM or a local prepaid SIM, and many cafés and hotels offer Wi-Fi.
  • Cards work almost everywhere, but keep a little cash for markets, small bars and some taxis.
  • Use bank-branded ATMs and avoid standalone exchange booths.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Check your passport's Schengen rules before booking, including whether ETIAS applies on your travel dates (check current).
  • Book the Tabarca ferry ahead for weekends and holidays, and check the weather, since winter sailings can be cancelled.
  • You do not need a car. Parking in the centre is hard, and buses, trams and trains cover this itinerary.
  • Carry your EHIC or GHIC card for public healthcare, and keep travel insurance details with you. The emergency number is 112.

About $270-480 per person for 4 days, roughly $70-120 a day, excluding international flights. The estimate covers lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and real costs vary with the season and your spending habits.
