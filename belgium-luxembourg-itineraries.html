What links Bruges, Brussels and Luxembourg City?

Belgium and Luxembourg are neighbouring countries, and each of these three trips is a short stay in one city. Bruges and Luxembourg City are both Historic Old Towns trips, while Brussels is the Modern Cities one, so the first choice is between a medieval centre and a working capital. Bruges is covered on foot and by bicycle, Brussels adds metro and tram rides out to the Atomium and the EU Quarter, and Luxembourg City uses its trams and trains for Kirchberg and the airport.

Bruges has the lowest total, at $500-700 per person for three days, so it makes a sensible first pick for a long weekend. Brussels costs $650-900 per person for three days and works out at $260 a day, the highest daily rate of the three. Luxembourg is the longest at four days and $600-850 per person, yet its $180 a day is lower than Bruges at $200 or Brussels at $260. The fourth day is an outing to Vianden Castle, and the pace is listed as relaxed.

When is the best time to go?

Bruges is listed for April to June and again for September and October, with a bike ride out to Damme on the last day. Brussels is listed for one unbroken stretch, April to September, which makes it the easiest to fit around work or school dates. Luxembourg has two separate seasons: May to September for the ramparts and the castle day trip, and late November to December for the Christmas markets that the itinerary is built around.

If you want one window that suits all three, May and June sit inside every ideal season listed. December is the exception. Only Luxembourg is planned around it, so a winter visit means that city rather than the other two.

How do you combine the trips and keep costs down?

Brussels is the natural starting point. Brussels Airport says the direct train to Brussels-Central takes around 18 minutes. Bruges is about an hour from Brussels by direct train. Luxembourg City is a separate leg, since direct trains from Brussels take roughly three hours.

Doing all three back to back gives you ten days on the ground. Luxembourg is the easiest place to save on transport, because its buses, trams and second-class trains are free to ride. In Brussels the Grand Place costs nothing to see, and the Parlamentarium, the European Parliament's visitor centre, has free entry. The Luxembourg markets run only from late November, so book rooms early if that is the trip you want.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a trip to Belgium and Luxembourg cost?

Our 3 Belgium & Luxembourg itineraries cost from $500 to $900 per person for the whole trip, which is about $180 to $260 a day. Prices cover lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and exclude international flights.

Which Belgium & Luxembourg itinerary is the cheapest?

Bruges ($500-700), Luxembourg City ($600-850), Brussels ($650-900) are the three lowest, per person and excluding flights.

How many days do I need in Belgium and Luxembourg?

Our Belgium & Luxembourg itineraries run from 3 to 4 days, and the most common length is 3 days. Each page has a day picker with a price for every length from one day up to the full plan.

When is the best time to visit Belgium and Luxembourg?

It depends on the trip, and each itinerary lists its ideal season. For example: Bruges, Apr – Jun, Sep – Oct; Brussels, Apr – Sep; Luxembourg City, Late Nov – Dec (Christmas Markets), May – Sep. The table above shows the ideal season for every Belgium & Luxembourg trip.

Can I visit Bruges and Brussels in one trip?

Yes. They are about an hour apart by direct train and both itineraries run for three days, so a combined stay is six days. Fly into Brussels, take the airport train into the city, then continue to Bruges by rail.

Do I need a car for Belgium and Luxembourg?

No. These itineraries rely on trains, trams, buses, bicycles and walking. Public transport in Luxembourg is free, including the airport tram and second-class trains, although first class and international journeys are not. The centres of Bruges and Brussels are compact, and the Brussels itinerary notes that parking downtown is scarce and expensive.
