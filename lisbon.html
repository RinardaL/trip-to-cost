💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Not obligatory: round up or leave 5–10% at sit-down restaurants for good service.
  • No tipping expected at cafés, bars, or counter service.
  • Taxi/rideshare drivers: rounding up is common but not required.

👗 Dress Code

  • Casual dress is fine everywhere, including most restaurants.
  • Cover shoulders to enter Jerónimos Monastery and other churches.
  • Comfortable shoes are essential: Lisbon's cobblestones are steep and slick when wet.

🚋 Transit Nuances

  • Buy a Viva Viagem card at any metro station: it covers metro, tram, bus, and funiculars.
  • Tram 28 gets extremely crowded: ride at the terminus, early morning, or evening.
  • Pickpockets specifically target Tram 28: keep bags zipped and in front.

⚠️ Safety & Scam Warnings

  • Politely decline "free" bracelet or rose sellers near major tourist spots: they'll ask for money after.
  • Use metered taxis or Uber/Bolt: both are reliable and reasonably priced.
  • Watch belongings closely on crowded trams and in Baixa's busy squares.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases

  • Bom dia: good morning / hello
  • Obrigado / Obrigada: thank you (said by men / women respectively)
  • A conta, por favor: the bill, please

💳 Money & Payments

  • Cards widely accepted, including most trams and taxis.
  • Small tascas and traditional markets are often cash-only: carry some euros.
  • ATMs (Multibanco) are everywhere in central Lisbon.

✈️ Flights

  • Lisbon Humberto Delgado Airport (LIS) is the main gateway, just 20 minutes from the city center by metro.
  • The metro's red line runs directly downtown, no need to book a transfer or private driver in advance.
  • If arriving late at night when the metro has stopped running, a metered taxi or Uber/Bolt covers the same trip in about 20 minutes.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Uber and Bolt both operate reliably across Lisbon, often cheaper than street taxis for the same ride.
  • Metered taxis are easy to hail centrally; use the meter and skip flat-fare offers within the city.
  • For a driver to Sintra or Cascais instead of the train, agree a fixed return fare upfront rather than paying by the hour.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Not needed for the Lisbon days: trams, the metro, and walking cover Baixa, Alfama, and Belém easily.
  • Sintra and Cascais are both reached by direct train in this itinerary, so skip the rental for those day trips too.
  • Parking in Alfama's narrow lanes is scarce and its cobblestones are rough on tires, reasons enough to leave a car behind entirely.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Baixa: central grid streets close to Praça do Comércio and the Rua Augusta Arch, an easy walk to Cais do Sodré.
  • Alfama: steps from São Jorge Castle and the fado tascas, though rooms sit on steep, uneven lanes.
  • Chiado or Cais do Sodré: near Bairro Alto's nightlife and the train line out to Cascais.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • Not part of this itinerary: Sintra and Cascais are both reached by train, not ferry.
  • A short Tagus river cruise from Cais do Sodré is an easy optional add-on if you have a spare evening.
  • No inter-island or coastal ferry legs are required for this trip.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • EU/Schengen entry rules apply; non-EU visitors should confirm visa requirements before booking flights.
  • Travel insurance is worth it mainly for the Sintra and Cascais train tickets and any flight delays.
  • A Viva Viagem transit card, bought on arrival, covers metro, tram, bus, and funiculars for the whole week.
