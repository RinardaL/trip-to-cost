6 Days in Tuscany: A Self-Drive Road Trip

Cypress-lined roads, Renaissance Florence, medieval hill towns, and Chianti vineyards, a region-wide Tuscany itinerary built around a countryside home base and easy drives between towns.

Ideal SeasonApr – Jun, Sep – Oct
Est. Budget$1,100-1,500
Duration6 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Land in Florence or Pisa & pick up the rental car

Both airports have rental counters right in the arrivals hall. Reserve an automatic ahead of time, since most Italian fleet cars are manual.

🚗 ~1.5–2 hr drive to the Val d'Orcia 💵 ~€55/day rental 🕐 Book the automatic weeks ahead
Afternoon

Check into an agriturismo near Siena

A working farmhouse stay puts you close to Siena, San Gimignano, and the Val d'Orcia without ever touching a town center ZTL zone.

🚗 Countryside roads, no ZTL restrictions 💵 ~€150/night agriturismo 🕐 Check-in from 3 PM
Evening

First cypress-road sunset drive

A short loop past one of the region's famous cypress-lined lanes near San Quirico d'Orcia eases you into Tuscan driving before tomorrow's bigger day.

🚗 ~20 min loop drive 💵 Free 🕐 Golden hour, an hour before sunset
Morning

Drive in & the Duomo

Park at a periphery lot outside Florence's ZTL zone and walk or tram in, since the historic center is closed to non-resident cars during the day.

🚗 ~1 hr 15 min drive, park outside the ZTL 💵 ~€30 dome climb ticket 🕐 Book the timed dome entry weeks ahead
Afternoon

Uffizi Gallery

One of the world's great Renaissance collections, worth the pre-booked skip-the-line ticket to avoid the courtyard queue.

🚶 Walkable in the historic center 💵 ~€25 entry 🕐 Early afternoon, pre-book online
Evening

Ponte Vecchio & dinner in Oltrarno

Cross the medieval bridge at dusk, then head into the Oltrarno district for a quieter, more local dinner scene than the tourist center.

🚶 Across the Arno 💵 ~€35 dinner 🕐 Blue hour on the bridge
Morning

Piazza del Campo

Park at one of the lots just outside the old walls and walk in. The shell-shaped piazza is quietest right after breakfast, before the tour buses unload.

🚗 ~1 hr drive, park outside the walls 💵 ~€2/hr parking 🕐 Early morning, before the tour buses
Afternoon

Siena Duomo

The striped marble cathedral holds an inlaid marble floor that's only fully uncovered part of the year, so check ahead if timing matters to you.

🚶 Short walk uphill from the Campo 💵 ~€15 OPA SI Pass entry 🕐 Midday, cool inside the nave
Evening

Torre del Mangia & a contrada Palio museum

Climb the tower for a rooftop view over the racetrack shape of the Campo, then duck into one of the small contrada museums to learn the neighborhood rivalries behind the Palio horse race.

🚶 400 steps up the tower 💵 ~€10 tower entry 🕐 Late afternoon light over the rooftops
Morning

San Gimignano's medieval towers

Fourteen surviving stone towers give the skyline its nickname, "medieval Manhattan." Arrive before the day-trip buses fill the lanes.

🚗 ~40 min drive from the Val d'Orcia 💵 ~€5 Torre Grossa climb 🕐 Before 9:30 AM, ahead of the tour buses
Afternoon

Chianti wine estate tasting

A working estate near Greve in Chianti or Radda pairs a cellar tour with a seated tasting among the vine rows.

🚗 ~45 min drive into the Chianti hills 💵 ~€40 tour & tasting 🕐 Book a midday tasting slot
Evening

Dinner in a Chianti hill town

Radda or Panzano both have small trattorias overlooking the vineyard rows, a quieter way to end the day than driving back to base.

🚗 Short drive within Chianti 💵 ~€45 dinner 🕐 Sunset over the vines
Morning

Pienza, the Renaissance "ideal city"

Pope Pius II rebuilt his home village into a model Renaissance town in the 1400s. Browse the pecorino cheese shops lining the main street.

🚗 ~25 min drive from base 💵 Free to wander, ~€10 cheese tasting 🕐 Mid-morning
Afternoon

Montepulciano & a Vino Nobile cellar

The hilltop town's underground cellars, some carved from Etruscan tunnels, are the place to taste Vino Nobile di Montepulciano.

🚗 ~15 min from Pienza 💵 ~€15 cellar tasting 🕐 Early afternoon
Evening

Cypress-road photography at golden hour

The rolling roads around San Quirico d'Orcia are Tuscany's most photographed stretch of tarmac, best with the low, warm light of early evening.

🚗 ~20 min drive to the photo stops 💵 Free 🕐 Golden hour, before sunset
Morning

Drive to Pisa & climb the Leaning Tower

Book the tower climb slot in advance. Entry is timed and capped, and same-day tickets often sell out by mid-morning.

🚗 ~2 hr drive from the Val d'Orcia 💵 ~€20 tower climb 🕐 Book the climb slot ahead, arrive early
Afternoon

Piazza dei Miracoli, Duomo & Baptistery

The whole complex sits on one lawn, so this is an easy last stop, even with luggage in the car.

🚶 Walkable, same piazza as the tower 💵 ~€10 combo ticket 🕐 Early afternoon, before departure
Evening

Return the rental car & fly out

Pisa's airport is one of the closest in Europe to a city center, minutes from the drop-off counters.

🚗 ~10 min to Pisa Airport 💵 Rental already paid 🕐 Arrive 2 hrs before your flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is generally included (servizio compreso), so tipping isn't obligatory anywhere in Tuscany.
  • Rounding up or leaving a few euros for great service at a trattoria is appreciated, not expected.
  • At wine estate tastings, a small tip for a guide who spent real time with your group is a nice gesture, not a requirement.

👗 Dress Code

  • Cover shoulders and knees to enter the Duomo in Florence, Siena, and most other churches on this route.
  • Smart-casual works fine for wine estate tastings and evening dinners, no need to pack anything formal.
  • Bring layers: hill towns like San Gimignano and Montepulciano stay breezy even on warm days.

🚗 Getting Around: Self-Drive & ZTL Zones

  • Nearly every historic center on this route, Florence, Siena, San Gimignano, has a ZTL (Zona a Traffico Limitato) that fines non-resident cars caught driving in on camera.
  • Always park in a signed lot just outside the old walls and walk in. Agriturismo hosts can point you to the closest ZTL-free lot for each town.
  • Rural Tuscan roads are narrow and winding. A GPS with offline maps downloaded helps when signal drops between hill towns.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • ZTL fines are mailed months later and can be steep, so never treat a "residents only" sign as optional, even briefly.
  • Pickpocketing occurs around the Uffizi queue and the Ponte Vecchio, the two most crowded spots on this itinerary.
  • Rest stops (autogrill) on the autostrada are safe and normal for fuel and coffee breaks between towns.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Italian

  • Buongiorno: good morning/hello
  • Grazie mille: thank you very much
  • Il conto, per favore: the bill, please

💳 Money & Payments

  • The euro is used throughout Italy, and cards are accepted almost everywhere, including small agriturismo farm shops.
  • Keep some cash for parking meters outside old town walls and small-town market stalls that skip card readers.
  • Autostrada tolls are usually cash or card at the booth, so keep a card handy in the car door pocket.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🅿️

Park outside the walls, always

Lots just outside the historic centers of Siena, San Gimignano, and Florence cost a fraction of anything inside, and avoid ZTL fines entirely.

🍝

Pranzo fisso lunch menus

Fixed-price lunch specials at family-run trattorias run far cheaper than the same kitchen's dinner menu, and the food is just as good.

🏡

Agriturismo over hotel

A countryside farmhouse stay is usually cheaper per night than a comparable town hotel, and often includes a home-cooked breakfast.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🏘️

Pienza and Montepulciano over crowded San Gimignano

If San Gimignano's lanes feel overrun by midday, these two Val d'Orcia hill towns offer the same medieval charm with a fraction of the tour buses.

🌾

The Crete Senesi back roads

The clay hills south of Siena have their own rolling, cypress-dotted scenery, and almost none of the traffic of the main Val d'Orcia photo spots.

🌙

Stay in a hill town after the day-trippers leave

San Gimignano and Montepulciano empty out by early evening, so a late dinner there feels like a completely different, quieter town.

✈️ Flights

  • Florence Airport (Peretola, also called Amerigo Vespucci) and Pisa Airport (Galileo Galilei) are the two real gateways for this route.
  • Flying into one and out of the other saves backtracking, since Pisa sits closer to Day 6's final stop.
  • Both airports have rental counters right in the terminal, so you can be on the road within the hour.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • This is a self-drive road trip, so taxis and rideshare are a minor part of the plan at best.
  • Uber has limited coverage in Tuscany outside Florence; local radio taxis are the fallback in Siena and smaller towns.
  • A taxi is useful for a wine tasting where someone wants to drink freely, back to the agriturismo and no driving involved.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • The essential service for this itinerary: no train line links San Gimignano, Chianti's back roads, and the Val d'Orcia the way a car does.
  • Reserve an automatic transmission well ahead of time, since most Italian rental fleets default to manual.
  • Never drive into a historic center marked ZTL: park in a lot just outside the walls in Florence, Siena, and San Gimignano and walk in.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • An agriturismo near Siena or in the Val d'Orcia, as used on Day 1, works as a single base for Siena, San Gimignano, and Chianti day trips.
  • Staying in Florence itself for Day 2 saves the evening drive back, if you'd rather split the trip into two bases.
  • Montepulciano or Pienza make a scenic overnight option on Day 5 for anyone who prefers two shorter drives to one long base.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • Not applicable on this route: inland Tuscany is landlocked, and no leg of this itinerary touches the coast.
  • Pisa is the closest this trip gets to the sea, and even there the focus stays on the Piazza dei Miracoli, not the water.
  • Travelers wanting a coastal add-on could extend west to the Tuscan Riviera, though it isn't part of this 6-day plan.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Standard Schengen entry rules apply, so non-EU visitors should confirm visa requirements before booking flights.
  • Non-EU drivers should carry an International Driving Permit alongside their home license for the rental car.
  • Travel insurance is worth adding at this budget tier, given the rental car, tastings, and timed museum tickets all add up if a day gets disrupted.
