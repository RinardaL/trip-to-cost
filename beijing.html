4 Days in Beijing: The Forbidden City, the Great Wall & the Hutongs

Hutong lanes and Peking duck, the Forbidden City on a timed ticket, a day on the Great Wall at Mutianyu, then the Temple of Heaven and the Lama Temple: four practical days in Beijing.

Ideal SeasonApr – May, Sep – Oct
Est. Budget$300-520
Duration4 Days
PaceActive
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival & the Airport Express

Beijing Capital (PEK) lies about 25 km north-east of the centre. The Capital Airport Express runs from Terminal 3 to Dongzhimen in roughly 25 minutes for a flat ¥25 (about $3.50), with Lines 2 and 13 at the end. Daxing (PKX) is to the south: its express reaches Caoqiao in about 20 minutes for about ¥35 (check current).

🚆 ~25 min by Airport Express to Dongzhimen 💵 ¥25 Capital Express, ~¥35 Daxing Express (check current) 🕐 Allow 1 to 1.5 hours from landing to the platform
Afternoon

Nanluoguxiang, the Hutongs & the Drum Tower

After dropping your bags, take Line 6 or 8 to Nanluoguxiang and walk the hutongs, the old courtyard lanes. The main lane is crowded, so turn into quieter side lanes such as Mao'er Hutong, then walk to the Drum Tower and the lakes of Shichahai. Wandering is free. Pedicab tours often overcharge, so agree the price first.

🚇 Line 6 or 8 to Nanluoguxiang, then on foot 💵 Free to wander, ~¥30 for a coffee 🕐 Mid-afternoon, quieter on weekdays
Evening

Peking duck dinner

Peking duck is roasted until the skin turns glassy, carved at the table and eaten in thin pancakes with sweet bean sauce, spring onion and cucumber. A whole duck at a well-known chain costs roughly ¥200-300 ($28-42) and feeds two or three, so share one. Book through the restaurant's app, WeChat or your hotel, as queues are long.

🚇 Subway or a short Didi ride from your hotel 💵 ~¥200-300 for a whole duck, shared (check current) 🕐 Book ahead, dinner from about 6 PM
Morning

Tiananmen Square

Tiananmen Square is free but needs a real-name reservation, made 1 to 7 days ahead on the official platform or an approved app. Pick a morning slot and bring your original passport, as there are ID and bag checks. Take Line 1 to Tiananmen East or West. You do not need the flag-raising slot, which means a pre-dawn start.

🚇 Line 1 to Tiananmen East or West 💵 Free (reservation and passport needed) 🕐 Morning slot, allow time for security
Afternoon

The Forbidden City

Tickets are online only, released 7 days ahead, and need your passport details: ¥60 from April to October, ¥40 from November to March. The palace is closed on Mondays, opens at 08:30 and has last entry around 16:10 (check current). Enter at the Meridian Gate, follow the central axis for about 3 hours and leave by the north gate.

🚶 ~10 min on foot from the square to the Meridian Gate 💵 ¥60 (Apr-Oct) or ¥40 (Nov-Mar), +¥10 for the Treasure Gallery 🕐 Late-morning slot, closed Mondays, last entry ~16:10
Evening

Jingshan Park at sunset

Jingshan Park faces the Forbidden City's north gate and costs only ¥2. A steady 15-minute climb to the central pavilion gives the classic view over the golden roofs, best in the hour before sunset. Closing time is earlier in winter, so check it. Afterwards, eat noodles or dumplings near your hotel for about ¥30-50.

🚶 Across the road from the Forbidden City north gate 💵 ¥2 entry, ~¥30-50 for dinner 🕐 About an hour before sunset
Morning

Getting to Mutianyu (or Jinshanling)

Mutianyu is about 70 km north. The cheapest route is the subway to Dongzhimen, express bus 916 to Huairou, then a local bus or taxi, about 2 hours in all. A tour or Didi (¥200+ one way) is easier. For a quieter, steeper wall, choose Jinshanling instead (about ¥65 in season, 2.5 to 3 hours each way, easiest by tour).

🚌 ~2 hours by subway, bus 916 and a local bus or taxi 💵 Public transport is cheap; Didi ¥200+ one way (check current) 🕐 Leave by 8 AM to reach the wall before the tour groups
Afternoon

Walking the wall & the toboggan

Entry is about ¥40, and the cable car is about ¥100 one way or ¥140 return (check current); you can also walk up. Walk between the restored watchtowers, then slide down on the toboggan, about ¥100-140 and weather dependent. Wear shoes with grip, because the steps are steep and uneven. Allow 3 to 4 hours on the wall.

🚠 Cable car up, toboggan or walk down 💵 ~¥40 entry + ~¥140 cable car return (check current) 🕐 Cable car and toboggan close in the late afternoon
Evening

Wangfujing & a simple dinner

Back in town, ride Line 1 or 8 to Wangfujing. The snack street is theatre, with scorpion skewers priced for photos, so treat it as a walk. For a real dinner, try noodles or dumplings in a side lane for ¥30-60. Go to bed early: the Temple of Heaven opens at 6 AM and is best before the crowds.

🚇 Line 1 or 8 to Wangfujing 💵 ~¥30-60 for dinner 🕐 After you return from the wall, around 7 PM
Morning

The Temple of Heaven & its park

Take Line 5 to Tiantandongmen (Exit A) for the East Gate. The park opens at 6 AM and the halls at 8 AM. The combined ticket (park plus the Hall of Prayer, Echo Wall and Circular Mound) is about ¥34, the park alone ¥15. Book with your passport on the park's mini-program. Go before 8 AM for local tai chi.

🚇 Line 5 to Tiantandongmen, Exit A 💵 ~¥34 combined ticket, ¥15 park only 🕐 Go at 7 AM, halls open at 8 AM (check Monday closures)
Afternoon

The Lama Temple (or the 798 Art District)

Take Line 2 or 5 to Yonghegong. The Lama Temple costs ¥25, and morning slots sell out; book on the official mini-program with your passport or buy at the desk. Look for the Wanfu Pavilion and its 18 m sandalwood Maitreya. Prefer art? Swap in the 798 district, free to wander though galleries charge, about 40 minutes by taxi.

🚇 Line 2 or 5 to Yonghegong 💵 ¥25 entry (check current), 798 is free to walk 🕐 Early afternoon, last entry about 30 minutes before closing
Evening

Transfer to the airport

Take the Airport Express from Dongzhimen or Sanyuanqiao (¥25) to Terminal 3, or the Daxing Express from Caoqiao. Reach the airport at least 3 hours before an international flight, since passport and security queues are long. A taxi or Didi to Capital costs about ¥100-150 (check current), but expressway traffic can double the time.

🚆 ~25 min by Airport Express from Dongzhimen 💵 ¥25 train, ~¥100-150 taxi (check current) 🕐 Be at the airport 3 hours before departure

💴 Tipping & Everyday Etiquette

  • Tipping is not customary in China, in restaurants, taxis or hotels, and staff may run after you to return the money. Tour guides and drivers on private tours are the usual exception, and a small tip is welcome there.
  • Politeness is quiet and indirect. Greet people with a nod or a smile, hand over cards, tickets or money with both hands, and avoid raised voices, which cause people to lose face.
  • Queues can be loose at stations and attractions. Stay patient and keep your place, but do not expect strict one-metre spacing.

🥢 Eating Like a Beijinger

  • Dishes are shared. Order a few for the table and eat from the common plates. Never stick chopsticks upright in your rice, which resembles incense at a funeral, and do not point with them. Use the serving chopsticks if provided.
  • Peking duck comes with thin pancakes, sweet bean sauce, spring onion and cucumber. Wrap a slice of skin and meat, eat it in two bites, then ask for the bones to be made into soup if you like. Zhajiangmian (noodles with soybean paste) and jianbing (savoury crêpes) are cheap local staples.
  • Hot water or tea is served free in many restaurants, and tap water is not drinkable, so boil it or buy bottles. Many places take orders by scanning a QR code at the table, and the host usually pays rather than splitting the bill.

🌫️ Seasons, Air Quality & Crowd Periods

  • The best months are April to May and September to October, with mild days and clearer skies. Winter is cold and dry, typically around or below freezing, with fewer crowds, and the Great Wall can be snowy or icy. July and August are hot, humid and rainy.
  • Air quality varies and is worse on still winter days, when heating runs. Check an app such as IQAir or the AQI shown in a weather app, and carry a few FFP2 or N95 masks for bad days.
  • Avoid Chinese New Year (late January or February; 6 February in 2027), when many sites and shops close or are packed, and the Golden Week holidays (1 to 7 October, and around 1 May), when attractions fill and tickets sell out days ahead.

⚠️ Safety, Scams & Etiquette Warnings

  • Tea-house and art-student scams: friendly young people near Wangfujing or Tiananmen invite you to a tea ceremony or an art show, then present a huge bill. Politely decline invitations from strangers and never follow them into a venue.
  • Use only metered taxis from the official rank, or Didi. Ignore unlicensed drivers at the airport, stations and tourist sites, who quote high fixed prices. Hutong pedicab tours also overcharge, so agree a price before you board.
  • Do not photograph police, military sites or government compounds, and be careful around Tiananmen. Avoid discussing sensitive politics such as Tiananmen 1989, Tibet, Xinjiang or Taiwan in public, and do not criticise the government or carry political or religious literature.
  • Carry your passport at all times, as many sites ask for it. Check your government's official travel advice (for example the UK FCDO or US State Department) for current rules, including detention and exit-ban warnings.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Mandarin

  • "Nǐ hǎo" (hello), "Xièxie" (thank you), "Bú kèqi" (you're welcome), "Duìbuqǐ" (sorry, excuse me)
  • "Wǒ bù chī là" (I do not eat spicy food), "Mǎidān" (the bill, please), "Bù yào, xièxie" (no, thank you)
  • "Zhège duōshao qián?" (how much is this?), "Wǒ yào qù ..." (I want to go to ...), "Cèsuǒ zài nǎlǐ?" (where is the toilet?)
  • "Nǐ huì shuō Yīngyǔ ma?" (do you speak English?), "Wǒ tīng bù dǒng" (I do not understand)

💳 Money & Payments

  • China uses the renminbi (yuan, ¥ or CNY). Roughly ¥7 is one US dollar, though rates vary (check current). Most daily payments use Alipay or WeChat Pay, and you can link a foreign Visa or Mastercard in both apps, using your passport, before you arrive.
  • Foreign cards are accepted at big hotels and some larger shops, but many restaurants, markets and taxis expect a mobile payment. Carry some cash as backup: shops are legally required to take it, and ATMs at major banks take foreign cards, with a withdrawal fee.
  • In-app limits apply to foreign cards and fees can be around 3 per cent on larger payments (check current terms), so test your setup before you travel. Do not exchange money on the street.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🚌

Skip the tour and take bus 916

A day tour to the Great Wall costs about $50-75 per person. Going by subway, bus 916 and a local bus or taxi, or by the Mu Bus shuttle, costs a fraction of that. Book your Mutianyu ticket yourself online (check current).

🍜

Eat where the locals queue

A bowl of noodles or a plate of dumplings costs ¥25-50, against ¥150+ in tourist restaurants. Look for busy local canteens with picture menus, and share dishes to try more for less.

🎟️

Book official tickets, not resellers

The Forbidden City is ¥60 and the Temple of Heaven ¥34 on the official channels, while resellers add a fee of several times that. Reserve directly with your passport and ignore anyone selling tickets near the gate.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

Jinshanling instead of Mutianyu

Jinshanling is steeper, wilder and much quieter on weekdays, with partly unrestored wall. It is a 2.5 to 3 hour trip each way, so go by tour or private car and take water and good shoes.

🏮

Hutongs beyond Nanluoguxiang

Wudaoying Hutong and the lanes around Shichahai and Fangjia Hutong are calmer, with small cafés, shops and courtyard houses. Walk early in the morning to see locals and not just tour groups.

🥋

Join the park mornings at 6 AM

Beijingers use parks for tai chi, calligraphy with water, singing and dancing from dawn. The Temple of Heaven park at 6 AM is free to watch and feels like a different place from midday.

✈️ Flights

  • Beijing Capital (PEK) is the older hub, about 25 km north-east of the centre, with the Capital Airport Express to Dongzhimen (¥25). Beijing Daxing (PKX) is the large airport to the south, with its own express to Caoqiao (about ¥35, check current).
  • Many international airlines use Capital, but check which airport your flight uses before you book your hotel and transfer. Daxing is much farther from the north of the city.
  • Compare fares including Shanghai, since you can combine Beijing with a high-speed train to Shanghai and fly home from there.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Beijing taxis are metered, and fares start at about ¥14 (check current). Hand the driver your destination written in Chinese, as few drivers speak English. Use the official taxi rank at the airport and ignore touts.
  • Didi, China's ride-hailing app, has an English interface and works with foreign cards or Alipay. It is usually cheaper than a street taxi and removes the language problem.
  • Rush hours (about 7 to 9 AM and 5 to 7 PM) make everything slow, so use the subway for longer central trips.

🚇 Public Transport & Tickets

  • The Beijing subway is cheap and covers most sights, with fares starting at about ¥3 and rising with distance (check current). It runs from about 5 AM to 11 PM, and every station has a bag X-ray and security check, so allow a few minutes.
  • Buy single-journey tickets at machines (cash is accepted) or scan a subway QR code through Alipay or WeChat Pay once your foreign card is linked.
  • Line 1 serves Tiananmen and Wangfujing, Line 2 the Lama Temple and Dongzhimen, Line 5 the Temple of Heaven, and Lines 6 and 8 the hutongs.
  • Buses are cheap but signs and announcements are mostly in Chinese, so use the subway where you can.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Dongcheng near Wangfujing or Dengshikou: central, walkable to the Forbidden City, with many hotels that accept foreign guests.
  • Hutong areas near Nanluoguxiang and Gulou: courtyard guesthouses and hostels with local character, and good evening food.
  • Qianmen: close to Tiananmen and the Temple of Heaven, with budget hotels, though the streets are touristy.
  • Near Dongzhimen or Sanyuanqiao: handy for the Airport Express and good for a late or early flight.
  • Hotels must register foreign guests with the police, so expect the desk to scan your passport. Confirm the property takes foreigners before booking, and keep the receipt.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Google, WhatsApp, Instagram and many other Western services are blocked in mainland China. Roaming plans and travel eSIMs usually route your data through your home network, so these services still work. Set one up before you land.
  • VPNs are not officially permitted for domestic use and many stop working without notice, so do not rely on them. Download offline maps and a translation app before you travel.
  • Hotel and café Wi-Fi often needs a local mobile number, so a roaming eSIM is easier. Set up Alipay or WeChat Pay with your foreign card and carry some cash.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Visa: many nationalities, including the UK, Canada, Australia and most EU countries, can currently enter visa-free for up to 30 days for tourism, but the policy is time-limited (announced to end on 31 December 2026, so check if it is extended). US citizens are not on that list. Check yours on the official NIA or embassy site.
  • The 240-hour visa-free transit applies if you fly onward to a third country, for about 57 nationalities including the US. An onward ticket is required, and the border officer decides on entry.
  • Arrival cards are optional online through the official NIA service, but check the current rule before you fly.
  • Book the Forbidden City, Tiananmen Square, the Temple of Heaven and the Lama Temple online ahead, because walk-up tickets are limited for foreigners. The Great Wall is best on a weekday outside holiday periods.
