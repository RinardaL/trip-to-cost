4 Days in Quebec City: Old Town, Château Frontenac & Montmorency Falls

The only walled city north of Mexico, a UNESCO-listed Old Town wrapped in 300-year-old ramparts, a castle-hotel silhouette that defines the St. Lawrence skyline, cobblestone lanes older than Canada itself, a waterfall taller than Niagara just outside the city, and a rural island of farms and cider houses a short drive away, four days across Québec's most historic city.

Ideal SeasonJun – Sep
Est. Budget$700-1,000
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival & check-in in Vieux-Québec

Québec City Jean Lesage International Airport sits about 20 minutes west of the Old Town, and unlike some European city pairs there's no direct rail link, so a taxi, rideshare, or hotel shuttle is the way in. Settle into a room inside the walls and get your first look at the ramparts before dinner.

🚕 ~20 min taxi/rideshare from YQB 💵 ~$35-40 CAD (~$26-30 USD) fare 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time
Afternoon

Château Frontenac & the Dufferin Terrace boardwalk

The Fairmont Le Château Frontenac, the turreted castle-hotel that has defined this skyline since 1893, is worth admiring even if you're not staying there; step inside the lobby, then walk the Dufferin Terrace boardwalk that runs along the cliff edge beside it for sweeping views over the St. Lawrence River and Lévis on the far shore.

🚶 Central, inside the Old Town walls 💵 Free to wander the terrace 🕐 Late afternoon, for golden-hour light on the river
Evening

Rue du Trésor & a first Québécois dinner

Rue du Trésor is a narrow lane just off Place d'Armes where local artists have hung their prints and watercolors for decades, a good five-minute wander before dinner. Follow it with a first taste of Québécois cooking, tourtière or a classic poutine, at one of the bistros tucked into the Upper Town's stone buildings.

🚶 On Rue Sainte-Anne, steps from Place d'Armes 💵 ~$25-35 CAD dinner 🕐 Any evening, lantern-lit after dark
Morning

The Fortifications & the Citadelle

Walk a stretch of the 4.6 km ring of walls that makes Old Quebec the only fortified city north of Mexico, then continue to the star-shaped Citadelle, a still-active Canadian Armed Forces base and the Governor General's official Quebec residence. Guided tours cover its history back to the 1750s, and in summer the Changing of the Guard draws a crowd.

🚶 ~10 min walk from the Upper Town 💵 ~$18-20 CAD guided Citadelle tour 🕐 Morning; Changing of the Guard runs late June to early September
Afternoon

Funicular down to Petit-Champlain & Place Royale

The Old Quebec Funicular (or the free, steep Casse-Cou "Breakneck" Stairs) drops you into the Lower Town and Quartier Petit-Champlain, a cobblestone shopping street dating to the 1600s and among the oldest commercial streets on the continent. A few steps away, Place Royale and the Notre-Dame-des-Victoires church mark the spot where Samuel de Champlain founded the city in 1608.

🚡 Funicular, ~1 min ride, or free stairs 💵 ~$4.25 CAD funicular fare 🕐 Early-to-mid afternoon
Evening

Poutine & a Lower Town dinner

Petit-Champlain's restaurants are a good spot to eat properly Québécois: poutine done right, with squeaky fresh cheese curds, alongside dishes like tourtière or maple-glazed pork. It's touristy by day but genuinely charming once the crowds thin after 7 PM.

🚶 Central within Petit-Champlain 💵 ~$30-40 CAD dinner 🕐 After 7 PM, once day-trippers clear out
Morning

The Plains of Abraham

This wide urban park was the site of the 1759 Battle of the Plains of Abraham, the 20-minute clash that decided the fall of New France to Britain and still shapes Québec's identity. Today it's a green, walkable expanse dotted with Martello towers, with the Musée national des beaux-arts du Québec anchoring one edge if you want an hour indoors.

🚶 ~10 min walk from the Old Town walls 💵 Free to walk the park 🕐 Morning, before the afternoon falls trip
Afternoon

Montmorency Falls

Just 15 minutes from downtown, Montmorency Falls drops 83 metres, roughly 30 metres taller than Niagara, into the St. Lawrence. A cable car and a suspension bridge put you right over the plunge, and via ferrata or zipline add-ons are bookable for a more active afternoon; in winter the spray freezes into a climbable ice cone.

🚌 RTC bus 800/802, or ~15 min by car 💵 ~$16 CAD cable car; park entry free 🕐 Early-to-mid afternoon
Evening

An easy evening back on Dufferin Terrace

After a full day outdoors, keep it simple: a sunset lap of Dufferin Terrace as the lights come up across the river, then a low-key dinner back inside the walls. It's a good night to skip the reservations.

🚌 ~15 min back into the Old Town 💵 Free to walk the terrace 🕐 Early evening, for sunset over the St. Lawrence
Morning

Crossing to Île d'Orléans

A 15-minute drive over the Pont de l'Île bridge trades the walled city for six small farming villages strung along a rural island in the St. Lawrence. It's the easiest genuine countryside day trip from any major North American old town, a patchwork of orchards, sugar shacks, and 17th- and 18th-century stone houses that have barely changed in outline.

🚗 ~15 min drive from Old Quebec 💵 Rental car or guided tour needed 🕐 Morning, to make time for several farm stops
Afternoon

Orchards, cideries & strawberry fields

Spend the afternoon working your way along Chemin Royal, the road that circles the island: a cidery tasting flight, maple products from a family sucrerie, and, in season, U-pick strawberry fields that locals drive out from the city for. Nearly every stop sells direct from the farm, so it's an easy way to bring a taste of Québec home.

🚗 Self-drive loop around Chemin Royal 💵 ~$10-15 CAD per tasting or basket 🕐 Early-to-mid afternoon
Evening

Transfer to Jean Lesage Airport for departure

Return your rental car (or catch a pre-booked taxi) and head to the airport, budgeting the same 20 minutes from the Old Town as your arrival, plus extra time to drop off the car.

🚕 ~20 min taxi/rideshare to YQB 💵 ~$35-40 CAD fare 🕐 Leave at least 2.5 hrs before a domestic flight, 3 hrs for international

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Unlike much of Europe, tipping is expected in Canada: 15-20% at sit-down restaurants is standard, not optional.
  • Card machines are everywhere and prompt for a tip percentage automatically when you pay.
  • A few dollars is appropriate for a farm tasting guide or a Citadelle tour, though it isn't strictly required.

👗 Dress Code & Footwear

  • Old Quebec is genuinely hilly: sturdy, comfortable shoes matter every day, on cobblestones, the funicular alternative stairs, and the ramparts.
  • Layer regardless of season; the St. Lawrence brings sudden wind and temperature swings even in July.
  • Notre-Dame-des-Victoires and other active churches expect modest, covered dress, though there's no strict enforcement.

🚶 Getting Around Quebec City

  • The Old Town is compact enough to walk end to end in under 30 minutes; you won't need transit inside the walls.
  • RTC city buses cover Montmorency Falls and the wider city for around $4 CAD a ride.
  • Île d'Orléans has no public transit, so a rental car, taxi day-rate, or guided tour is the only way to see the island.

❄️ Two Very Different Seasons, Book Accordingly

  • Summer (June-September) is when the Old Town is at its best for walking: outdoor cafes, warm evenings on Dufferin Terrace, and every farm on Île d'Orléans open, with September and October adding fall foliage.
  • Early-to-mid February brings the Quebec Winter Carnival, one of the world's largest winter festivals, plus the seasonal Hôtel de Glace ice hotel nearby; it's a genuinely bucket-list-worthy alternative if you don't mind the cold, but hotels sell out and prices spike months ahead.
  • This itinerary is built around the warm-weather version of the trip; if you're chasing the Carnival instead, expect Île d'Orléans farm stops to be replaced with sugar-shack visits and the outdoor terrace evenings to move indoors.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in French

  • Bonjour: hello (the standard Quebec greeting, day or night)
  • Merci: thank you
  • L'addition, s'il vous plaît: the bill, please

💳 Money & Payments

  • The Canadian dollar (CAD) is the currency; contactless and tap cards are accepted almost everywhere, including small Île d'Orléans farm stands.
  • Withdraw CAD from bank-branded ATMs rather than standalone tourist machines, which carry steep fees.
  • Sales tax (about 15% combined) is added at the register, not included in listed prices, so budget a bit above the sticker total.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🪜

Take the free Casse-Cou stairs instead of the funicular

The "Breakneck Stairs" run parallel to the paid funicular between Upper and Lower Town and cost nothing, a five-minute walk that saves a few dollars each way.

🚗

Rent a car for one day only

The Old Town needs no vehicle at all, so book a rental car just for the Île d'Orléans day rather than for the whole trip, and use taxis or your own feet the rest of the time.

🍽️

Eat your big Québécois meal at lunch

Many Old Town restaurants run a table d'hôte lunch menu at a noticeably lower price than the identical dinner plates, so make lunch the splurge and dinner the light meal.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

Petit-Champlain before 9 AM

Cruise-ship passengers and day-trippers fill the street by late morning; walking it at opening time gets you the same cobblestones with almost nobody in frame.

🏰

The Martello towers on the Plains

Four squat 19th-century defense towers sit scattered across the Plains of Abraham park, largely ignored by visitors headed straight for the museum or the battlefield markers.

🚶

Walk the ramparts near Porte Saint-Jean

The stretch of fortification wall near this gate gets a fraction of the foot traffic of Dufferin Terrace, with the same views over the rooftops of the Upper Town.

✈️ Flights

  • Québec City Jean Lesage International Airport (YQB) is small and about 20 minutes from the Old Town, but direct international routes are limited.
  • Connecting through Montréal-Trudeau (YUL) and continuing by a short flight, train, or roughly 3-hour drive is often cheaper than flying direct into YQB from outside North America.
  • Book Montmorency Falls activity add-ons and any Winter Carnival-dates accommodation well before you land.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Uber operates in Quebec City alongside licensed taxis, both reliable for the airport transfer and evenings out.
  • Inside the walls, you'll rarely need either; the Old Town is walkable end to end.
  • For Montmorency Falls, RTC buses 800 and 802 are a cheaper alternative to a taxi if you're not driving.

🚌 Day-Trip Transport

  • Montmorency Falls: RTC public buses reach the park directly, no car needed.
  • Île d'Orléans: no public transit crosses the bridge, so a rental car, private driver, or guided tour is required for Day 4.
  • Guided Île d'Orléans tours are a good option if you'd rather not navigate farm-stop logistics yourself.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Upper Town (Vieux-Québec intra-muros): puts Château Frontenac, Dufferin Terrace, and Day 1's evening within a five-minute walk.
  • Petit-Champlain / Lower Town: closer to Day 2's afternoon, with a livelier, slightly less formal feel.
  • Grande Allée or outside the walls: a short walk or bus ride from the Old Town, generally at a lower nightly rate.

🎫 Guided Tours

  • The Citadelle can only be visited by guided tour, run on a fixed schedule through the day.
  • Free or low-cost walking tours of the Old Town run daily in summer and are a good low-cost orientation on Day 1.
  • Île d'Orléans cidery and farm tours bundle several stops with tastings included, useful if you don't want to plan the route yourself.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Standard Canadian entry rules apply; confirm visa or eTA requirements for your passport before booking flights.
  • Remember the Canadian dollar (CAD), not the US dollar, is the local currency, and combined sales tax adds about 15% at checkout.
  • Travel insurance is a smart add-on if you're booking the Montmorency Falls via ferrata or zipline, or traveling during the high-demand Winter Carnival dates.
