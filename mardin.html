4 Days in Mardin: Stone Old Town, Monasteries & the Plain

Honey-coloured stone lanes above the Mesopotamian plain, madrasas and Syriac monasteries, and a day out to Dara and Midyat: four quiet, cheap winter days in Mardin.

Ideal SeasonNov – Mar
Est. Budget$350-500
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Mardin Airport & the ride up to the old town

Mardin Airport is about 20 km from the old town, with flights from Istanbul in roughly two hours. The Havaş shuttle takes about 30 minutes to Eski Mardin for around 220 TL; a taxi is listed at about 1,250 to 1,400 TL, so agree the price first. Ask your hotel about pickup, as lanes are steep.

🚌 ~30 min by Havaş shuttle or taxi, longer by municipal bus via the new town 💵 ~220 TL Havaş shuttle (about €4), ~22 TL municipal bus, ~1,250-1,400 TL taxi (about €23-25), check current 🕐 Plan around your landing time, then drop your bags
Afternoon

Cumhuriyet Caddesi & the old town lanes

Cumhuriyet Caddesi is the old town's main street, running about 2 km along the hillside past stone mansions and tea houses, with the Ulu Camii on it. Side lanes drop in steps to the bazaar below, so wander freely. The sun sets around 17:00 in winter, so start early, and wear shoes with grip.

🚶 On foot, with a steep climb up and down the lanes 💵 Free to walk, tea at a café costs little (check current) 🕐 Start by 14:00, because the sun sets around 17:00 in winter
Evening

Terrace dinner & the plain at dusk

Old-town terraces face south over the Mesopotamian plain, but in winter many move indoors, so ask about seating first. Try kaburga dolması, lamb ribs stuffed with rice, and sembusek, a fried pastry with minced meat. Ask the rib dish's price before ordering, and look one lane back from the main street for lower prices.

🚶 Short walk from your hotel, with steps 💵 ~€10-15 for dinner (check current) 🕐 Early evening, then an early night
Morning

Zinciriye & Kasımiye Medreses

Zinciriye (Sultan Isa Medresesi) dates from 1385, with a madrasa, mosque and mausoleum around two courtyards. Kasımiye, on the old town's western edge, has a large two-storey courtyard. Winter hours are listed as 08:30 to 17:00 and 17:30 respectively, with fees reported at 30 to 200 TL. They are about 2 km apart.

🚶 Short climb to Zinciriye, then a ~2 km walk or short taxi to Kasımiye 💵 Roughly 50-100 TL each, but sources vary widely (check current) 🕐 Soon after opening, when the courtyards are quiet
Afternoon

Deyrulzafaran (Saffron) Monastery

Deyrulzafaran, the Saffron Monastery, lies about 4 km east of the old town. The ticket includes a guided tour on the hour and is reported at 150 to 200 TL (check current). Hours are listed as 09:00 to 17:00, with a possible lunch break from 12:00 to 13:00. Go after lunch; a taxi takes 10 to 15 minutes.

🚕 ~10-15 min by taxi from the centre 💵 ~150-200 TL entry with guided tour, about €3-4 (check current), plus taxi 🕐 Early afternoon, and aim to arrive by 15:30
Evening

Ulu Camii & dinner

The Ulu Camii, the Great Mosque, stands on Cumhuriyet Caddesi, and its tall carved minaret is the best landmark for finding your way. Enter outside prayer times, remove your shoes and cover shoulders and knees. For dinner, try Mardin kebab and ikbebet, stuffed meatballs in sauce, then menengiç coffee, a caffeine-free drink made from terebinth berries.

🚶 On foot along Cumhuriyet Caddesi 💵 Mosque free, ~€10-15 for dinner (check current) 🕐 Late afternoon light, dinner from 19:00
Morning

Dara, the ancient frontier city

Dara lies about 30 km south-east of Mardin, in the village of Oğuz. This early fortress city on the Persian frontier has rock-cut tombs, cisterns and stretches of wall. Entry is listed as free, 08:00 to 17:00, closed Mondays. No public transport serves it, so go by taxi or tour and agree the price first.

🚕 ~45 min by taxi or tour vehicle from Mardin 💵 Entry free per the official listing, taxi or tour share extra 🕐 Leave Mardin by 08:30, and avoid Mondays when the site is closed
Afternoon

Lunch in Midyat & Mor Gabriel Monastery

Many tours stop for lunch in Midyat, then go on to Mor Gabriel, about 23 km south-east on the Tur Abdin plateau. Visits are listed 09:00 to 11:30 and 13:00 to 17:00, though one guide gives a 16:30 close, so arrive by 14:30. Entry is about 100 to 150 TL. Go by taxi or tour.

🚕 ~1-1.5 hr from Dara to Midyat, then ~25-30 min to the monastery 💵 ~150 TL entry (March 2026 guide, check current), lunch ~€6-10 🕐 Arrive by 14:30, afternoon visits close around 16:30-17:00
Evening

Midyat old town & Syriac wine

Midyat's old town has honey-coloured stone houses with carved doorways, and workshops where you can watch telkari, filigree silver, being made. A few shops sell Syriac wine, and tasting before you buy is normal. Minibuses to Mardin take about 1 to 1.5 hours and thin out late afternoon, so check the last departure.

🚐 ~1-1.5 hr by minibus or taxi back to Mardin (about 68 km) 💵 ~100 TL minibus (2024 report), coaches from ~250 TL (check current), wine tasting free or a few euros 🕐 Leave Midyat by 17:30 in winter, and check the last minibus
Morning

The Mardin Museum

The Mardin Museum, in an arcaded building put up in 1895 as a Syriac Catholic patriarchate, holds more than 45,000 objects. Winter hours are listed as 08:00 to 17:00, closed on Mondays. Foreign visitors pay 7 euros, charged in lira. Allow 1.5 to 2 hours, and skip it if your flight leaves before midday.

🚶 ~10-15 min on foot from most old-town hotels 💵 7 euros for foreign visitors, charged in lira (check current) 🕐 Opening time, closed Mondays
Afternoon

Bazaar, soap, almonds & Syriac wine

The covered streets below Cumhuriyet Caddesi sell copper, kilims, silver and local food. The Kayseriye Çarşısı is listed as open daily from 09:00 to 23:00, while the coppersmiths' market closes on Sundays and at midday prayer. Smell a few hand-cut soaps before choosing, and pack Syriac wine in checked luggage.

🚶 On foot, with steps and slopes 💵 Soap and sweets a few euros each, wine priced by bottle (check current) 🕐 Mid-afternoon, before shops start to close
Evening

Transfer to Mardin Airport

Leave the old town about 2.5 hours before your flight; the airport is about 20 km away. The Havaş shuttle takes about 30 minutes and costs around 220 TL but runs only a few times a day, so ask your hotel which departure fits. Winter fog or snow can delay flights, so avoid tight connections in Istanbul.

🚌 ~30 min by Havaş shuttle or taxi 💵 ~220 TL Havaş, ~22 TL municipal bus, ~1,250-1,400 TL taxi (check current) 🕐 Leave the old town at least 2.5 hours before your flight

💶 Tipping, Tea & Payments

  • Tipping is appreciated but not fixed. In restaurants, about 10% is usual if no service charge is on the bill, and in taxis you can simply round up. Leave a small cash tip for guesthouse staff who carry your bags up the steps.
  • Offers of tea are hospitality, not a sales tactic. Accept one if you have time, and a polite "teşekkürler" is enough if you do not want a second.
  • Ask the price before you order a dish sold by weight or portion, such as kaburga dolması, and before you get in a taxi without a meter.

🍷 Food, Drink & Ramadan

  • Try kaburga dolması (stuffed lamb ribs), sembusek (fried meat pastries), ikbebet (stuffed meatballs) and Mardin kebab, and finish with menengiç coffee made from terebinth berries. Almond sweets are a good edible gift.
  • Mardin is mostly Muslim, with a Syriac Christian community that makes wine. Syriac wine is sold in old-town and Midyat wine shops, and some restaurants serve alcohol, but many do not. Do not drink in the street or near mosques.
  • During Ramadan, which in 2027 runs roughly from 8 February to 9 March (check current), many locals fast from dawn to sunset. Some daytime restaurants close, and it is polite not to eat or drink in public in front of people who are fasting. The evening meal (iftar) is the best time to see the town come alive.
  • Lunch is usually 12:00 to 14:00 and dinner from about 19:00 to 20:00. Simple lokantas serve daily set dishes that cost far less than terrace restaurants.

⛪ Mosques, Churches & Monasteries

  • In mosques, remove your shoes, cover shoulders and knees, and women should carry a scarf for the head. Avoid visiting during prayer times, which shift through the year, and never walk in front of someone praying.
  • Deyrulzafaran and Mor Gabriel are active monasteries with set visiting times, and tours may be in Turkish only. Dress modestly, speak quietly, and ask before taking photos inside the chapels.
  • Do not enter a church or monastery during a service unless you are invited, and do not touch icons or manuscripts. Donations are welcome in boxes at the entrance.

⚠️ Safety, Scams & Etiquette Warnings

  • Mardin is in south-eastern Turkey, about 60 km by road from Nusaybin on the Syrian border. Check your government's current travel advice for the province and the border area before booking, and again before you travel, since advice can change.
  • Expect police or military checkpoints on roads in the south-east. Carry your passport, be polite, and do not photograph soldiers, police posts or checkpoints.
  • Unofficial guides may offer to lead you to a shop or a monastery, and they often take a commission. Agree a price in advance for any guide or taxi, or use the official ticket desks.
  • Wet or icy stone steps can be dangerous on the old-town slopes. Wear shoes with grip, carry a light torch after dark, and keep a hand free for the rail.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Turkish

  • "Merhaba" (hello), "Günaydın" (good morning), "İyi akşamlar" (good evening), "Hoşça kalın" (goodbye)
  • "Lütfen" (please), "Teşekkür ederim" (thank you), "Rica ederim" (you're welcome), "Affedersiniz" (excuse me)
  • "Hesap, lütfen" (the bill, please), "Bu ne?" (what is this?), "Ne kadar?" (how much?), "Çok pahalı" (too expensive)
  • "İngilizce biliyor musunuz?" (do you speak English?), "... nerede?" (where is...?), "Taksimetre, lütfen" (meter, please)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Turkey uses the Turkish lira (TRY). Prices in lira change fast, so the figures on this page are rough and marked "check current". Cards and contactless work in most hotels, restaurants and larger shops.
  • Keep cash for minibuses, small bazaar shops, tea houses and some site tickets. Use bank ATMs in the centre and avoid street money changers.
  • If a card terminal or ATM offers to charge you in your home currency, choose lira. Haggle politely for copper, rugs and silver, but not in food shops, where prices are usually fixed.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🏨

Travel in winter for lower room rates

November to March is low season, and old-town guesthouses and stone hotels often drop rates and have rooms free at short notice. Listing sites show basic rooms from about $40 and restored stone hotels from about $95 to $150 a double, so message two or three places directly, ask for the winter price, and check that heating works.

🚐

Share a car or ride the minibus on day three

A private car for Dara, Midyat and Mor Gabriel is the biggest single cost, so split it between up to four people or join a small group tour. The Mardin to Midyat minibus is much cheaper, but it will not reach Dara or the monastery.

🍲

Eat at a lokanta, not on the terrace

Simple local restaurants one street back serve soup, rice, kebab and stews at a fraction of the price of terrace restaurants with a view. Have your main meal at lunch and a light dinner, and ask for the daily dishes.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

See the plain at dawn from a terrace

The Mesopotamian plain looks best in the first hour after sunrise, when winter haze is thin and the stone glows. Ask your hotel whether the roof terrace is open, and bring a warm layer and a flask of tea.

🍯

Look for small family shops in the side lanes

The back lanes above Cumhuriyet Caddesi hold small shops selling almond sweets, soap, copper and dried fruit that do not appear on the main street. Ask to taste before you buy.

🏞️

Add Savur or Hasankeyf with a fifth day

Savur, a stone town in a river valley, and Hasankeyf, the ancient cliff town on the Tigris, are each a long day trip. They do not fit into this plan, so add a day, ideally with a rented car and driver.

✈️ Flights

  • Mardin Airport (MQM) is the nearest airport, about 20 km from the old town. Turkish Airlines, AJet and Pegasus fly from Istanbul in roughly two hours, and AJet flies from Ankara. Check the winter timetable, which can be thinner. The Havaş shuttle to Eski Mardin takes about 30 minutes and costs around 220 TL.
  • Diyarbakır (DIY), about 95 km north-west, has more flights, and intercity buses to Mardin take about 1.5 hours and are listed from around 400 TL, so compare fares if Mardin is expensive. Batman is also an option, but check the road distances.
  • From abroad, you will usually connect through Istanbul or Ankara. Leave several hours between flights in winter, because fog and snow can delay planes.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Taxis wait outside the airport and run in the old town, and most should use a meter (taksimetre). Ask for it, or agree the fare before you get in. One 2026 airport fare table lists about 1,250 to 1,400 TL to Eski Mardin, so the Havaş shuttle is far cheaper.
  • Taxi apps may have limited coverage in Mardin, so rely on ranks and your hotel to call a driver. Check the apps for current cover.
  • For Dara, Midyat and Mor Gabriel, taxi drivers offer a fixed day price. Ask two or three, and agree the route, the stops and the waiting time before you go.

🚌 Public Transport & Tickets

  • Local minibuses link the old town with the new town, with lines M6, M7A and M8 serving Yenişehir and M12 running from the bus station to the old town. The old-town lanes themselves are for walking.
  • Minibuses to Midyat, Nusaybin, Kızıltepe and other towns leave from the Mardin otogar, and the Midyat run takes about 1 to 1.5 hours. A 2024 traveller reported about 100 TL for Midyat, and coach tickets are listed from about 250 TL in 2026, so check the fare and the last departure.
  • Intercity buses link Mardin with Diyarbakır, Şanlıurfa and other cities, and many companies offer a free shuttle to the bus station.
  • Buses and minibuses are cash-based in many places, so carry small notes.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Old town, near Cumhuriyet Caddesi: restored stone houses and small hotels, the best atmosphere and the best base, but expect steps and narrow lanes with luggage.
  • Old town side lanes: a little cheaper and quieter, still walkable to the sights, but check that heating works in winter.
  • Yeni Mardin (new town): modern hotels, easier parking and flat streets, with cheaper rooms, but you need a taxi or bus to reach the old town.
  • Midyat: an option for one night if you want to spend more time on the plateau, with stone guesthouses in the old town.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Turkey is not in the EU, so check your roaming plan or buy an eSIM. Registering a physical SIM bought locally needs your passport.
  • Wi-Fi is common in hotels and cafés, but signal can drop in the stone lanes and on the plain.
  • Cards work at most hotels and restaurants, but keep lira in cash for minibuses, tea and small shops. Use bank ATMs and decline conversion to your home currency.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • The $350-500 budget per person assumes two people sharing a room and covers about three nights in a mid-range stone guesthouse or small hotel (roughly $95-150 a double), meals, local transport, a shared car for the Dara and Midyat day and entry fees, but not flights. Basic guesthouses from about $40 a room bring it nearer $300, and boutique stone hotels push it higher.
  • Winter weather: January highs average about 7°C and lows about 1°C, with rain and snow for a week or two between December and March. Summers are very hot, with July highs around 36°C. Winter opening hours are shorter, and days end at around 17:00.
  • Check your passport and visa rules for Turkey before booking. If you need an e-visa, apply only through the official government site.
  • You do not need a car for the old town. For Dara, Midyat and Mor Gabriel, a driver is easier than renting, and roads can be icy in winter.

How much does a 4-day trip to Mardin cost?

About $350-500 per person for 4 days, roughly $90-125 a day, excluding international flights. The estimate covers lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and real costs vary with the season and your spending habits.
