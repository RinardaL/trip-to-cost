4 Days in Curaçao: Willemstad, West Coast Beaches & Snorkelling

Pastel Willemstad, the turquoise coves of the west coast, a hike up Mount Christoffel and a wreck snorkel: four warm, mostly dry days in the Dutch Caribbean, outside the hurricane belt.

Ideal SeasonYear-round (Feb – Jun driest)
Est. Budget$450-700
Duration4 Days
PaceRelaxed
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Curaçao International Airport (CUR)

Curaçao International (CUR) is about 12 km and 15 to 20 minutes from Willemstad. Taxis use set fares by zone, roughly US$30 to 40 to the centre (check current), mostly cash. Konvoi buses (routes 4B, 2A, 2C) cost about Cg 2.50 to 3, but waits can be long. Complete the free digital immigration card online before flying.

🚕 ~15-20 min by taxi, ~40 min by konvoi bus 💵 ~US$30-40 taxi, ~Cg 2.50-3 bus (check current) 🕐 Plan around your landing time, then drop your bags
Afternoon

Punda, the Handelskade & the floating market

Punda is the older half of Willemstad, and with Otrobanda it forms a UNESCO World Heritage site. Walk the Handelskade for the pastel Dutch gables, then browse the floating market, where boats sell fish and fruit. It is liveliest in the morning. Lunch at nearby local stalls costs about US$8 to 12 (check current).

🚶 Everything within ~10 min on foot 💵 Free to wander, ~US$8-12 for a local lunch 🕐 Floating market best before early afternoon
Evening

Queen Emma Bridge, Otrobanda & Rif Fort

The Queen Emma Bridge is a floating pontoon walkway that swings open for ships, with a free ferry running meanwhile. Cross to Otrobanda and walk the old Rif Fort, now full of restaurants and shops, with a view back to the lit Punda waterfront. Eat in the side streets for lower prices, about US$15 to 25.

🚶 ~5 min across the bridge, ~5 more to Rif Fort 💵 Bridge free, ~US$15-25 for dinner 🕐 Dusk for the lights, dinner from about 7 PM
Morning

Pick up the car & Grote Knip (Kenepa Grandi)

Collect a small rental car (about US$30 to 60 a day, booked ahead) and drive on the right to Grote Knip, about an hour west. Entry and parking are generally free, though a small donation is sometimes asked (check). Arrive before 10 AM for parking. Cas Abao nearby charges about US$6 to 7 per car (check current).

🚗 ~1 hour by car from Willemstad 💵 Car ~US$30-60/day, beach usually free (check current) 🕐 Pick up the car by about 8:30, beach before 10 AM
Afternoon

Playa Lagun & snorkelling with turtles

Playa Lagun is a small sheltered cove about 15 minutes north of Grote Knip, and the calm water makes an easy snorkel. Green turtles often feed here, most reliably early in the day, but sightings are never guaranteed. Bring a mask, or hire gear for roughly US$10 (check current). Keep your distance from the turtles.

🚗 ~15 min drive from Grote Knip 💵 Gear hire ~US$10, parking and sunbeds extra (check current) 🕐 Earlier is better for turtles and for space
Evening

Westpunt, Playa Forti & sunset

Westpunt is the quiet northwest tip, and Playa Forti has the cliff platform used for jumping into deep water. Jump only if you swim well and the swell is low: there are no lifeguards. Stay for sunset around 6 PM and a snack-bar dinner, about US$15 to 25. Drive back early, as rural roads are poorly lit.

🚗 ~10 min from Lagun, then ~1 hour back to town 💵 ~US$15-25 for dinner, plus fuel 🕐 Sunset around 6 PM, leave by about 7:30
Morning

Christoffel National Park & Mount Christoffel

Christoffel Park is about an hour from Willemstad by car. It opens at 7:30 AM (6:00 on Sundays) and closes at 4 PM. Mount Christoffel, 375 m, is the island's highest point, a steep rocky climb of about two hours return. Start at opening with plenty of water. Entry is roughly US$10 to 15 (check current).

🚗 ~1 hour by car from Willemstad 💵 Entry ~US$10-15 (check current) 🕐 Be on the trail by about 8 AM, before the heat
Afternoon

Landhuis Chobolobo, home of Curaçao liqueur

Landhuis Chobolobo is the old country house where Curaçao liqueur is made, in Salinja about 10 minutes from Punda by car. Guided tours with a tasting cost roughly US$15 to 20 (check current), and the bitter laraha orange gives the drink its flavour. Check opening days and tour times before you go.

🚗 ~10 min drive from Punda 💵 Tour ~US$15-20 with a tasting (check current) 🕐 Check tour days and times first
Evening

Pietermaai, a restored colonial district

Pietermaai is a few minutes east of Punda on foot, a district of restored colonial houses with small restaurants and bars. Try keshi yena, baked cheese with a spiced filling, or stobá, a slow-cooked stew. A main course costs about US$15 to 25. Streets are quiet late at night, so take a taxi back.

🚶 ~10 min on foot from Punda 💵 ~US$15-25 for dinner 🕐 Dinner from about 7 PM
Morning

Tugboat Beach, a shallow wreck

Tugboat Beach is a small cove near Caracas Bay, about 15 minutes by car from Willemstad. The wreck lies in only around 5 m of water, a short swim from shore, so it suits beginners with a mask and snorkel. Go early before it fills up. Wear water shoes for the rocky entry, and do not touch the coral.

🚗 ~15 min from Willemstad 💵 Free to swim (check current for any parking charge) 🕐 Early morning, calmer water and fewer people
Afternoon

Jan Thiel Beach or Mambo Beach

Jan Thiel is a calm sandy beach with beach bars, a short drive east of town. Entry is about US$3 per person until 5 PM, with sunbeds extra (check current). Mambo Beach, beside the Seaquarium, is a similar paid beach. Take valuables with you and leave nothing in the car.

🚗 ~15-20 min from the centre 💵 Entry ~US$3-3.50, sunbed ~US$4 extra (check current) 🕐 Late morning to mid-afternoon
Evening

Return the car & fly home

Refuel the car and return it at the airport, where the rental desks are, photographing it before handover. Check in about three hours before an international flight, and check whether your ticket includes the departure tax. Without a car, a taxi to CUR costs about US$30 to 40 (check current), or take a konvoi bus.

🚗 ~15-20 min from Willemstad to CUR 💵 ~US$30-40 taxi (check current), car return free 🕐 Arrive at the airport about 3 hours before departure

💶 Tipping & Payments

  • Many restaurants add a service charge of about 10 to 12 percent. Check the bill first. If it is not included, 10 percent is a normal tip for good service.
  • Taxi drivers do not expect a large tip, so round the fare up. Taxis are mostly cash only, so carry small US dollar or guilder notes.
  • Cards (Visa and Mastercard) work in most hotels, restaurants and shops. Some small beach bars and snack stalls take cash only, so keep notes handy.

🍲 Local Food & Drink

  • Keshi yena is baked cheese with a spiced chicken, beef or vegetable filling. Stobá is a slow-cooked stew, and pastechi are fried pastries filled with cheese, ham or meat, a cheap snack.
  • Fresh fish, funchi (a cornmeal side) and Dutch influences such as bitterballen all show up on menus. Local places and food stalls cost much less than beachfront restaurants.
  • Try the local Curaçao liqueur in a cocktail, and tap water on the island is safe to drink, so you do not need to buy bottled water.

🌴 Language, Beaches & Etiquette

  • Dutch, Papiamentu and English are official or widely spoken, and Spanish is common too. Most hotels, restaurants and shops use English with visitors.
  • Greet people with a friendly "bon dia" in shops and taxis, as it is polite. Beach clothing belongs on the beach, so cover up in town and in restaurants.
  • Carnival runs from January to March, with big parades, extra crowds and higher prices in some weeks. Book ahead if you travel then.

⚠️ Safety, Sun & Sea Warnings

  • Car break-ins at beaches and remote parking spots are the most common crime against visitors. Leave nothing in the car, not even in the boot, and use attended car parks where you can.
  • The sun is very strong at 12 degrees north: use SPF 50, a rash vest and a hat. Wear water shoes on rocky entries, because sea urchins sit on the rocks and reefs.
  • The north and east coasts have rough water and strong currents, so swim only at sheltered west and south coast beaches, and never jump from cliffs alone.
  • Official advice for your country is worth reading before you go. The US State Department rates Curaçao Level 1 (normal precautions), and the UK and Canadian governments publish travel advice too.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Papiamentu

  • "Bon dia" (good morning), "Bon tardi" (good afternoon), "Bon nochi" (good evening)
  • "Danki" (thank you), "Por fabor" (please), "Di nada" (you're welcome)
  • "Kon ta bai?" (how are you?), "Mi ta bon" (I'm fine), "Te aworo" (see you later)
  • "Kuantu e ta kosta?" (how much is it?), "Un biér, por fabor" (a beer, please), "Mi no ta komprondé" (I don't understand)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Curaçao uses the Caribbean guilder (Cg, code XCG), which replaced the Netherlands Antillean guilder on 31 March 2025 at 1 to 1. It is pegged at about Cg 1.79 to the US dollar.
  • US dollars are widely accepted, but you usually get change in guilders at a slightly worse rate (around 1.75 to 1.82). Paying in guilders or by card is often cheaper.
  • ATMs (look for the Bankomatiko sign) accept foreign cards and give US dollars or guilders. If a terminal offers to charge in your home currency, decline and pay in local currency.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🚰

Drink the tap water

Curaçao tap water is safe to drink, so a refillable bottle saves a few dollars a day. Fill it at your hotel or apartment before heading to the beaches, where bottled water costs more.

🥟

Eat where locals eat

Snack stalls and local restaurants selling pastechi, stobá or keshi yena cost much less than beachfront or Rif Fort places. A supermarket picnic for the beach is cheaper again.

🚗

Rent the car only for the days you need it

Willemstad is walkable and the konvoi buses cover town, so rent a small car for the west coast and Christoffel Park days only. Book ahead online and compare the airport price with an in-town office.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

Beat the crowds at the west coast beaches

Grote Knip and Cas Abao are quietest before 10 AM, before the tour buses arrive. Kleine Knip, the smaller beach beside Grote Knip, is a calmer alternative.

🌊

See the wild north coast from the land

Shete Boka National Park shows waves crashing into rock caves along the north coast, which is too rough for swimming. Stay on the paths and well back from the edge.

🤿

Add a boat dive to Mushroom Forest

Divers can reach the mushroom-shaped coral formations of Mushroom Forest by a short boat trip from the Lagun area. Book with a dive operator, and check prices before you commit.

✈️ Flights

  • Curaçao International (CUR) is the island's main airport, about 12 km from Willemstad, with direct flights from Amsterdam and several US cities. Check which airlines serve your home airport.
  • December to April is high season, with the highest fares and room rates, and Carnival adds pressure from January to March. Book early or travel in shoulder months.
  • Compare fares into nearby Aruba or Bonaire only if your route and bags allow it, since an extra short flight adds cost and time.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Taxis are not metered and use fixed fares by zone, so agree the price before you set off. Many drivers take cash only.
  • Pre-booked airport transfers cost roughly €30 to 50 depending on the area, with higher fares at night (check current).
  • Ride-hailing coverage is limited, so ask your hotel to call a taxi, especially late at night.

🚌 Public Transport & Tickets

  • The large konvoi buses run fixed routes from the Punda and Otrobanda terminals, and smaller minibuses ("bus") run more like shared taxis. Fares are about Cg 2.50 to 3 (check current).
  • Buses reach Westpunt and the airport, but they are infrequent, especially on Sundays and holidays, and they do not suit beach-hopping, where a car is easier.
  • Pay the driver on boarding, carry small change, and ask the driver or other passengers to confirm your stop.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Pietermaai: restored colonial houses, restaurants and bars, walkable to Punda. Good for a first stay.
  • Punda and Otrobanda: central for the UNESCO area and Rif Fort, and often the best choice for small guesthouses and apartments.
  • Jan Thiel and Mambo: resort-style beach areas with a car needed for sightseeing, and usually pricier.
  • Westpunt: quiet villas and small hotels near the best beaches, but far from the city and nightlife.
  • Self-catering apartments with a kitchen save on food costs, which are high on the island.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Curaçao is outside the EU, so European roaming may not apply. Buy a local SIM or an eSIM, or rely on Wi-Fi in hotels and cafés.
  • Cards work in most places, but carry cash for taxis, snack stalls and some beach entry fees.
  • Use ATMs at banks, and decline any offer to convert to your home currency.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Citizens of the EU, US and UK can enter visa-free for short stays, but rules vary by nationality, so check yours. Complete the free digital immigration card (DI card) on the official site before you fly.
  • Your passport must be valid for your whole stay, and you may be asked for a return ticket, proof of accommodation and funds.
  • Drive on the right. Rental cars cost about US$30 to 60 a day, and taking full insurance and photographing the car before you leave the desk avoids disputes.
  • Curaçao lies outside the main Atlantic hurricane belt and is rarely hit, but the season runs from June to November, so winter trips are not affected.

How much does a 4-day trip to Curaçao cost?

About $450-700 per person for 4 days, roughly $115-175 a day, excluding international flights. The estimate covers lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and real costs vary with the season and your spending habits.
