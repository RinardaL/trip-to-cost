4 Days in Borovets: Skiing, Rila Monastery & Sofia

A pine-forest ski resort in the Rila Mountains with lift pass prices you can plan around, a day at Rila Monastery, and Sofia on the way out: four winter days, 70 km from the airport.

Ideal SeasonDec – Mar
Est. Budget$480-700
Duration4 Days
PaceActive
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Sofia Airport & the transfer to Borovets

Sofia Airport is about 70 km from Borovets, roughly 1 hour 20 to 1 hour 30 by road and longer in snow. A shared shuttle usually costs about €19 to €38 per person, a private car typically €65 to €80 for up to four. Give the company your flight number so they can track delays.

🚐 ~1 hr 20 to 1 hr 30 by road, about 70 km 💵 Shared ~€19-38 per person, private car ~€65-80 (quotes vary by operator) 🕐 Book a transfer that matches your landing time
Afternoon

Checking in & the resort centre

Borovets sits at about 1,350 m, and the compact base area is easy to walk. After check-in, head to the central square for the lift stations, hire shops and ski school. Sort out tomorrow today: choose a hire shop, buy a lift pass (a reloadable card was 5% cheaper in 2025/26) and check lift status.

🚶 Short walks around the base area 💵 Free to look around, hire and pass costs follow tomorrow 🕐 Mid to late afternoon, before the shops close
Evening

A mehana dinner & an early night

A mehana is a traditional Bulgarian tavern. Start with shopska salad, then try kavarma, a slow-cooked pork or chicken stew, or grilled kebapche. Base-area prices are higher than in Samokov or Sofia, so check the menu first. Keep the evening short, as tomorrow means walking uphill with skis.

🚶 Walk to dinner from your hotel 💵 ~€15-25 for dinner with a drink at the base (check current) 🕐 Dinner from 7 PM, in bed by 10 PM
Morning

Hire, a lesson & the beginner slope

Arrive early at the hire shop. Skis, poles and boots cost about €20 a day at the resort's rental, with a helmet €5 extra. Beginners should book a lesson: group classes run only on Sundays at about €54 for four hours, or a private lesson is €86 for 2 hours, shared by up to four.

🚶 Hire shops and the beginner area are a short walk from most hotels 💵 Hire ~€20 (helmet €5), morning pass ~€43, private lesson €86 for 2 hrs (up to 4 people) 🕐 Lifts from 8:30, queue at the hire shop before it opens
Afternoon

The Yastrebets cabin lift & the Markudjik area

The Yastrebets cabin lift is the gondola from the base area, from 1,340 m to 2,369 m, with Markudjik above at about 2,550 m. Intermediates can ski the open runs; beginners can ride the two-way ticket (about €21) for the view and cafés. Check lift status first, as wind or fog can close high lifts.

🚡 Yastrebets cabin lift up from the resort 💵 Afternoon pass ~€37, or ~€21 two-way lift ticket without skiing (2025/26 list, check the resort) 🕐 12:00 to 16:30 on an afternoon pass, check lift status first
Evening

Floodlit slopes, après-ski & dinner

Night skiing runs 18:30 to 22:00, Wednesday to Saturday, for about €32 (2025/26 list; check current, as snow can change the schedule). Others can watch from a terrace with greyano vino (mulled wine). For dinner, walk a few minutes from the slope-side terraces, where prices are highest, and pick a mehana with a full menu.

🚶 Walk between the slope, bars and restaurants 💵 Night pass ~€32, dinner ~€15-25 (check current) 🕐 Night skiing 18:30 to 22:00, Wednesday to Saturday only
Morning

Samokov: the old town & the Bayrakli Mosque

Samokov is about 10 km down the valley from Borovets, on the way to Rila. The Bayrakli Mosque, built in 1845, is now a museum building with a minaret worth a stop. No hours or fee are published, so ask your hotel (check current). Allow about an hour; Rila Monastery is then roughly 1 hour 40 minutes on.

🚗 ~10 km to Samokov, then ~86 km (about 1 hr 40) to the monastery 💵 Private transfer from ~€20 per person each way, depending on group size (check current) 🕐 Leave about 8:00 to reach the monastery by late morning
Afternoon

Rila Monastery

Rila Monastery is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, founded in the 10th century. The courtyard and church are free, and a single €12 ticket covers all museums, sold only on the grounds. Museums open daily 8:30 to 16:30, the church from 6:30 to 18:00 or 18:30. Cover shoulders and knees; no photography inside.

🚶 Everything is inside the walled courtyard 💵 Courtyard and church free, combined museum ticket €12 🕐 Allow 2 to 3 hours, the museums close at 16:30 outside summer
Evening

Back in Borovets: spa, snowshoes or a quiet mountain evening

The drive back takes about 1 hour 40 minutes, reaching Borovets around 6 PM. A hotel sauna or pool is the easy way to warm up, and some larger hotels admit non-guests for a fee (check current). If you skip the monastery, swap in cross-country skiing, horseback riding or a Ski Doo trip.

🚗 ~1 hr 40 back from the monastery, or a short walk if you stay local 💵 Spa and activity prices vary, ask for a quote (check current) 🕐 Back by about 6 PM, dinner from 7 PM
Morning

The transfer from Borovets to Sofia

Borovets is about 95 km from central Sofia, roughly 1 hour 30 to 2 hours by road. A private car is quoted at about €65 for up to three people, while a Samokov bus takes about 1 hour 10 and is usually cheapest (check current). Ask to be dropped near Serdika metro station.

🚐 ~1 hr 30 to 2 hr by road to central Sofia 💵 Shared shuttle or bus, a few euros to ~€25 per person, private car ~€65 (check current) 🕐 Depart by about 9:30 to reach the centre before lunch
Afternoon

Alexander Nevsky Cathedral & the crypt

Alexander Nevsky Cathedral is free to enter, with hours listed as roughly 7:00 to 19:00 (check current); cover shoulders and knees. The crypt holds the Museum of Christian Art, open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00. An adult ticket costs €10, and the National Gallery charges €2 on Thursdays. It is closed on Mondays.

🚶 Sofia University metro station is the closest stop 💵 Cathedral free, crypt €10 (€2 on Thursdays) 🕐 Crypt open Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00, closed Mondays
Evening

Vitosha Boulevard & the metro to the airport

Vitosha Boulevard is the pedestrianised main shopping street, with cafés for a last meal. From Serdika metro station, take the M1 line and change at Mladost 1 to the M4, which ends at Sofia Airport, about 30 minutes. A single ticket costs €0.80. Book a flight after about 9 PM so you are not rushing.

🚇 ~30 min by metro from Serdika to Sofia Airport, one change 💵 Metro €0.80, a meal ~€10-20 (check current) 🕐 Leave the centre at least 3 hours before your flight

💶 Tipping & Service

  • Tipping is customary in Bulgaria but not large. Rounding up the bill or leaving about 10% in restaurants and bars is normal when service was good, and many places do not add a service charge. Check the bill first.
  • Tip in cash where you can, because tips added to a card payment do not always reach the staff. Round up for taxi drivers, and a euro or two for hotel porters is plenty.
  • Ski instructors and guides are tipped by some visitors at the end of a course, but it is optional and there is no fixed amount.

🍲 Eating & Drinking in a Mehana

  • A mehana is a traditional Bulgarian tavern. Start with shopska salad, then try kavarma or gyuvech (stews baked in clay pots), grilled meats such as kebapche, and banitsa (a cheese pastry) for breakfast. Tarator, a cold yoghurt and cucumber soup, is mainly a summer dish.
  • Rakia is a fruit brandy served before or with meals, and local red wines from the Thracian lowlands are good value. Mulled wine (greyano vino) is the standard drink on the slopes.
  • Menus in the base area are priced for resort visitors, so a main course there costs more than the same dish in Samokov or Sofia. Look at the prices before you sit down, and ask what comes with a dish.

⛪ Monasteries, Dress & Opening Hours

  • Cover shoulders and knees in churches and monasteries, including Rila Monastery and Alexander Nevsky Cathedral. Men should remove hats inside, and photography is restricted in many church interiors, so look for signs.
  • Bulgarians shake their heads from side to side to mean yes and nod to mean no. If a waiter does the opposite of what you expect, check with a word as well as a gesture.
  • Museum and church hours drop in winter, and the Alexander Nevsky crypt is closed on Mondays. Check the day before you travel, and carry a little cash, because small museums may not take cards.

⚠️ Slope Safety, Etiquette & Insurance

  • Stay on the marked pistes. Outside them, the Rila Mountains have avalanche and cliff hazards, and fog can make it hard to find your way back. Do not go off-piste without a guide, a transceiver and avalanche training.
  • Wear a helmet and, if you can, a back protector as a beginner. Hire shops usually rent helmets, and the cost is small compared with a head injury.
  • Follow the international ski rules: the skier below you has the right of way, look uphill before you start or merge, and never stop in a narrow spot or just below a rise. Stay sober on the slopes, since rakia at lunch and skiing do not mix.
  • Check that your travel insurance covers skiing, mountain rescue and medical evacuation, because many basic policies do not. Call 112 in an emergency. Report accidents to the ski patrol or the nearest lift staff.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Bulgarian

  • "Добър ден" (Dobar den, good day), "Здравейте" (Zdraveyte, hello), "Довиждане" (Dovizhdane, goodbye)
  • "Моля" (Molya, please or you're welcome), "Благодаря" (Blagodarya, thank you), "Извинете" (Izvinete, excuse me)
  • "Една маса за двама, моля" (Edna masa za dvama, molya, a table for two, please), "Сметката, моля" (Smetkata, molya, the bill, please)
  • "Колко струва?" (Kolko struva?, how much is it?), "Говорите ли английски?" (Govorite li angliyski?, do you speak English?), "Наздраве!" (Nazdrave!, cheers)

💳 Money & Payments

  • Bulgaria adopted the euro on 1 January 2026, at a fixed rate of 1.95583 lev per euro. The lev circulated alongside it for January and is no longer legal tender, so you only need euros. Prices in Borovets, including lift passes, are quoted in euros.
  • If an old lev note turns up in your change, it can still be exchanged free of charge and without a time limit at the Bulgarian National Bank. Commercial banks' free exchange ended on 30 June 2026, and they may now charge a fee. Ask for euros when you pay in cash.
  • Cards and contactless work at hotels, lift ticket offices, larger restaurants and most shops. Carry some cash for small museums, taxis, tips and cafés in smaller towns. Use bank-branded ATMs, and choose to be charged in euros if a terminal offers your home currency.
  • Some resorts add a small local tourist tax per person per night, usually paid at the hotel, so ask when you book.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Match the pass to the day

On the resort's 2025/26 price list a 1-day adult pass is €54, but a morning pass is €43 and an afternoon pass €37. A beginner will not use a full day, so a half-day pass saves money. Multi-day passes drop the daily price: 2 days is €97 and 4 days €188. A reloadable card is 5% cheaper. Check the resort's price list for this season.

🍞

Eat away from the slope

Terraces at the base and next to the lifts charge resort prices. Walk a few minutes to a mehana with a full menu, or pick up bread, cheese and fruit from a supermarket in Samokov on the Rila day and carry a picnic for the cabin lift café stop.

🚐

Share the transfer

A shared shuttle is the cheapest door-to-door option, and a private car is priced per vehicle, so splitting it between three or four people can match a shared seat each. Book both directions at once, and give your flight number so the company can track delays.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🚡

Ride the cabin lift as a sightseer

You do not need to ski to enjoy the Yastrebets cabin lift. A two-way ticket costs about €21 on the 2025/26 price list, with cafés near the upper station and a wide view over the forest and the Samokov valley.

🥾

Try something other than skiing

The resort lists cross-country skiing, horseback riding, Ski Doo trips and a sledge zone alongside the pistes, and a hotel can often arrange a guided snowshoe walk, which needs no skill. These suit a rest day or a non-skier in the group. Ask your hotel what is running that week, and what it costs (check current).

❄️

See Rila Monastery in winter

Winter brings fewer visitors than summer and snow on the roofs and the hills behind, which suits the striped arcades. The trade-offs are shorter opening hours and slower mountain roads, so leave early and check conditions with your driver (check current).

✈️ Flights

  • Sofia Airport (SOF) is the nearest international airport, about 70 km from Borovets. It has two terminals, Terminal 1 and Terminal 2, linked by a free shuttle bus.
  • Check which airlines fly to Sofia from your home city, including budget carriers. Midweek stays are usually cheaper than weekends in ski resorts, so compare several travel dates.
  • The M4 metro line runs from Sofia Airport into the centre, which is useful on your last evening, with a ticket costing €0.80.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Pre-booked private transfers from Sofia Airport to Borovets are priced per car, typically €65 to €80 for three or four passengers and €80 to €95 for a minivan, depending on the operator. The price is usually fixed, with no extra for a delayed flight.
  • In Sofia, use licensed taxis or a ride-hailing app, and agree on the fare or the meter before you set off. Do not accept offers from drivers who approach you in the arrivals hall.
  • In Borovets, taxis can be scarce in the evening or in snow, so ask your hotel to book your return in advance.

🚌 Transfers, Buses & Ski Transport

  • Shared airport shuttles run to Borovets and Samokov on a pickup window, and they are the cheapest door-to-door option (check current times and fares).
  • Buses between Sofia and Samokov take about 1 hour 10, and a taxi or local bus covers the 10 km up to Borovets. Check the timetable the day before.
  • The main lifts are within walking distance of most hotels in the base area. Ask your hotel about the local ski shuttle if you are staying farther out.
  • Sofia buses, trams and the metro share one ticket system, with a single ticket at €0.80 (valid for 30 minutes, including transfers) and a day pass at about €2 (check current).

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Near the base area and the cabin lift: the most convenient place to stay, with a short walk to the lifts and hire shops, but the highest prices.
  • Elsewhere in Borovets: quieter streets and often lower prices, with a longer walk to the lifts in ski boots, so check the distance.
  • Samokov (about 10 km away): cheaper rooms and local restaurants, but you need a bus or taxi to the lifts every morning, which adds time and cost.
  • Sofia: a good base for the first and last nights if you want city time, though it adds a long transfer each way.
  • Check whether your room price includes breakfast or half board, which can be a good deal in the resort, and ask about the tourist tax.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • Bulgaria is in the EU, so EU SIM holders roam at home rates. Other visitors can use an eSIM or a local prepaid SIM, and most hotels offer Wi-Fi.
  • Bulgaria now uses the euro, so no currency exchange is needed if you carry euros. Cards work almost everywhere in the resort, and cash helps at small museums and taxis.
  • Use bank-branded ATMs and avoid standalone exchange booths.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Check your passport's Schengen rules before booking, including whether ETIAS applies on your travel dates (it is scheduled for the last quarter of 2026, but no start date had been confirmed at the time of writing).
  • Check the resort's lift status page and weather forecast before you go up, because high lifts can close and snow cover varies by season.
  • Book a lesson ahead if you are a beginner, particularly at weekends and school holidays, and ask whether beginner group classes start on a set day.
  • A hire car is not needed for this itinerary and mountain roads need winter tyres or chains. Transfers and buses cover everything here.
