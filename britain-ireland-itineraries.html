Six trips: three city breaks and three self-drive routes

This hub covers England, Scotland and Ireland through six itineraries. London is the large capital with the longest city stay at five days, Edinburgh and Dublin are compact historic cities, and three are self-drive trips: Scottish Highlands around lochs and glens, the longer North Coast 500 loop and a one-way section of the Wild Atlantic Way. Each suits a different kind of traveller.

First-timers who want the big sights should start with London, which spreads royal London, museums, the West End and Greenwich over five days. Travellers who like castles and walkable old streets will find Edinburgh a better fit, and its last day reaches Stirling Castle. Dublin suits a mix of Trinity College, Kilmainham Gaol and the Guinness Storehouse, with a day out to Glendalough in the Wicklow Mountains at the end. The pace differs as well: London pairs the Tower with Borough Market on one day and Notting Hill with Camden on another, while Dublin gives a whole day to Kilmainham Gaol and the National Museum.

If scenery matters more than cities, Scottish Highlands starts at Inverness and loops through Loch Ness, Glenfinnan, Glen Coe and Eilean Donan. Day three combines Glenfinnan, Glen Coe and Glen Nevis, so expect plenty of time in the car. The page has you collect a hire car at Inverness Airport and spend the second night in Fort William.

North Coast 500 is the longer Scottish drive: 6 days from and back to Inverness Airport, about 1,025 km with the airport transfers and side trips, while the official loop is 516 miles (830 km). Wild Atlantic Way is a 7-day, one-way section of about 810 km from Cork to Shannon, not the whole signposted coast of about 2,600 km, so the page says what is left out.

What do the daily costs tell you?

Scottish Highlands is the lowest at $140 per day, and its total of $450-680 per person is the smallest on the hub. North Coast 500 and Wild Atlantic Way both come to about $170 a day, at $845-1,185 for 6 days and $965-1,410 for 7. Edinburgh is $200 a day, London $240 and Dublin $250. Dublin has the highest daily figure, though its four-day total of $850-1,150 still sits below London's five-day $1,000-1,400. Compare the per-person totals as well as the daily rate before you decide how long to stay.

Remember what each figure covers. The two newer drive pages include each person's half of the car for two travellers and show the car share separately, $245-405 for the North Coast 500 and $325-515 for the Wild Atlantic Way, so a third or fourth traveller lowers it. Currency differs too: the Republic of Ireland uses the euro, while England and Scotland use the pound.

When should I go, and can I combine them?

Late May, June and September are the overlap, because all six trips list them. London lists April to June and September, Edinburgh and Dublin list May to September, Scottish Highlands lists April to June and September to October, and North Coast 500 and Wild Atlantic Way both list late May to June or September. Pack a waterproof whatever the month.

Edinburgh and the Highlands combine easily on a map: ScotRail runs direct trains from Edinburgh Waverley to Inverness, which take roughly three and a half hours. You would need to plan that leg yourself, as the Highlands page begins at Inverness Airport. To save money, look for free sights: the British Museum and the Natural History Museum in London have free general admission, though special exhibitions are charged, and Arthur's Seat in Edinburgh is free to climb.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a trip to Britain and Ireland cost?

Our 6 Britain & Ireland itineraries cost from $450 to $1,410 per person for the whole trip, which is about $140 to $250 a day. Prices cover lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and exclude international flights.

Which Britain & Ireland itinerary is the cheapest?

Scottish Highlands ($450-680), Edinburgh ($700-900), North Coast 500 ($845-1,185) are the three lowest, per person and excluding flights.

How many days do I need in Britain and Ireland?

Our Britain & Ireland itineraries run from 4 to 7 days, and the most common length is 4 days. Each page has a day picker with a price for every length from one day up to the full plan.

When is the best time to visit Britain and Ireland?

It depends on the trip, and each itinerary lists its ideal season. For example: London, Apr – Jun, Sep; Edinburgh, May – Sep; Scottish Highlands, Apr – Jun, Sep – Oct. The table above shows the ideal season for every Britain & Ireland trip.

Do I need a car for the Scottish and Irish drives?

Yes. The Scottish Highlands, North Coast 500 and Wild Atlantic Way pages are all written as self-drive trips, and their day plans assume a hire car, with the car cost shown separately on the two newer pages. Public transport does exist around Loch Ness, since Citylink coaches run to Fort William, but you would need to rework the timings and check current services. Drive on the left in both Scotland and Ireland, and note that the Wild Atlantic Way section is one-way, so check the one-way fee when you price the car.

Is Ireland part of the United Kingdom?

The Republic of Ireland, where Dublin is, is a separate country from the United Kingdom, which includes England, Scotland and Northern Ireland. In practice that means a different currency, the euro rather than the pound, so keep both in mind when you plan a trip that crosses the Irish Sea.
