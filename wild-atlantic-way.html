7 Days on the Wild Atlantic Way: Kerry, Dingle & the Cliffs of Moher

A one-way section of the Wild Atlantic Way, not the whole 2,600 km: Kinsale, the Beara, the Ring of Kerry, Dingle, the Shannon ferry and the Cliffs of Moher, Cork to Shannon by car.

Ideal SeasonLate May to June, or September (long days; weather changes fast in any month)
Est. Budget$965-1410
Duration7 Days
PaceModerate

Last updated: November 2026

7-day Wild Atlantic Way itinerary

How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Cork Airport & collecting the car

Cork Airport (ORK) is about 30 km from Kinsale. Collect the car at the hire desks in arrivals with your passport, a full physical licence and a credit card in the driver's name. Photograph every scratch, check the fuel level and confirm manual or automatic. Enterprise's desk opens 8 am to 8 pm, so check hours for a late flight.

🚗 ~30 km, 35 min to Kinsale 💵 Hire ~€40-70 a day in season (check current) 🕐 Desk hours vary: Enterprise 8 am to 8 pm
Afternoon

Charles Fort, Kinsale

Charles Fort is a large 17th-century star-shaped fort above the harbour entrance, about 3 km from the town centre. Entry is €5 for adults (Heritage Ireland), open 10 am to 6 pm from 16 March to 31 October, last admission 5 pm. Allow about 1.5 hours. Park on site, and keep left on the narrow, hedged approach roads.

🚗 ~3 km, 8 min from Kinsale centre 💵 €5 adult (check current) 🕐 Open 10 am to 6 pm, last entry 5 pm
Evening

Kinsale harbour & first dinner

Kinsale is a compact harbour town of painted shopfronts and seafood restaurants, easy to cover on foot. Park once in a pay-and-display car park and leave the car for the night. Walk the waterfront, browse the lanes behind the harbour and book dinner, since popular places fill up in summer. Expect about €25-40 for a main course and a drink.

🚶 Walkable, leave the car parked 💵 ~€25-40 dinner, parking ~€1-3 an hour (check current) 🕐 Dinner from 6:30 PM, book ahead in season
Morning

Kinsale to Glengarriff

Leave by 9 am for about 100 km west by Bandon and the N71 to Glengarriff, roughly 1 hour 40 minutes without stops. The road passes Bantry Bay, and Glengarriff has a sheltered harbour with boats to the garden island of Garinish (check the current fare and times). Fill the tank here: fuel is thinner on the Beara.

🚗 ~100 km, 1 h 40 to Glengarriff 💵 Fuel top-up ~€40-60, Garinish boat extra (check current) 🕐 Leave Kinsale by 9 am
Afternoon

The Healy Pass (R574)

The Healy Pass crosses the Caha Mountains between Cork and Kerry on a narrow road of hairpin bends with open sides and roaming sheep. Take it slowly in low gear, use the pull-ins to let others pass and stop at the summit viewpoint over Glanmore Lake. Avoid it in thick fog. Kenmare is about 30 km beyond.

🚗 ~45 km, 55 min over the pass to Kenmare 💵 Free 🕐 Clear afternoon; skip in fog or heavy rain
Evening

Kenmare: town walk & dinner

Kenmare is a tidy market town at the head of the Kenmare River, with good restaurants along two main streets and an easy walk round the centre. Use a pay-and-display car park or your B&B's own spaces, then walk to dinner. The town has several traditional pubs, and music nights are common in summer. Expect €25-40 for dinner.

🚶 Walkable, leave the car parked 💵 ~€25-40 dinner, parking ~€1-2 an hour (check current) 🕐 Dinner from 6:30 PM
Morning

Kenmare to Sneem & Staigue Fort

Drive the N70 west by Sneem, then turn off for Staigue Fort, an Iron Age dry-stone ring fort. Entry is free, with a donation box. The access road is narrow with blind bends, so use the passing places. Tour coaches conventionally run the ring anticlockwise, so you meet them head-on; set off by 8:30 am.

🚗 ~50 km, 1 h 5 to Staigue Fort 💵 Free, small donation welcome 🕐 Leave Kenmare by 8:30 am, before the coaches
Afternoon

Waterville, Cahersiveen & Ballycarbery Castle

Continue past Caherdaniel and Waterville along the coast to Cahersiveen, with sea views on the left. Stop in Waterville for lunch on the seafront. Ballycarbery Castle, an ivy-covered ruin, is a short drive west of Cahersiveen on a lane across private farmland, so keep to the track. Skellig Michael landings are not part of this itinerary.

🚗 ~75 km, 1 h 30 to Cahersiveen 💵 Free sights, ~€12-18 lunch 🕐 Lunch in Waterville around 1 pm
Evening

Killorglin to Killarney & Ross Castle

Follow the N70 east to Killorglin, then the N72 to Killarney, about 70 km in total. Check into your B&B, park, and take a golden-hour stroll to Ross Castle on the edge of Lough Leane, about 3 km from town (the castle interior is a paid guided visit; check current hours). Dinner is back in town.

🚗 ~70 km, 1 h 20 to Killarney 💵 Grounds free, dinner ~€25-40 🕐 Ross Castle at golden hour, dinner 7 PM
Morning

Torc Waterfall & Muckross House

Muckross House is about 6 km from Killarney, with free parking and free gardens. The house costs €9 (2026 price, check current) and opens about 9:15 am. Torc Waterfall is a short walk from its car park nearby. The Gap of Dunloe is a narrow road shared with jaunting cars and cyclists, so it is left out.

🚗 ~10 km, 15 min to Muckross and Torc 💵 House ~€9 (check current), gardens and waterfall free 🕐 Arrive by 9:30 am before the coaches
Afternoon

Inch Beach & the drive to Dingle

Drive back through Killorglin and Castlemaine to Inch, a 5 km sandy beach on Dingle Bay with mountains across the water. Park at the dunes and walk the sand; do not drive onto soft sand, which a hire agreement will not cover. From Inch, go via Anascaul to Dingle, about 30 km, arriving mid-afternoon.

🚗 ~85 km, 1 h 45 to Inch and on to Dingle 💵 Free beach, fuel ~€15 🕐 Inch around 2 pm, in Dingle by 4 pm
Evening

Dingle town: harbour & music pubs

Dingle is a small fishing port with a lively evening scene. Leave the car at your guesthouse or a pay car park, then walk the harbour and Main Street. Many pubs have live traditional music, often free to enter, from about 9 pm. Book dinner ahead in season; signs here show the Irish name An Daingean.

🚶 Walkable, leave the car parked 💵 ~€25-40 dinner, live music usually free 🕐 Dinner 6:30 PM, music from about 9 PM
Morning

Slea Head Drive

The Slea Head loop is about 47 km from Dingle by Ventry, Slea Head and Dunquin, with Dunmore Head and Coumeenole beach on the way. Most guides drive it clockwise, to match coach traffic. The cliff road is narrow, with cyclists and blind bends; allow 1 hour 15 minutes without stops, and use pull-ins to photograph.

🚗 ~50 km loop, 1 h 15 without stops 💵 Free, parking at pull-ins 🕐 Leave Dingle by 9 am, before the coaches
Afternoon

The Blasket Centre, Dún Chaoin

The Blasket Centre at Dún Chaoin tells the story of the island community that lived on the Great Blasket until 1953. Entry was €5 for adults in 2026 (Heritage Ireland), with the season running from March to early November. Check the current opening dates before you go. The terrace has a long view of the islands.

🚗 ~20 min on the loop road 💵 €5 adult (check current) 🕐 Open about 10 am to 6 pm in season (check current)
Evening

Gallarus Oratory & dinner in Dingle

Gallarus Oratory is a small dry-stone chapel shaped like an upturned boat, free to visit with parking beside it (check current). It sits on the last stretch of the loop, about 8 km from Dingle. Late light suits the stone. Then return to Dingle for dinner; a second night here means no packing up.

🚗 ~15 min to the oratory, ~15 min back to Dingle 💵 Free (check current), dinner ~€25-40 🕐 Late afternoon light, dinner 7 PM
Morning

The Conor Pass to Tralee

Leave Dingle by 9 am to cross the Conor Pass, one of the highest roads in Ireland, a narrow road with passing places and sheer sides. Kerry County Council signs advise against large vehicles. Stop at the viewpoint car park, then continue to Tralee, about 60 km in total. Avoid it in low cloud, and let oncoming drivers through.

🚗 ~60 km, 1 h 15 to Tralee 💵 Free, fuel ~€15 🕐 Cross by 10 am, clear weather only
Afternoon

Tralee, Listowel & the Tarbert-Killimer ferry

From Tralee take the N69 by Listowel to Tarbert, about 50 km. The car ferry crosses to Killimer in about 20 minutes. A car with passengers costs €22.50 online or €25 at the quay (Shannon Ferries); tickets must be bought at least 30 minutes before boarding. In June boats leave about every 30 minutes from 9:30 am to 6:30 pm.

🚗 ~50 km, 55 min to Tarbert, then a 20 min ferry 💵 €22.50 online, €25 on the quay per car (check current) 🕐 Half-hourly in June, last boat about 9:30 pm
Evening

Killimer to Doolin

From Killimer drive by Kilrush and Miltown Malbay to Doolin, about 85 km. Doolin is a small village with several pubs known for traditional music, a short walk from most B&Bs. Check in, park, then walk to dinner. Fuel in Ennistymon or Lahinch if the tank is below half, as stations thin out.

🚗 ~85 km, 1 h 30 to Doolin 💵 Dinner ~€20-35, music free 🕐 Arrive by 7 PM, music from about 9 PM
Morning

The Cliffs of Moher

The Cliffs of Moher are 8 km from Doolin. The official 2027 rates are €9 off-peak (before 11 am), €14 at peak and €7 late evening, against €16 at the gate; admission includes parking and a 2-hour visit slot. In May to August the site opens at 8 am. Book online and stay inside the barriers.

🚗 ~8 km, 15 min from Doolin 💵 Adult €9 off-peak, €14 peak online, gate €16 (2027) 🕐 8-11 am, the cheapest and quietest slot
Afternoon

The Burren & Poulnabrone dolmen

From the cliffs take the R478 and R480 through Lisdoonvarna to Poulnabrone dolmen, a Neolithic portal tomb about 5,000 years old on bare limestone pavement. It is free, with a small car park. The ground is uneven and slippery when wet, so keep to the path and do not climb the stones. Allow 30 minutes.

🚗 ~30 km, 40 min via Lisdoonvarna 💵 Free 🕐 Early afternoon, most visitors have gone
Evening

Return the car at Shannon Airport

From Poulnabrone drive by Corofin and Ennis to Shannon Airport (SNN), about 62 km in just over an hour. Refuel close to the airport, since hire firms add a refuelling charge, then return the car and photograph it. Arrive at least 2.5 hours before an international flight; Shannon has US border preclearance, so allow more for US flights.

🚗 ~62 km, 1 h 5 to Shannon Airport 💵 Refuel ~€25, no tolls on this route 🕐 Reach the airport 2.5 hours before your flight

Wild Atlantic Way: what to know before you go

💶 Tipping, Pub Rounds & Payments

  • In restaurants, 10 to 15% is normal for good service if no service charge is on the bill. Check the bill first, because some places add a service charge for larger groups.
  • At the bar, tipping is not expected. Buying a round is the custom when you drink with a group: each person takes a turn, so do not leave before it is your turn.
  • B&B owners and taxi drivers do not expect tips, though rounding up a taxi fare is polite. Pay for a pint, a coffee or a small shop by card or in cash at the counter.

🍽️ Eating & Drinking

  • A full Irish breakfast (egg, rashers, sausage, black and white pudding, soda bread) is usually included at B&Bs and sets you up for a long drive. Seafood chowder with brown bread is the standard pub lunch on the coast.
  • Pubs often serve food until about 9 pm. In many, you order at the bar and pay as you go. Pints are poured slowly, especially stout, so expect a short wait.
  • Ireland has a strict approach to drink-driving, so decide who drives before the evening starts and treat the pub as a place for food or a soft drink.

🚗 Driving Etiquette on Narrow Roads

  • Drive on the left and keep left. On a single-track road, whoever is nearer a passing place pulls in; if the oncoming car is nearer, you wait. Raise a hand or a finger from the wheel to say thanks.
  • Do not stop on the road for photos. Use pull-ins and car parks, and give way to cyclists, walkers and sheep. Slow right down for blind bends and sheep on the road.
  • Let faster local drivers overtake at a safe place. Coaches often hold the centre of the road, so pull in early and let them pass instead of squeezing by.

⚠️ Safety, Weather & Scam Warnings

  • Weather changes fast on the Atlantic coast, so rain, wind and fog can arrive within an hour. Check the Met Éireann forecast (met.ie) and any warnings each morning, and skip the Healy and Conor passes in fog.
  • Stay inside the barriers at the Cliffs of Moher and other cliff edges. Do not walk on unfenced edges, and keep away from them in strong wind.
  • Do not leave bags or valuables visible in a parked car at viewpoints, beaches and car parks. Take the hire paperwork with you and keep valuables out of sight.
  • For official travel advice, check your own government: gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/ireland for UK travellers and travel.state.gov for US travellers. The emergency number is 112 or 999.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Irish

  • English is spoken everywhere, but in Gaeltacht areas such as the Dingle Peninsula road signs show only the Irish place names, for example An Daingean (Dingle) and Dún Chaoin (Dunquin).
  • "Dia duit" (DEE-ah gwit, hello), "Slán" (slawn, goodbye), "Sláinte" (SLAWN-cha, cheers)
  • "Go raibh maith agat" (guh rev mah UG-ut, thank you), "Le do thoil" (leh duh hull, please)
  • "Fáilte" (FAWL-cha, welcome), "Bóthar" (BOH-har, road), "Trá" (traw, beach)

💳 Money & Payments

  • The Republic of Ireland uses the euro. Cards and contactless work in nearly all hotels, restaurants, filling stations and larger shops, but carry some cash for small car parks, rural pubs and farm gate stalls.
  • Use ATMs attached to banks and choose to be charged in euros if a machine offers your home currency. Avoid standalone exchange booths.
  • Pay-and-display car parks take coins or card; some towns use parking discs sold in shops. Read the sign, because fines are real.

Insider tips for Wild Atlantic Way

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Book the Cliffs of Moher online for the early slot

The official 2027 online rate is €9 off-peak (before 11 am) against €16 at the gate, and parking is included. The cliffs also open at 8 am from May to August, which suits a start from Doolin.

🍲

Make lunch the main meal

Pubs and cafés serve chowder, sandwiches and daily specials for about €10-18 at lunch, compared with €25-40 for dinner. A B&B breakfast included in the price keeps the morning meal free.

⛽

Fill up in bigger towns

Stations in Cork, Killarney, Tralee and Ennis are usually cheaper and more plentiful than on the peninsulas. Fill up before the Beara, Dingle and the Burren, where pumps are sparse.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌅

Go early and late

The Ring of Kerry, Slea Head and the Cliffs are quietest before 9:30 am and after 5 pm. Long June days leave light until about 10 pm for a late stop.

🏰

Add a free stone fort

Staigue Fort and Gallarus Oratory are free, uncrowded and photogenic, and each takes about 30 to 45 minutes. Both have small car parks, so arrive early in July and August.

🌊

Look for pull-ins, not queues

On narrow roads the best views are at the pull-ins, such as the Slea Head lay-bys and the Conor Pass viewpoint. Stop there, not on the road, and the traffic keeps flowing.

Travel services for Wild Atlantic Way

✈️ Flights

  • Fly into Cork (ORK) and out of Shannon (SNN) on an open-jaw ticket. Both airports have hire desks, and the route runs south to north so the first drive is short and the Cliffs of Moher come last.
  • The reverse also works: fly into Shannon and out of Cork. Compare both directions, because fares and the one-way hire fee can differ.
  • Dublin (DUB) is cheaper for some routes but adds a 3 to 4 hour drive each way, so it is not used here.

🚗 Car Hire

  • Bring a full, valid licence (digital licences are not accepted at Enterprise Cork) and photo ID. For licences not in English or the Latin alphabet, check whether an International Driving Permit is needed; an IDP alone is not enough.
  • Minimum age is 25 at Enterprise Cork; some firms rent from 21 with a young-driver fee, and some have an upper age limit. A credit card in the driver's name is required, and debit cards are usually refused.
  • CDW is bundled by hire firms and the excess is high (Enterprise Cork lists about €2,000 for a manual economy car and €2,700 for most others). The hold on your card equals this unless you take Super CDW, often about €20-35 a day (check current).
  • Manual cars are the norm and cheaper; automatics cost more and sell out, so book early. A one-way hire Cork to Shannon carries a fee that varies (check at booking). Return full-to-full.

🛣️ Driving Rules & Roads

  • Drive on the left, steering wheel on the right. Speed signs are in km/h; limits depend on the road (60 default on rural local roads since February 2025, 80 or 100 on many R and N roads, 120 on motorways). Obey signs, since limits are under review (gov.ie).
  • Hazards here: single-track roads with passing places, hairpins on the Healy and Conor passes, sheep and cyclists, and fog or rain over high ground. No tolls are on this route; the M50 is in Dublin and is not used.
  • Fuel is thin on the Beara, parts of the Ring and the Burren: fill up in Kenmare, Killarney, Dingle and Ennis. Check road closures and weather with Transport Infrastructure Ireland (tii.ie), Kerry County Council and Met Éireann (met.ie).
  • Ring of Kerry: tour coaches conventionally run anticlockwise, which is why this plan goes clockwise from Kenmare and starts early. This is local custom, and I could not confirm it as an official rule.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Overnight stops are Kinsale (1 night), Kenmare (1), Killarney (1), Dingle (2) and Doolin (1), each within walking distance of dinner.
  • B&Bs and guesthouses typically cost about €100-160 a night for a double in summer, with breakfast (approximate, check current). Hostels and self-catering cost less.
  • Book 4 to 6 months ahead for June and September, and earlier for July and August, as Dingle, Kenmare and Doolin fill up.
  • Choose a place with parking, as town car parks charge by the hour and some close overnight.

📱 Connectivity & Cash

  • EU SIM holders roam at home rates. Other visitors can buy a prepaid SIM or an eSIM; coverage is patchy on the Beara, Conor Pass and Slea Head, so download offline maps first.
  • Cards work almost everywhere, but carry coins for pay-and-display machines. Some towns use parking discs sold in shops.
  • There are no road tolls on this route, so no tag or toll account is needed. The ferry takes card online or at the quay.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Ireland is not in the Schengen area, and entry rules differ from the rest of the EU. Check your nationality on the official site irishimmigration.ie before booking. I found no Irish electronic travel authorisation scheme, but check for any change.
  • Skellig Michael is not included. Landing tours run only in season (about mid-May to end of September) through licensed boats with capped numbers; book early with an operator named by the OPW (heritageireland.ie).
  • Take out travel insurance that covers car hire excess if you decline the desk's Super CDW, and check whether your card cover applies in Ireland.
  • In the car: a rain jacket, water, snacks, a phone charger, a printed copy of bookings and coins for car parks.

The Wild Atlantic Way route, distances and driving times

This is a 7-day, one-way section of the Wild Atlantic Way, not the whole route: the full signposted coast is about 2,600 km and takes weeks, so this keeps the best-known stretch, about 810 km. You fly into Cork, drive north by Kinsale, the Beara, the Ring of Kerry and Dingle, cross the Shannon by ferry and finish at Shannon Airport. South to north suits it: an easy first drive, the Ring of Kerry clockwise from Kenmare, and the Cliffs of Moher as the finale. Galway and Connemara would need two more days.

Can you drive the whole Wild Atlantic Way in 7 days?

No. The signposted route is about 2,600 km and needs two to four weeks at a comfortable pace, so 7 days covers a section. This itinerary keeps the best-known stretch, from Kinsale to the Cliffs of Moher, and leaves Galway, Connemara and the north for another trip. Rushing the whole route would mean mostly driving.

Which way round the Ring of Kerry, and can you do it without a car?

This plan drives the ring clockwise from Kenmare and starts early, because tour coaches conventionally go anticlockwise (local custom, not confirmed as an official rule). Without a car, Killarney has day tours around the Ring and the Dingle Peninsula, but you lose the freedom to stop at the forts, beaches and pull-ins.

Will my credit card cover the hire car in Ireland?

Many card policies exclude Ireland or only apply if you decline the hire firm's CDW, so get written confirmation from your card issuer before you travel. If you decline, expect a card hold of the full excess (Enterprise Cork lists about €2,000 to €3,000), which can stay held for weeks. At pick-up, photograph every scratch and check the fuel level.
