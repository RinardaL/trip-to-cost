4 Days in Colmar: Christmas Markets & Alsace Villages

Four days of Christmas in Colmar: lit market squares, Petite Venise at night, and the village markets of Riquewihr, Kaysersberg and Eguisheim.

Ideal SeasonNov – Dec (Christmas Markets)
Est. Budget$750-1,000
Duration4 Days
PaceRelaxed
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival at Gare de Colmar and check-in near the old town

Colmar's station connects directly to Strasbourg, and onward to Basel-Mulhouse airport or Paris with a single change, with the market squares only a fifteen-minute walk from the platform. Old-town hotels fill early in market season, so book first and ask whether they can hold your bags until check-in.

🚶 ~15 min walk from Gare de Colmar to the old town 💵 ~€10-15 taxi if you're carrying luggage 🕐 Plan around your train or transfer's arrival time
Afternoon

Place de l'Ancienne Douane and the Koifhus market

The Koïfhus, Colmar's 15th-century customs house, anchors this market. About 50 small huts around the Schwendi fountain on Place de l'Ancienne Douane sell Christmas decorations and crafts, about 20 craft stalls fill the hall inside, and the Renaissance façade and staircase are lit in the evening. In 2026 the markets run from 23 November to 29 December (check current), with vin chaud and roasted chestnuts on the surrounding squares.

🚶 Steps from Place de l'Ancienne Douane 💵 Free to browse, ~€4-6 per vin chaud 🕐 2026 hours: 11 AM-7 PM Mon-Thu, 10 AM-8 PM Fri-Sun (check current)
Evening

Petite Venise strung with lights, and a first cup of vin chaud

The canal quarter along the Lauch is dressed in Christmas lights, and the children's market on Place des Six-Montagnes-Noires adds a carousel and a giant letterbox for letters to Père Noël. After dark the reflections on the water make a quieter, more atmospheric scene than the crowded central squares.

🚶 ~10 min walk from the Koifhus 💵 ~€4 for a cup of mulled wine (plus a refundable mug deposit) 🕐 After dusk, around 5 PM in December
Morning

Maison Pfister and the carved facades of the old town

Built in 1537 for a wealthy hatmaker, Maison Pfister's painted medallions, carved wooden gallery, and octagonal corner turret make it one of the most photographed buildings in Colmar. In December the streets around it are dressed for Christmas, and early mornings are quiet before the market stalls open at 10 or 11 AM.

🚶 On Rue des Marchands, ~5 min from the Koifhus 💵 Free to view from outside 🕐 Before 10 AM, ahead of the crowds
Midday

Wandering Rue des Marchands and the Grand'Rue

These two streets string together most of Colmar's finest half-timbered facades, small ateliers, and bakeries, and in December many houses are outlined in lights or hung with garlands. Look for bredele, the small Alsatian Christmas biscuits, and pain d'épices in the bakery windows, an easy midday walk between the morning's sights and the afternoon's markets.

🚶 Connected on foot, ~15 min end to end 💵 Free to stroll 🕐 Late morning into lunch
Afternoon

Place Jeanne-d'Arc, Place des Dominicains and the Marché Couvert

Place Jeanne-d'Arc is the market for Alsatian produce, with charcuterie, foie gras, wine, gingerbread and bredele. Place des Dominicains has the largest market, about 60 chalets around the Dominican church, whose 14th-century stained glass is lit at dusk. The 19th-century covered market nearby is the stop for lunch or for foie gras and kougelhopf to take home, but check its current opening days.

🚶 ~10 min walk from Rue des Marchands 💵 ~€12-18 lunch at the covered market 🕐 Markets close at 7 PM Mon-Thu and 8 PM Fri-Sun (2026 hours, check current)
Morning

Riquewihr's walled village market

Riquewihr's Christmas market, about 12 km from Colmar, sets more than 40 chalets among the half-timbered houses of the walled village, around a large Christmas tree. Expect Alsatian crafts, bredele, kougelhopf, gingerbread and mulled wine. In 2026 it runs from 27 November to 20 December, so it is over by late December (check current).

🚗 ~20 min drive from Colmar, or a weekend Christmas shuttle (check current) 💵 Free to browse, ~€15 per wine tasting flight (varies by cellar) 🕐 Opens 10 AM, so go early before the weekend crowds
Midday

Kaysersberg, voted France's favorite village

Kaysersberg's market sets up in the Cour de l'Arsenal and behind the Sainte-Croix church, below the ruins of the imperial castle. It opens Friday to Sunday only, from 27 November to 20 December in 2026 (check current), so a weekday visit finds the village without its market. The village was voted France's favorite village in 2017.

🚗 ~15-20 min drive from Riquewihr 💵 Free to browse, ~€5-8 for lunch from a stall 🕐 10 AM-8 PM Fri-Sun in 2026 listings (check current)
Evening

Back to Colmar for a second evening among the market lights

Back in Colmar by early evening, take a second pass through the lit old town, including Place des Dominicains, where the church's stained glass is lit at dusk, then book a winstub dinner ahead, since tables fill up in market season.

🚗 ~20 min drive back to Colmar 💵 ~€20-25 dinner at a winstub 🕐 Early evening, before dinner reservations
Morning

Eguisheim's circular village market

Eguisheim's medieval streets curl in concentric rings around the Château Saint-Léon, and the village is officially named one of France's most beautiful villages. About 30 chalets surround Place du Château Saint-Léon, with more stalls in the lanes, and a giant Advent calendar opens its windows on the square each evening. The 2026 market is listed from 27 November to 30 December, and in 2025 it was closed 24 to 26 December (check current).

🚗 ~15 min drive or bus from Colmar 💵 Free to browse 🕐 Walk the rings in the morning; in 2025 stalls opened at 10 AM Fri-Sun and noon Mon-Thu until mid-December
Midday

Last-minute Christmas shopping: pain d'épices, bredele and the Marché Couvert

Back in Colmar, the squares and the covered market are the easiest stops for last gifts: pain d'épices (Alsatian spiced gingerbread), bredele biscuits sold by weight at dedicated stalls, and bottles of Gewürztraminer packed for travel. The Colmar markets run to 29 December in 2026 (check current).

🚗 ~15 min drive back from Eguisheim, then on foot 💵 Varies by what you pack home 🕐 Late morning into early afternoon
Afternoon

Transfer to Gare de Colmar for departure

Regional trains connect Colmar back to Strasbourg, on to Basel-Mulhouse airport, or onward to Paris with a change. Budget extra time if market crowds are thick between your hotel and the station, and check train timetables if you leave on 24 to 26 December, when holiday services can differ.

🚆 Regional train to Strasbourg, Basel-Mulhouse, or Paris 💵 Varies by route 🕐 Leave at least 2 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Service is included by law (service compris) in French restaurants and cafes, so tipping is a small gesture rather than an expectation.
  • Market stall vendors don't expect tips, though rounding up for a cup of vin chaud is common and appreciated.
  • At a winstub dinner, rounding up the bill or leaving a euro or two for good service is standard practice.

🧥 What to Wear in Winter Alsace

  • Layer heavily: evening temperatures at the markets regularly sit near or below freezing through December.
  • Waterproof, grippy boots matter more than style, cobblestones turn slick with winter rain, frost, or the occasional snow flurry.
  • Bring gloves, a scarf, and a warm hat, most of each day here is spent outdoors moving between market squares and villages.

🚗 Getting Around Colmar & the Village Markets

  • Colmar's old town is compact and fully walkable, so save transport for the trips out to the village markets.
  • A rental car is the most flexible way to reach Riquewihr, Kaysersberg, and Eguisheim in a single day. Listings for 2026 also show weekend Christmas shuttles to Riquewihr (about €17 per person, with a park-and-ride at NaturOparC), so check current options if you skip the car.
  • Regional trains link Colmar to Strasbourg in about 35 minutes, with onward connections to Basel-Mulhouse airport or Paris.
  • Village markets keep shorter calendars than Colmar's: Kaysersberg opens Friday to Sunday only, it and Riquewihr end on 20 December in 2026, and Eguisheim runs to 30 December (check current). Build Day 3 around those dates.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Cobblestones get slick with winter rain, frost, or occasional snow, so grippy, waterproof boots aren't optional.
  • Christmas market crowds, especially on December weekends, are prime pickpocket territory, keep bags zipped and worn to the front.
  • Most vin chaud stalls charge a small deposit for the mug, refunded when you return it (about €2 was reported at Colmar stalls in 2025, check current), so don't walk off with it by accident.

🎄 Colmar's Christmas Markets, Square by Square

  • Colmar's own tagline is "Colmar, l'âme de Noël" (the soul of Christmas), and Alsace points to written records of Christmas markets from the 1570s, with Strasbourg's Christkindelsmärik the best known.
  • Alsatian bredele biscuits, pain d'épices and foie gras sit beside mulled wine, which Alsace serves as white vin chaud as often as red.
  • In 2026 the five traditional markets run 23 November to 29 December and are free to enter: Place des Dominicains (decorations and gifts), Place de l'Ancienne Douane (crafts), Place Jeanne-d'Arc (Alsatian produce), Petite Venise (children's market) and the indoor Koïfhus craft market. The Marché Gourmand and the Place Rapp fairground run to 3 January.
  • Listed hours for 2026: 11 AM-7 PM Monday to Thursday, 10 AM-8 PM Friday to Sunday, 11 AM-5 PM on 24 December and 10 AM-8 PM on 25 December (check current).

💳 Money & Payments

  • The euro is the currency, and contactless cards are accepted almost everywhere, including most market stalls.
  • Carry small notes and coins regardless, some individual stalls skip card readers or set a minimum purchase.
  • Entry to the markets is free. The Ferris wheel (listed at €10 per adult for 2026) and fairground rides are paid extras.
  • ATMs are common throughout Colmar's old town, and bank-branded machines avoid the higher fees some standalone kiosks charge.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🚗

Split a rental car for Day 3

A single day's rental divided among a group costs a fraction of separate taxis or tours to Riquewihr and Kaysersberg, and weekend Christmas shuttles are the alternative if nobody drives (check current).

🍷

Ask for a tasting flight, not a full bottle

Wine cellars along the route will pour a small flight of three or four wines for a modest fee, cheaper than committing to a bottle at every village stop.

🥐

Lunch at the covered market instead of a sit-down restaurant

A plate from one of the Marché Couvert's stalls costs a fraction of a winstub lunch and tastes just as regional, and the Marché Gourmand food market, open to 3 January in 2026 listings, is another place to eat (check current).

🎟️

Spend on food and gifts, not entry

Colmar's Christmas markets and the village markets on this route are free to enter, so budget for mulled wine, bredele and gifts rather than tickets.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌙

Petite Venise after dark, not at midday

The canal quarter's lights and reflections are best appreciated once the day-trip buses have already left for the evening.

🏘️

Start Day 3 at opening time

Riquewihr's market opens at 10 AM, so arrive then and move on to Kaysersberg, whose market runs Friday to Sunday only, before the afternoon crowds build (check current).

🕯️

Weekday mornings beat December weekends

Early-season weekday visits in late November are far calmer than the packed weekends once December arrives, but put Day 3 on a Friday to Sunday so Kaysersberg is open.

🎄

See Eguisheim's Advent calendar after dark

Its giant Advent calendar opens a window on the castle square each evening, so if you can swap Day 4's order, a dusk visit shows the village at its most festive (check current).

✈️ Flights

  • Basel-Mulhouse-Freiburg (EuroAirport) is the closest major airport, about an hour from Colmar by car, or by train with a connection.
  • Strasbourg and Frankfurt also work as connecting hubs, both reachable by regional train in under two hours.
  • Flying into Paris CDG and taking the TGV to Strasbourg, then a short regional train to Colmar, is a strong option for a single-connection trip.
  • Late-November and December weekends are peak market weeks, so book flights and trains early (check current fares).

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Uber and local taxis both operate in Colmar, easy to book from a phone at any hour.
  • The old town itself is fully walkable, so you'll mainly need either for the train station or a village day trip if you skip renting a car.
  • A taxi to Eguisheim or Riquewihr is workable for a single village, but adds up quickly if you're covering more than one in a day.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • A car earns its keep on Day 3, letting you reach both Riquewihr and Kaysersberg in one day instead of relying on weekend shuttles or reduced local buses.
  • Keep it through Day 4 morning for Eguisheim, then return it once you're back in Colmar for the afternoon.
  • Inside Colmar itself, skip it entirely: the old town is largely pedestrianized during market season and parking near the squares is limited.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Old Town, near Place de l'Ancienne Douane: puts Days 1 and 2's markets and half-timbered streets within easy walking distance.
  • Near Petite Venise: quieter at night, with the canal's Christmas lights just outside the door.
  • Near the train station: practical if you're renting a car for Day 3 and want a fast pickup, or want the shortest walk for Day 4's departure.
  • Book as soon as your dates are fixed: market-season rooms in and near the old town fill first, and December rates are often non-refundable (check cancellation terms).

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • Petite Venise's flat-bottomed punt tours run through the warmer months but pause or cut back sharply in winter, so don't count on one for this itinerary.
  • In market season the Lauch is part of the Christmas lights instead: the official market listing mentions illuminated boats for children's choirs there (check current).
  • If you're building a return trip for the warm season instead, the boat tour is worth adding back in then.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Standard Schengen entry rules apply, so non-EU visitors should confirm visa requirements before booking flights.
  • Travel insurance is worth adding given the pre-booked rental car and wine tasting slots, which are usually non-refundable.
  • Check Christmas week hours before locking in which days go where: the main Colmar markets are listed at 11 AM-5 PM on 24 December and 10 AM-8 PM on 25 December for 2026, while Eguisheim's market was closed 24 to 26 December in 2025.
