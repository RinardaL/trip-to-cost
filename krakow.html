4 Days in Krakow: Old Town, Wawel & Auschwitz-Birkenau

One of Europe's largest medieval squares, a hilltop royal castle above the Vistula, the revived lanes of the Kazimierz Jewish quarter, a solemn day at the Auschwitz-Birkenau Memorial, and a cathedral carved from salt, four days across Poland's most historic city.

Ideal SeasonApr – Jun, Sep
Est. Budget$500-700
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival & check-in near the Old Town

John Paul II International Airport (Balice) sits about 11 km west of the center, and a direct train or the 208/252 bus lines get you to the Old Town in roughly 20 to 30 minutes.

🚆 ~20 min train from Balice to Kraków Główny 💵 ~9 PLN (~$2.30) 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time
Midday

Rynek Główny & the Cloth Hall

Krakow's Main Market Square is one of the largest medieval town squares in Europe, and the 14th-century Cloth Hall (Sukiennice) running down its middle is still a working trading arcade, worth a slow wander for amber jewelry and wooden crafts.

🚶 Central, walkable from most Old Town hotels 💵 Free to wander 🕐 Any time, though early afternoon is liveliest
Evening

St. Mary's Basilica & the hourly bugle call

Step inside to see Veit Stoss's carved wooden altarpiece, then listen for the Hejnał mariacki, a bugle call played live from the taller tower every hour, on the hour, that famously cuts off mid-note in memory of a trumpeter struck down mid-warning centuries ago. Follow it with a first Polish dinner of pierogi and a local beer.

🚶 On Rynek Główny itself 💵 ~15 PLN church entry, ~35 PLN dinner 🕐 On the hour, any time from 9 AM to 11 PM
Morning

Wawel Castle & Cathedral

For centuries the seat of Poland's monarchy, Wawel Hill holds a royal castle with state rooms and a treasury, and a cathedral where nearly every Polish king is buried. Climb the Sigismund Bell tower for a view over the Vistula, and peek into the Dragon's Den cave at the base of the hill on your way down.

🚶 ~15 min walk from Rynek Główny 💵 ~55 PLN combined castle ticket 🕐 Right at the morning opening
Afternoon

Schindler's Factory Museum & Kazimierz's synagogues

Oskar Schindler's former enamelware factory is now a museum on Krakow's occupation during World War II, a sobering counterpart to the morning's royal history. A short walk away, Kazimierz's cluster of historic synagogues, including the Old Synagogue and the Remuh Synagogue and cemetery, traces centuries of the district's Jewish life.

🚋 Tram 3 or 24 to Kazimierz/Podgórze 💵 ~30 PLN factory museum 🕐 Early afternoon; book factory tickets ahead
Evening

Plac Nowy & Kazimierz after dark

Once a neglected corner of the city, Kazimierz has become Krakow's liveliest food and nightlife district, its candlelit courtyard bars and late-night zapiekanki stalls around Plac Nowy's round "Okrąglak" market hall drawing locals as much as visitors.

🚶 Central within Kazimierz 💵 ~10 PLN zapiekanka, drinks vary 🕐 After 7 PM, once the bars fill in
Morning

Travel to Oświęcim & Auschwitz I

The former Auschwitz I camp, about 70 km west of Krakow, is preserved today as a memorial and museum documenting the site where Nazi Germany murdered more than a million people, most of them Jews, between 1940 and 1945. A guided tour through the original camp buildings and exhibitions provides essential historical context; this is a memorial site, and visits are conducted with the quiet, respectful pace that calls for.

🚌 ~1 hr 15 min by pre-booked transfer or coach 💵 Free entry; ~80-150 PLN for a guided tour with transport 🕐 Morning entry slots; book weeks ahead
Afternoon

Auschwitz II-Birkenau

A short shuttle ride from Auschwitz I, the much larger Birkenau site holds the ruins of the camp's wooden barracks and the rail line that carried victims to the gas chambers. The grounds are vast and largely open-air; guides recommend the visit as the second half of the same day, allowing time to walk the site in silence.

🚐 Free shuttle between the two sites 💵 Included with the Auschwitz I visit 🕐 Early to mid-afternoon
Evening

Return to Krakow

Most visitors return to the city by early evening. It's worth leaving the rest of the day unstructured, a quiet walk along the Vistula or a low-key dinner near your hotel suits the day better than sightseeing or nightlife.

🚌 ~1 hr 15 min back to Krakow 💵 Included with the transfer 🕐 Early evening arrival back in the city
Morning

Wieliczka Salt Mine

A working salt mine since the 13th century, Wieliczka's underground route descends past carved chapels, salt-crystal chandeliers, and brine lakes to St. Kinga's Chapel, an entire church hewn from salt by miner-sculptors. It's one of the most-visited sites in Poland, and tickets for a specific entry time sell out on summer weekends.

🚌 ~40 min minibus from central Krakow 💵 ~130 PLN guided tour 🕐 Morning slot; book ahead for a fixed entry time
Afternoon

A milk-bar lunch & last-minute shopping

Back in the center, a bar mleczny (milk bar), a cafeteria-style canteen serving pierogi, żurek soup, and other Polish staples at communist-era prices, is one of the best-value lunches in Europe. Follow it with a last pass through the Cloth Hall for amber jewelry or wooden souvenirs before heading back.

🚶 Back in the Old Town 💵 ~20-25 PLN milk-bar lunch 🕐 Early afternoon, ahead of departure
Evening

Transfer to Kraków-Balice for departure

The same train or bus combination that brought you in runs back to Balice, so budget about 30 to 40 minutes depending on where you're staying.

🚆 ~20 to 30 min train or bus to the airport 💵 ~9 PLN 🕐 Leave at least 3 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Tipping isn't obligatory, but 10% at restaurants is common and appreciated when service is good.
  • Hand the tip directly to the server when paying, or say "razem" ("together") to include it in a card payment.
  • At milk bars and cafeteria-style counters, tipping isn't expected at all.

👗 Dress Code

  • Churches and synagogues, including St. Mary's Basilica and Wawel Cathedral, expect shoulders and knees covered.
  • For the Auschwitz-Birkenau visit specifically, dress modestly and comfortably out of respect for the site: covered shoulders, no beachwear or costume-like clothing, and shoes suited to walking largely outdoors regardless of weather.
  • Sturdy, comfortable shoes matter every day here, given cobblestones in the Old Town and long walking distances at Wawel and Birkenau.

🚋 Getting Around Krakow

  • Krakow's tram network covers the Old Town, Wawel, and Kazimierz, and a single ticket (~5 PLN, about $1.30) is valid for 20 minutes.
  • A 24-hour pass (~19 PLN) is worth it on Day 2, when you're moving between Wawel and Kazimierz multiple times.
  • The Old Town, Wawel Hill, and Kazimierz are close enough to walk between at a relaxed pace, so save transit tickets for the airport transfer.

⚠️ Respectful Conduct at Auschwitz-Birkenau

  • This is a memorial site and a former crime scene, not a tourist attraction: visitors are asked to remain quiet, avoid eating within the grounds, and keep phone use to a minimum.
  • The museum discourages posed or smiling photographs, especially on the railway tracks or gate structures; photography for personal remembrance is permitted in most areas but should be handled with sensitivity.
  • Children under 14 are not recommended for the visit given the subject matter, and the full route involves several hours of walking with limited seating.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Polish

  • Dzień dobry: good day/hello
  • Dziękuję: thank you
  • Poproszę rachunek: the bill, please

💳 Money & Payments

  • The Polish złoty (PLN) is the currency, not the euro, despite Poland's EU membership, and having some cash helps at milk bars and market stalls.
  • Contactless cards are accepted almost everywhere in Krakow, including trams and most souvenir stalls.
  • Withdraw złoty from bank-branded ATMs and decline any "dynamic currency conversion" prompt offering to charge you in your home currency instead.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Buy the Wawel combined ticket, not single entries

The combined ticket covering the State Rooms, the Cathedral, and the Treasury costs less than paying for each separately, and covers most of what's worth seeing.

🍽️

Eat at a milk bar at least once

A full pierogi lunch at a bar mleczny can cost a third of the same meal at a Rynek Główny restaurant, and it's a genuine slice of everyday Polish life.

🎫

Book Auschwitz-Birkenau and Wieliczka tickets weeks ahead

Both sites cap daily visitor numbers, and entry slots for the exact dates you want routinely sell out in high season.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌳

Kraków Barbican over the crowded Cloth Hall

This 15th-century fortified gatehouse, just north of the Old Town walls, gets a fraction of the visitors and is one of the best-preserved of its kind left in Europe.

⛪

St. Mary's tower climb, booked separately

Climbing the taller of the Basilica's two towers, where the hourly bugle call is actually played from, requires a separate ticket most visitors never notice at the door.

🕯️

Kazimierz in the late morning

Before the evening crowds arrive, Kazimierz's synagogues and quiet courtyards feel completely different, closer to a lived-in neighborhood than a nightlife district.

✈️ Flights

  • John Paul II International Airport Kraków-Balice (KRK) is the city's only major airport, about 11 km west of the center.
  • A direct train connects Balice to Kraków Główny station in about 20 minutes; buses 208 and 252 also run into the city.
  • Book Auschwitz-Birkenau and Wieliczka tickets before you land, since both routinely sell out for popular dates.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Bolt and Uber both operate well in Krakow and are generally more transparently priced than hailing a taxi on the street.
  • Street taxis near major tourist sites and the airport have a reputation for overcharging tourists, so booking through an app is safer.
  • For the Auschwitz-Birkenau day, a pre-booked minibus transfer or guided tour is more reliable than arranging transport on arrival.

🚌 Day-Trip Transport

  • Auschwitz-Birkenau: regular trains and coaches run from Kraków Główny to Oświęcim, but a pre-booked guided tour that includes transport removes the logistics on a day that deserves your full attention.
  • Wieliczka Salt Mine: minibuses depart frequently from near the Old Town, and the ~40-minute ride is included in most guided tour packages.
  • Both sites cap daily visitors, so book specific entry times in advance rather than showing up.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Old Town (Stare Miasto): puts Rynek Główny, St. Mary's Basilica, and Day 1's evening within a five-minute walk.
  • Kazimierz: a lively, slightly more affordable base for Day 2, an easy walk or short tram ride to both the Old Town and Wawel.
  • Podgórze: a quieter option across the river near Schindler's Factory, convenient for an early Day 3 departure to Oświęcim.

🎫 Guided Tours

  • A licensed guide is required to enter Auschwitz-Birkenau during peak visiting hours; independent visitors are grouped with a guide at the entrance.
  • Wawel Castle's State Rooms and Cathedral can be visited independently, but a guide adds useful context on Poland's royal history.
  • Free walking tours of the Old Town and Kazimierz run daily and are a good low-cost orientation on Day 1.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Standard Schengen entry rules apply, so non-EU visitors should confirm visa requirements before booking flights.
  • Remember that Polish złoty (PLN), not the euro, is the local currency, and budget a little time to withdraw cash on arrival.
  • Travel insurance is a smart add-on given the pre-booked Auschwitz-Birkenau and Wieliczka tickets, which are usually non-refundable.
