What ties a safari, a lemur island and a beach trip together?

This hub covers three places in East Africa and the western Indian Ocean: Tanzania on the mainland, and the islands of Madagascar and Mauritius. All three are built around nature rather than city sightseeing, whether that means wildlife, forest or coast. Their daily costs are far apart, so the choice matters.

Mauritius is the cheapest at $180 a day, $720-1,060 per person over five days. The plan covers Le Morne, the Seven Coloured Earths at Chamarel, Black River Gorges and a catamaran day to Île aux Cerfs, and it ends with a day in Port Louis.

Madagascar is $300 a day, $1,550-2,050 per person over six days, and it suits people drawn to wildlife found nowhere else: indri in Andasibe-Mantadia, the baobab avenue at sunset and night walks in Kirindy. Tanzania is $450 a day, $1,800-2,700 per person over five days, with game drives in Ngorongoro and the Serengeti. It is for travellers for whom a safari is the whole point.

Does the season matter for all three?

Yes, and the windows overlap only from June to October: Madagascar runs from April to November, Mauritius from May to December and Tanzania from June to October. Madagascar's window avoids the heavy rains of December to March, and Tanzania's matches the dry season, when game gathers around water. Mauritius runs from its cooler months into early summer, and January to March carries the highest cyclone risk.

The Serengeti window is the narrowest. The trip page starts the Madagascar indri walk by 7 AM, so plan an early start there whenever you go. Mauritius reaches furthest into the year, ending in December.

If your dates are fixed outside June to October, Madagascar still works in April, May and November, and Mauritius in May, November and December, while the safari plan does not match. In that case choose one of the islands and keep the safari for another year.

Planning, combining and saving

Each trip starts at its own airport: Kilimanjaro International for Tanzania and Ivato in Antananarivo for Madagascar. Nothing in the itineraries links the three, so combining them means separate flights and a separate budget. Add the totals, not the daily figures, and check airline routings yourself.

The windows of Tanzania and Mauritius both cover June to October, so those two can share a season. The safari ends with the road back on day five, so leave a spare day before an onward flight. Madagascar also includes a flight of about an hour from the capital to Morondava, which needs its own buffer.

For savings, look at length as well as daily cost. Madagascar runs a day longer than Tanzania and still has the lower total. The balloon safari on day four of the Tanzania plan is marked optional on the trip page, so ask what is included before comparing quotes. Mauritius has the lowest total of the three.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a trip to East Africa and the Indian Ocean cost?

Our 3 East Africa & Indian Ocean itineraries cost from $720 to $2,700 per person for the whole trip, which is about $180 to $450 a day. Prices cover lodging, meals, local transport and activities, and exclude international flights.

Which East Africa & Indian Ocean itinerary is the cheapest?

Mauritius ($720-1,060), Madagascar ($1,550-2,050), Tanzania ($1,800-2,700) are the three lowest, per person and excluding flights.

How many days do I need in East Africa and the Indian Ocean?

Our East Africa & Indian Ocean itineraries run from 5 to 6 days, and the most common length is 5 days. Each page has a day picker with a price for every length from one day up to the full plan.

When is the best time to visit East Africa and the Indian Ocean?

It depends on the trip, and each itinerary lists its ideal season. For example: Madagascar, Apr – Nov; Mauritius, May – Dec; Tanzania, Jun – Oct. The table above shows the ideal season for every East Africa & Indian Ocean trip.

Does the Madagascar trip involve internal travel?

Yes. Day one is a road transfer of roughly three and a half to four hours, about 150 km, from Antananarivo to Andasibe. Day three is a flight to Morondava for the baobabs and Kirindy, because the trip page rules out the 700 km road as impractical. Allow a spare day around your return flight, because internal schedules can shift.

Can Mauritius be added to a safari or Madagascar trip?

It can be added as a separate itinerary, and the seasons overlap from June to October, which helps. But the trips do not connect: each has its own airport and budget. Add both totals, check flight routings, and allow a rest day in between.
